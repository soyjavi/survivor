# Home Canning Basics

Canning seals cooked food in jars and heats it to kill microbes, so it lasts for months or years without power. Safety depends on the food's acidity and on heating it long enough at the right temperature. Canning is unforgiving: a shortcut in one step can make the whole jar dangerous even when it looks perfect.

![Two panels split by line-dashed: left pot-with-lid labelled Water-bath, right pot-with-lid labelled Pressure, jar beside with bracket over headspace; labels Water-bath canner, Pressure canner, Headspace; shows the two equipment types. NEEDS: jar-headspace](assets/home-canning-basics-1.svg)

## Why it matters

Canned food is one of the most compact ways to store a meal. A jar of beans, a jar of tomatoes or a jar of fruit is ready to eat without fuel, water or a fridge, and it survives moves, floods and long periods of neglect far better than fresh food. Because the risks are serious, learning the rules before you start is the most important part of the skill, not a formality.

## Two kinds of food

- **High-acid foods** such as fruit, tomatoes with added acid, pickles and jam can be processed in boiling water.
- **Low-acid foods** such as vegetables, meat, fish and beans need a pressure canner. Boiling water does not reach a high enough temperature to kill the dangerous microbes in them.

> @image Pressure canner with its gauge and a row of filled jars on a kitchen bench

Tomatoes are the difficult case. Many tomato varieties sit close to the acid line, so recipes add lemon juice or citric acid to make them safe in a water bath. Use tested recipes for any food you are unsure about, and never change the proportions of acid, sugar or salt in them.

## Equipment

- Clean glass jars with new lids and rings.
- A large pot with a rack for boiling-water canning, or a pressure canner for low-acid food.
- Jar lifter, funnel and clean cloths.
- A timer and a thermometer for checking temperatures.

A pressure canner needs its gauge checked regularly, and its vent must be clear before you start. A gauge that reads wrongly means the food is not heated to the temperature the recipe requires.

## Steps

1. Wash the jars and keep them hot.
2. Fill with food, leaving the headspace the recipe specifies.
3. Remove air bubbles and wipe the rim clean.
4. Put on the lid and ring, finger-tight.
5. Process for the time the food needs.
6. Let the jars cool undisturbed, then check that the lids are sealed. A sealed lid does not flex when pressed.

![Cross-section: jar outline with sealed lid, bracket on the top gap, line-dashed food level, leader labels; labels Headspace, Food level, Sealed lid; both food level and gap must be right before heating.](assets/home-canning-basics-2.svg)

Use a non-metallic tool to slide along the inside of the jar and release trapped air. Then recheck the headspace, because removing bubbles can lower the food level.

## Adjusting for altitude

Water boils at a lower temperature at higher altitude, so the processing time for boiling-water canning must be increased. Pressure canners also need a higher pressure setting above a set height. Check the altitude correction in the recipe you are following, because a recipe written for a low-lying kitchen will under-process food on a hill.

## Common mistakes

- **Using the wrong method:** processing vegetables in a water bath is the most serious error.
- **Wrong headspace:** too little or too much space prevents a proper seal.
- **Cracked or old lids:** they fail to seal, and the food spoils.
- **Moving the jars too soon:** a jar that is disturbed while cooling may not seal.

## Checklist

- [ ] The food is classified as high-acid or low-acid before you start.
- [ ] Jars, lids and rings are clean and new.
- [ ] Headspace matches the recipe.
- [ ] Each jar is sealed and labelled before storage.

## Storage

Store sealed jars in a cool, dark, dry place. Label them with the contents and date. Use any unsealed jar within a few days, or refrigerate it.

## Troubleshooting

If a jar leaks liquid during processing, the headspace or the packing was wrong, and the jar should be refrigerated and eaten quickly. If food floats or the seal fails in many jars at once, check the lids and the processing time. If a jar loses its seal after a few weeks, treat it as unsealed. Never assume a jar is safe because the lid looks tight.

## Practice before you need it

Can one jar of a simple, tested recipe, such as plain tomatoes with added acid, before you attempt a large batch of low-acid food. Practise the lid check, the headspace measurement and the cooling routine. Writing down each batch and its processing time gives you a record to compare against the next one.

## When it fails

If a lid does not seal, the jar can be reprocessed with a new lid only if the recipe allows it, otherwise refrigerate it and eat it within a few days. If a jar is bulging, leaking or spurting when opened, discard it without tasting.

**Warning:** never can low-acid food in boiling water alone, because it can cause botulism. Discard any jar that is bulging, leaking, spurting when opened or smells wrong, and do not taste the contents of a suspect jar, since botulism toxin can be present without any change in smell.
