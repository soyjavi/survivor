# Storing Potatoes, Onions and Root Crops

Potatoes, onions, carrots, beetroot and similar crops can feed a household for months when stored well. Their enemies are heat, moisture, light, physical damage and rot organisms that spread from one damaged item to its neighbours. Success depends on harvesting carefully, curing where needed, and keeping each crop in the conditions it prefers.

## Harvesting With Storage in Mind

Harvest on a dry day when the soil is not waterlogged. Lift crops with a fork rather than a spade to avoid cuts and bruises. Damaged roots and bulbs rot first, so set them aside for immediate use. Avoid harvesting too early, since immature crops store poorly, and avoid leaving them in the ground after the tops have died back for long, since wet weather can start rot.

1. Stop watering several weeks before harvest for potatoes and onions.
2. Lift gently and shake off loose soil without washing.
3. Sort the crop into three groups: perfect for storage, minor damage for early use, and rotten for disposal.

## Curing

Curing hardens skins and dries cut or bruised surfaces so they can heal. Onions and garlic need it most. Lay bulbs in a single layer on a dry, shaded, well-ventilated surface for one to two weeks. Potatoes benefit from a short curing period of about two weeks in the dark at a mild temperature, which thickens the skin.

- Keep curing crops out of direct sun, which turns potatoes green and bitter.
- Protect against rain and heavy dew.
- Do not stack crops more than a few layers deep during curing.

## Ideal Storage Conditions

Most root crops store best cool, dark and humid but not wet. Potatoes do well around 4 to 10 degrees Celsius. Onions prefer something drier and cooler, around 0 to 4 degrees Celsius, with good airflow. Carrots and beetroot keep well packed in damp sand or sawdust in a cool place.

1. Store potatoes in ventilated sacks or crates, never in sealed plastic.
2. Keep onions hung in mesh bags or braided, in a dry, airy room.
3. Pack carrots and beetroot upright or in layers in clean, slightly damp sand, with the crowns exposed.
4. Keep potatoes away from onions, since onions release gases that speed potato sprouting.

## Checking Stored Crops

Inspect the store every one to two weeks. Remove any item that is soft, shrivelled, sprouting heavily or showing mould. A single rotten item can infect many others within days, so act early.

- Potatoes that sprout slightly can be used, but remove the sprouts and any green areas.
- Onions with soft necks should be used immediately, since they will not keep.
- Carrots that have gone limp or slimy should be discarded.

## Choosing Varieties for Storage

Some varieties keep far longer than others. Main-crop potatoes with firm skins generally store better than early or waxy types, which bruise and sprout sooner. Onions with dry, papery skins and tight necks keep longer than those with thick, soft layers. Where you have a choice, plant and save the varieties that have stored best in past seasons, and note them in your records.

Carrots and beetroot respond well to being left in the ground until the first hard frost, which sweetens them and strengthens the root. Lift them on a dry day, twist off the leaves close to the crown without cutting into the root, and store only sound roots.

## Pest and Damage Control

Mice, rats, slugs and insects find stored roots attractive. Keep stores closed when not in use, lay traps outside the storage area, and inspect bags and crates for gnawing. Light, regular checks reveal problems early, before they spread. Remove any roots with holes or tunnels and use them immediately.

## Recording the Store

Write a simple log of each crop, the date stored, the location, and the number of items. Record each inspection, noting losses and their causes. Over a season, this record shows which crops store best in your conditions and which need improvement, and it helps you plan how much to plant and when to eat each crop.

**Warning:** Discard any potato that is green under the skin or tastes bitter, since solanine can cause nausea and worse. Never store potatoes in a sealed container where gases can build up. Rotting crops produce mould spores that can cause respiratory irritation, so wear a simple mask when clearing out a badly affected store and dispose of rotten material well away from the stores.
