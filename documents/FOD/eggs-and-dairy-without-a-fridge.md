# Eggs and Dairy Without a Fridge

Eggs and dairy spoil quickly in heat, and they are among the most valuable foods to protect during a shortage because they supply protein and fat. Some keep much longer than expected if you choose them carefully and store them correctly. The aim is to keep what keeps well and use the rest first.

## Why it matters

A single hen laying through the week can feed a household protein for many days, and a few litres of milk turned into hard cheese or ghee can outlast the power cut by months. Losing these foods to heat is costly, and eating them when they have turned can cause illness. Knowing which dairy keeps and which does not lets you plan the week's meals around the foods that will not last.

## Eggs

- Eggs keep for a few weeks in a cool place if they are unwashed. Commercially washed eggs must be refrigerated.
- Store them point down, in a cool, dark place.
- Test an egg before use: a fresh egg sinks in water, an old one floats.
- Crack each egg into a separate bowl before adding it to food, so one bad egg does not spoil the whole dish.

> @image Unwashed brown eggs packed point down in a shallow tray, in a cool cellar

Mark the date on each egg with a pencil, or keep a record of when each batch was collected. Use the oldest eggs first, and cook any eggs in doubt in a dish where they will be fully heated, such as an omelette or a cake.

## Hard cheese and butter

- Hard, aged cheese keeps much longer than soft cheese.
- Butter keeps for a few days in a cool place. Clarified butter (ghee) keeps longer.
- Wrap cheese in cloth and keep it in a cool, dark, dry place.

Check cheese each day. Cut away any mould that is only on the surface of hard cheese, but discard soft cheese that shows mould.

## Milk

- Fresh milk spoils within hours in heat. Boil it before use and drink it quickly.
- Powdered milk keeps for months when sealed and dry.

![Pot-with-lid open on coals, wooden spoon stirring at the base, arrow-curved around the base; labels Coals, Stir; constant stirring stops milk scorching on the bottom. NEEDS: wooden-spoon, milk](assets/eggs-and-dairy-without-a-fridge-3.svg)

## Dried and preserved dairy

- Store powdered milk in airtight containers.
- Use it in cooking as soon as you open the packet.

Powdered milk reconstituted with clean water can replace fresh milk in porridge, bread and sauces. Mix it to the strength on the packet, then boil it, because reconstituted milk spoils as fast as fresh milk once it is made up.

## Adjusting for conditions

Cool cellars and shaded north-facing rooms slow spoilage. A room that stays above about 20 °C, or a stretch of hot weather, shortens every timeline in this guide. In hot weather, prefer hard cheese and powdered milk, and buy or collect fresh milk only in the cool hours of the morning. Keep eggs away from stoves, windows and direct sun.

## Common mistakes

- **Washing eggs:** the protective coating is removed, and they spoil faster.
- **Storing eggs in warm places:** heat speeds up spoilage.
- **Leaving milk in the sun:** it can turn within an hour.
- **Keeping soft cheese too long:** it softens and sours quickly.

## Checklist

- [ ] Eggs are unwashed and stored point down.
- [ ] Each egg is cracked into its own bowl before use.
- [ ] Hard cheese is wrapped in cloth and kept dry.
- [ ] Milk is boiled before drinking.

## Troubleshooting

Eggs that have been kept too long may still look normal in the shell, so the float test and the crack-in-a-bowl rule are your checks. If a milk or cheese smell is only faintly off, cooking will not make it safe, so discard it. Dairy that separates or turns grainy after boiling is usually past its best and should not be eaten.

## Practice before you need it

Test the float method and the boiling routine in a normal week, with eggs you have just bought. Learn how long your butter lasts in your pantry, and how your cheese changes over a week of cool storage. Small notes of this kind make the difference when supplies are short.

## When it fails

If an egg floats or smells sulphurous, throw it away. If milk has curdled or has a sour smell, do not drink it and discard it. If cheese turns slimy, discard it.

**Warning:** discard any dairy or egg that smells sour, looks strange or has been warm for longer than a few hours. Raw eggs can carry bacteria, so cook them thoroughly before eating.
