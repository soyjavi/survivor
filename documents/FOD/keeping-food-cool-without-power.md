# Keeping Food Cool Without Power

A closed fridge keeps food cold for a few hours. A closed freezer keeps it frozen for about a day and a half if it is full. Plan for the outage before it happens, because the first hour decides how much food is still safe to eat when the power returns.

![Row of three panels joined by line-dashed: hut as cool room, tin as insulated box with ice-cube, stream-bed as cold stream; labels Cool room, Cool box, Cellar or stream; each option suits different foods. NEEDS: cellar, cool-box](assets/keeping-food-cool-without-power-1.svg)

## Why it matters

A power cut that lasts a day can spoil meat, dairy and leftovers that would otherwise feed a household for several days. Most of the loss happens quietly, because the fridge looks closed and cold while the food inside warms slowly. Knowing how long each store lasts and acting early turns a crisis into a manageable few hours of work.

## Equipment and materials

- A thermometer for the fridge and freezer, which should be readable from outside the door.
- Clean containers for water, which can be frozen as blocks.
- An insulated cool box and a supply of ice packs.
- A marker and a notebook for logging the time the power went out.
- Towels or blankets, to wrap the cool box and cover the freezer.

Keep the cool box lid sealed with tape when it is not in use, so that cold air does not leak away each time someone looks inside.

## Before an outage

- Freeze water in clean containers, so the freezer holds a thermal mass.
- Keep a thermometer inside the fridge.
- Know where the coolest room is in the house.
- Keep a small cool box and some ice packs ready.

> @image Plastic water bottles frozen solid and packed around food in a freezer

Fill a freezer to its capacity before an outage, and keep frozen bottles in the empty spaces. A full freezer holds its cold far longer than an empty one, because the frozen mass absorbs heat slowly.

## First hours

1. Keep the fridge and freezer doors closed.
2. Group food by type so you can find it fast.
3. Move the most perishable food to the coldest place.
4. Write down the time the power went out, so you can judge how long food has been warm.

## Cold storage without power

- **Cool room:** the ground floor on the shady side of the house, away from the stove.
- **Cool box:** place food in an insulated box with ice or frozen water bottles.
- **Cellar or cold stream:** keep sealed containers in cold running water, not in direct contact with the water if it is not clean.

## Adjusting for conditions

In hot weather, the timelines shrink, and a cool box in the sun may warm faster than the fridge did. In cold weather, an unheated room can hold food cool for days, but freezing temperatures can split bottles and damage packs. Wrap containers in cloth in the cold, and move them away from draughts. A room that is warm from cooking should not be used for storage, even if it looks cool.

## Use ice safely

- Freeze water in clean containers before the outage starts.
- Keep ice in a separate container from drinking water.
- Use ice from clean water only.

## Decide what to eat first

1. Cooked food and leftovers.
2. Raw meat, fish and dairy.
3. Fruit and vegetables.
4. Dried, canned and sealed food, which lasts longest.

## Common mistakes

- **Opening the freezer repeatedly:** the cold escapes in minutes.
- **Mixing ice with drinking water:** the ice may carry contamination.
- **Keeping food in direct sun:** even a cool box fails in a hot car or on a sunny step.
- **Waiting too long to decide:** the food may already be unsafe by the time you check it.

## Troubleshooting

If the cool box warms up faster than expected, check the seal of its lid and the amount of ice. Add more frozen containers and reduce how often the box is opened. If a freezer thermometer shows a rise above freezing, decide quickly which food to cook or preserve first, since refreezing is only sensible if the food still feels very cold.

## Practice before you need it

Run a half-day practice once a year. Close the fridge and freezer, fill the cool box and note the temperature every hour. The results show you how long each store actually holds, which is far more useful than the general timelines in this guide.

## When it fails

If the power stays off longer than expected, cook or preserve the food that is still good, such as drying or canning it. If a freezer has thawed but the food still feels cold with ice crystals, it can be refrozen. If food is warm, slimy or smells wrong, discard it.

**Warning:** once food has been above about 5 °C for more than two hours, do not keep it. See Food Spoilage and Safety for the full rules on warm food.
