# Pest Control in the Pantry

Insects and rodents destroy food and carry disease. Prevention is far easier than removal, because once pests are established, they spread through stored grain, flour and dried goods quickly. A pantry that is sealed, clean and checked regularly rarely needs active pest control.

![Pantry side view: ground-line, jar, tin and bucket on a raised shelf above it, trap beside the wall, line-solid wall; labels Raised storage, Trap along wall; raised storage and wall-side traps stop pests early. NEEDS: rodent-trap, shelf](assets/pest-control-in-the-pantry-1.svg)

## Why it matters

A single pest pair can contaminate a sack of grain in weeks, and rodents carry bacteria in their droppings and urine. Food that is nibbled, soiled or webbed is often unusable, so a small problem can cost a season's stock. Pests also damage packaging, which lets in moisture and speeds up spoilage in the food that is still sound. The cost of prevention is a few sealed containers and some time each month.

## Equipment and materials

- Glass, metal or thick plastic containers with tight lids.
- Snap traps or humane traps, with bait that is safe to handle.
- A torch, gloves and a mask for inspection and cleaning.
- Steel wool or metal flashing to close gaps.
- A freezer or a sealed bag for suspicious food.

Use gloves whenever you handle traps, bait or dead animals, and wash your hands afterwards even when you wore them.

## Prevent

- Store food in sealed containers made of glass, metal or thick plastic. Rodents chew through paper and thin plastic.
- Keep the pantry clean. Sweep spilled food the same day.
- Seal gaps around doors, windows and pipes.
- Keep food off the floor and away from walls.

## Insects

1. Inspect new stock for holes, webbing and live insects.
2. Freeze suspicious grain or flour for two to three days, then store it sealed.
3. Use dried bay leaves in containers as a deterrent.
4. Remove infested food at once and clean the shelf.

> @svg Close-up of a grain kernel with a hole, leader to hole, caution; labels Exit hole, Check whole stock; an exit hole means an insect already left. NEEDS: grain-kernel, insect

Check the stock on a schedule, not only when you notice a problem. Insect larvae often hide inside grain for weeks before any adult appears, so a monthly look at the containers is the earliest warning you will get.

## Rodents

- Set traps in the pantry and along walls, with bait on the trigger.
- Use a cat, if you have one, but do not rely on it.
- Dispose of dead rodents carefully, wearing gloves.

> @image Snap traps set with bait along the base of a pantry wall, beside a sealed storage tin

Place traps where rodents travel: along walls, in corners and behind stored items. Check them every day, and replace bait that has been touched or dried out.

## Keep rodent droppings out of food

Remove droppings and urine stains with a damp cloth and a bleach solution. Do not sweep them dry, because the dust carries disease.

## Adjusting for conditions

In warm, humid seasons, insects multiply quickly, so inspect stock twice as often and keep containers in the coolest, driest part of the house. In cold seasons, rodents come indoors looking for warmth, so check the gaps around pipes and doors before the first cold snap. After a flood or a storm, check the pantry for signs of rodents that moved in when the house was damaged.

## Common mistakes

- **Leaving food in paper sacks:** insects and rodents enter easily.
- **Cleaning dry:** sweeping droppings raises dust that can carry disease.
- **Storing damp grain:** moisture attracts insects and mould.
- **Ignoring small signs:** a few insects today become a full infestation within weeks.

## Checklist

- [ ] All food is in sealed containers off the floor.
- [ ] Gaps around doors and pipes are closed.
- [ ] New stock is inspected before it is stored.
- [ ] Traps are checked and cleaned each week.

## Troubleshooting

If you see insects flying near a light, look for the source in the nearest container, not in the air. If you find gnawed packaging, check every container on that shelf, since rodents often return to a food source. If a trap is set off but the bait is untouched, move it closer to the wall and check the route the animal takes.

## Practice before you need it

Set and check traps in an empty corner in a normal month, so you learn how they work, how to reset them safely and how to dispose of an animal. Practise the monthly inspection of containers and shelves, and keep a record of what you find. A household that has done the routine before will notice the first sign of trouble quickly.

## When it fails

If an infestation spreads despite these steps, remove all food from the affected shelves, freeze or discard what is infested, and clean every surface with a bleach solution. Check the wider house, because pests move between rooms.

**Warning:** do not use rat poison near food or children. Poisoned rodents can also poison pets and wild animals. Wear gloves and a mask when cleaning droppings.
