# Storing Grains, Flour and Legumes

Grains, flour and dried beans keep for months only when moisture, air and insects stay out. Each of these three threats acts in a different way, so a good store addresses all of them together: dry food, sealed containers and regular checks.

![Layout: bucket with lid centre, three leader lines to step-box labels Dry food, Sealed container, Regular checks, bay leaf leaf beside it; moisture, air and insects each need their own defence. NEEDS: bay-leaf, insect](assets/storing-grains-flour-and-legumes-1.svg)

## Why it matters

Grains, flour and beans make up the bulk of most household stores, because they supply most of the energy a person needs. Losing a sack to damp or insects can mean losing weeks of food at once. Good storage costs little more than a clean container and a regular look, and it is one of the highest returns on effort in any food plan.

## Equipment and materials

- Food-grade buckets with tight lids, or glass jars with clamp lids.
- Metal tins with sealing gaskets, for small quantities.
- A moisture-check method, such as a clean spoon and a sample on a plate.
- A freezer or a sealed bag for treating suspicious food.
- Dried bay leaves, which help but do not replace sealing.

Label each container with the contents and the date of filling. Grains and beans look alike in the dark, and an unlabelled container is easy to forget.

## Containers

- Use airtight containers with lids: food-grade buckets, glass jars or metal tins.
- Avoid paper sacks and thin plastic bags for long storage. Insects chew through them.
- Keep containers off the floor and away from walls.

> @image Food-grade bucket with a tight lid, standing on a wooden pallet in a dry storeroom

## Keep them dry

- Store food where humidity stays low.
- Check containers for condensation after rain or in humid weather.
- Dry any grain or beans that feel damp before storing them.

Fill containers to the top, or fill the empty space with a clean cloth, so less air is left to carry moisture and insect eggs.

![Cross-section: bucket, grain filled to the top, cloth packed in the empty space, arrow-straight pointing to the cloth; labels Grain, Cloth, Less air; a fuller container leaves less air. NEEDS: cloth](assets/storing-grains-flour-and-legumes-2.svg)

## Control insects

1. Check dry products for webbing or holes before storing them.
2. If you suspect insects, freeze grain or flour for two to three days, then seal it.
3. Add dried bay leaves to the container. They help but do not replace sealing.
4. Check the stock every month.

## Shelf life

- **White rice:** very long when dry and sealed.
- **Whole-grain flour:** shorter, because its oils go rancid. Use it within a few months.
- **Dried beans:** long, but old beans take much longer to cook.

Oats and whole-grain products sit between these two. Keep them dry and cool, and open a small test sample each month to check the smell.

> @image Jars of dried beans and rice on a shelf, each labelled with a filling date

## Adjusting for conditions

In humid climates, store food on raised shelves with a gap from the wall, and add a moisture absorber such as a packet of dry rice or a clean cloth bag of salt in the storeroom. In hot climates, keep flour in the coolest part of the house, since heat speeds up the oil spoilage in whole-grain products. In cold climates, avoid storing food in unheated sheds where condensation forms on the inside of containers.

## Common mistakes

- **Storing damp food:** moisture leads to mould and insects.
- **Using paper sacks:** they let in insects and dust.
- **Keeping flour too long:** whole-grain flour turns bitter as its oils spoil.
- **Overlooking old beans:** they may never soften fully, so they waste fuel.

## Checklist

- [ ] Containers are airtight and off the floor.
- [ ] Food is dry before storage.
- [ ] Suspicious grain or flour has been frozen and sealed.
- [ ] The stock is checked each month.

## Troubleshooting

If you find insects in a container, remove the food, freeze or discard it, and wash the container with hot soapy water before refilling. If flour smells bitter or stale, use it for cooking only in small amounts or discard it. If beans stay hard after long soaking, discard them.

## Practice before you need it

Open one container of each grain in a normal month and check it for smell, colour and insects. Write down how long each food stayed good in your store. These notes tell you which containers need attention first, and they show you how your climate affects the shelf life of each food.

## When it fails

If you find insects in a container, follow the steps above and check the containers beside it, since insects spread from one to the next. If the container has a damp mark, dry it fully before refilling.

**Warning:** discard grain or flour with a sour smell, mould or insects. Do not eat food that has been stored in a damp place where mould is visible.
