# Salting and Drying Meat and Fish

Salt draws water out of meat and fish and makes it hard for microbes to grow. Drying removes the rest of the water. Together they preserve food without power, and they turn a single animal into food that can last for weeks. Success depends almost entirely on starting with fresh, clean material and keeping insects away while the meat dries.

## Why it matters

A single animal or a day's catch can feed a household for weeks if it is preserved in time. Fresh meat and fish spoil within a day in warm weather, so the window to act is short. Salting and drying give that window a chance to become a store, and they let a household keep protein for the lean months without any fuel or refrigeration.

## Equipment and materials

- Non-iodised salt, weighed on a kitchen scale.
- A sharp knife, a clean board and a sturdy basin.
- A container with a lid, for salting in layers.
- Cord, clean cloth and wooden sticks for hanging strips.
- Fine insect mesh, to make a screen around the drying area.

Keep one knife and one board for raw meat and fish, and clean them with hot water and soap after each use. Cross-contamination is a common route for spoilage.

## Start with fresh, clean meat

- Use only fresh meat and fish that smell clean.
- Cut it into thin strips, no thicker than about 1 cm, so it dries evenly.
- Remove all fat, because fat goes rancid.

> @svg Board with thin strips laid side by side, knife cutting across them, arrow-straight showing the cut direction, bracket for strip thickness; label Thin even strips; thin strips dry at the same rate. NEEDS: meat-strip, board

Cut against the grain, which makes the strips tender and easier to chew after drying. Keep the pieces cold until the moment they are salted, and work quickly so the raw material does not warm up.

## Salting

1. Rub the pieces with non-iodised salt, using about 10 to 15 per cent of the weight.
2. Pack them in a container with a little salt between layers.
3. Leave them in a cool place for 12 to 48 hours, depending on thickness.
4. Rinse off the salt and pat dry.

Thinner strips take the shorter time, and thicker pieces need the full period. Fish, which is thinner and fattier, needs careful timing.

## Drying

- Hang the strips in a breezy, shaded place with insect protection.
- Drying takes several days. The meat should be firm and dry, not soft or sticky.
- Keep the air moving and keep flies away.

## Adjusting for conditions

In humid weather, meat takes much longer to dry and the surface can spoil first, so choose the coolest, driest day and bring the strips in at night. In very dry, windy air, the surface may harden before the centre dries, so cut strips thinner and hang them further apart. Fish dries faster than thick meat, but its oils go rancid sooner, so use it within days.

## Common mistakes

- **Using old or unclean meat:** salt cannot fix spoiled material.
- **Leaving fat on the piece:** it goes rancid and spoils the batch.
- **Thick strips:** the centre stays wet and grows microbes.
- **Hanging in the sun or in damp air:** the surface cooks or spoils before the inside dries.

## Checklist

- [ ] Meat or fish is fresh and smells clean.
- [ ] Strips are no thicker than about 1 cm, with fat removed.
- [ ] Salt is weighed, not guessed.
- [ ] Insect screens are in place during drying.

## Check before eating

- The meat should be dry, firm and dark.
- It should smell clean. Any sour, rotten or ammonia smell means discard.
- Mould on the surface can be wiped off only if the meat underneath is firm and clean. When in doubt, discard it.

## Troubleshooting

If the strips are too salty, soak them in clean water for a short time before cooking and change the water once. If the surface turns hard and dark while the middle stays soft, the air was too dry and too fast, so cut thinner pieces and hang them in a more sheltered spot. If flies have laid eggs on the strips, discard any strip with visible eggs or maggots.

## Practice before you need it

Preserve a small batch of a cheap cut in a normal week, with a weighed salt and a timed salting. Keep a record of thickness, salting time, drying days and the final texture. A small trial shows the right thickness for your climate before a whole animal depends on it.

## When it fails

If the strips stay soft after several days, the air is too humid or the pieces are too thick. Move them to a drier spot and cut thinner pieces next time. If they smell off during drying, discard them without tasting.

**Warning:** dried meat and fish are not safe if they were not fresh to begin with. Cook them thoroughly before eating if you are unsure, and never eat meat or fish that smells sour, rotten or like ammonia.
