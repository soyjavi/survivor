# Food Safety With Limited Water

When water is scarce, the temptation is to skip washing or to reuse dirty water until it is gone. Both raise the risk of illness. Food safety with little water relies on choosing how you use each litre: wash what carries the most risk, cook thoroughly, and keep food and hands separate from contamination. Small, consistent habits matter more than any single technique.

## Priorities for Scarce Water

Water should be allocated in a clear order. Drinking comes first, then cooking, then hand hygiene, and finally washing food and dishes. Keep a written tally of how much water is available each day so decisions are made calmly rather than in a rush.

1. Set aside a fixed daily ration for drinking and cooking.
2. Use any clean water left after cooking for washing hands or produce.
3. Reuse water only in the order described, never for drinking after it has touched contaminated items.

## Washing Produce

Produce that is eaten raw carries the highest risk, since no cooking step removes bacteria or parasites. Leafy greens, berries and herbs need the most attention. Root crops that will be peeled or cooked can be brushed rather than soaked.

- Use a bowl rather than running water, so each litre is used in a controlled way.
- Immerse produce, swish it gently, then lift it out so dirt falls to the bottom.
- Change the water once or twice rather than rinsing repeatedly under a trickle.
- Brush firm vegetables with a clean dry brush before any wet washing.

## Hand Hygiene With Little Water

Hands spread most household contamination. If soap is scarce, wash with a small measure of water and rub thoroughly for at least twenty seconds, covering all surfaces, fingertips and thumbs. A dry alternative such as an alcohol-based gel works when available, but it does not remove dirt, so use it only on clean hands.

1. Wet hands with a small cup of water.
2. Apply soap or ash, then rub palms, backs, between fingers and under nails.
3. Rinse with a controlled trickle, or pour gently from a jug.
4. Dry with a clean cloth or let air dry fully.

## Cooking With Little Water

Cooking needs less water than most people expect. Use a lidded pot to trap steam, choose quick-cooking foods where possible, and use stews or soups so that the cooking liquid is eaten. Bring water to a rolling boil for at least one minute at sea level before using it for cooking or for drinking if it has been treated.

- Cook grains in exact measured amounts so no water is wasted in draining.
- Reduce cooking liquid rather than pouring it away.
- Cook meat and eggs until steaming hot throughout and fully firm.

## Water Quality Basics

Water that looks clear can still be unsafe. Treat any water of uncertain origin before using it for drinking or for washing food that will be eaten raw. Boiling is the most reliable household method when fuel allows. Where boiling is not possible, use a recognised chemical treatment such as chlorine tablets, following the instructions carefully, and allow the full contact time before use.

Let suspended dirt settle before treatment. Pour the clear water off the top, or pass it through a clean cloth, since turbid water reduces the effectiveness of disinfection. Store treated water in clean, covered containers and label each container with the treatment date.

## Keeping Food Preparation Areas Clean

Work surfaces should be wiped with clean water and dried after every use. Keep a dedicated board and knife for raw meat and a separate set for vegetables eaten raw. Cover food when it is stored, and keep pests away from the preparation area, since they carry contamination from waste to food.

## Planning Water Use Each Day

Make a daily plan before the day begins. Estimate how much water is needed for drinking, cooking and hand hygiene, and set aside the rest for washing food. A written plan prevents rushed decisions when stocks run low. Review it each evening and adjust for the next day.

**Warning:** Raw produce washed in unsafe or contaminated water can carry illness. Never wash food in water you would not drink, and never use water that has been used for washing bodies or waste for food preparation. Keep cooked and raw food apart, and discard anything left out for more than two hours in warm conditions.
