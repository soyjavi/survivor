# Solar Food Dryers

A solar dryer uses sunlight and airflow to dry food. It needs no fuel and can preserve a large harvest in a few days of good weather. Compared with drying on open racks, a dryer keeps insects, dew and dust away from the food and dries it more evenly.

## Why it matters

A harvest that arrives in one week needs to be preserved in the next few days, before it spoils. A solar dryer lets a household handle that peak without fuel, and it works in places where open-air drying is hard because of dust, animals or fast-changing weather. The same box can dry fruit, vegetables and herbs, so one piece of simple equipment serves most of the year.

## How it works

Air enters at the bottom, is warmed by the sun in a dark collector, and rises through the food trays. Moist air leaves through vents at the top. The warmth and the moving air together remove water from the food.

## Equipment and materials

- A wooden or cardboard box, with a clear top of glass or plastic.
- Dark paint or dark cloth for the base.
- Insect mesh for the vents and the trays.
- Wooden strips for the tray supports, and a stick to prop the glass open.
- Sealant or tape for the joints.

Use a sheet of glass rather than plastic if you can, because glass resists scratches and holds heat better over long periods.

## Simple cabinet dryer

1. A wooden or cardboard box with a glass or clear plastic top, angled to face the sun.
2. A dark base inside, such as painted metal or dark cloth, to absorb heat.
3. Air vents at the bottom and at the top, covered with insect mesh.
4. Racks of mesh for the food, above the heated base.

Build the box so the glass can be lifted for loading. Seal the joints so that air moves through the vents and not around the edges.

## Using it

- Face the glass to the sun and adjust the angle as the sun moves.
- Cut food into thin, even pieces.
- Load the racks without overlapping.
- Bring food in at night to keep out dew and insects.

## Adjusting for conditions

In the hottest hours, the dryer may run too hot for delicate herbs, so prop the glass open slightly to vent heat. In the early and late day, the sun is low and the dryer barely warms, so food may need a second sunny day. Wind can cool the box and push dust in through the vents, so place the dryer behind a wall or fence if the wind is strong.

## Expected results

Fruit usually dries in one to three days, herbs in a few hours, and vegetables in one to two days. Check the food daily.

## Common mistakes

- **Overloading the trays:** air cannot reach the food, so it dries slowly or spoils.
- **Blocked vents:** moist air stays inside and the food sours.
- **Leaving the dryer closed on a cloudy day:** the food does not reach a useful temperature.
- **Skipping the daily check:** food that is dry on one side may still be wet in the middle.

## Checklist

- [ ] The glass faces the sun and the angle is adjusted.
- [ ] Vents are open and covered with insect mesh.
- [ ] Trays are loaded with a single layer.
- [ ] Food is brought in at night.

## Troubleshooting

If the inside of the glass fogs up, the dryer is working but the vents are too small to carry the moist air out. Widen the top vent and check that the bottom vent is not blocked by dust. If one tray dries much faster than the others, move the trays round during the day, since the hottest spot is usually near the top of the box.

## Practice before you need it

Dry one small batch in a normal week, and time each stage. Note the angle of the glass at each hour, the weather and how the food looked at the end of the day. Those notes will tell you how the dryer behaves in your actual climate, which is more useful than a general timetable.

## When it fails

If the food is still wet after the expected time, move it to a sunnier spot, reduce the load and open the top vent wider. If the food smells sour or grows mould, discard it and check the cleaning of the trays before the next batch.

## Storage

Condition and store dried food as described in Drying Fruit at Home.

**Warning:** dry food only in clean conditions. Keep animals and insects away from the trays, and discard any dried food that shows mould, a sour smell or insects.
