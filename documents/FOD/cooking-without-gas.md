# Cooking Without Gas

When gas stops, you still need hot food and safe water. The people who cope best plan the fuel first, then the meals. Fuel runs out faster than most households expect, so a simple inventory of what can burn, what can heat and what needs no heat at all is the starting point.

## Why it matters

Cooking takes more of your fuel than any other daily task, and most of it is wasted when the pot is uncovered or the flame is too large. A household that treats fuel as a limited store, measured in days rather than in bottles, eats more regularly and with less stress. Heat is also needed for water: boiling makes stored or doubtful water safe to drink, so the fuel plan must cover drinking water as well as food.

## Equipment and materials

- A stove or fire source you can run safely outdoors.
- Lidded pots in two or three sizes, so small quantities do not waste heat.
- A windshield or simple wall of stones or sheet metal to shield the flame.
- A long-handled spoon, oven gloves and a heatproof mat.
- A written fuel log, kept on the wall next to the cooking place.

## Choose a heat source

- **Wood or charcoal outdoors:** the most flexible option. Never cook inside a closed room with it.
- **Solar oven:** slow, but uses no fuel. See Solar Ovens.
- **Camping stove with bottled fuel:** good for small amounts, used outdoors only.
- **Hot coals in a metal box:** simple and controllable.

> @image Camping stove with a pot of water on a flat outdoor table, daylight

Each source has a different cost per meal. Count how many boils a store of fuel gives you, not just how many hours it burns. A bottle of gas that boils four litres of water is worth more than one that is spent on a low simmer.

## Save fuel

1. Cover every pot. A lid keeps heat in and can cut fuel use by a large share.
2. Soak dried beans and grains before cooking. They cook in about half the time.
3. Cut food into small pieces, so it cooks faster and more evenly.
4. Cook one large meal rather than several small ones, and reuse the heat.
5. Use the heat that stays in the pot after you turn off the flame.

Set the flame to the lowest level that keeps the food moving. Once a pot reaches a boil, a gentle simmer uses far less fuel than a roaring flame, and the food cooks just as well.

## Simple methods

- **Boil:** rice, pasta, legumes and soups.
- **Steam:** vegetables over a little water in a covered pot.
- **Fry or toast:** on a flat pan over coals, with a little oil.
- **Retained heat:** bring a pot to a boil, wrap it in a blanket and let it cook slowly for an hour or more.

![Layered cutaway: pot-with-lid centre, bracket around it for blanket, box and lid layers, leader to each; labels Pot, Blanket, Box, Lid; the insulation layers hold heat through a long, slow cook. NEEDS: blanket, box](assets/cooking-without-gas-3.svg)

Retained-heat cooking works best with grains and legumes, which absorb water and soften over a long, gentle period. It is a poor choice for fish or meat that must reach a set temperature.

## Adjusting for conditions

- **Cold or windy days:** fuel burns faster and pots lose heat quickly. Use more lids, shelter the flame and cook one bigger pot instead of two small ones.
- **Rain:** keep fuel and matches dry in a sealed tin. Never move a stove or fire into a garage, tent or enclosed porch.
- **High altitude:** water boils at a lower temperature, so beans and grains need longer cooking. Soak them for longer too.

## Common mistakes

- **Cooking with fuel indoors:** smoke and gases build up quickly in a closed room.
- **Letting the flame run high:** most of the heat goes around the sides of the pot.
- **Forgetting to ventilate:** even a sealed room needs fresh air when a flame is burning.
- **Storing fuel near the stove:** a spill or a spark can start a fire.

## Checklist

- [ ] The fuel supply is counted and written down.
- [ ] Cooking happens outdoors or in a well-ventilated space.
- [ ] Pots have lids that fit.
- [ ] A fire extinguisher, sand or water is within reach.

## Food safety

- Cook meat until it is steaming hot all the way through.
- Reheat leftovers until steaming hot.
- Do not leave cooked food out for more than two hours in warm weather.

## When it fails

If a pot takes too long to heat, the fire is too small or the lid is missing. Add dry fuel, cover the pot and cut the food smaller. If food is undercooked after the planned time, bring it back to a boil and keep it covered until it is soft.

**Warning:** never leave a fire or stove unattended. Keep children and animals away, and do not use any fuel-burning device inside a closed room.
