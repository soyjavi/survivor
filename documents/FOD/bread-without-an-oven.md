# Bread Without an Oven

Flatbread needs only flour, water, salt and heat. It is quick, filling and stores better than many foods, which makes it one of the most useful skills when the oven is out of reach. A household that can bake bread on a pan or in a pot keeps a reliable source of starch even when gas and electricity have stopped.

## Why it matters

Bread is dense in energy and easy to share out, so it stretches a small store of flour further than almost any other single food. Flour, water and salt store for months in dry conditions. A loaf baked over coals is often the most reliable meal of the day, and it gives children and older people something soft and familiar to eat.

## Materials

- 500 g flour (wheat, or a mix with other grain flour).
- About 300 ml water, added slowly.
- 1 teaspoon salt.
- Optional: 1 tablespoon oil.
- A heavy, dry pan, or a heavy pot with a tight lid.
- A flat board or clean cloth for shaping.

Thin pans hot-spot and scorch the bread in one place, so choose a thick base.

## Basic dough

Mix the ingredients until they form a soft, slightly sticky dough. Add the water gradually, since flour absorbs different amounts. Knead for five minutes, until the dough feels smooth and springs back when pressed. Rest it for 20 minutes under a cloth so the flour fully absorbs the water.

> @image Hands kneading a soft dough on a wooden board, with flour dusted on the surface

Press a thumb into the dough. If the dent slowly fills back in, the dough is ready.

## Flatbread in a pan

1. Divide the dough into 6 to 8 balls.
2. Flatten each ball to about 5 mm thick, turning it often so it does not stick.
3. Cook in a dry, heavy pan over medium heat for about two minutes per side, until spotted brown.
4. Stack the finished breads under a cloth so they stay soft.

Keep the heat steady. If the surface blackens before the centre sets, the pan is too hot and the bread will taste bitter.

> @image Flatbreads cooking in a dry heavy pan on a camp stove, one turned to show golden spots

## Bread in a covered pot

1. Oil the inside of a heavy, lidded pot.
2. Place the dough in the pot and cover it.
3. Set the pot on or over low coals, with a few coals on the lid.
4. Bake for 30 to 40 minutes, checking that the bottom does not burn.
5. Lift the lid carefully and tap the base. A hollow sound means the loaf is ready.

![Cross-section: pot-with-lid outline, bracket on the top and base crust, leader to the pale centre; labels Crust sets first, Centre sets last; colour alone does not show doneness. NEEDS: loaf-cross-section](assets/bread-without-an-oven-2.svg)

The coals on the lid do most of the work of an oven. Heat from above sets the top of the loaf while the base cooks from the coals below. Rotate the pot a quarter turn halfway through the bake so that the loaf colours evenly.

## Adjusting for conditions

- **Humid weather:** flour takes up less water, so hold back some of the liquid and add it only if the dough is stiff.
- **Cold weather:** dough rises slowly. Keep it in a warm spot, wrapped in cloth, and allow an extra hour before shaping.
- **Windy sites:** wind pulls heat away from the pot. Build a low wind wall of stones or sheet metal around the coals.

## Common mistakes

- **Too much water:** the dough sticks to everything and becomes heavy. Add flour in small amounts.
- **Too much heat from below:** the base burns while the top stays pale. Move the pot off the hottest coals.
- **Skipping the rest:** the bread is tough and crumbly.
- **Opening the lid too often:** heat escapes and the loaf sinks.
- **Cutting too soon:** a hot loaf is still cooking inside. Let it cool for at least 20 minutes before slicing.

## Troubleshooting

When bread goes wrong, the cause is usually visible in the crumb. A gummy, wet crumb means too little bake time or too much water. A crumbly loaf that falls apart means too little water or too little kneading. A dark crust over a pale top points to uneven coals, which you can fix by moving the pot.
## Checklist

- [ ] Dough is soft, not stiff or runny.
- [ ] Pan or pot is heavy and clean.
- [ ] Coals are low and even.
- [ ] The centre is dry before you stop cooking.

## Practice before you need it

Bake one batch in a normal kitchen before relying on the method in a shortage. Time the bake, note how the coals behaved and learn the sound a finished loaf makes when tapped.
## When it fails

If the centre is doughy, return the loaf to the pot for ten more minutes on lower heat. If the base is burnt, cut it away and eat the rest. If the flatbread turns hard, wrap it in cloth while it is still warm so it softens as it cools.

## Keep it

Bread goes stale within a day or two. Keep it wrapped in cloth, or toast the slices and store them dry.

![Before and after: two panels split by arrow-straight; left soft loaf, right sliced strips on grate above coals then bag; labels Fresh, Toasted dry, Cloth bag; drying the slices is what slows mould. NEEDS: dough-loaf, cloth](assets/bread-without-an-oven-4.svg)

Dry toasted slices keep longer than soft bread because they have less moisture for mould to use.

**Warning:** make sure the bread is cooked through before eating. The centre should be dry, not doughy.
