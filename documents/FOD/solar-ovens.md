# Solar Ovens

A solar oven cooks with sunlight. It uses no fuel, but it is slow, so plan meals early. Solar cooking is most useful for grains, beans and soups, which tolerate long, gentle heat, and it frees fuel for the foods that need a fire.

![Cutaway: box outline, glass lid on top, insulation layers in the walls, reflector-wall flap, dark pot-with-lid inside, arrow-straight into the lid; labels Glass lid, Insulation, Reflector, Pot; three parts trap sunlight as heat. NEEDS: sun, glass, insulation](assets/solar-ovens-1.svg)

## Why it matters

Fuel is often the scarcest resource in a shortage, and cooking grains and beans can take hours on a fire. A solar oven turns free sunlight into a slow, steady cook, so the fire can be saved for boiling water or for frying. It also cooks without smoke indoors, which makes it useful for households that have no safe place for a fire.

## How it works

Sunlight passes through a clear cover and heats the dark inside. The trapped heat cooks food slowly, like a low oven. Insulation keeps the heat in, and a reflector adds light to the box.

## Equipment and materials

- A sturdy cardboard box with a lid, or a wooden box.
- Dark paper or foil for the inside.
- Crumpled newspaper or straw for insulation.
- A sheet of clear glass or heavy plastic for the cover.
- Tape, a dark lidded pot and a foil reflector.
- An oven thermometer, if one is available.

Choose a glass cover over plastic if you can. Glass keeps its clarity and seal for longer, while plastic yellows and tears in strong sun.

## Simple box oven

1. Use a cardboard box with a lid. Line the inside with dark paper or foil.
2. Insulate the sides and base with crumpled newspaper.
3. Cover the top opening with a sheet of clear glass or plastic, sealed with tape.
4. Place a dark pot with a lid inside.
5. Use a foil reflector to direct more light into the box.

Make the inner box dark and the outer box light coloured, if you can, since a dark outer surface absorbs heat that should stay in the oven.

## Using it

- Place the oven in full sun and turn it every hour or so to follow the sun.
- Preheat for 30 to 60 minutes before adding food.
- Cut food into small pieces and use dark, covered pots.
- Expect two to four hours for rice or beans.

## Good foods

- Rice, grains and soups.
- Soaked dried beans and lentils.
- Vegetables and small pieces of meat or fish.

## Adjusting for conditions

Thin cloud can cut the heat by half, and a thick haze may stop cooking altogether. Work with the sun's strongest hours, roughly late morning to mid-afternoon, and start a grain dish earlier on hazy days.

> @image Solar oven set on a sunny doorstep in the early afternoon with a cloud passing overhead In cold weather, the oven loses heat to the air through its walls, so add extra insulation or use a smaller box. Wind can cool the cover, so shelter the oven behind a low wall where possible.

## Common mistakes

- **Light-coloured pots:** they reflect the heat instead of absorbing it.
- **Large pieces of food:** they stay raw in the centre.
- **Opening the glass repeatedly:** the heat escapes and the cooking stalls.
- **Using a worn or torn cover:** the seal fails and the oven loses heat.

## Checklist

- [ ] The box has a sealed, clear cover.
- [ ] The inside is dark and the pot has a lid.
- [ ] The oven is preheated before food goes in.
- [ ] The oven is turned to follow the sun.

## Limits

- It does not work well on overcast days or in winter.
- It may not reach temperatures that kill bacteria quickly, so cook meat in small pieces and check it is hot through.

## Troubleshooting

If the inside stays cool, check the cover for cracks and the tape for gaps, since a small leak can drain most of the heat. If the food cooks unevenly, rotate the pot halfway through the cook, and check that the reflector is angled toward the glass. If the box sags, reinforce the base with a second layer of cardboard before the next use.

## Practice before you need it

Run a full cook in a normal week, on a clear day, with rice or beans you already know. Note the start time, the time the grain was soft and how many turns were needed. This test tells you the real length of a meal, which is the figure that matters when planning a day.

## When it fails

If the food is still raw after four hours, move the oven to a sunnier spot, cut the food smaller and check the seal. If the oven does not heat above warm, the sun is too weak for solar cooking that day, and the meal should be finished on a fire or stove.

**Warning:** when in doubt about meat, cook it over a fire or stove instead. Never leave raw meat in the solar oven for hours without checking that it is fully cooked.
