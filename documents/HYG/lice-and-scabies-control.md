# Lice and Scabies Control

Lice and scabies spread quickly in crowded shelters and wherever people share bedding and clothing. They cause itching, skin damage and secondary infection, and the itching often keeps children awake. Control works best when everyone in the household is checked and treated together, because one untreated person can reinfect the rest.

## Why it matters

Lice and scabies are not signs of dirt or poor character. They travel on hair, clothing and skin, and they thrive wherever people sleep close together. Their itch disturbs sleep, and scratching breaks the skin, which lets bacteria in and can cause serious infection. Because both spread from person to person, a single case left untreated can lead to a whole family being affected again within weeks.

## Equipment and materials

- A fine-toothed metal comb, sterilised in boiling water before and after use.
- White paper or a white cloth for wiping the comb after each stroke.
- Hot water, soap and a bucket for washing bedding and clothes.
- A bag or box for items that must be left unused for three days.
- A notebook to record who has been checked, treated and rechecked.

## Head lice

- Signs: itching, small eggs (nits) stuck to hair near the scalp, and live lice moving on the head.
- Comb wet hair with a fine-toothed comb, removing nits and lice on each pass. Wipe the comb on white paper after each stroke.
- Repeat every few days for about two weeks, because newly hatched lice must be caught before they lay eggs.
- Wash bedding, hats and hair ties in hot water where possible.

## Step-by-step in detail

Wet the hair with water and a little conditioner, which makes the lice easier to comb out. Divide the hair into sections, and work through each section from the scalp to the tips. Comb in a slow, steady stroke, pulling the comb firmly along the hair shaft. After each stroke, wipe the comb on white paper, and look for lice and nits. Keep a record of each session, so that the full two-week cycle is completed without gaps.

## Body lice

- Signs: itching, especially at the waist and armpits, and small bites that look like red spots.
- Wash clothes and bedding in hot water, and dry them on high heat.
- Iron seams where you can, to kill eggs. Pay attention to the stitching on underwear and socks.

## Scabies

- Signs: itching that is worse at night, and small red lines or spots, often between the fingers, on the wrists and around the waist.
- Scabies spreads by close skin contact. Treat everyone in the household at the same time, even those who do not yet itch.
- Wash bedding and clothes in hot water, and leave items unused for three days if washing is not possible. Mites cannot survive long away from skin.

## Adjusting for conditions

- **Shelters with shared mats:** remove mats from circulation for three days, or disinfect and air them in the sun.
- **Cold weather:** keep people warm but wash bedding in the warmest water available, and hang it in the sun when possible.
- **Children with long hair:** plait or tie it up, and check the nape of the neck and behind the ears each evening.
- **Babies and pregnant women:** always seek medical advice before any treatment, since many products are unsuitable.

## Prevent

- Do not share combs, hats, towels and bedding.
- Check children regularly, particularly behind the ears and at the nape of the neck.
- Keep clothes and bedding clean and dry, and air them in the sun.

## Checklist

- Everyone in the household checked on the same day.
- Combs cleaned in hot, soapy water after each use.
- Bedding and clothes washed in hot water or isolated for three days.
- Treatment completed for the full course, as advised by a health worker.
- Checks repeated after about two weeks.

## Common mistakes

- Treating only the child who is itching.
- Stopping the comb routine as soon as the hair looks clear.
- Sharing a sleeping mat with someone who has not been treated.
- Assuming that bad hygiene causes lice. Lice and scabies spread to clean people in crowded places too.

## Practice before you need it

Learn to recognise nits on a helper's hair before a crisis, because they are easy to mistake for dandruff or dust. Practise combing a single section on a family member, timing each pass. Keep a spare comb and a labelled bag for bedding, so the routine can start on the same day a case is found.

## When it fails

If itching continues after two weeks of combing, or if the skin becomes swollen, oozing or painful, the infection may be secondary. Keep the area clean, do not scratch, and seek help from a health worker. Do not switch treatments without advice, because repeated misuse can irritate the skin.

**Warning:** do not use insecticides on skin or near food, and never apply them to the scalp of a child without guidance. Seek medical advice for scabies treatment, especially for babies, pregnant women and people with weakened immunity.
