# Mould Removal

Mould grows in damp conditions and can cause coughing, asthma flare-ups, eye irritation and skin rashes. Remove it early, while the patch is small, and fix the source of the damp. Cleaning mould without drying the room only lets it return within weeks.

## Why it matters

Mould releases spores into the air, and breathing them in irritates the airways and worsens asthma and allergies. Young children, older people and anyone with a weak immune system are most at risk. Mould also damages plaster, timber, clothing and stored food, so a small patch left alone can cost a household much of its shelter and possessions. Because damp is usually the cause, the work is as much about the building as about the stain.

## Equipment and materials

- Disposable gloves, a dust mask rated for particles and goggles.
- A stiff brush, a scraper and a bucket of warm water with detergent.
- Household bleach diluted to about 1 part to 10 parts water.
- Clean cloths, sealable bags and a fan or a sheet of plastic for the doorway.
- A simple moisture meter, if one is available, or a thin wooden rod to test the wall.

## Protect yourself

Wear gloves, a mask rated for dust, and eye protection. Keep children and people with asthma away from the area while you work. Wash your hands and face when you finish, and change your clothes before you sleep.

## Remove surface mould

1. Ventilate the room by opening windows and doors, and keep them open for the whole job.
2. Scrub hard surfaces with detergent and water, then wipe with a diluted bleach solution. Work from the outside edge of the patch inwards.
3. Dry the area fully, using air movement, a fan and sun where possible.

## Step-by-step in detail

Before you start, seal the doorway with plastic and tape, so that spores do not drift into other rooms. Mist the patch lightly with water so that spores stay put. Brush gently from the outside edge towards the centre, and bag the brush head and cloths immediately. Wipe the patch with the diluted bleach, leave it wet for ten minutes, and then wipe it clean. Close the bags and remove them from the building the same day.

## Adjusting for conditions

- **Rainy season:** dry the room with a fan for several days, and check the walls for cold patches after every heavy rain.
- **Dry season:** the air is less damp, but walls may still stay wet near the ground, so check the base of the wall.
- **Cold nights:** keep the room warm but ventilated, because condensation forms easily when warm air meets cold walls.
- **Small shelters:** use a dehumidifier or a bucket of salt to draw moisture out, and replace the salt when it is wet.

## Fabric and soft items

Wash fabric in hot water with detergent. Dry it fully in sun, turning it so that both sides dry. Discard items that are still mouldy after washing, because the spores lodge deep in the fibres and keep coming back.

## Deep mould

Plaster, wood and insulation that are deeply mouldy usually cannot be saved. Remove and dispose of them in sealed bags, then treat the surface beneath with a disinfectant solution and dry it fully before replacing anything.

## Stop it returning

- Fix leaks and cracks, including gutters and roof gaps, before cleaning.
- Keep air moving through the room, by opening windows on opposite sides.
- Keep humidity low, and dry wet areas within 48 hours.
- Move furniture a little away from cold walls, so that air can circulate behind it.

## Checklist

- Room ventilated before work starts.
- Mask, gloves and eye protection worn.
- Surface scrubbed, disinfected and dried.
- Soft items washed and dried in sun.
- Source of damp located and repaired.

## Common mistakes

- Painting over mould without cleaning it first.
- Using a stronger solution, which does not kill spores better.
- Leaving the room closed while cleaning.
- Ignoring the leak that caused the damp.

## Practice before you need it

Learn to spot early mould before the rainy season starts. Walk the house with a torch and look at corners, behind furniture, under windowsills and inside cupboards. Keep a labelled kit of brushes, cloths, bleach and a mask in a dry place, so that a small patch can be treated the same day it is seen.

## When it fails

If mould returns within a few weeks, the source of moisture has not been found. Check the walls for cold patches, check the roof and pipes, and measure the damp with a simple meter if one is available. If symptoms such as persistent cough or wheezing develop, seek medical help.

**Warning:** do not dry-sweep mould, because the spores spread into the air and can reach the lungs. Wipe or wet the area first, and avoid using a fan that blows spores towards other people.
