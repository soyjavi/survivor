# Laundry Without Power

Clean clothes and bedding protect the skin and help prevent lice and infection. Hand washing works well with a bucket, soap and a little patience, and it does not need electricity. Clothes that are washed regularly last longer too, because dirt and sweat break down the fibres when they stay in place.

## Why it matters

Soiled clothes hold sweat, oil and skin cells, which feed bacteria and attract lice and fleas. Bedding that stays damp becomes a home for mould and dust mites, and the people sleeping on it breathe the spores. Regular washing is also a matter of dignity and morale, because clean clothes help people feel ready for the day. Done well, hand washing removes most of the dirt and germs without any power at all.

## Equipment and materials

- Two buckets, one for washing and one for clean rinse water, both with lids.
- A scrubbing brush or a clean, stiff cloth.
- Bar soap, grated soap or a small amount of liquid detergent.
- A washing line or two strong cords, and pegs or clips.
- A large tub or sheet of waterproof cloth for blankets and bedding.

## Wash by hand

1. Fill a bucket with warm water if you can, or use cold water. Warm water lifts oil and sweat more easily.
2. Add soap, and soak the clothes for 15 to 30 minutes. Soaking loosens the dirt before you scrub.
3. Rub the dirtiest areas, such as collars, cuffs and the seat of trousers, and scrub gently to avoid tearing the fabric.
4. Rinse twice in clean water, changing the water when it becomes cloudy.
5. Wring out the water by twisting the cloth, then shake each item out before drying.

## Step-by-step in detail

Sort the clothes before you start, separating whites, colours and heavily soiled items. Soak the lightest items first in the cleanest water, then move to the dirtier ones. When you scrub, rub the fabric against itself rather than against a rough surface, which wears it thin. Rinse each item until the water runs clear of soap, because leftover detergent irritates the skin and leaves stiff patches. Keep the wash bucket covered between sessions to stop insects and dust getting in.

## Adjusting for conditions

- **Very little water:** wash the cleanest items first, rinse once in a bucket, and use the rinse water for the next load.
- **Cold weather:** warm the water with a clean pot heated outdoors, and dry the items indoors near a warm wall, not over a fire.
- **Rainy days:** collect rainwater for rinsing, and dry items under cover with a gap between each one.
- **Children's clothes:** change them more often, and wash the items that touch the skin most.

## Heavy items

Blankets and bedding need more water and time. Soak them, then wash them in a large tub or a clean patch of ground covered with a sheet. Use a second rinse, and wring them out with two people, one at each end, twisting in opposite directions.

## Sanitise

- Boil cloth where possible, or wash it in hot water.
- Use a diluted bleach solution on cloth that has been in contact with illness, and rinse well so that no residue stays on the skin.
- Wash the bucket and the scrubbing brush after each load.

## Dry

- Dry clothes in sun and wind, spread out so that they dry fast and do not grow mould.
- Do not dry clothes over fire, because they can scorch and catch fire.
- Bring them in before dew falls, because damp clothes can smell and spread mould.

## Save water

Use the same water for several items, starting with the cleanest and finishing with the dirtiest. Use the last rinse water for floors or for flushing a latrine. Collect rainwater in a clean container for the first rinse when it rains.

## Checklist

- Bucket cleaned before use.
- Clothes sorted from lightest to dirtiest.
- Two rinses completed.
- Heavy items soaked and wrung with help.
- Drying line set in full sun, away from smoke.

## Common mistakes

- Washing soiled clothes with clean ones, which spreads dirt.
- Using dirty rinse water, so the clothes come out grey.
- Leaving wet clothes in a heap, where they go mouldy.
- Drying clothes in smoke, which leaves a strong smell and traces of soot.

## Practice before you need it

Wash one full load at home with the same bucket, soap and line you would use in a crisis. Time the soak, the scrub and the drying, and note how much water it took. Practise wringing a sheet with a partner, since it is much harder than it looks and two people do it far more easily than one.

## When it fails

If clothes still smell after washing, soak them again in warm water with a little more soap, then dry them fully in sun. If mould appears, wash the item in hot water with detergent and dry it fully. Discard items that are still mouldy or stained after repeated washing.

**Warning:** do not wash clothes in a water source that people drink from. Detergent and dirt in the water can make others ill.
