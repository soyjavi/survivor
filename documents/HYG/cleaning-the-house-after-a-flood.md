# Cleaning the House After a Flood

Floodwater leaves mud, sewage and mould behind, and it can carry dangerous chemicals from roads and fuel stores. Clean in a careful order, keep safe while you work, and do not rush the drying stage, because a damp building becomes a home for mould within days.

## Why it matters

Floodwater is not clean water. It carries sewage from blocked drains, animal waste, fuel, pesticides and sharp debris, and it soaks into plaster, timber and insulation where germs survive for weeks. People who return too early often get cuts, skin infections, diarrhoea and breathing problems from mould. A methodical clean protects health, prevents the building from rotting, and makes it safe to live in again.

## Equipment and materials

- Waterproof boots, heavy gloves, a fitted dust mask and goggles.
- Buckets, squeegees, brooms and a stiff brush.
- Detergent, household bleach and clean water in labelled containers.
- Heavy-duty rubbish bags and a marked bin for sharp items.
- Fans or a dehumidifier if available, and a torch that does not need a flame.

## Protect yourself

Wear waterproof boots, heavy gloves, a mask and eye protection. Wash your hands and any cuts often, and cover open wounds with waterproof dressings before you enter the water. Children should stay out of the building until it is clean and dry.

## Order of work

1. **Safety first.** Check for electrical and structural hazards before you enter. Switch off the power at the main supply if it is safe to reach.
2. **Remove water and mud** with buckets, squeegees and brooms. Work from the top of the room downwards, so that dirty water does not run onto clean surfaces.
3. **Throw away** food, soft furnishings and anything that cannot be cleaned.
4. **Scrub hard surfaces** with clean water and detergent, starting with walls and floors.
5. **Disinfect** with a diluted bleach solution, left on for several minutes, then rinsed.
6. **Dry** the building fully, with ventilation and sun.

## Step-by-step in detail

Before the first bucket is filled, photograph the damage and write down what is lost, which helps with insurance and aid claims. Open every door and window that can be opened, and leave them open for the whole job. Clear the stairs and furniture first, so that you have room to move. When the room is empty, scrub the highest surfaces first, then the middle of the walls, and finish with the floor. Change the cleaning water whenever it turns cloudy, because dirty water only moves dirt around.

## Adjusting for conditions

- **Hot, humid weather:** work early in the morning, take frequent breaks, and drink water before you feel thirsty.
- **Cold weather:** heat the room with a safe source away from the work area, and dry walls more slowly to avoid cracking.
- **Water still standing:** do not start cleaning until the level has dropped and the structure has been checked.
- **Contaminated sewage water:** treat all tools as contaminated, and disinfect them before they go back into use.

## Mould

Clean mould from hard surfaces with detergent and a bleach solution. Remove mould-damaged plaster and wood, which cannot be cleaned properly. Wear a dust mask while you scrape, and keep the area wet to limit the spores that drift into the air.

## Furniture and fabric

Wood and fabric that were soaked often cannot be saved. Clean what can be cleaned, and dry it fully before use. Metal furniture can usually be scrubbed and oiled to prevent rust.

## Waste

Keep mud and waste in sealed bags. Dispose of them away from water sources, and do not stack them against the walls of the house.

## Checklist

- Power and gas switched off before entry.
- Boots, gloves, mask and eye protection on.
- Water and mud removed from the top down.
- Ruined items bagged and removed.
- Disinfection done, then rinsed.
- Air moving through the building for several days.

## Common mistakes

- Starting to scrub before the water is removed.
- Closing the building up while it is still damp.
- Mixing bleach with other cleaning products.
- Keeping soaked mattresses and bedding because they look dry on top.

## Practice before you need it

Walk through the steps with the household before a flood season begins. Decide who checks the power, who carries the buckets and who keeps the children away from the work. Store a basic flood kit in a high, dry place, including bags, gloves, a mask and a bottle of detergent, so that the first day after water recedes does not depend on shops reopening.

## When it fails

If the building smells strongly of damp after a week, or walls feel wet to the touch, the water is still inside them. Open the walls where possible, remove the damaged material, and run fans for longer. Do not move back in until the air and surfaces are dry.

**Warning:** never use a generator, heater or fuel indoors while cleaning. Carbon monoxide builds up quickly in closed rooms and can kill within minutes.
