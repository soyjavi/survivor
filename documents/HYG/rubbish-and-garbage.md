# Rubbish and Garbage

Rubbish left uncovered attracts flies, rats and disease. Reduce it, store it well and dispose of it regularly. In a crisis, the amount of rubbish often grows because packaging piles up and collection stops, so a clear plan from the first day prevents a smell problem from becoming a health problem.

## Why it matters

Rubbish is more than an eyesore. Food scraps rot within a day in warm weather, and the smell draws flies that land on food and skin. Rats carry leptospirosis and other diseases, and loose waste blocks drains, which turns rainwater into a breeding ground for mosquitoes. Clear storage and disposal rules keep the living area clean, protect water sources and reduce the effort the household spends fighting pests.

## Equipment and materials

- A lidded bin for dry rubbish and a second bin for food waste.
- Strong bags, a roll of tape and a few spare liners.
- A shovel and a pit marker, to dig and label the disposal pit.
- Gloves, a stick or tongs for lifting sharp or dirty items.
- A brush and bucket for cleaning the bins after emptying.

## Reduce

- Use food scraps for animals, compost or soup.
- Reuse containers, bags and cloth for storage and repairs.
- Avoid packaging where you can, and buy food in bulk when it is available.

## Store

1. Keep rubbish in a covered container with a tight lid, and line it with a bag.
2. Keep it away from living areas, food stores and water sources.
3. Keep food waste separate from dry rubbish, and empty it at least once a day in warm weather.

## Step-by-step in detail

Each day, separate the waste at the point it is created, so that food scraps never go into the dry bin. Tie each bag at the top before moving it, and carry it away from the body. Place the bag in the bin, close the lid, and wash your hands. Once a week, empty the bins completely, scrub the inside with soapy water, rinse, and dry them in the sun before they are used again.

## Adjusting for conditions

- **Rainy season:** raise bins off the ground and keep lids tight, because wet waste rots faster and attracts more insects.
- **Dry season:** dust and wind spread light rubbish, so weigh down bags and keep the bin in a sheltered spot.
- **Crowded shelters:** share one bin per group of families, and place it at least a few steps from any sleeping area.
- **No collection service:** plan for a longer storage period by burying or burning dry waste on a fixed schedule.

## Dispose

- Bury rubbish in a pit, covering it with soil, away from water. Fill the pit in layers, and add soil after each layer.
- Burn paper, card and clean wood where local rules allow. Burn in an open area, upwind of living spaces.
- Do not burn plastic, rubber or chemicals. They release toxic smoke.
- Take rubbish to a collection point if one is available, and keep bags tied until you get there.

## Pests

Rats and insects are drawn to rubbish. Keep bins closed, clean up spills and check for holes in containers. Store bags off the ground, and place bins on a stand so that animals cannot reach them easily.

## Hazardous waste

Batteries, oil, paint and medicines need special disposal. Keep them separate from ordinary rubbish, in a labelled container, and away from children.

## Checklist

- Lidded bin in place, away from living space.
- Food waste separated and emptied daily.
- Pit dug and marked before it is needed.
- Hazardous items kept apart.
- Bins washed and checked each week.

## Common mistakes

- Leaving the bin open after each use.
- Putting food waste in with dry rubbish, where it rots quickly.
- Burning plastic because it is quick.
- Digging the rubbish pit close to a well or stream.

## Practice before you need it

Dig a practice pit in a suitable spot before the crisis, and mark it clearly with a stake and a sign. Run a week of separate food and dry waste at home, and note how often the bin needs emptying. Agree on one person who checks the bins each day and reports problems early.

## When it fails

If rubbish attracts large numbers of flies or rats, cover it with soil, close the bin and move it further from the shelter. Clean the area with a disinfectant solution, and check whether the pit is too shallow. Report persistent pest problems to the group leader so that the whole camp can respond.

**Warning:** do not burn plastics or treated materials. They release toxic smoke that can cause breathing problems and poisoning, especially for children.
