# Disinfecting Surfaces

Clean surfaces first, then disinfect them. Cleaning removes dirt and grease, and disinfection kills microbes. Disinfectant does not work well on dirty surfaces, because the dirt shields the germs underneath. Skipping the cleaning step is the most common reason a disinfection fails.

## Why it matters

In a crowded household, a few surfaces carry most of the germs. Door handles, taps and toilet seats are touched many times a day, and a cloth used on one of them can carry germs to the next. Disinfecting these points breaks the chain of spread, especially when someone is ill with diarrhoea or a respiratory infection. The work is quick, but only when it is done in the right order and with the right strength.

## Equipment and materials

- Household bleach at 5 to 6 per cent strength, stored in its original labelled bottle.
- A clean measuring cup or a marked bottle for measuring water.
- Two cloths, one for cleaning and one for disinfecting, and a brush for grime.
- Soap or detergent, and a bucket of clean water for rinsing.
- Gloves, and a spray bottle only if the solution is clearly labelled.

## Bleach solution

Mix household bleach (5 to 6 per cent) with clean water. Measure carefully, because too little bleach will not kill germs and too much leaves residue and damages surfaces.

- For general disinfection: about 1 part bleach to 50 parts water. That is roughly 20 ml of bleach per litre of water.
- For contaminated areas, such as after diarrhoea or vomiting: about 1 part bleach to 10 parts water.

Make fresh solution each day. It loses strength over time, especially when it is exposed to light and heat, so mix only what you need and store the rest in a dark, sealed container.

## Steps

1. Clean the surface with soap or detergent and water. Scrub any sticky or greasy areas.
2. Rinse and dry the surface, so the solution is not diluted by leftover water.
3. Apply the disinfectant solution with a cloth or spray, and leave it wet for at least 10 minutes.
4. Rinse with clean water if the surface touches food, and let it air dry.

## Step-by-step in detail

Start at the top of the room and work downwards, so that drips do not land on surfaces you have already done. Wipe in one direction, using a fresh section of cloth for each area. For the toilet, clean the seat, lid, flush handle and the floor around the base, then disinfect them in the same order. Keep the cloth wet for the full contact time by re-wetting it if the surface begins to dry. Label the cloths so that the toilet cloth never goes near food.

## Adjusting for conditions

- **Hot weather:** solution loses strength faster, so mix a fresh batch each morning and keep it out of sun.
- **Cold weather:** bleach works more slowly at low temperatures, so allow a longer contact time.
- **Hard water or dirty water:** clean with more detergent first, because minerals and silt reduce the effect of the disinfectant.
- **Wooden or painted surfaces:** test a small hidden patch first, since bleach can discolour finishes.

## What to disinfect

- Door handles, taps, toilet seats and light switches, which many hands touch every day.
- Cooking surfaces, cutting boards and utensils, after rinsing off food.
- Toys and objects used by sick people, cleaned separately from other items.

## Safety

- Ventilate the room while you work, by opening a window or door.
- Wear gloves to protect the skin, and wash your hands afterwards.
- Store bleach out of reach of children, in its own clearly labelled container.

## Checklist

- Surface cleaned before it is disinfected.
- Solution measured and freshly mixed.
- Surface kept wet for at least 10 minutes.
- Food surfaces rinsed with clean water.
- Room ventilated and gloves removed before eating.

## Common mistakes

- Using a stronger solution, thinking it works better.
- Wiping the surface dry too quickly, so the solution has no time to act.
- Using the same cloth on the toilet and on the food area.
- Keeping an old solution that has lost its strength.

## Practice before you need it

Mix a small batch of solution with the household, measuring it out loud so that everyone knows the ratio. Practise a full cycle on one table or one door handle, timing the ten minutes with a clock. Writing the ratios on a card and taping it inside the storage cupboard prevents guessing when someone is tired or in a hurry.

## When it fails

If a surface still smells, looks stained or feels sticky after rinsing, it has not been cleaned properly. Repeat the cleaning step with soap, then disinfect again. Replace cloths that are heavily soiled, because they spread germs instead of removing them.

**Warning:** never mix bleach with vinegar, ammonia or other cleaning products. The fumes are toxic and can cause serious lung damage within minutes.
