# Menstrual Hygiene

Periods do not stop in a crisis, and poor management causes infection, discomfort and distress. Plan supplies early, and make sure there is a private place to change and wash. Girls who lack these basics often miss school or work, so planning for menstruation is part of protecting the whole household.

## Why it matters

Blood left on skin or in cloth for long periods causes odour, irritation and rashes, and it creates a damp environment where bacteria and yeast grow. Urinary and genital infections become more likely when a person cannot change or wash regularly. Beyond physical health, the lack of privacy and supplies can keep girls away from school, women away from work and people away from the shared spaces they need. Good planning protects health, dignity and participation at the same time.

## Equipment and materials

- Disposable pads or cloth pads, enough for each day of the period plus a margin for delays.
- Soap, a small basin, a clean cloth and a small towel.
- Two sealed bags, one for used items and one for clean ones.
- A small screen made from a sheet or blanket, with cord or pegs.
- A drying line in a sunny place that is not visible from walkways.

## Supplies

- Pads or cloths, enough for each period plus a margin for delays.
- Disposable pads or tampons, if available, kept in a sealed bag to protect them from damp.
- Reusable cloth pads, if you can wash and dry them properly. Make several so that one is always clean.
- Soap, a small towel and a bag for used items.

## Change and wash

1. Change pads or cloths often, at least every four to six hours, and more often on heavy days.
2. Wash your hands with soap before and after changing, using a little water if none is available.
3. Wash the body with clean water and a cloth, keeping the genital area clean and dry. Wipe from front to back.
4. Rinse used cloths in cold water first, then wash in hot water with soap, and dry them fully in sun.

## Step-by-step in detail

Before changing, prepare the clean item, the bag and the cloth so that everything is within reach. Use the screened space, and keep the bag open beside you. Remove the used item carefully and fold it inwards, then place it straight into the bag. Clean the area with a damp cloth from front to back, and dry it gently. Fasten the clean item and wash your hands. Seal the bag and move it away from the sleeping area.

## Adjusting for conditions

- **Hot weather:** change more often, and dry cloths quickly in full sun to prevent odour.
- **Cold weather:** dry cloths indoors near a warm wall, and do not hang them over a cold, damp surface.
- **Limited water:** use the cold rinse water for the first rinse, and save a little clean water for hand washing.
- **Teenagers and girls starting periods:** explain the process in advance, so that the first period does not come as a shock.

## Privacy

Find a private space for changing and washing. Use a sheet or blanket to make a screen where a room is not available. Tell a trusted adult or group leader where the space is, so that the need for it is recognised and respected.

## Waste

Wrap used disposable items in a bag, and dispose of them with other waste. See Latrines and Waste. Never leave used items in open ground, where they draw flies and animals.

## Pain and health

Pain relief can help cramps, and rest with a warm cloth on the lower abdomen often helps too. Seek care for heavy bleeding that soaks through a pad every hour, for bleeding lasting more than two hours, or for fever, severe pain or unusual discharge.

## Checklist

- Enough clean pads or cloths for each day.
- Soap and water ready at the changing area.
- Private space identified and screened.
- Used items sealed and disposed of.
- Cloths washed and fully dried after use.

## Common mistakes

- Wearing the same pad for too long, which causes infection and odour.
- Drying cloths damp in a dark place, where they grow mould.
- Hiding soiled cloths in a shared area.
- Ignoring signs of infection, such as itching, a strong smell or a rash.

## Practice before you need it

Plan a monthly supply count, so that stocks are checked before each cycle rather than during it. Practise the changing and washing steps with the screen in place, and time how long a full cycle takes. Agree in advance who will look after the private space and how it is kept clean and dry.

## When it fails

If cloths or pads run out, use clean cloth folded to fit, change it often, and wash it at the first opportunity. If irritation or a rash appears, stop using the product, wash and dry the area, and switch to a different option. Seek care if the problem continues.

**Warning:** never flush menstrual waste in a latrine that is shared, unless it is designed for it. Blocked latrines spread waste and disease quickly, and they are costly to fix in a crisis.
