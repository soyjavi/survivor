# Handling Waste From the Sick

Waste from an ill person can carry bacteria and viruses that spread through touch, splashes and contaminated surfaces. Careful handling keeps caregivers safe and stops the illness spreading to the rest of the household. The core principles are barrier protection, containment, disinfection and correct disposal, applied consistently rather than only when something looks dirty.

> @image Caregiver in a plastic apron and disposable gloves tying off a heavy waste bag beside a bucket and a bed with a washable sheet.

## Protecting Yourself

Wear disposable gloves that cover the wrists, a waterproof apron, and eye protection if splashing is likely. Use a face covering when handling vomit or diarrhoea. Put on gloves before touching waste or bedding and before cleaning any surface the patient has touched. Keep a supply of gloves, aprons and bags, and do not reuse disposable items.

1. Put on the apron first, then the eye protection and mask.
2. Put on gloves last, pulling cuffs over the apron sleeves.
3. Keep the gloves on until all cleaning is finished.
4. Remove gloves by peeling them inside out, then wash your hands.

## Collecting and Bagging

Work from the cleanest area toward the most contaminated. Handle soiled items as little as possible, and avoid shaking them, since shaking releases particles into the air. Place soiled linen directly into a leak-resistant bag or a bucket with a lid. Tie bags tightly with the first knot and seal with a second, leaving a small amount of air.

- Place sharp items such as needles and broken glass in a rigid container, never loose in a bag.
- Use double bags for heavily contaminated waste.
- Label bags that contain waste from a sick person.

## Disinfecting Surfaces and Linen

Clean surfaces the patient touched, such as bedside tables, door handles, taps and toilet seats, with a disinfectant. Dilute bleach is effective for many organisms when used at the correct strength and left in contact for the stated time. Wash soiled linen in hot water with detergent, and dry it fully. Wash contaminated clothing separately from the rest of the laundry.

1. Clean obvious soil first with detergent and water.
2. Apply disinfectant and leave it for the required time.
3. Wipe dry or allow to air dry.
4. Discard single-use cloths in the waste bag.

## Disposal

Dispose of sealed waste away from water sources, food, children and animals. A deep pit with lime added, or a designated incinerator, works where local rules allow. Never burn plastic waste in open fires, since toxic fumes result. Wash the outside of containers before reuse, and disinfect the inside before storing them again.

## Preparing a Dedicated Space

Where possible, set aside one area for the patient, with a dedicated toilet or bucket, bedding and cleaning kit. Keep the space ventilated, and place a container with disinfectant and a bin with a lid at the entrance. Caregivers should enter and leave in a consistent order, always putting protection on before entering and removing it before leaving.

Label the waste bin clearly and keep it separate from household rubbish. Use a container that can be closed and carried without spilling. If the patient must be moved, prepare a clean route in advance so that contaminated items are not carried through shared rooms.

## Removing Protection Safely

Take off protective equipment in a set order to avoid touching contaminated surfaces. Remove the gloves first, peeling each one inside out and dropping it into the waste bag. Then remove the apron by untying it at the back and rolling it away from the body. Remove eye protection and the mask last, handling them only by their straps. Wash your hands thoroughly afterwards with soap and water, even if the gloves appeared intact.

## Caring for Bedding and Clothing

Remove soiled bedding gently and avoid shaking it. Place it straight into a bag or a tub filled with disinfectant solution, and leave it soaking for the period recommended for the product in use. After soaking, wash with hot water and detergent and dry completely in sunlight or a warm, airy place. Launder the caregiver's clothing separately if it became soiled.

**Warning:** Treat all waste from a sick person as potentially infectious, and never touch it with bare hands. Never reuse disposable gloves or aprons. Seek medical advice quickly if you develop symptoms after contact, and keep the patient separate from others where possible. Keep sharp items well away from children, since puncture wounds can transmit infection.
