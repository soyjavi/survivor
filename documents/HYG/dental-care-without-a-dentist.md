# Dental Care Without a Dentist

Poor dental health causes pain, infection and difficulty eating, and a painful mouth makes it harder to drink enough water and eat well. Simple daily care prevents most problems, and it costs almost nothing. Start the routine early, before pain appears, because tooth decay develops quietly.

## Why it matters

Teeth and gums are a gateway for infection. Bacteria from a decaying tooth or an inflamed gum can spread into the jaw, the face and even the bloodstream in serious cases. Dental pain also changes behaviour: people eat less, drink less, sleep badly and become irritable. A few minutes of cleaning each day removes most of the bacterial film that causes these problems, and it does so with equipment most households already have.

## Equipment and materials

- A soft toothbrush, or a twig with frayed ends if no brush is available.
- Fluoride toothpaste if you have it, or a pinch of salt or baking soda.
- Thread, dental floss or a thin strip of clean cloth for cleaning between teeth.
- A small cup for rinsing, and warm water.
- A clean, dry place to store the brush, away from the latrine.

## Clean your teeth

1. Brush twice a day with a soft brush and a small amount of fluoride toothpaste, if you have it.
2. Without toothpaste, use a little salt or baking soda mixed with water.
3. Brush for about two minutes, covering all surfaces, the gum line and the tongue. Use short, gentle strokes.
4. Clean between teeth with thread or a thin strip of cloth, pulling it gently in a C shape around each tooth.

Brushing too hard wears down enamel and damages the gums, so light pressure is better than a vigorous scrub.

## Step-by-step in detail

Hold the brush at a slight angle to the gum line, so that the bristles reach just under it. Move the brush in small circles or short back-and-forth strokes over two or three teeth at a time, then move to the next group. Brush the chewing surfaces with a firm but gentle sweep, and clean the inner surfaces, which are often missed. Brush the tongue lightly from back to front. Rinse the mouth with a small amount of clean water, spit it out, and rinse the brush before you put it away.

## Adjusting for conditions

- **Very little water:** wipe the teeth with a damp cloth after eating, and rinse once with a few sips rather than a full cup.
- **Cold or dry weather:** chapped lips and a dry mouth make the gums more fragile, so drink water regularly.
- **Children:** help them brush until they can do it well, and use only a smear or a pea-sized amount of toothpaste.
- **Pregnancy or illness:** keep the routine going, because gum problems can worsen in pregnancy and during infections.

## Limit sugar

Sugar feeds the bacteria that cause decay. Keep sweet food and drink to meal times, rather than snacking through the day, and rinse the mouth with water afterwards. Dried fruit and sweet tea count too, because they stick to the teeth.

## Keep gums healthy

- Rinse with warm salt water if the gums are sore, several times a day.
- Bleeding gums or swelling need attention. If they do not settle within a few days, seek help.
- Chew firm, fibrous food where possible, because it cleans the teeth naturally.

## Toothbrush care

- Rinse the brush after use and store it upright to dry.
- Do not share toothbrushes, because they can pass infection between people.
- Replace a brush when the bristles are worn, or every few months.

## Checklist

- Brush morning and night.
- Clean between teeth at least once a day.
- Limit sugar to meal times.
- Rinse with salt water when gums are sore.
- Check the mouth each week for swelling or sores.

## Common mistakes

- Brushing straight after acidic food, which softens enamel.
- Using sharp metal or sticks to clean between teeth.
- Ignoring a sore tooth until the face swells.
- Sharing a toothbrush or cup with someone who is unwell.

## Practice before you need it

Build the habit while teeth are healthy and water is plentiful. Set a fixed time for brushing, such as after breakfast and before sleep, and keep the brush in the same place each day. Practise flossing with thread on a few teeth until the movement feels natural, because it is much harder to learn under pressure or in the dark.

## Dental emergencies

For severe pain, swelling, a knocked-out tooth or bleeding that will not stop, seek help from the nearest clinic or health worker. If the face swells and fever appears, seek help quickly, because the infection can spread into the jaw and throat.

## When it fails

If pain continues for more than two days despite salt water rinses and careful cleaning, the tooth may be infected. Keep the mouth clean, avoid chewing on that side, and seek care from the nearest clinic or health worker. Do not apply pain relief directly to the gum, because it can burn the tissue.

**Warning:** do not use sharp tools to pick or dig at teeth. You can damage the gum and cause infection that spreads deep into the jaw.
