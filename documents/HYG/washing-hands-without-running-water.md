# Washing Hands Without Running Water

Clean hands stop the spread of diarrhoea, respiratory infection and skin infection. Hand washing needs less water than most people think, and the method matters more than the amount. A thin stream of water with soap and firm scrubbing removes far more germs than a quick splash under a tap.

## Why it matters

Germs from faeces, raw food and sick people sit mainly on the hands, under the nails and between the fingers. A quick rinse moves them around without removing them. Proper washing lifts the dirt and germs off the skin, and soap breaks down the fatty layer that holds them there. Few habits protect a household as well, and few cost so little to do.

## When to wash

- After using the toilet or handling waste, including nappies.
- After caring for a sick person, or touching animals.
- Before preparing or eating food, and before feeding a child.
- After handling raw meat, fish, rubbish or dirty objects.

## Equipment and materials

- A clean basin used only for hand washing, and a jug with a narrow spout.
- Soap, or ash from a cold fire if soap has run out.
- A clean cloth or towel for drying, changed daily.
- A covered container of clean water, placed by the basin.
- Alcohol gel with at least 60 per cent alcohol, kept for emergencies.

## Method with a basin

1. Pour about half a litre of clean water into a basin. Keep the basin for hand washing only.
2. Wet your hands, then rub soap or ash over them. Ash works as a cleaning agent when soap is gone.
3. Scrub the backs of the hands, palms, between the fingers, around the thumbs and under the nails for at least 20 seconds. Count slowly to help you keep time.
4. Rinse with a small amount of clean water, poured from a jug over the hands. Let the water run off, not back into the basin.
5. Dry on a clean cloth or let them air dry.

## Method with a jug

Hold a jug above the basin, and pour a thin stream over the hands while scrubbing. This uses far less water than rinsing under a tap, and it leaves less dirt on the skin. Refill the jug from a covered container, never by dipping a cup into it.

## Step-by-step in detail

Rub the palms together first, then interlock the fingers and rub the back of each hand. Clasp the fingers and rub the knuckles against the opposite palm. Rub each thumb with a twisting motion held in the other hand. Rub the fingertips into the palm, and then rinse. Count to twenty while you scrub, or hum a short song that lasts about twenty seconds. Repeat for children, helping them with the thumbs and nails, which they often miss.

## Adjusting for conditions

- **Cold weather:** warm the water in a covered pot before use, and dry the hands fully to prevent cracking.
- **Hot weather:** wash more often and use a shaded basin so the water stays cool and does not grow bacteria.
- **Muddy or greasy hands:** wash with soap and water first, because gel cannot cut through grease or dirt.
- **Infants and toddlers:** wash their hands at the same moments as adults, and keep their faces and toys clean too.

## Alcohol gel

If you have hand gel with at least 60 per cent alcohol, use it when water is not available. Rub the gel over all surfaces until dry, which usually takes about 20 seconds. Gel does not remove dirt or some germs, so wash with water when you can.

## Make it routine

Place a wash station by each toilet and each food area. Keep it stocked with soap and clean water, and check it each day. A station that is always ready is used far more often than one that has to be set up.

## Checklist

- Basin and jug kept clean.
- Soap or ash within reach.
- Water refilled from a covered container.
- Hands scrubbed for at least 20 seconds.
- Gel kept for times when water is short.

## Common mistakes

- Rinsing the hands in the basin water after scrubbing.
- Skipping the thumbs, fingertips and nails.
- Using gel on dirty hands, where it does little.
- Leaving the wash station empty for hours.

## Practice before you need it

Practise the six-step method at home with a timer, and check the result by looking at the hands under good light. Ask each family member to show the steps, and correct any missed areas. Practise with the jug and basin as well as with tap water, so that the method is familiar before the water runs short.

## When it fails

If skin becomes cracked, red or sore from frequent washing, use a little oil or cream after drying and reduce the use of harsh soap. If the wash station runs out of water, move hand washing to the nearest clean source and tell the group. Never use water from a source that is known to be contaminated.

**Warning:** do not use alcohol gel on visibly dirty hands, and keep it away from children, who may swallow it. Clean the hands with water first when they are greasy or muddy.
