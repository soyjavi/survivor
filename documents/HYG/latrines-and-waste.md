# Latrines and Waste

Human waste spreads disease through water, flies and hands. A simple latrine, sited and managed well, prevents most of that spread. The most important decisions happen before any digging starts: where the latrine goes, how far it sits from water, and who is responsible for keeping it clean.

## Why it matters

Faeces carry bacteria, viruses and parasites that cause diarrhoea, cholera and worm infections. Where toilets are not available, people defecate in the open, and rain carries the waste into wells, streams and food gardens. Young children are especially at risk, because they play on the ground and put their hands in their mouths. A well-sited latrine that is kept covered and clean breaks this chain at its first link.

## Equipment and materials

- Spade, mattock and a long-handled shovel for digging.
- Timber or bricks for the seat, and a lid that fits tightly.
- Ash or dry soil stored in a covered container beside the seat.
- Sturdy bags, a bucket with a lid and gloves for the bucket toilet.
- A hand wash station with soap and water, placed near the exit.

## Siting

- At least 30 metres from any well, spring, stream or water store.
- Downhill from water sources, so rain does not carry waste into them.
- Away from living areas and food preparation, and private enough to use safely at night.
- Not in ground that floods or where the water table is close to the surface.

## Simple pit latrine

1. Dig a pit about 1 metre deep and 60 cm wide. Keep the top edge firm, and support loose soil with stones or timber.
2. Build a sturdy seat or platform over the pit, with a lid that closes tightly.
3. Build a simple shelter for privacy, with a door that closes from the inside.
4. After each use, cover the waste with soil or ash. This reduces smell and keeps flies away.
5. Fill and close the pit when it is nearly full, and dig a new one in a new place.

## Step-by-step in detail

Mark the pit site with stakes and string before you dig, and check the distance from water with a rope or measuring line rather than by eye. Dig in the dry season if you can, because wet soil collapses. Line the top edge with stones or a timber frame so the rim does not crumble under weight. Once the seat is fixed, test it by standing on it and leaning, and check that the lid closes without gaps. Write the date the pit was started on a marker beside it, so that the team knows when it will need replacing.

## Adjusting for conditions

- **Rocky ground:** dig a shallower pit with a wider rim, or use a raised platform above the surface.
- **Sandy or loose soil:** line the pit with stones or a sturdy liner, and avoid digging close to the edge of a slope.
- **Heavy rain season:** raise the seat and cover the ground with a slope that carries water away.
- **Shared latrines:** assign a cleaning rota, and provide one for women and girls with a private screened entrance.

## Bucket toilet

Line a bucket with a plastic bag and place it under a seat. After each use, cover the waste with ash or dry soil. Seal the bag when it is full, and dispose of it away from water, in a pit dug for the purpose.

## Rubbish

- Keep bins covered, away from living areas.
- Burn or bury rubbish away from water sources, if local rules allow.
- Do not leave rubbish where animals or children can reach it.

## Flies

Flies carry disease from waste to food in minutes. Keep latrines covered and clean, and cover food at all times, even for short periods. Use fly screens on food stores where they are available.

## Checklist

- Latrine at least 30 metres from water and downhill from it.
- Lid kept closed when not in use.
- Ash or soil ready beside the seat.
- Hand wash station placed close by.
- Pit checked each week for overflow or collapse.

## Common mistakes

- Digging too close to a well, because it is convenient.
- Leaving the lid open, which attracts flies.
- Building the latrine uphill from a water source.
- Letting the pit fill up before anyone plans the next one.

## Practice before you need it

Before the crisis, walk the site with the household and mark the pit, the latrine and the water sources on a simple sketch. Agree who will check the lid, add ash and report problems. Practise filling the bucket toilet and sealing the bag, so the first real use is not a first attempt.

## When it fails

If the latrine smells strongly, overflows or sinks, stop using it and cover it with soil. Build a new latrine in a safe place. If the water source has been contaminated, boil or treat the water before drinking, and tell the group so that others do not use it.

**Warning:** never dispose of waste into streams, wells or open ground near homes. Contaminated water can cause severe diarrhoea and can kill young children and older people quickly.
