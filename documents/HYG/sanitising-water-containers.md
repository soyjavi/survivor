# Sanitising Water Containers

A clean container protects water after it has been treated. Dirt, biofilm and residues in a jerrycan or bottle can recontaminate water within days, even when the source was safe. Cleaning removes visible soil, sanitising kills remaining microbes, and proper drying and storage stop new ones from growing. The process should be repeated whenever a container is emptied and before it is refilled.

## Cleaning First

Sanitising cannot work through dirt, so start by removing all visible residue. Empty the container completely, then rinse it with clean water to loosen loose material. Scrub the inside with a brush that reaches the base and corners. Narrow-necked containers need a long brush or a small amount of coarse material and water shaken vigorously inside.

1. Pour out all remaining water and dispose of it away from the source.
2. Rinse with clean water until it runs clear.
3. Scrub the interior, neck, cap and threads.
4. Rinse again to remove loosened debris.

## Sanitising

Sanitising uses a chemical or heat to reduce microbes to safe levels. The most accessible option is a dilute chlorine solution. Household bleach of about five percent strength is typically diluted to around one teaspoon per litre of water for surfaces, but always check the label and follow local guidance. Fill the container with the solution, cap it, and shake it so every surface contacts the liquid. Let it stand for at least thirty minutes.

- Use only plain bleach without added fragrance or detergents.
- Mix fresh solution each time, since strength falls quickly once opened.
- Clean the caps and seals with the same solution.

## Rinsing and Drying

After sanitising, empty the solution and rinse the container two or three times with water that you are going to use for drinking. This removes the chlorine taste and residue. Then dry it fully. Turn the container upside down with the cap off and leave it in clean air until no water runs from the neck. Moisture left inside encourages growth, so dry thoroughly even if it takes a day.

1. Rinse once with clean water and drain.
2. Repeat if the container smells of chlorine.
3. Place upside down on a clean rack in shade.
4. Check that no droplets remain before capping.

## Storing

Store containers capped, in a clean, dry, shaded place, off the ground. Keep them away from chemicals, fuel, pesticides and waste. Label each container with its purpose and fill date. Do not use one container for both drinking water and other liquids. Inspect stored containers each month for cracks, odours and loose caps.

## Choosing the Right Container for Each Purpose

Not every container should hold drinking water. Keep one set for water that will be drunk, a second for washing and a third for waste. Mark each one clearly with paint, tape or a permanent marker, and never move a container from one group to another without a full cleaning and sanitising cycle. Mixing purposes is the most common cause of contaminated drinking water.

Narrow-mouthed containers are harder to clean. Choose wide-mouthed vessels where possible, since they can be scrubbed and dried more thoroughly. If a narrow container is the only option, use a small amount of clean sand and water inside, shake it firmly, then rinse until clear.

## Drying Conditions

Dry containers in a place where air moves freely, such as a rack in a breezy yard or a shelf beside an open door. Sunlight speeds drying and helps reduce surviving microbes, but do not leave plastic in strong sun for days, since heat weakens it and can cause it to crack. Once dry, cap the container or cover the opening with a clean cloth.

## Routine Checks Between Uses

Before refilling, check each container for cracks, residual sediment, odour or a film inside. Lift the cap and look at the seal, and replace any cap that no longer closes firmly. A quick check each time costs little and catches problems early.

**Warning:** Never mix bleach with ammonia, vinegar or other cleaners, since toxic gases can form. Wear gloves and eye protection when handling concentrated bleach, and keep it well away from children. Do not use any container that once held fuel, pesticide or a toxic substance for drinking water, even after cleaning. Discard any container that is cracked, leaching colour or smells persistently after treatment.
