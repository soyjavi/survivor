# Meeting Points

A meeting point is a place where everyone goes when they cannot reach home or each other. Choose them before the crisis, and make sure everyone knows where they are. A meeting point only works if people can reach it on foot, in the dark, with a child and without help.

![Map sketch: map with person-standing home at centre, three waypoint-pin points each on a different arrow-straight direction; labels: home, near home, outside neighbourhood, further away; one blocked route still leaves another.](assets/meeting-points-1.svg)

## Why it matters

When a crisis strikes, the first hour is confused. Roads are blocked, phones fail and people move in different directions. A known meeting point gives each person a fixed target, so they stop searching and start moving. Without one, families often wander the streets for hours, each looking for the others in the wrong place.

Meeting points also give rescuers and officials a way to find people. A named place with a posted list is far easier to check than a dozen houses scattered across the district.

## Equipment and materials

- A laminated card for each meeting point, with the address, a photo and a landmark.
- A pencil and a waterproof notepad, fixed to the card with a cord.
- A small torch and a whistle kept in the household bag.
- A sealed envelope containing a copy of the family contact list.
- Walking shoes and a light bag, ready by the door.

## Choose three

1. **Near home:** a point within walking distance, such as a neighbour's gate or a street corner. Use it for fires or small emergencies.
2. **Outside the neighbourhood:** a place such as a school or community building, reached by a route you have walked.
3. **Further away:** a point outside the area, in a different direction, for evacuations.

## Criteria

- Safe from traffic, floods, falling trees and fire, and not on a flood plain or under power lines.
- Easy to find, with a landmark that everyone recognises.
- Open at all hours, or at least accessible without a key.
- Known to the whole household, including children and older relatives.

## Step-by-step in detail: walking the route

1. Leave home at a normal pace, and time the walk to the first point.
2. Note every crossing, slope and blocked path, and mark them on your map.
3. Arrive at the point, and check the landmark, the entrance and any notice board.
4. Time the walk to the second and third points, and record the totals on the card.
5. Walk the route again in the dark, with the torch, to see what changes.
6. Write the final times on the family card, and keep a copy in the bag.

## Use them

- Go to the nearest point and wait for a set time, such as two hours.
- If no one arrives, move on to the next point and leave a note.
- Do not return home alone if the area is dangerous.
- Count the people who arrive, and tell the group who is still missing.

## Adjusting for conditions

- **Flood risk:** choose a point on high ground, and walk the route at the lowest tide or water level you expect.
- **Night:** the landmark must be visible with a torch, so pick one that reflects light or has a clear shape.
- **Mobility limits:** check for steps, kerbs and steep hills, and pick a second route for wheelchairs or prams.
- **Winter:** plan for ice and short days, and keep a flask of warm drink in the bag.

## Common mistakes

- **Choosing a point you have never visited.** Walk the route before you need it.
- **Waiting too long.** Set a time limit and stick to it.
- **Choosing one point only.** Use three, so one blocked route does not leave you stranded.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Likely cause | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Point cannot be found | Landmark changed or removed | Use the second landmark on the card |
| Route blocked | Fallen tree or flooding | Take the alternative path marked on the map |
| Group split up | Different walking speeds | Agree one leader and one rear person |
| No one at the point | Family went elsewhere | Leave a note and move on as planned |

## Practise

Walk to each point at least once a year with the whole family. Children should know the route by heart, and each adult should be able to lead a child there.

## Checklist

- Three points chosen, each visited on foot this year.
- Cards laminated, with addresses and landmarks.
- Walking times written on the family card.
- Torch, whistle and waterproof notepad in the household bag.
- Route checked after any change to roads or buildings.

## What to do when it fails

If a point is closed or unsafe, go to the next one and leave a clear note at the closed point. If you arrive and cannot find anyone, leave your name and time at the site, then continue to the next point.

**Warning:** do not wait at a point if it becomes unsafe. Move to the next point and leave a clear note.
