# Charging Batteries Without a Grid

Devices are only useful while they have power. Plan the charging capacity you need, then choose a source that you can rely on. Most households underestimate how much energy their phones, radios and torches consume in a week, so start with the numbers.

## Why it matters

Every communication method in this section depends on stored energy. A radio, a phone, a torch and a radio transmitter all stop when their cells are flat, and a flat cell in the middle of a storm can cut off the one message that mattered. Charging is not a luxury task. It is the quiet support behind every other plan.

Power is also limited by the calendar. Solar output drops in winter and on cloudy days, and a car can only charge while its engine runs. A realistic plan assumes the lowest output you are likely to see, not the best.

## Equipment and materials

- A solar panel of suitable wattage, with a weatherproof cable.
- A charge controller, sized for the panel and the battery.
- A sealed deep-cycle battery or a power bank with enough capacity.
- A hand-crank generator, a car charger and a set of spare cables.
- A multimeter, so you can check voltages before connecting anything.

## Estimate the need

1. List each device and its battery capacity, in mAh or Wh, from the label.
2. Note how often each device is used, and for how long each day.
3. Add up the total energy you need each day, with a margin of about one third for losses in cables and conversion.
4. Divide the total by the number of days you expect to be without the grid, to set the storage size.

## Sources

- **Solar panel with a charge controller:** the most reliable for steady use. Place it in full sun, facing the right direction, and clean the glass regularly.
- **Car charger:** only while the engine runs. Never run a car engine in a closed garage.
- **Power bank:** charge it fully whenever any power is available, and keep it cool and dry.
- **Hand crank:** slow, but useful for short emergency charges of a radio or torch.

## Step-by-step in detail: a solar set-up

1. Choose a mounting spot with at least five hours of direct sun in the middle of the day.
2. Fix the panel firmly, angled towards the sun for your latitude, with the cable run out of the weather.
3. Place the charge controller in a dry, ventilated spot, away from the battery box.
4. Connect the panel to the controller, following the wiring sequence below.
5. Check the battery voltage on the controller display, and note it in your log.
6. Connect one device at a time, and watch the charge indicator for the first hour.

## Adjusting for conditions

- **Winter and short days:** expect a fraction of summer output, and reduce the daily load to match.
- **Cloudy weather:** a panel still produces some power, so keep it connected rather than stored.
- **Dust and pollen:** wipe the glass with a soft cloth every week, since a dirty surface can cut output noticeably.
- **Heat:** batteries and controllers lose efficiency when hot, so keep them in the shade.

## Keep devices working

- Switch off devices when not in use, and close apps that run in the background.
- Turn on airplane mode or power saving to reduce drain.
- Keep lithium batteries between 20 and 80 per cent charge where possible, for longer life.

## Wiring the system

Connect the panel to the controller first, then the battery, then the loads. Reversing the order can damage the controller, so follow the manufacturer's sequence.

## Common mistakes

- **Shading the panel.** A single shadow can cut output sharply, so mount it clear of trees and chimneys.
- **Using the wrong charger.** A charger for one battery type can overheat another.
- **Leaving batteries flat for long periods.** Recharge them as soon as you can.

## Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Likely cause | First action |
|---|---|---|
| Controller shows no input | Loose panel connector or dirty glass | Tighten the plug, clean the panel |
| Battery stays low | Panel in shade or wrong angle | Move the panel into full sun |
| Device charges slowly | Cable too thin or long | Use a shorter, thicker cable |
| Controller gets hot | Load above its rating | Reduce the connected devices |

## Checklist

- Daily energy need calculated, with a one-third margin.
- Panel cleaned and the mounting checked after each storm.
- Battery voltage recorded in the crisis log each day.
- Charger and cables for each battery type kept separate and labelled.
- Power bank charged whenever any power is available.

## What to do when it fails

If the battery does not charge, check the connections, the controller display and the panel output in direct sun. If the panel gives no voltage, check for cracks or disconnected leads before you try a different source.

**Warning:** do not leave lithium batteries charging unattended, never charge damaged batteries, and never charge them in a sealed bag.
