# Offline Maps and Information

When the internet stops, paper maps and downloaded information are the only sources you have. Navigation apps, search engines and online guides all depend on a connection, so prepare them before they are needed. A map you can read by torchlight is worth more than a perfect app with a dead battery.

![Paper map: map, waypoint-pin home circled, waypoint-pin meeting points, water-drop, tent shelter, two arrow-straight routes one line-dashed; labels: home, meeting points, water, shelter, planned route, alternative; one route is planned, the other the alternative.](assets/offline-maps-and-information-1.svg)

## Why it matters

Maps answer questions that no other source can. Which road leads out of the valley, where the nearest high ground lies, which bridge crosses the river, and where the nearest water point is. In a crisis, a wrong turn can cost hours, and a map that shows the whole region lets you plan a second route before the first one fails.

## Equipment and materials

- A waterproof map case, large enough to fold the map without tearing it.
- A pencil, an eraser and a small ruler for marking routes.
- A magnetic compass, with a clear baseplate.
- A USB stick or memory card, sealed in a plastic bag.
- A printed copy of the core guides, in a plastic folder.

> @image Folded paper road map on a table with a pencil and a compass, photographed in soft daylight

## Paper maps

- Keep a paper map of your region, at a scale that shows roads, rivers and high ground.
- Add a second map of the wider area, for routes out of the region.
- Mark your home, your meeting points, water sources and any shelters on the map, using a pencil so you can update it.
- Keep the map in a waterproof sleeve, folded so the key area is visible.

## Downloaded information

- Store key guides on a device as files, not in an app that needs the internet.
- Keep a printed copy of the most important guides: water, first aid, fire and shelter.
- Keep a USB stick or memory card with copies, in a sealed bag.
- Save guides in a format that opens without an app, such as plain PDF or text.

## Set up a simple reference folder

1. Create one folder with a clear name, and place the most important guides at the top.
2. Name each file with its subject and the date it was saved.
3. Copy the folder to a second device, if you have one.
4. Test that each file opens on a device with no connection.

![Folder tree: folder root, elbow-connector to five files; labels: water, first aid, fire, shelter, maps; the most important guides sit at the top level for quick access. NEEDS: folder, file](assets/offline-maps-and-information-3.svg)

## Step-by-step in detail: marking a paper map

1. Fold the map so your home sits near the centre of the visible panel.
2. Draw a small circle around your home, and label it with a pencil.
3. Mark each meeting point with a triangle, and add its name beside it.
4. Mark water sources with a wavy line, and shelters with a square.
5. Draw your main route out of the area in one colour, and the alternative in another.
6. Add the compass bearing for each route, and write the distance in the margin.

## Adjusting for conditions

- **Rain and damp:** use a clear sleeve, and keep the map folded under the sleeve's flap.
- **Night:** use a small red torch, which preserves night vision and still shows the map clearly.
- **Unfamiliar region:** buy a map at the largest scale you can carry, and mark landmarks you can see from the road.

## Charge and power

Devices drain fast. Keep them off most of the time, and use a solar charger or car charger when possible. See Charging Batteries Without a Grid.

## Common mistakes

- **Relying on a map app.** Apps fail when the battery or signal does.
- **Storing guides only in the cloud.** Download them to the device before the crisis.
- **Forgetting the paper copy.** Keep the printed sheet in the same bag as the map.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Likely cause | Action |
|---|---|---|
| File will not open | Missing reader or corrupt copy | Use the printed copy or the second device |
| Map unreadable | Faded pencil or creases | Redraw the key routes on a clean copy |
| Route no longer exists | Road closed or flooded | Use the alternative route marked on the map |
| Storage device not recognised | Port or adaptor fault | Try a second port or the backup memory card |

## Checklist

- Paper map of the region and the wider area in a waterproof case.
- Meeting points and water sources marked in pencil.
- Reference folder copied to a second device and a memory card.
- Printed guides for water, first aid, fire and shelter.
- Dates written on every guide, with a review date set.

## What to do when it fails

If the device will not open a file, use the printed copy. If the map is damaged, redraw the key routes and meeting points from memory, then check them against a second source as soon as one is available.

**Warning:** do not rely on a single copy of anything important. Keep a second copy somewhere separate.
