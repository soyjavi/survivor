# Family Communication Plan

When the networks fail, families get separated. A written plan lets everyone find each other without phones. Most families assume they will simply call each other, and that assumption breaks first, so the plan should work even when every phone is dead.

![Household sheet: table-grid with table-header sections; labels: contact list, out-of-area contact, three meeting points, medical notes; everything needed to reconnect fits on one page.](assets/family-communication-plan-1.svg)

## Why it matters

In a sudden emergency, people act on instinct. A parent at work may head straight for the school, a teenager may head for a friend's house, and a grandparent may stay put with no idea where anyone is. A plan written in advance turns those separate instincts into one shared set of expectations, so each person knows where to go and when to report in.

The plan also protects children. A child who knows the meeting point, the out-of-area number and the name of the adult who will collect them is far less likely to wander or to accept a stranger's offer of help.

## Equipment and materials

- One laminated contact card per person, kept in a bag or coat pocket.
- A small waterproof notebook for the household copy of the plan.
- A paper map with the meeting points marked in pencil.
- A list of the out-of-area contact, written in full with the country code.
- A spare pen, and a few spare paper sheets for notes.

## Write it down

1. **Contact list:** each person's full name, phone numbers, and addresses, on paper, in each person's bag.
2. **Out-of-area contact:** choose one relative or friend far away. Local lines often jam, but calls to a distant place may still work. Everyone calls that person to report in.
3. **Meeting points:** one near home, one outside the neighbourhood, and one further away.
4. **Medical information:** allergies, medicines and any conditions for each person, with doses written clearly.

## Agree the rules

- If you are separated, go to the nearest meeting point and wait for the agreed time.
- Leave a note at home or at the meeting point saying where you have gone and when.
- Send short messages when you can, not long ones that slow the network.
- Agree who collects children from school, and write the name on the contact card.

## Step-by-step in detail: the first hour of separation

1. Stay where you are if it is safe, and try the out-of-area contact once.
2. If the house is unsafe, go to the nearest meeting point and wait for the agreed time.
3. Leave a note at home with the time, your destination and your next check-in.
4. At the meeting point, look for the notes left by others, and add your own.
5. Call the out-of-area contact to report in, even if you have nothing new to say.
6. Wait for the agreed period, then move to the next meeting point if no one has arrived.

## Practise

Practise the plan once or twice a year, including the walk to the meeting point and a call to the out-of-area contact. Children learn it best by doing it, so rehearse the route with them, not only with adults.

## Adjusting for conditions

- **Young children:** keep the card on a cord, and agree a safe adult they can approach in any uniform or setting.
- **Older relatives:** write the plan in large print, and check that they can reach the meeting point without stairs or steep paths.
- **Pets:** note which shelters accept animals, and keep a leash and carrier by the door.
- **Shared custody or several homes:** keep a copy of the plan at each address and agree one out-of-area contact for all of them.

## Common mistakes

- **Relying on one contact method.** Keep at least a written list and a second person to call.
- **Forgetting old numbers.** Update the list when a number or address changes.
- **Leaving children without the card.** Each child should carry a card with a name and a meeting point.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Likely cause | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Out-of-area contact does not answer | Phone switched off or lines jammed | Try again at the next agreed time |
| Meeting point is closed | Authorities moved the site | Go to the next point and leave a note |
| Child not at the point | Child followed the wrong route | Check the school and the nearest point |
| Contact card lost | Bag left behind | Rewrite it from the household copy |

## Checklist

- Contact cards written, laminated and carried by everyone.
- Out-of-area contact agreed and written in full.
- Three meeting points visited on foot this year.
- Medical details up to date for every person.
- Household copy of the plan placed with the emergency radio.

## What to do when it fails

If the out-of-area contact cannot be reached, choose the next person on the list and agree this in advance. If a meeting point is unsafe or empty, move to the next point and leave a clear note. Keep waiting only as long as the plan allows.

**Warning:** do not rely on a phone battery. Plan for the messages to fail, and keep the paper copy safe.
