# Phone Use with a Limited Battery

A phone is a valuable tool when the grid is down, but its battery is finite. Decide when to use it, and turn off everything else. A phone at ten per cent that can still make one emergency call is more useful than a phone that has been streaming all afternoon.

![Settings checklist: three step-box rows each with do mark; labels: airplane mode, lowered brightness, background data off; these three switches cut the largest drains and each takes seconds.](assets/phone-use-with-a-limited-battery-1.svg)

## Why it matters

A phone is the fastest way to reach emergency services, family and an out-of-area contact. Its usefulness depends entirely on the charge left in it at the moment you need it. Most phones lose their charge through things you rarely notice: a search for signal, a bright screen, background refresh and location tracking. Each one is small, but together they can empty a battery in a single afternoon.

Planning the phone's energy turns a vague worry into a concrete budget. You decide how much charge to spend each day, and you protect the reserve for the calls that matter.

## Equipment and materials

- A power bank with a known capacity, charged before every expected outage.
- A short charging cable and a wall or car adaptor, kept in a dry bag.
- A paper list of numbers, so you do not need to open the phone to find them.
- A small notebook for logging each time the phone was switched on.
- Optional: a cheap feature phone with a spare SIM, for calls only.

> @image Phone on a kitchen table with its screen dimmed and a small power bank beside it, in soft lamplight

## Save power

1. Turn the phone off between uses, and switch it on only at set times.
2. Lower the screen brightness and set the screen to turn off quickly.
3. Turn off Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, location and background data.
4. Turn on airplane mode between uses, and switch to a power saving mode.
5. Close apps that run in the background, and disable automatic updates.

## Step-by-step in detail: a daily charge budget

1. Check the battery level each morning, and write it in the notebook.
2. Decide how many minutes of use the day allows, such as thirty minutes in total.
3. Plan each use in advance, with the message or the check written on paper.
4. Switch the phone on, send or read, then switch it back to airplane mode.
5. Record the time you used it and the level afterwards.
6. Keep at least a fifth of the battery in reserve for one emergency call.

## Send messages well

- Send short text messages, which need less signal than calls.
- Send to the out-of-area contact when possible, since that route is more likely to work.
- Use the phone to check important information briefly, then switch it off.
- Group messages to the same person, so one connection carries several updates.

## Adjusting for conditions

- **Cold weather:** lithium cells lose capacity quickly, so keep the phone inside a coat pocket between uses.
- **Weak signal area:** move to a higher floor or a window once, send everything, and return to airplane mode.
- **Hot weather:** keep the phone in the shade, because heat drains the battery and can shut it down.
- **Several people sharing one phone:** agree a rota, and keep the list of times on the notebook.

## Keep spare power

- Carry a power bank and keep it charged when you can. See Charging Batteries Without a Grid.
- Keep the charger and cables together in a dry bag.
- Use a car charger only while the engine runs, and never in a closed space.

## Common mistakes

- **Searching for signal.** A phone in a weak area drains quickly, so move to a better spot or wait.
- **Leaving the screen on.** The display is the largest drain, so keep it off.
- **Waiting too long to charge.** Top up whenever any power is available.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Likely cause | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Battery drops in minutes | Signal search or background apps | Enable airplane mode and close apps |
| Phone switches off at a level it showed | Cold or failing cell | Warm the phone and recharge slowly |
| Messages will not send | No signal at this spot | Move to a higher point and try once |
| Charger gets warm | Damaged cable or wrong adaptor | Stop charging and use a different cable |

## Plan for failure

Assume the phone will fail at some point. Keep the family contact list on paper. See Family Communication Plan.

> @image Paper contact list pinned beside a dead phone on a windowsill, with a torch resting on top

## Checklist

- Power bank charged, and cables kept in a dry bag.
- Paper contact list stored with the phone charger.
- Daily budget written in the notebook.
- Reserve charge kept for one emergency call.
- Background apps and location services switched off.

## What to do when it fails

If the phone is dead, use a paper contact list, the radio for updates and a runner or written note for messages. If you still have some charge, reserve it for one emergency call, and make that call short.

**Warning:** do not let the phone battery run completely flat when you may need an emergency call.
