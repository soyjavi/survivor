# Keeping a Crisis Log

A written log keeps the household informed, helps you spot patterns, and gives you a record for insurance, medical care and later planning. Memory fades under stress, so a few lines written each day are worth more than detailed notes written once. Keep it on paper, because paper does not need power.

![Log page: table-grid with table-header columns, one row per day; labels: date, water, food, fuel, medicines, events; comparing stock down a column shows a falling supply.](assets/keeping-a-crisis-log-1.svg)

## Why it matters

Under pressure, people forget what they have already done. They open the same tin twice, ask the same question of three neighbours or give the last of the medicine to the wrong person. A log stops that repetition and gives the household one shared memory.

It also protects you later. Insurers, doctors and officials often ask for dates and details weeks after an event, and a memory written in the middle of a crisis is far more reliable than one reconstructed from stress.

## Equipment and materials

- A hardbound notebook with numbered pages, about the size of a paperback.
- A pencil, because ink can run when damp and pencil survives most weather.
- A waterproof bag or a zip-top plastic sleeve to hold the notebook.
- A ruler and a few coloured pens for marking sections.
- A second notebook, kept in a different location, as a backup copy.

## What to record

- **Daily:** date, weather, water and food stock, fuel left, medicines used.
- **Events:** announcements from officials, supply runs, any problem and how it was solved.
- **People:** who was where, who needs medical care, who has left or arrived.
- **Decisions:** what was decided, by whom, and why.

## How to keep it

1. Use a notebook that can be carried in a waterproof bag.
2. Write the date and time for every entry.
3. Keep entries short and factual, with one event per line.
4. Record quantities in the same units every time, such as litres of water or days of food.
5. Mark any unconfirmed report with the word unconfirmed.

## Step-by-step in detail: a daily entry

1. At the same time each evening, open the notebook to the next blank page.
2. Write the date, the time and the weather in one line.
3. Count the water in litres, the food in days, and the fuel in litres or hours.
4. List each medicine given, with the dose and the person who received it.
5. Write one line for each event, decision or visitor from the day.
6. Read the page back, then initial it so you know it is complete.

## Use it

- Review the log each evening with the household.
- Use it to plan the next day's water, food and fuel, and to spot a stock running low.
- Share key entries with the group, to keep everyone informed.
- Note who has been told what, so the same message is not repeated or contradicted.

## Adjusting for conditions

- **Cold or wet days:** write quickly and keep the notebook inside a coat, because pencil smudges easily when wet.
- **Low light:** keep a small torch clipped to the notebook, and use larger handwriting.
- **Several households:** give each one a separate colour, so the shared record stays readable.
- **Children:** let them draw the supply counts as pictures, and copy the numbers in for them.

## Common mistakes

- **Writing long narratives.** Short entries are easier to read in a hurry.
- **Changing units.** Litres one day and cans the next make totals useless.
- **Skipping days.** A gap hides the moment a supply ran out.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Likely cause | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Totals do not add up | Mixed units | Convert old entries and note the conversion |
| Entries are illegible | Hurried or damp writing | Rewrite the page from memory and mark it |
| Forgotten day | Exhaustion | Fill the gap with a note and the date |
| Two people wrote conflicting figures | No single recorder | Assign one recorder and check counts together |

## After the crisis

Keep the log. It helps with claims, medical history and planning the next time. A dated record of what happened is also useful when you talk to officials about what your household needed.

## Checklist

- Notebook and pencil kept in a waterproof bag.
- One named person responsible for each evening entry.
- Units agreed and written on the first page.
- Backup copy kept at the meeting point.
- Unconfirmed reports labelled with a time.

## What to do when it fails

If the notebook is lost or wet, rewrite the most important entries from memory as soon as you can, and mark them as rewritten. If the main copy cannot be found, keep the second copy and continue from there.

**Warning:** keep the log in a second place, such as a sealed bag at a meeting point, in case the main copy is lost.
