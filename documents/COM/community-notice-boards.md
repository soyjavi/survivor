# Community Notice Boards

A notice board in a shared space gives a neighbourhood a single place for news, requests and offers. It works without any network, and it reaches people who have no phone, no battery and no internet. A board that is kept tidy and up to date becomes trusted quickly, which is what makes it useful.

![Board zones: table-grid with table-header rows stacked top to bottom; labels: official news, requests, offers, lost and found, meetings; official news sits highest so it is read first.](assets/community-notice-boards-1.svg)

## Why it matters

When phones fail, people do not stop needing information. They need to know where water is being handed out, whether the school is open, who has a generator and who needs a lift to the clinic. A board answers these questions for everyone at once, so volunteers are not forced to repeat the same answer at every doorstep.

A board also reveals the hidden needs of a street. An elderly neighbour who cannot walk to the shop may never phone for help, but a request on the board will reach the right person.

## Equipment and materials

- A weatherproof board, or a sheet of hardboard with a clear plastic cover.
- Coloured card for each section, so readers can find categories quickly.
- Drawing pins, tape, a permanent marker and a pencil.
- A stack of blank notice sheets, with a printed template on the back.
- A small sealed box to hold spares and a copy of the current contact list.

![Single notice sheet: table-grid with five rows, table-header column left; labels: date, source, text, contact, removal date; every notice carries source and expiry so old items are removed. NEEDS: notice-sheet](assets/community-notice-boards-2.svg)

## Choose a place

- A wall or board in a central, sheltered place, such as a shop front, a school gate or a community hall.
- Keep it visible from the street and protected from rain, wind and direct sun.
- Choose a spot with room for people to stand together without blocking the pavement.
- Ask the owner of the wall or building for permission before you fix anything to it.

> @image Weatherproof notice board fixed to a shop wall, with people reading a handwritten sheet in daylight

## Organise it

1. Divide the board into sections: official news, requests for help, offers of help, lost and found, and meetings.
2. Date every notice, and remove notices after a set time, such as seven days.
3. Write in clear, large print, using one notice per sheet.
4. Put a clear heading at the top of each section so people can find what they need quickly.

## Step-by-step in detail: posting a notice

1. Write the notice in plain words, with the what, the where and the when.
2. Add the source, such as the official body or the person who saw the event.
3. Add a contact name and a route, such as a house number or a meeting place.
4. Write the date and the removal date at the top.
5. Read the notice aloud once, to check it makes sense to someone who has not heard the story.
6. Pin it in the right section, with the newest notice at the top.

![Sequence: step-1 write, step-2 source, step-3 contact, then date, read aloud, pin, arrow-straight between steps; labels: write, source, contact, date, read aloud, pin; the spoken test comes before pinning.](assets/community-notice-boards-3.svg)

## Rules

- Post only facts you can confirm, and say where the information came from.
- Do not post personal details of people without their permission.
- Keep the tone calm and practical, and avoid rumours or speculation.
- Mark anything unconfirmed as unconfirmed, so readers can judge it.

## Adjusting for conditions

- **Heavy rain:** use a clear sleeve for each sheet, and tape the edges with waterproof tape.
- **Wind:** pin each corner, not only the top, and keep spares in the sealed box.
- **Low literacy or many languages:** add simple symbols, or write key notices in two languages.

## Keep it going

- Assign one or two people to update the board each day, and name them on the board.
- Copy important notices by hand for households without access, and deliver them to those who cannot walk.
- Keep a spare set of pins, tape and blank sheets in a sealed box nearby.

![Before and after: left table-grid cluttered with faded sheets, arrow-straight, right table-grid with do-marked dated sheets in sections; labels: before, after; refreshing is a daily removal step. NEEDS: notice-sheet](assets/community-notice-boards-4.svg)

## Common mistakes

- **Letting old notices stay up.** They mislead people and fill the board.
- **Mixing offers and requests.** Keep them in separate sections, so people do not miss a request.
- **Posting an unchecked alert.** Copy official alerts exactly, and add the time you saw them.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Notices blow away | Only one pin used | Pin all four corners and add tape |
| People stop reading | Board is cluttered | Remove expired sheets and tidy each section |
| Requests are missed | Requests buried under news | Move requests to the top-left section |
| Conflicting messages | Two people posted different facts | Post a dated correction at the same spot |

> @image Volunteer pinning a dated sheet to a board in the rain, with a clear plastic sleeve over the notice

## Checklist

- Board sited, with written permission from the owner.
- Sections labelled and visible from the street.
- Named volunteers for each day of the week.
- Spare pins, tape and blank sheets in a sealed box.
- Expired notices removed at the same time each day.

## What to do when it fails

If the board is damaged or stolen, move the notices to a second place and say where the new board is. If a notice turns out to be wrong, replace it with a correction at the same spot, so the error does not survive.

**Warning:** a notice board can spread rumours as easily as news. Check important items before posting, and see Verifying Rumours.
