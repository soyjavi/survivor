# Sending Messages Without Internet

When the internet fails, mobile networks may still work for short texts. When they fail too, you need other methods. Know them before you need them, and practise at least two, because any single method can fail without warning.

![Ranked ladder: ladder with five rungs; labels bottom to top: mobile text, short-range radio, amateur radio, runners, written notes; the top two need no power or network, so they form the backup plan. NEEDS: folded-note, person-walking](assets/sending-messages-without-internet-1.svg)

## Why it matters

Messages carry the information that keeps people apart from danger: where someone is, whether a child has been collected, whether a supply run succeeded. When the usual channels go silent, families fill the gap with guesses, and guesses cause panic. A reliable way to send a short message turns a silence into a fact.

Different methods suit different distances, and no method works everywhere. Choosing two that match your neighbourhood, and practising them, gives you a backup when one fails.

## Equipment and materials

- A pad of paper and pencils, kept in a waterproof bag.
- Sealable plastic bags, one for each message.
- Two short-range radios, charged, with spare cells.
- A pin, a tape roll and a marker for fixing notes to doors and boards.
- A printed list of contacts, so a runner can confirm a reply.

## Methods, from most to least reliable

1. **Written notes:** pinned to a known place, such as a door, a board or a meeting point.
2. **Runners:** a trusted person walks the message to its destination. Use this for nearby families.
3. **Amateur radio:** if you hold a licence and have a set. See Amateur Radio Basics.
4. **Short-range radio:** family walkie-talkies, useful within a few kilometres.
5. **Mobile text messages:** short, when the network is partly working.

## Write a good note

- Put the date and time at the top.
- Write who it is from, who it is for, and what they need to know.
- Keep it short, in plain language, with one message per note.
- Sign it, and say where you can be found.

## Send by runner

1. Write the note, and seal it in a plastic bag so it stays dry.
2. Agree the route, the arrival time and the reply the runner should bring back.
3. Give the runner a copy of the address and a way to confirm delivery.
4. Ask the recipient to reply by the same route, so the loop closes.

## Step-by-step in detail: a short-range radio exchange

1. Switch the radio on, and set it to the channel agreed with the family.
2. Listen for a few seconds to confirm the channel is clear.
3. Press the key, state the name of the person you are calling, then your own name.
4. Give the message in one or two short sentences, then release the key.
5. Wait for the reply, and confirm the key word agreed in advance.
6. Switch off to save power, and note the time in your log.

## Keep messages safe

- Do not leave messages with information that could help a thief, such as empty houses or valuables.
- Keep copies of important messages, and record when each one was sent.
- Check that a runner is trusted before you give them a message about a child or an older relative.

## Adjusting for conditions

- **Rain and wind:** pin notes in two places, and use clear sleeves rather than paper alone.
- **Dark nights:** write in large capitals, and fix the note at eye height with a torch nearby.
- **Hilly or built-up areas:** radio signals drop behind buildings and ridges, so test the channel from both ends.
- **Language barriers:** use simple pictures or symbols beside the written words.

## Common mistakes

- **Writing long notes.** Short notes are easier to read and less likely to be lost.
- **Leaving notes unsealed.** Rain and wind destroy them.
- **Assuming delivery.** Ask for confirmation, or check the reply.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Likely cause | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Radio gives only static | Out of range or wrong channel | Move to higher ground and check the channel |
| Note missing from door | Wind or passer-by | Re-pin it and leave a copy at the meeting point |
| Runner returns late | Route blocked | Check the alternative route and the next meeting point |
| Reply not received | Recipient did not reply | Send a second note by a different method |

## Checklist

- Two methods chosen and practised this year.
- Walkie-talkies charged and set to the family channel.
- Sealed bags, pins and tape kept together.
- Trusted runners named on the family plan.
- Copies of sent messages kept in the crisis log.

## What to do when it fails

If a message does not arrive, send a second copy by a different method, and mark the first as unconfirmed. If a runner does not return, go to the meeting point and check the board there.

**Warning:** do not rely on a single method. Use two where you can, and check that the message arrived.
