# Well Water Safety

A well is only as safe as its surroundings and its cover. Most well problems come from surface water, animals and neglect. Check the well regularly, not only when something goes wrong, because contamination often appears gradually.

![Well head section: ground-line, raised cover above ground, slope descending away from casing, arrow-both-ways to a pit; labels: Cover, Casing, Distance, Waste pit, Slope away; distance and ground slope matter as much as the cover. NEEDS: well-casing, waste-pit](assets/well-water-safety-1.svg)

## Protect the well

- Keep the top of the well sealed and above ground level.
- Keep livestock, toilets and waste away from the well.
- Do not let surface water pool around the well head.
- Check the cover after storms, because wind and debris can loosen it.

> @image Photo of a well head with a raised concrete apron and a sealed cover

## Signs of a problem

- A change in taste, colour or smell.
- Cloudiness that appears after rain.
- A metallic taste or rust-coloured water, which can point to a fault in the well casing.
- Sudden illness in people who drink from the well.

## Make it safer

1. Boil the water before drinking it, or dose it with bleach.
2. Filter it first if it is cloudy. See Filtering Water at Home.
3. Test it when you can, especially after floods or a long dry spell.

## Common mistakes

- Leaving the cover off, so leaves, insects and rain enter.
- Storing fuel or pesticides near the well head.
- Assuming a well that worked last year is safe after a flood.

## Disinfect a well after contamination

Dose the well with bleach in proportion to its volume, let it stand for several hours, then pump it until the chlorine smell is faint. Repeat if the water still tastes or smells wrong. Wear gloves and eye protection when handling bleach, and keep children and animals away from the well during treatment.

> @image Photo of a bleach dosing bucket beside a well head

## When it fails

If the water stays cloudy, smells or causes illness, stop drinking it and use stored or treated water from another source. Disinfect the well, then test it before resuming use.

## Why it matters

A private well supplies a household that has no mains connection, and its water can change without any visible sign. Keeping the well in good order costs far less than treating a contaminated supply for weeks, and it protects the people who depend on it.

## Equipment and materials

- Bleach of the standard strength, with a dropper or measuring cup.
- A bucket for mixing the dose, and gloves for handling it.
- A test kit for bacteria, if one is available.
- A clean container for collecting samples.

> @image Test kit with sample bottle and gloves laid out beside a well head

## Adjusting for conditions

- **After heavy rain:** surface water can wash into the well. Treat the water until a test confirms it is clear and safe.
- **In a drought:** the water level drops and minerals concentrate. Check the taste, and pump the well gently to avoid drawing silt.
- **In winter:** ice can build up around the cover. Clear it carefully so the cover is not damaged.

## Troubleshooting

- **Water clears but a chemical taste remains:** pump the well again until the smell fades, then test it.
- **Cover has cracked:** repair or replace it before using the well, because a cracked cover lets in surface water.
- **Pump runs but water is dry:** the level may be low. Stop pumping and wait for the level to recover.

## Recording well checks

Keep a short record of each inspection: the date, the state of the cover, any change in taste or colour, and the result of any test. Patterns in the record show problems early, such as a slow rise in cloudiness after each storm. Store the record with the well's other papers.

## Practice before you need it

Once a year, check the cover, measure the distance from the well to waste or animals, and note where surface water runs when it rains. Keep a bucket and the bleach in a labelled place beside the well, so that treatment can start at once.

## Who should do the work

Treating or testing a well should be done by one person who has been shown the steps and who understands the bleach dose. Two people work better, one to pour and one to read the dose, so that errors are caught before they reach the water.

## Keeping the surroundings clear

Mow grass around the well head so that animals do not graze close to it, and remove any rubbish or debris that could fall into the opening. Keep the ground sloped away from the cover, so that rainwater runs off rather than pooling against the casing.

**Warning:** do not drink from a well that has been flooded until it has been disinfected and tested.
