# Disinfecting Water with Bleach

Household bleach is one of the most reliable ways to disinfect drinking water when boiling is not possible. Chlorine kills most bacteria and viruses that cause waterborne illness, and it stays active in the water after treatment. It is cheap, widely available and stores well if kept sealed. It works only with the right product, the right dose and enough time, and it has clear limits that you must respect.

## Choose the right bleach

- Use plain, unscented household bleach that contains **5 to 6 per cent sodium hypochlorite**. This is the strength on most supermarket labels. Check the label before you buy.
- Do not use bleach with added fragrance, colour, detergent, thickeners or "extra" cleaning agents. These can be harmful to drink.
- Check the date. Bleach loses strength over time, especially in heat and light. If it has been stored for more than a year, the dose may be too weak.
- Keep it in its original bottle, sealed, away from sunlight and out of reach of children.

## Dose

Measure with a clean dropper, a medicine spoon or a measuring cup. Estimating by eye leads to errors.

| Water condition | Bleach per litre |
|---|---|
| Clear water, room temperature | 2 drops |
| Cloudy or cold water | 4 drops |
| Very cloudy water | Filter it first, then use the cloudy dose |

For larger volumes, use about 1/4 teaspoon of bleach per 10 litres of clear water. The 2-drops-per-litre figure is an approximate guide. Check the strength of your bleach and adjust if it is weaker or stronger than 5 to 6 per cent.

## Steps

1. **Filter cloudy water** through a clean cloth or a filter first. Bleach cannot work through particles. See Filtering Water at Home.
2. **Add the measured bleach** to the water and stir well.
3. **Cover the container** and wait at least **30 minutes**. In cold water, wait longer, up to an hour.
4. **Check the smell.** The water should smell faintly of chlorine. If it smells of nothing, the dose was too low or the bleach too weak. Add the same dose again and wait another 15 minutes.
5. **Drink or store.** If the smell is very strong, let the water stand uncovered for a while before drinking, which lets the chlorine dissipate.

## Taste and smell

A faint chlorine smell means the water is protected. A strong chlorine smell means too much bleach was used. Some people taste chlorine more than others, and children may refuse water with a smell. Leaving the water uncovered for an hour, or pouring it between two containers, reduces the taste.

## Limits of chlorine

- **Parasites:** some parasites, such as *Cryptosporidium*, are resistant to chlorine at normal doses. If water may be contaminated with parasites, filter it well, or boil it.
- **Chemicals and metals:** bleach does not remove heavy metals, salt, pesticides, fuel or industrial pollutants.
- **Turbid water:** chlorine cannot disinfect water that is full of particles. Clear it first.
- **Cold water:** chlorine works more slowly at low temperatures. Extend the contact time.

## Storage and handling

- Use bleach-treated water within a few days, or store it in a sealed, clean container.
- Keep bleach separate from fuel, acids and other chemicals.
- Wash your hands after handling bleach and do not touch your eyes.

## Common mistakes

- Using scented or "thick" bleach, which may contain additives.
- Using the same dose for cloudy water as for clear water.
- Drinking after only a few minutes of contact time.
- Storing diluted bleach for weeks, after it has weakened.

## Checklist

- Bleach is 5 to 6 per cent, unscented and in date.
- Water is clear, or filtered first.
- Correct dose measured with a dropper or spoon.
- Contact time of at least 30 minutes, longer if cold.
- Chlorine smell present after treatment.

## Why it matters

Chlorine is a standard tool in water systems because it keeps working after the first dose, protecting water as it stands. A bottle of plain bleach in a cupboard gives a household a treatment method that needs no fuel and no electricity, provided the bottle is still strong and the dose is measured.

## Troubleshooting

- **No chlorine smell after 30 minutes:** the dose was too low or the bleach is weak. Add the same dose again and wait 15 minutes, as the steps describe.
- **Smell is very strong:** too much bleach was used. Leave the container uncovered for an hour before drinking.
- **Water turns cloudy after dosing:** the water contains particles or organic matter. Discard it and filter a fresh batch before treating.

## Practice before you need it

Measure out a litre of clean tap water with your bleach and dropper, and note the smell after thirty minutes. Repeat the test once a year with the bottle you actually keep, because the strength you test is the strength you will rely on.

**Warning:** never mix bleach with vinegar, ammonia, acids or other cleaning products. The fumes can cause serious lung damage.
