# Water with Broken Pipes

A burst or cracked pipe can drain a supply and let dirt enter the system. Act quickly to stop the loss and protect what remains. Every minute the pipe stays open, more debris and contamination can enter.

## First steps

1. Turn off the main water valve.
2. Open a tap at the lowest point of the house to drain pressure.
3. Store clean water in containers before the system is opened.

> @image Photo of a main shut-off valve beside a meter with a red handle

## Protect the water in the system

- Do not connect hoses or tools to the pipes without a backflow device.
- Keep open ends covered.
- Do not drink from taps while the pipes are being repaired.

## Repair simply

- Use a pipe repair clamp or a rubber patch with a hose clamp for small cracks.
- Clean the pipe ends before fitting any clamp, so grit does not get trapped under it.
- For a larger break, shut off the affected section and plan a proper repair when possible.

## Common mistakes

- Reopening the main valve before the break is sealed.
- Using unclean hoses or tools that carry soil into the pipes.
- Drinking from a tap because the water flows, when the pipes are still open.
- Assuming a repaired pipe is sealed without a pressure check.

## Checklist

- [ ] Main valve closed
- [ ] Lowest tap opened to relieve pressure
- [ ] Clean water stored before repair
- [ ] Open ends covered during repair

## After repair

1. Flush the system by running taps until the water runs clear.
2. Disinfect the tank or pipes with bleach if they were open to dirt.
3. Test or boil the water before drinking it.

## When it fails

If the flushed water still looks dirty or smells wrong, shut the supply again and repeat the flush. If the break cannot be sealed, use stored water and call a plumber as soon as one is available.

## Why it matters

A burst pipe can drain a tank in hours and fill the system with soil and grit. The water that remains in the pipes after a break is not safe until the system has been flushed. Quick action limits both the loss and the contamination.

## Equipment and materials

- A pipe repair clamp of the correct size, or a rubber patch and two hose clamps.
- A wrench or pipe key to close the main valve.
- Clean containers for stored water.
- Gloves and a clean cloth for cleaning the pipe ends.

> @image Repair clamp and rubber patch laid beside a length of copper pipe with a cracked section

## Adjusting for conditions

- **Frozen pipes:** do not force a valve or heat a pipe with an open flame. Thaw it slowly with warm cloths, and check for splits when it thaws.
- **Older homes:** metal pipes may be corroded and brittle, so expect more than one leak and plan for a larger repair.
- **Shared systems:** if neighbours share the supply, tell them before you close the main valve.

## Troubleshooting

- **Water keeps leaking after the patch:** the pipe end may not be clean, or the clamp is too loose. Remove the patch, clean the surface and fit it again.
- **Air in the lines makes taps spit:** open each tap in turn until the flow runs steady, then flush again.
- **Pressure drops after repair:** check for other leaks along the same run before you assume the repair failed.

## Preventing future breaks

Lag exposed pipes before cold weather, and keep the loft or crawl space ventilated so that pipes do not freeze. Know where the pipes run, so that a digging job does not strike them. Replace any section that has already cracked, rather than relying on a patch that will fail again.

## Practice before you need it

Find the main shut-off valve, the drain tap and the meter now, not during a leak. Turn the main valve off and on once a year to make sure it still moves. Keep the clamp, patch, wrench and clean containers together in one labelled place.

## Calling a professional

Call a plumber as soon as a break is reported, and tell them where the main valve is and whether you have already closed it. Describe the leak, the age of the pipes and any water quality concerns, so that the visit is prepared. Keep the receipt and any notes, because the repair may need to be reported to an insurer.

## Keeping a repair log

Record each leak, the date it was found, the section of pipe affected and the repair used. A log shows repeated failures in one run of pipe, which points to a deeper problem that a patch will not solve.

**Warning:** do not drink water from taps after a flood or a pipe burst until it has been flushed and treated.
