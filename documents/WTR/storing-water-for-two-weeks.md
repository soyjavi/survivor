# Storing Water for Two Weeks

Store water before a crisis starts. Once taps stop, filling containers safely becomes hard, and contaminated containers can spoil a good supply. A two-week store is a practical target for most households.

![Shelf plan: ground-line shelf with jerrycan row, tin tray beneath each, lid labels facing out, and a separate fuel shelf marked do-not between; labels: Water, Tray, Cool dark corner, Fuel; water and fuel never share a shelf. NEEDS: shelf](assets/storing-water-for-two-weeks-1.svg)

## Choose containers

- Use food-grade plastic containers with tight lids.
- Never use containers that held chemicals, oil or solvents.
- Avoid glass that can break and thin bags that can leak.

> @image Photo of stacked food-grade water containers with labelled lids on a shelf

## Clean the container

1. Wash it with hot water and soap.
2. Rinse it well.
3. Fill it with water that is already safe, or add a little bleach first.
4. Close the lid tightly and write the date on the container.

## Where to keep it

- Keep containers cool and dark, away from direct sunlight.
- Keep them away from fuel, pesticides and cleaning products.
- Use shelves where possible, so the floor stays dry and clear.

![Rotation calendar: table-grid with timeline-marker for each container, three markers per row; labels: Fill date, Check date, Replace date 6 months on; every container runs the same cycle, so the oldest is always visible.](assets/storing-water-for-two-weeks-2.svg)

## Rotate the supply

- Replace tap water you stored yourself about every six months.
- Use the oldest container first, then refill it.
- Check the seals every few weeks.

## Common mistakes

- Storing water in a container that was not cleaned after its previous use.
- Keeping containers in a hot garage or next to a heater.
- Forgetting to date containers, so nobody knows which water is oldest.
- Filling containers from an unknown source and assuming it is safe because it looks clear.

## Checklist

- [ ] Containers food-grade, sealed and labelled
- [ ] Each container dated when filled
- [ ] Store cool, dark and off the floor
- [ ] Rotation schedule written down

## How much to store

Store at least **3 litres per person per day** for fourteen days. Add extra for cooking, hygiene and pets. The guide on daily water needs shows how to calculate your total.

## When it fails

If a container smells, turns cloudy or develops a film, empty it and clean it before refilling. Do not drink from a container that has been opened and left in the sun. Keep a spare container ready, so that a failed batch can be replaced without delay.

## Why it matters

A stored supply turns a crisis into an inconvenience. Water stored in advance does not depend on whether pumps work, taps flow or roads are open. A two-week store covers most short disruptions and gives time to arrange longer-term supplies.

## Labelling system

- Write the fill date, the source and the treatment used on each container.
- Use a second label for the check date, so you know when to inspect it.
- Keep a simple list on the wall of every container, its location and its next check.

> @image Container lid with fill date, source and treatment written on a tape label

## Adjusting for conditions

- **Hot climates:** store containers in a cool, shaded room and check them more often, because heat speeds the growth of microbes.
- **Cold climates:** water can freeze and crack containers. Leave headspace for expansion, and move containers indoors before a hard frost.
- **Small homes:** use flat containers that fit under beds or along walls, or spread the supply across several locations.

## Refill and treat

When a container is emptied, clean it before refilling, even if it looks clean. Treat the new water with boiling or bleach before it goes in, rather than trusting the source. Record the refill on the list, so the rotation stays accurate.

## Troubleshooting

- **Container lid bulges:** do not open it near your face. Move it outdoors and dispose of the contents.
- **Taste changes after several weeks:** pour the water out and refill from a fresh, treated source. Do not blend old and new water.

## Practice before you need it

Once a season, carry out a full rotation: empty one container, clean it, refill it and log the change. This practice shows how long the job takes and reveals any container that is hard to move or open.

## Protecting containers in transit

Move containers with the lids on and the caps tight. Use a crate or a box to carry several at once, so that they do not knock together. Never roll a full container across rough ground, because a split seam can open and the water is lost before it reaches the store.

## Preventing spills and leaks

Place each container on a tray or a sheet of plastic, so that any leak is caught and does not stain or weaken the floor. Check the tap and the seal every few weeks, and replace any container whose seal has worn.

**Warning:** do not store water in containers that held chemicals or fuel, even after washing.
