# Solar Disinfection (SODIS)

Solar disinfection uses sunlight and clear plastic bottles to kill many germs in water. It needs no fuel and suits small amounts of water that already look clean. It is slow and weather-dependent, so plan for it before you need water in a hurry.

![Sun timing: bottle centre, timeline-axis below with two timeline-marker points; labels: Strong sun 6 hours, Overcast 2 days; sky cover, not the clock, sets the wait, so a cloudy spell stretches treatment by days. NEEDS: sun](assets/solar-disinfection-sodis-1.svg)

## What you need

- Clear, colourless PET bottles, up to 2 litres. Remove the labels.
- Water that is clear, not cloudy.
- Strong, direct sunlight.

> @image Photo of several clear PET bottles lined up on a sunny rooftop

## Steps

1. **Filter cloudy water** through a clean cloth until it is clear. Cloudy water blocks the light.
2. **Fill the bottle** to the top and close the cap tightly.
3. **Shake the bottle** for about 20 seconds to add air.
4. **Place it in full sun** on a flat surface. A metal roof works well if it is safe to reach.
5. **Leave it for six hours** in strong sun. On overcast days, leave it for two days.
6. **Drink from the bottle,** or pour the water into a clean cup.

![Roof setup: bottle on slope, arrow-straight from sun to bottle, dark surface beneath marked with tip; labels: Sun angle, Bottle, Dark surface; the bottle faces full sun at a steep angle and the dark base adds heat in winter. NEEDS: sun, roof](assets/solar-disinfection-sodis-2.svg)

## Common mistakes

- Using cloudy or coloured water, which the sunlight cannot pass through.
- Placing bottles in partial shade, or leaving them under a window with glass.
- Reusing bottles that have been scratched, cracked or used for other liquids.

## Checklist

- [ ] Bottles clear, labelled removed and clean
- [ ] Water filtered until clear
- [ ] Bottles full and tightly capped
- [ ] Six hours of strong sun, or two days if overcast

## Limits

- It works only in direct sunlight. Shade and overcast days need much longer.
- It does not remove chemicals or salt.
- It is slow. Use it when fuel is short, not when you need water quickly.
- Cloud cover, rain and winter sun all reduce the effect, so check the weather before relying on it.

## When it fails

If the water is still cloudy after filtering, do not use the method. If the bottles were in shade or the day was cloudy, extend the exposure time. If in doubt, boil the water instead.

## Why it matters

Solar disinfection suits a household that needs a small, steady amount of drinking water and has no fuel. A row of bottles on a sunny roof can produce several litres a day, and it needs no special skill beyond keeping the bottles clean and in the sun.

## Choosing the bottles

- Use bottles marked PET, or with the recycling number 1 for PET.
- Look for bottles that are clear all the way through, not tinted or frosted.
- Choose smaller bottles, up to 1 litre, when the sun is weak, because they warm faster.

> @image Row of clear one-litre bottles and a two-litre bottle compared side by side on a dark surface

## Adjusting for conditions

- **Summer:** six hours of direct sun is usually enough, but check that the bottles are not overheating.
- **Winter or high latitude:** sunlight is weaker. Use two days of exposure and place the bottles on a dark surface to absorb more heat.
- **Dusty air:** clean the bottle surfaces before filling, because dust blocks light.

## Planning a batch

Fill enough bottles for the next day in the morning, so that the treated water is ready in the evening. Keep a fixed place on the roof for the batch, and move bottles only once the treatment is finished. Keep untreated bottles separate, with a marker on each cap, so that treated and untreated water never mix.

## Troubleshooting

- **Bottles cloudy after exposure:** the water was not clear enough to start with. Discard it and filter a fresh batch.
- **Water tastes of plastic:** this is normal and harmless, but store the bottle in the shade afterwards.

## Practice before you need it

Run a test batch on a sunny day and compare it with untreated water. Keep a log of the start time and the weather. Repeating this a few times shows how long your local sun needs, so the schedule is reliable when you depend on it.

## Bottle care

Store the bottles out of the sun when they are not in use, because ultraviolet light gradually weakens the plastic. Wash them with clean water and a soft brush, and rinse them well, since residue reduces the amount of light that reaches the water. Replace any bottle that has become scratched or yellowed.

## Safety on the roof

Use a stable, flat surface that is safe to reach, and never climb onto a roof in wet or windy weather. Keep bottles away from the edge, where a gust can knock them down. Lift bottles with both hands, because a full bottle is heavy and the caps can loosen when knocked.

**Warning:** do not use bottles that once held chemicals, oil or solvents.
