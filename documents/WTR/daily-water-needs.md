# Daily Water Needs

Water is the most urgent need in any crisis. People can survive a few weeks without food, but only a few days without water. Work out how much you need before you plan how to get it, because the number decides everything else: how many containers you need, how often you must collect, and how much fuel you will spend boiling it.

## Minimum amounts

Plan for at least **3 litres per person per day**. This covers drinking and basic cooking in a temperate climate. It is the figure most emergency guidance uses as the floor, not the target.

The 3 litres breaks down roughly like this:

- **Drinking:** 1.5 to 2 litres. The body loses this much through breath, sweat, urine and stool even at rest.
- **Cooking:** about 0.5 to 1 litre per meal that uses water, such as rice, pasta, porridge or soup.
- **Basic hygiene:** a few litres for washing hands, face and teeth, and cleaning utensils.

Washing the body, doing laundry and cleaning the house need much more. Plan for them only once the drinking and cooking needs are covered. See Rationing Water for Hygiene.

## Adjust for conditions

The minimum rises in several situations:

- **Hot weather:** increase drinking water by about half. Sweat removes water and salt quickly, and heat exhaustion can start within hours.
- **Illness:** diarrhoea and vomiting can drain several litres a day. See Diarrhoea and Dehydration.
- **Children, pregnant or breastfeeding people:** these people need more fluid, and children cannot always say when they are thirsty.
- **Physical work:** digging, carrying loads or walking long distances can double the need.
- **Altitude and dry air:** the body loses more water through breathing at altitude.

## Plan for a household

1. **Count the people** who will drink from the supply, including children and visitors.
2. **Multiply by 3 litres** for each day.
3. **Multiply by the number of days.** Fourteen days is a good starting point. Some authorities recommend more.
4. **Add about 20 per cent** for heat, illness, spillage and unexpected needs.

**Example:** a family of four for fourteen days needs about 168 litres for drinking and cooking, plus the margin. That is about 200 litres in total, which is roughly eight 25-litre containers, or about forty 5-litre bottles.

## Work out the collection plan

Storage does not last for ever, and some sources will need collecting daily. Decide:

- How far the nearest safe source is, and how long the round trip takes.
- How many containers one person can carry safely, usually no more than about 10 to 15 litres.
- How many trips per day are realistic without exhausting the people doing them.

## Do not cut drinking water

Going below what the body needs causes headaches, weakness and confusion within hours. If the supply runs short, cut washing and cleaning first, and then cooking where possible. Keep drinking water last. Watch for the signs of dehydration: dark urine, dry mouth, dizziness and reduced urine output.

## Check the figures

Review the numbers every few weeks. Household members change, seasons change, and stored water loses value over time. See Storing Water for Two Weeks.

## Why the numbers matter

A household that guesses its water needs usually guesses low. Hot days, a child with diarrhoea or a family that carries water for neighbours can each double the figure within a day. Writing the numbers down turns a vague worry into a plan that can be checked.

## Measuring the supply

- Use a clearly marked container, such as a 5-litre bottle with lines drawn on it, to measure each portion.
- Keep a simple log beside the storage: date, litres used, litres left.
- Count the days of supply left by dividing the remaining litres by the daily total.

## Troubleshooting

- **The supply runs out faster than planned:** check for leaks, spillage at the tap, or extra visitors. Recalculate the days left.
- **A member drinks much less than the minimum:** encourage small, regular sips and watch for dark urine or dizziness.

## Practice before you need it

Run the plan for a single day at home. Measure every litre, log each use and note where the time goes. This shows whether the collection trips and the containers match the numbers.
