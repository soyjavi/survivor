# Rationing Water for Hygiene

When water runs low, drinking comes first, then cooking, then hygiene. Hygiene still matters, because unwashed hands and wounds spread disease quickly. The aim is to protect health with the smallest amount of water that works.

NEEDS: pyramid](assets/rationing-water-for-hygiene-1.svg)

## Priority order

1. **Drinking:** at least 2 litres per person per day.
2. **Cooking and food hygiene:** enough to cook and wash food.
3. **Hand washing:** a small amount after toilets and before meals.
4. **Wound care:** clean water for cuts and burns.
5. **Body washing and laundry:** only when the supply allows.

## Plan the day

1. Work out the total litres available and divide by the number of days you expect the shortage to last.
2. Set a daily allowance for each person, and write it down where everyone can see it.
3. Keep a small reserve for emergencies, such as an injury or a sick child.

## Low-water methods

- Wash hands with a small amount of water and a cloth, or with alcohol gel.
- Wipe the body with a damp cloth instead of bathing.
- Wash dishes in a bowl, then rinse with a little boiled water.

![Two bowls: bowl left, bowl right, water-drop above the rinse bowl, arrow-straight left to right; labels: Soapy wash, Rinse with boiled water, Dishes; two bowls and a small rinse keep water use low.](assets/rationing-water-for-hygiene-2.svg)

- Use a bucket for washing clothes, and reuse the rinse water for floors.

## Common mistakes

- Taking water from the drinking supply for washing when the washing supply is empty.
- Washing hands with dirty water, which spreads germs instead of removing them.
- Ignoring small cuts, which become infected in dirty conditions.

## Checklist

- [ ] Daily allowance calculated and written down
- [ ] Drinking water kept separate from washing water
- [ ] Hands washed before food and after toilets
- [ ] Wounds cleaned with clean water

## Watch the signs

- Dry or cracked skin, dark urine and headaches show that intake is too low.
- Skin infections and diarrhoea show that hygiene has fallen too far.

## When it fails

If illness appears in the household, move the sick person to the cleanest water you have, and separate their washing from everyone else's. Raise the drinking allowance first, then review the hygiene plan.

## Why it matters

Hygiene keeps illness away. A household that cannot wash hands after toilets, or cannot clean a cut, will see diarrhoea and skin infection spread faster than any shortage of drinking water. Rationing is about spending the water where it prevents the most harm.

## Equipment and materials

- Two clearly labelled buckets, one for drinking and one for washing.
- A bowl and a small cup for washing dishes and hands.
- Alcohol-based hand gel as a backup where water is very limited.
- Clean cloths for washing bodies and wounds.
- A lidded container for used water, so it can be reused for floors or flushing.

## Adjusting for conditions

- **Hot weather:** increase the drinking allowance first. Do not reduce it to make room for washing.
- **Children and older people:** they often need help with hand washing, so allocate a little extra for them.
- **Illness in the household:** keep the sick person's washing separate and use the cleanest water for their drinking.

## Stretching the water

Wash the least dirty items first, so the same water serves more purposes. Use the rinse water from hand washing to flush the toilet or clean the floor. Cover washing bowls between uses so that dust does not contaminate the water while it waits.

## Practice before you need it

Run a two-day rationing trial at home, using the allowance and the two-bucket method. Note where the water goes and where people struggle. Adjust the plan before the real shortage, not during it.

## Sharing the supply

- Let everyone know the allowance and the reason for it.
- Give each person their own labelled cup, so that cups are not shared.

## Keeping order in the household

Name one person to hold the water store and issue each day's allowance. Rotate the job, so that everyone understands the limits. Keep the cups, buckets and cloths in fixed places, so that nobody has to search for them while tired or in the dark.

## Who does the work

Children can carry small containers, adults should handle the heavy loads, and anyone with an injury or illness should be excused from carrying water. Spread the trips across the day, rather than sending one person on every journey, so that the work does not wear anyone out.

**Warning:** cutting drinking water to save water for washing is a mistake. Protect drinking water first.
