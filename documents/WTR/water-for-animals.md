# Water for Animals

Animals need water too, and they drink a lot. Plan their supply as part of the household plan, because a thirsty herd or pet can use up the water your family needs. Animals also carry germs, so keep their water and your water apart.

## Daily amounts (rough guide)

- **Chickens:** about 0.3 litres per bird per day, more in heat.
- **Goats and sheep:** about 2 to 4 litres per animal per day.
- **Dogs and cats:** about 50 ml per kilo of body weight per day.

Amounts rise in hot weather, when animals are pregnant or nursing, or when they eat dry food.

> @image Photo of goats drinking from a low trough filled with clean water

## Plan the supply

1. Count the animals and multiply by the daily amount for each type.
2. Add a margin for heat and for animals that are sick or nursing.
3. Store the total in containers marked for animals only.

## Separate supplies

- Keep animal water in a separate container, labelled, so it is not confused with drinking water.
- Use water from a clean source. Treat it if it is from a river or pond.
- Place containers where animals cannot step in them or contaminate them.

## Common mistakes

- Filling animal containers from the family drinking tank.
- Placing troughs where animals foul the water with dung or bedding.
- Forgetting that the animals drink more when they are hot or stressed.

## Protect people

- Wash your hands after handling animals or their water.
- Keep animals away from the household drinking supply and cooking area.

## When it fails

If an animal refuses water or shows signs of illness, separate it and seek veterinary advice. If the supply runs short, prioritise young, sick and nursing animals, and move the rest to a water source that is safe.

## Why it matters

Animals that go without water fall ill quickly, and sick animals produce less milk, eggs or work when you need them most. Planning their supply early protects the animals and saves the household time and fuel, because every extra trip to a source is a trip your family must make.

## Adjusting for conditions

- **Hot weather:** increase the totals by at least a quarter. Animals drink more, and heat stress spreads quickly through a herd.
- **Dry food:** animals that eat hay or dry pellets drink much more than those grazing on fresh grass.
- **Pregnancy and nursing:** these animals need extra water, so give them priority access to the cleanest supply.

## Feed and water together

Give dry feed and water at the same time, so animals drink after eating rather than before. Keep feed off the ground, where it collects dung and water. A raised feeder beside the trough reduces spillage and keeps both clean for longer.

## Troubleshooting

- **Animals drink from a stagnant container:** move the trough to a clean spot and refill it with fresh water daily.
- **Water in troughs turns green or smells:** empty, scrub and refill. Do not add chemicals to the trough without veterinary advice.
- **Animals refuse water they used to drink:** taste changes often cause this. Offer a small amount of plain boiled water and watch their behaviour.

## Practice before you need it

Set up the animal water station at home before a crisis. Check that the trough is the right height, that containers fill and empty easily, and that animals can reach the water without climbing. Practising the routine helps animals settle into it before their supply depends on it.

## Poultry and small livestock

Chickens and ducks foul water quickly, so use a nipple drinker or a shallow trough raised off the ground. Clean the trough every day in hot weather. Ducks need access to water deep enough to dip their heads, but a deep pool becomes a breeding ground for germs and should be avoided.

## Pets in a crisis

Keep dogs and cats on a fixed routine so that their drinking water is predictable. Use a bowl that is easy to clean and refill, and keep it away from the place where they eat. Pets that stop drinking or vomit repeatedly need prompt veterinary attention, even when the rest of the household is coping well.

**Warning:** do not give animals water from flood areas or standing pools that people also use for washing.
