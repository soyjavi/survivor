# Rain Water Catchment

Rain is one of the most reliable sources when other supplies stop. Collect it cleanly, store it well and treat it before drinking. A roof with a clean surface and a short gutter run makes the difference between useful water and a bucket of debris.

## Set up the catch

1. Use a clean roof without tar, moss or bird droppings.
2. Direct the gutter into a covered container.
3. Discard the first flush of water after a dry spell. It carries dust and debris.
4. Collect the rest into clean containers with lids.

![Diverter detail: arrow-straight down into a standpipe, water-drop filling it, arrow-straight sideways from a clear outlet to barrel; labels: First flush, Standpipe, Slow drain, Outlet, Tank; dirty water stays in the pipe while clean water passes on. ## Keep it clean

- Screen the gutter with mesh to stop leaves and insects.
- Keep containers in shade to limit algae.
- Clean the catch and containers twice a year.

> @image Photo of a gutter fitted with a mesh screen above a covered barrel

## Treat before drinking

Rainwater is usually soft and low in microbes, but it can carry bird and roof contamination. Boil it for one minute, or filter it and dose it with bleach.

## Common mistakes

- Collecting from a roof that has been painted, sealed or treated with chemicals.
- Leaving containers open, so insects and leaves enter.
- Skipping the first flush after a dry spell, when the roof is most dirty.
- Storing treated water in a container that was not cleaned.

## Use the water

- Drink and cook with treated rainwater.
- Use untreated rainwater for washing and cleaning.
- Label each container with its fill date, so you know when to treat or replace it.
- Do not use water from a roof treated with pesticides, or fed by lead flashing.

## When it fails

If the water smells, turns green or has a film, empty the container and clean it before refilling. If you find insects or larvae, filter the water through cloth before treating it.

## Why it matters

Rain is free and arrives without a pump. A household that can catch even a modest downpour adds several days of supply to stored water. The catch depends on roof area, so a small roof collects less than people expect.

## Equipment and materials

- Clean gutters, downpipes and a mesh screen.
- A first-flush diverter, or a length of pipe with a cap.
- Covered containers, ideally opaque to keep out light.
- A tap or hose for drawing water off without disturbing sediment.

## Adjusting for conditions

- **Heavy storms:** the first flush carries more debris. Discard more of it, and check the screen afterwards.
- **Dry spells:** the roof is dusty, so clean it before the first rain and discard a larger first flush.
- **Cold climates:** ice in gutters can block flow. Clear it with warm water rather than sharp tools that damage the gutter.

## Reading the roof

Pick the roof with the cleanest surface and the shortest run to the tank. Avoid roofs with overhanging trees, since leaves and seeds fall directly into the gutter. Check the gutter joints for leaks, because a dripping joint wastes water before it reaches the container.

## Practice before you need it

Measure your roof area in square metres. Every millimetre of rain falling on one square metre gives about one litre, so the roof area multiplied by the rainfall gives a rough yield. Test the estimate with a bucket after the next rain.

## Troubleshooting

- **Water tastes of metal:** the gutter or flashing may contain lead or zinc. Do not drink it; use it for cleaning only.
- **Algae in the tank:** shade the container and scrub the inside with clean water, then refill.

## Sizing the tank

Match the container size to the roof and the rainfall. A large tank on a small roof stays half empty, while a small tank on a large roof overflows in the first storm. Plan a second, smaller container for the surplus, and route the overflow away from the foundations so that it does not soak the ground beside the house.

## Seasonal maintenance

- Clear leaves from the gutters before the autumn rains.
- Check the mesh screen after strong winds, since debris can tear it.
- Inspect the tank lid for cracks, and reseal any gap that lets light in.
- Scrub the inside of the containers before the wet season begins.

**Warning:** stored rainwater can go bad in warm weather. Use it within a few weeks or treat it again before drinking.
