# Distillation at Home

Distillation heats water until it turns to vapour, then cools the vapour back into liquid. Salt, most metals and most microbes stay behind. It is the only practical method for sea water, which is why it deserves careful setup even though it is slow.

## Simple still

1. A large pot with a lid.
2. A heat-proof bowl that fits inside the pot, placed on a rack or an upturned cup.
3. Water to fill the pot around the bowl, not the bowl itself.
4. A clean cloth or plate over the pot, with a weight in the middle so it sits lower, pointing down.

Heat the pot. Water vapour rises, condenses under the lid and drips into the bowl. Replace the cold water on the lid if it warms up.

> @image Photo of a finished home still with a bowl inside a pot and an inverted lid

## Steps

1. Fill the pot with the water you want to distil, leaving space for the bowl.
2. Heat gently until steam forms. Do not boil hard, or steam can escape past the lid.
3. Collect the drips in the bowl and keep the lid cool.
4. Discard the first small amount, then collect the rest in a clean container.

## Common mistakes

- Filling the bowl with the water to be distilled. The bowl must collect only the drips.
- Letting the lid warm up, so vapour no longer condenses.
- Boiling too hard, which pushes steam out at the rim.
- Storing the output in a container that was not cleaned first.

## Checklist

- [ ] Bowl sits above the water line and does not float
- [ ] Lid is inverted, with a weight in the centre
- [ ] Cold water ready to replace on the lid
- [ ] Output collected in a clean, covered container

## What it removes

- Salt and most minerals.
- Most metals and many chemicals.
- Most bacteria, parasites and viruses.

## Limits

- Distillation uses a lot of fuel.
- The output tastes flat. Pour it back and forth between containers to add air.
- Keep the distilled water clean and do not mix it with other water.

## When it fails

If the drips are slow or stop, check the seal at the rim and the temperature of the lid. If the output tastes or smells wrong, discard it and clean the whole still before running it again.

## Why it matters

Sea water and some brackish sources are the only water many coastal households can reach in a crisis. Distillation is slow and fuel-hungry, but it is the one method in this guide that removes salt. Knowing how to build a still before the situation arises saves hours of trial and error.

## Adjusting for conditions

- **Hot weather:** the lid warms faster, so replace cold water more often, or place the lid in a shaded spot with a fresh cloth over it.
- **Windy conditions:** wind cools the lid unevenly. Shelter the still behind a wall or a bank of soil.
- **Small yields:** a single still produces only a few cups an hour. For drinking needs, run several stills at once or plan for long sessions.
- **Cold weather:** the still runs more slowly, so start early and keep the fire low to conserve fuel.

## Fuel and time

A batch of distilled water can take several hours from start to finish, and the fuel use adds up quickly. Plan for the still to run in the morning or evening, when the air is cooler and the lid stays cold longer. Keep a single fire going rather than restarting it, because each restart wastes the heat already stored in the pot.

## Troubleshooting

- **Output is salty:** the bowl is probably touching the sides of the pot, letting brine drip in. Reposition the bowl and discard the first batch.
- **Output smells of the pot:** clean the pot and lid with hot water before running a new batch.
- **No drips after a long run:** check that the lid is not sealed flat against the pot rim, which traps steam. Lift the lid slightly with a stick.

## Practice before you need it

Build the still at home using dirty tap water with a little salt, run it for an hour, and taste the output. This confirms the setup works and shows how much time one batch takes, so you can plan how much water to aim for.

**Warning:** do not distil water from a fuel or chemical container. Some substances vaporise dangerously.
