# Boiling Water

Boiling is the most reliable way to make water safe when you have a fire or a stove. Heat kills the bacteria, viruses and parasites that cause most waterborne illness, including diarrhoea, cholera and hepatitis A. It does nothing to remove salt, chemicals or heavy metals, so the water must be as clear as you can make it before you start.

## Why boiling works

Most disease-causing microbes die at temperatures well below boiling point, but a rolling boil gives a safe margin for every organism that matters in a crisis. You do not need to cook the water. You need it to reach a full boil and stay there for long enough.

## Steps

1. **Start with clear water.** If the water is cloudy, let it settle for an hour, then pour off the clear layer, or pass it through a clean cloth. See Filtering Water at Home.
2. **Fill a clean pot** and place it on the heat with the lid on.
3. **Bring it to a rolling boil.** Large bubbles should break the surface continuously, not just a few at the edges.
4. **Keep it boiling for one minute.** At altitudes above about 2,000 metres, boil for three minutes, because water boils at a lower temperature there.
5. **Let it cool with the lid on.** A cover keeps out ash, insects and dust while it cools.
6. **Store it in a clean, covered container** that has been washed and dried. Label it with the date.

## Saving fuel

Boiling uses a lot of fuel, so plan for it:

- Boil one large pot rather than several small batches. Heat is lost quickly from small quantities.
- Use a pot with a tight lid, and keep the lid on while heating.
- Use a wind shield around the fire, so heat goes into the pot and not into the air.
- Use the boiled water for cooking, such as rice, pasta or soup, so the heat is not wasted.
- Let the pot finish cooking food with the fire banked down.

## Improving the taste

Boiled water tastes flat because the air has been driven out. Pour it back and forth between two clean containers several times to add air back in. You can also let it cool and then store it in a container that has been aired.

## Storing boiled water

- Boiled water stays safe in a sealed, clean container for several days.
- Keep it in a cool, dark place. Warm water can grow bacteria again if it is exposed.
- Use a clean cup or ladle to take water from the container. Do not dip used cups into the store.

## When boiling is not enough

- **Chemicals and heavy metals** remain in the water. Avoid polluted rivers, areas near factories, and water with an oily sheen.
- **Salt water** stays salty. It needs distillation. See Distillation at Home.
- **Some toxins** from algae are not destroyed by boiling. Do not drink water with a strong algae bloom, even after boiling.

## Common mistakes

- Starting the timer as soon as the pot is warm, rather than when it is boiling.
- Boiling a few centimetres of water in a deep container, which may not reach a full boil.
- Storing boiled water in dirty containers, which contaminates it again.
- Pouring boiled water into a container that still contains untreated water.

## Checklist

- Pot and lid are clean.
- Water is as clear as you can make it.
- You have timed one minute from a full rolling boil.
- Container for storage is clean and labelled.

## Why it matters

Waterborne illness spreads fastest when people drink untreated water during a shortage, because water that looks fine can still make the household sick days later. Boiling needs no chemicals and works with almost any pot and fuel.

## Equipment and materials

- A pot with a tight lid, large enough for the volume you need.
- A ladle or clean cup for pouring.
- Two clean containers with lids: one for cooling, one for storage.
- A cloth or glove for handling the hot pot.

## Adjusting for conditions

- **Cold water** takes longer to reach a boil. Allow extra fuel for the first heating, but keep the one-minute timer for the boil itself.
- **Wind** carries heat away from the pot. Use a shield or move the pot to a sheltered spot.
- **Large volumes** can take twenty minutes or more to reach a rolling boil, so plan the fuel first.

## Troubleshooting

- **No rolling boil:** the fire is too small, the pot is too full, or wind is blowing the heat away. Add fuel, shield the pot or reduce the volume.
- **Smoky or sooty taste:** let the water cool, then pour it gently into a clean container, leaving sediment behind.

## Practice before you need it

Run the setup at home with the same pot and fuel. Time how long a full boil takes, note how much fuel it uses, and practise pouring with a cloth on your hands. Keep the pot, lid and containers together in one kit so they are ready on the day they are needed.

**Warning:** never boil water in a sealed container. Steam pressure can burst it, causing burns and injury.
