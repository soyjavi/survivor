# Repairing a Tent

A tent that leaks or tears is a serious problem in bad weather. Most damage can be repaired with a few basic items, and a quick repair in the field often lasts until you reach a proper workshop.

![Inside view: tent torn area, line-dashed stitching lines around the patch edge, bracket across the overlap on every side; labels: tear, patch, stitching; the patch overlaps the tear on every side, not only across it. NEEDS: patch, tear](assets/repairing-a-tent-1.svg)

## Why it matters

A tent is the main barrier between a family and the weather. A small tear that is ignored can grow in one night of wind, and a leaking seam can soak sleeping bags and clothing that are hard to replace. Repairs made early are quick and cheap, while repairs made in a storm are slow and often fail. Knowing how to fix the tent also means you can keep using it when a replacement is not available.

## Kit to carry

- Repair tape or patch material matching the tent fabric.
- Strong thread and a needle, or a sewing kit.
- Seam sealer or silicone sealant.
- Spare pole sleeves or a short length of pole repair sleeve.
- Scissors or a sharp knife, and a small piece of card to protect the fabric while cutting.

> @image Repair kit laid out on a table with patch tape, seam sealer, needle and thread (product photograph)

## Patch a tear

1. Clean the area around the tear and dry it.
2. Cut a patch with rounded corners, larger than the tear by at least 2 cm on each side. Rounded corners stop the patch from lifting.
3. Apply the patch on the inside, and press it firmly to remove any air.
4. For strength, stitch around the patch, then seal the stitches.

## Seal seams

- Apply seam sealer to the stitching lines on the inside of the fly and floor.
- Let it cure fully before packing away, which often takes several hours.
- Seal the corners of the floor first, since they take the most wear.

![Plan view: tent floor corner, line-solid seam lines, cured patch at the corner, arrow-straight pointing to the corners; labels: seam sealer, patch, first wear points; corners are sealed first. NEEDS: seam-sealer, patch](assets/repairing-a-tent-2.svg)

## Adjusting for conditions

- **In cold weather,** warm the patch material and sealant in a pocket before use, since cold sealant spreads poorly and cures slowly.
- **In rain,** shelter the tent under a tarp while you work, and wipe the area with a dry cloth before patching.
- **For nylon fabric,** use a patch of the same material and a solvent-free sealant, since some glues react badly with coated nylon.
- **For polyester fabric,** choose a patch of matching weave, and allow more cure time before the tent is pitched.

## Repair poles

- Splint a cracked pole with a sleeve and tape.
- Replace a broken section with a stick of similar diameter, bound tightly.
- Check the pole joints each time you put the tent up, since a loose joint will fail under wind.

## Check after use

Dry the tent before packing it. Damp fabric grows mould, and mould weakens the fabric quickly. Store it loosely, not compressed, so the coating does not crack.

## Practice before you need it

Repair a small test tear on an old tent or spare fabric at home, so the steps are familiar. Practise applying the seam sealer and timing its cure. Pack the kit in a small waterproof pouch, and check the sealant has not dried out in its tube before each trip.

## Storing the tent

Keep the tent in a dry place, out of direct sun, which breaks down coatings over time. Store the poles and pegs in a separate bag, so sharp ends do not tear the fabric. Check the seams and zips before each storage period, and repair anything small before it becomes large.

## Inspect the fabric

Inspect the tent in good light, from the inside, running a hand over the fabric and seams. Small pinholes show as bright spots when the fabric is held up to daylight, so check that way too. Mark each problem with a strip of tape before packing, so it is not forgotten at the next pitch.

## Common mistakes

- Patching the outside, where water and wind can lift the edge.
- Using a square patch, which peels at the corners.
- Packing the tent while the sealant is still wet.

## Checklist

- Tear is cleaned and dried before patching.
- Patch is larger than the damage, with rounded corners.
- Seams are sealed on the inside and left to cure.
- Poles are splinted and joints are tight.
- Tent is fully dry before packing.

## When it fails

If a patch keeps lifting, remove it, clean the area with a little alcohol, and apply a fresh patch with more overlap. If a pole breaks in a place that cannot be splinted, use the tent as a lean-to with a tarp instead of a full pitch until it can be replaced.

**Warning:** do not dry a tent near an open fire. Synthetic fabric melts and burns.
