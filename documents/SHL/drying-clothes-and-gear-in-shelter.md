# Drying Clothes and Gear in Shelter

Wet clothing and equipment are a constant problem in any shelter. Damp fabric loses its insulation, draws heat from the body, and begins to smell and rot. Left wet for days, it grows mould that can cause skin irritation and breathing problems. Drying gear safely takes planning, because the methods that work fastest are often the ones that create fire risk or trap moisture.

## Preparing Items for Drying

Start by reducing how much water is being carried into the shelter.

1. Wring out clothing and gear by hand, pressing rather than twisting fabric to avoid damage.
2. Shake off loose snow, soil and debris before bringing items inside.
3. Separate damp items from dry ones, and store them in a different corner or bag.
4. Lay out large items such as tarps and sleeping bags so air can reach every surface.

## Safe Drying Methods

Choose a method that matches the shelter and the weather outside.

- **Air drying:** hang items on a line or rack in the main air flow, away from walls and bedding. This is slow but safe.
- **Body heat:** wear dry socks, gloves and hats to bed, and place smaller damp items inside your sleeping bag overnight. Keep them in a sealed bag so the moisture does not spread to your clothing.
- **Sunlight:** take items outside on sunny days and hang them in the wind, bringing them in before evening.
- **Warm, ventilated space:** where a heat source is safe and approved, place items at a distance and turn them regularly.

Avoid any method that adds heat close to fabric without constant supervision and clear ventilation.

## Hanging and Spacing

Good airflow is more important than heat.

1. Hang items with space between them so that air can circulate freely.
2. Keep lines high enough that items do not touch the floor, walls or bedding.
3. Turn garments inside out to dry the inner surfaces, which hold the most moisture.
4. Move items every few hours so the damp side is exposed.

## Avoiding Fire Hazards

Drying items near a heat source is one of the most common causes of shelter fires.

- Keep at least one metre between any heat source and drying clothes, and never place fabric directly on or against a stove.
- Never dry items on top of a heater, stove or cooking pot.
- Do not hang clothes over a flame, candle or lamp.
- Remove synthetic items such as nylon and polyester from direct heat, because they can melt and stick.
- Keep a bucket of water and a fire blanket close to any heat source you do use.

## Preventing Mould

Mould forms in damp, still, warm conditions, so the goal is to keep moisture moving out of the shelter.

1. Check gear every day for musty smell, dark spots or a fuzzy surface.
2. Brush off any early growth outdoors with a stiff brush, then dry the item fully in sunlight if possible.
3. Keep food and rubbish away from damp gear, because they attract pests and add moisture.
4. Leave a small ventilation gap in the shelter, even at night, so humidity from breathing and cooking can escape.
5. Use a thin layer of dry material, such as spare cloth or leaves, under bedding to absorb moisture from the ground.

Discard items that remain mouldy after drying in the sun, because they can carry spores that cause coughing and rashes.

## Keeping Important Gear Dry

Store sleeping bags, maps, electronics and medical supplies in sealed bags inside the shelter, and place them above the floor. Check seals each morning.
