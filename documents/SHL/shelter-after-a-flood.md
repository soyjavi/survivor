# Shelter After a Flood

Floodwater weakens buildings, leaves mould and carries contamination. Before you move back in, check that the building is safe. Water that has soaked into walls and floors can stay for weeks, so recovery is slow and needs patience.

![Four-step flow: step-1, step-2, step-3 then a fourth box, joined by arrow-straight; labels: entry check, power off, gas check, move to higher floor; each step must clear before the next, so gas and power are safe first. NEEDS: step-4](assets/shelter-after-a-flood-1.svg)

## Check before entering

1. Look for cracks, sagging floors or leaning walls. Do not enter a building that looks unstable.
2. Turn off electricity and gas at the main supply if you can reach it without standing in water.
3. Check for gas smell or hissing. If you smell gas, leave and call for help.
4. Watch for animals, snakes and sharp debris hidden in the water.

## Living in a damp building

- Open all windows and doors when the weather allows, to air the building.
- Remove wet carpets, mattresses and soft furnishings that cannot be dried quickly.
- Keep dry bedding off the floor, on a shelf or a raised pallet.
- Use fans or a small heater with care, away from wet areas. Never burn fuel indoors, since fumes build up quickly in a closed building.

## Mould

Mould grows within days in damp conditions. Keep the room well ventilated, and clean mould with a bleach solution on hard surfaces. Wear a mask and gloves when cleaning, and throw away porous materials that stay damp.

## Higher ground

If the building is unsafe or the water is still rising, move to higher ground or to a designated shelter. Do not wait for a second flood warning before leaving.

## Why it matters

Floodwater carries silt, sewage and chemicals, and it soaks into the materials of a building. What looks dry on the surface may still be wet inside, and the damage often continues for weeks. The first decisions, such as turning off the power and removing wet items, prevent the most lasting harm.

## Cleaning up safely

1. Wait until the water has fallen and the structure is confirmed safe.
2. Put on rubber boots, waterproof gloves and a mask before touching anything wet.
3. Remove silt with a shovel, starting from the highest area and working down.
4. Wash hard surfaces with clean water and a mild detergent, then disinfect them.
5. Lift skirting boards where the water line rose, so the wall cavity can dry.

> @image Volunteers scrubbing a muddy wall with brushes and buckets (documentary photograph of flood cleanup)

## Drying the structure

- Run a dehumidifier or fans with the windows open, placing the fan so air crosses the wettest wall.
- Keep the room temperature steady, since warm air holds more moisture and helps the walls dry.
- Check the wall with a hand after a day, and move the furniture away from it so air can reach the surface.
- Replace plaster or board that stays wet after a week of drying, since saturated material cannot be dried properly.

## Food and water safety

Throw away any food that touched floodwater, including sealed tins with damaged labels or dented lids, since the seal may have failed. Store drinking water in sealed containers, and boil tap water until the supply is confirmed safe. Wash hands thoroughly after any contact with floodwater, before eating or drinking.

## Health

Keep cuts covered with waterproof dressings, and clean them with clean water. Report stomach illness, fever, or any wound that becomes red or swollen to a health worker as soon as possible.

## Common mistakes

- Turning on the electricity before checking the wiring, which may be wet.
- Eating or drinking from water or food that touched floodwater.
- Sleeping in a room that has not been aired, where mould spores build up.

## Checklist

- Structure has been checked for cracks and sagging.
- Power and gas are off or confirmed safe.
- Windows and doors are open to air the building.
- Wet soft furnishings are removed.
- Dry bedding is raised off the floor.

## When it fails

If the damp returns after drying, look for a hidden source such as a leaking pipe or a rising water table, and treat that first. If the building cannot be made safe, move to a shelter and keep the family together while repairs are arranged.

**Warning:** do not sleep in a damp room with a fire burning unless the fire is in a proper stove with a flue.
