# Heating Without Power

When the heating stops, keep one room warm and use it as the family's base. Heating the whole house wastes fuel and spreads cold, while a single well-kept room stays livable for much longer.

![Plan view: table-grid of rooms, one heated room with flame, line-solid draught stoppers at each door, other rooms marked with caution; labels: warm room, draught stopper, cold rooms; one closed room is the warm base. NEEDS: door, curtain](assets/heating-without-power-1.svg)

## Why it matters

A whole house cools within a day or two when the heat stops, and each room you open to the cold makes the others harder to warm. Concentrating effort on one room also means fewer people are moving between cold and warm air, fewer doors stand open and less fuel is wasted. The room becomes the place where food is prepared, medicines are kept and sleep happens, so its comfort affects every other part of the plan.

## Pick one room

- Choose a room with a window that faces the sun, and few outside walls.
- Pick a room close to the main door, but not the one that opens to the outside.
- Close the doors to the rest of the house.
- Block draughts under doors with rolled towels or blankets.

> @image Family gathered in one small room with a fire stove, blankets and closed curtains (documentary photograph of a home during a power cut)

## Use a safe heat source

- A proper stove or fireplace with a working flue is the only safe choice indoors.
- Never use charcoal, barbecues, camping gas heaters or generators indoors. These produce carbon monoxide, which is odourless and can kill during sleep.
- Keep a clear space around the stove, and keep a fire guard in place when children are present.

## Keep the heat in

1. Close curtains at night.
2. Cover windows with blankets or plastic sheeting.
3. Use rugs on bare floors.
4. Hang blankets over doorways.

## Keep people warm

- Layer clothing and keep hats on, since much body heat escapes through the head.
- Sleep together in the warm room if you can.
- Drink warm fluids, and eat regularly, because food is fuel for the body's heat.

## Conserve fuel

- Burn fuel in the evening, when the room is used most.
- Keep the stove loaded but not overfilled, so it burns steadily.
- Store fuel close to the stove, under cover and away from the damp.

![Side view: stove with flue through the wall, smoke arrow-curved leaving, log-stack of fuel under cover beside it; labels: stove, flue, fuel store; smoke leaves through the flue and fuel stays dry close to the stove. NEEDS: stove, flue](assets/heating-without-power-2.svg)

## Adjusting for conditions

- **In very cold weather,** keep the stove burning through the night at a low, steady rate rather than letting it die and rebuilding it from cold.
- **In mild weather,** burn less fuel, and use the warm room only for the evening.
- **If the room has no fireplace,** choose the room with the best existing heat source, and accept a smaller comfort zone rather than heating a room the stove cannot reach.
- **If fuel runs low,** close off the room further, so the heat stays in a smaller space.

## Practice before you need it

Test the stove, flue and chimney before the cold arrives. Light a small fire in good daylight, check that smoke goes up the flue, and clean any soot that would catch the heat. Practise closing the doors and fixing the draught blockers, so the family can do it quickly in the dark.

## Food and rest

Cook simple meals on the stove where the fire is already burning, such as soups, stews and warm porridge. Eat small amounts often rather than one large meal, since digestion itself produces heat. Rest in the warm room during the day when possible, and sleep as a group under the same blankets. Keep a flask of hot drink near the stove for anyone who wakes cold during the night.

## Common mistakes

- Heating several rooms at once, which spreads the warmth too thin.
- Letting the fire go out overnight, then restarting it with fuel that is too wet.
- Forgetting to ventilate, so the room becomes stuffy and damp.

## Checklist

- One room is chosen and the others are closed off.
- Draughts are blocked at the door and window.
- The stove has a working flue and a clear space around it.
- Fuel is stored dry and close by.
- Air in the room is checked regularly.

## When it fails

If the room gets cold quickly, check the flue and the draughts first, then add a second layer of blankets to the windows. If the fire smokes back into the room, put it out safely, open a window, and do not relight it until the flue is cleared.

**Warning:** check the room for smoke and carbon monoxide every few hours while a fire is burning, and never leave a fire unattended with children nearby.
