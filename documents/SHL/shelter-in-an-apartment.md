# Shelter in an Apartment

A flat is often the only shelter a family has in a city. Use the space well, keep the building's routes open, and protect against the risks specific to flats, such as shared walls, lifts and stairwells. Planning before a crisis makes the difference between a calm household and a crowded one.

![Plan view: table-grid rooms with two exits, arrow-straight from each room to a door, wall as line-solid between zones; labels: sleeping, cooking, exit; cooking stays away from sleeping and both have an exit. NEEDS: door, exit, stove](assets/shelter-in-an-apartment-1.svg)

## Choose the room

- Choose a room with a window, away from the lift shaft and stairwell noise.
- Choose a room on a lower floor if the building may be damaged, or a higher one if flooding is possible.
- Keep a second route out of the flat clear: the door, a balcony, or a window that can be opened.

## Organise the space

- Store water, food and bedding in one place, low and out of the way.
- Use the kitchen only for cooking. Keep sleeping areas separate.
- Keep a clear path from the bed to the door, free of boxes and cables.
- Label stored items so anyone in the household can find them in the dark.

## Protect against risks

- Do not cook or heat with fuel inside. Fumes from fuel are dangerous in any closed flat.
- Keep the balcony free of fuel and flammable items.
- Keep the stairs and lobby clear for others.
- Keep a torch or battery lantern beside the door, so the exit can be found in the dark.

## Work with neighbours

- Agree who checks on whom, especially older or disabled residents.
- Share tools, water and information.
- Know who in the building needs extra help, and keep a short list of phone numbers.

## Why it matters

A flat depends on systems outside its own walls. Lifts stop, water pumps fail and neighbours cannot be reached quickly. Planning the flat as a self-contained unit, with its own water, light and exits, lets the household stay calm and safe while the building recovers.

## Power and water

- Keep a battery torch and spare batteries in each main room, not only by the door.
- Fill clean containers from the tap while the supply is running, and store them in the coolest room.
- Know where the main water valve is, and label it so anyone can close it in a leak.

## Keeping the flat safe

- Keep fire extinguishers away from exit routes, and check their gauges.
- Store cleaning chemicals and medicines high and out of reach of children.
- Check smoke alarms monthly, and replace their batteries before they chirp.
- Keep the front door unlocked from the inside at all times, so anyone can leave quickly.
- Keep a small first aid kit beside the water store, so minor injuries can be treated without searching.

## Communication and information

Keep a battery radio tuned to a local station, since flats often lose internet and mobile service together. Write down the phone numbers of the building manager, the utility companies and nearby family, and keep a paper copy by the door.

## Hygiene

If the water fails, keep a lidded bucket for emergency toilet use, and empty it outside regularly. Wash hands with a small amount of water and soap, or alcohol gel where available, and clean surfaces that people touch often, such as door handles and taps.

## Common mistakes

- Sleeping in the kitchen or hallway, where the exit is blocked.
- Keeping stored fuel on the balcony or in the stairwell.
- Waiting for neighbours to organise, rather than starting a simple check-in list.

## Checklist

- Two routes out of the flat are clear.
- Sleeping area is separate from the cooking area.
- Water, food and bedding are stored together and labelled.
- A light is kept by the door.
- Neighbours have agreed a simple check-in plan.

## When it fails

If the lift or stairs are unusable, use the stairs only if they are clear and lit, and go slowly with children and anyone who needs support. If the flat loses power for days, move the household into the room with the most daylight and ventilation, and consolidate water and food there.

**Warning:** never use a lift during a power cut or fire.
