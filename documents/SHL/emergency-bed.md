# Emergency Bed

A good bed matters for rest and warmth. Build one from what you have, and keep it off the cold, damp ground. A tired person who sleeps badly makes poor decisions the next day, so the bed is part of the plan, not an afterthought.

> @image Raised bed of logs and boughs off the ground

![Raised bed of logs and boughs off the ground](assets/emergency-bed.png)

## Why it matters

Sleep is when the body repairs itself and rebuilds its heat, and a bed that lets the cold through can cost hours of rest on each night of a crisis. Most people who wake up miserable in an emergency blame the weather, when the real cause is usually the ground beneath them. A bed built with care turns a hard situation into something bearable, and it helps children and older people keep their strength for the days ahead.

## Materials

- A mat, folded blanket, cardboard, or a bed of dry leaves or grass.
- A waterproof sheet, tarp or plastic bag.
- Blankets, coats or sleeping bags for warmth.
- Logs, crates or boards to lift the bed off the ground, if available.

## Base

- Use a mat, a folded blanket, cardboard, or a bed of dry leaves or grass.
- Lift the bed off bare floors or ground where possible. A thick layer of cardboard is better than nothing.
- Pack the base down so it does not shift during the night.

![Side view: two log-stack logs as the frame with leaf boughs across them, ground-line below, bracket on the gap under the bed; labels: logs, boughs, air gap; the frame lifts the sleeper above the cold ground.](assets/emergency-bed-1.svg)

## Layers

1. The base, to insulate from the ground.
2. A waterproof layer such as a tarp or plastic sheet, under the bedding, not on top of it.
3. Warm bedding: blankets, coats or sleeping bags.
4. A top cover to hold in warmth.

## Building the base step by step

Start by clearing the spot of sharp stones, twigs and debris. Lay the longest pieces of wood or boards first, parallel and evenly spaced, if you are lifting the bed. Fill the gaps between them with boughs, leaves or cardboard, pressing each layer down with your hands before adding the next. Test the surface by sitting on the edge, then lying down, so you find lumps before you are asleep on them.

## Adjusting for conditions

- **On cold, hard floors,** double the base and add cardboard under the mat, since a hard floor draws heat quickly.
- **On damp ground,** raise the bed higher, and place the waterproof layer below the base so the damp cannot climb into the bedding.
- **In wet weather,** keep a spare dry cover in a sealed bag, and bring bedding indoors each day to dry.
- **In warm weather,** use a thinner base and keep the bedding loose, so the body does not overheat.

## Make it comfortable

- Pad the head with clothing or a soft bag.
- Keep a spare set of clothes for sleep, dry and clean.
- Keep the bed near a safe light source for the night, such as a battery lantern.

## Sharing

Two people under one covering share warmth. Children and older people benefit most, so give them the warmest spot and the thickest layers.

## Practice before you need it

Make the bed once at home, with the materials you actually keep, and sleep on it for one night if you can. Note how long the build takes, how many layers keep you warm, and what keeps you awake. Store the materials together in one bag or box, so they are found quickly in a rush.

## Storing the bed

Keep the bedding, base material and waterproof sheet rolled together in one bag, so the bed can be rebuilt quickly. Dry everything thoroughly before packing, since damp material packed away grows mould within days and leaves a smell that stays in the bag.

## Common mistakes

- Putting the waterproof sheet on top of the bedding, where it traps sweat and moisture.
- Using a thin base, which lets the ground pull heat away all night.
- Sleeping in damp clothes that were worn during the day.

## Checklist

- Bed is raised or insulated from the ground.
- Waterproof layer sits underneath the bedding.
- Bedding is dry and clean.
- Head and feet are covered.
- A light is within reach.

## When it fails

If the bed feels cold, add more insulation under the body first, since the ground takes heat faster than the air does. If it becomes damp, dry the bedding in the sun during the day and move it to a drier spot.

**Warning:** do not use an electric blanket or heater that cannot be relied on in a power cut. Keep fire and fuel well away from bedding.
