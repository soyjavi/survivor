# Shelter in a Public Building

A public building such as a school, library, community hall or church can give strong protection from weather, cold and danger. Many people will arrive at the same time, often without planning, so the difference between a calm shelter and a chaotic one comes down to how quickly space is claimed, how the group is organised, and how basic hygiene is maintained.

## Using the Building Safely

Before you settle in, check that the building is actually safe to occupy.

1. Look for obvious structural damage, such as cracked walls, sagging ceilings or broken glass, and avoid those areas.
2. Locate the main exits, the fire exits and any alternative routes out.
3. Find the nearest working water supply, toilets and first aid point.
4. Identify where power, gas and water shut-offs are, and do not operate them unless trained or instructed to.
5. Keep clear of heating appliances that have not been inspected, and never light a fire or stove inside.

## Claiming a Space

Early arrivals tend to set the pattern for the whole shelter, so act deliberately.

- Choose a central area that is warm, dry and not directly beside an exit, so that people can move freely.
- Mark out family or small-group areas with bags, blankets or tape, leaving clear walking routes between them.
- Keep one area free for anyone who needs medical attention or quiet rest.
- Reserve space for medical supplies, food and water so these are not buried under personal belongings.

## Managing the Group

A shelter with many people needs simple structure to stay safe.

1. Appoint a coordinator and two or three helpers, and make sure everyone knows who they are.
2. Take a rough headcount of people, including children, older adults and anyone with special needs.
3. Set clear rules for quiet times, where people may and may not go, and how to ask for help.
4. Assign roles for water, food, cleaning and watch duties, rotating them every few hours.
5. Tell people about exits and the plan for leaving the building if it becomes unsafe.

## Children and Vulnerable People

Keep those who need the most support close to the coordinator and the first aid point.

- Keep children within sight of a responsible adult at all times.
- Make a clear, safe place for elderly people and those with disabilities, close to a toilet and with easy access to water.
- Ask people with medical conditions to tell the coordinator, and keep their medicines together and labelled.

## Hygiene

Hygiene problems spread quickly in crowded spaces, and they are harder to fix once they start.

- Set up a handwashing station near the toilets with water and soap, or with alcohol-based sanitiser if water is short.
- Use disposable gloves when handling waste or cleaning up after anyone who is ill.
- Designate a separate toilet area, and clean it regularly with a diluted disinfectant.
- Dispose of rubbish in sealed bags, and keep bins away from the sleeping and eating areas.
- Encourage people with coughs, vomiting or diarrhoea to stay apart and tell a helper.

## Food and Water

Share resources fairly and record what is used so the supply can be stretched.

- Keep food and water in one labelled, secured place, and distribute it at set times.
- Store food away from cleaning chemicals and rubbish, and cover all food at all times.
- Keep track of stocks on a simple board, updated by the helper on duty.

## Leaving the Building

When the situation changes, leave in an orderly way.

1. Announce the plan clearly and calmly.
2. Take people to the assembly point along the planned route, using the nearest safe exit.
3. Count people at the assembly point and report any missing person to the coordinator.
