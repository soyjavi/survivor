# Sanitation in a Shelter

Poor sanitation spreads disease fast in a crowded shelter. Set up toilets and washing before the first night, and keep them clean. A single contaminated toilet or water point can make many people ill within days.

## Why it matters

In an emergency, clean water and working toilets are often the first things to fail, and the diseases they prevent spread quickly through hands, food and flies. Diarrhoeal illness is especially dangerous for children and older people, because it causes rapid dehydration. Simple measures set up early cost little and prevent outbreaks that would otherwise fill the medical area within a week.

## Toilets

- Place toilets at least 30 metres from the water supply and sleeping areas, downhill from them if possible.
- Separate toilets for men and women, and one for children with an adult.
- Use a simple latrine if there is no plumbing: a trench or pit, with a seat or cover, kept closed when not in use.
- Cover waste with soil or ash after each use.

## Building a trench latrine

Choose a site with firm, well-drained soil, away from the water source and the wind direction from the shelter. Dig a trench about 40 cm wide, 1 metre deep and as long as the number of users requires. Build a simple seat over it from boards, with a gap that can be closed by a lid. Keep a heap of soil or ash beside the latrine, along with a spade, so every user can cover their waste at once.

## Hand washing

- Place a wash station with clean water, soap or ash, and a towel by each toilet and food area.
- Check the station is kept filled, and change the water when it becomes dirty.
- Teach children to wash their hands after the toilet and before eating.

## Adjusting for conditions

- **In heavy rain,** raise the latrine lid, cover the trench with a sloping roof and keep the ground around it sloping away, so runoff does not flow in.
- **In hot weather,** cover waste more frequently, since flies breed faster and the smell spreads further.
- **In cold weather,** keep a wash station near the shelter entrance, and make sure the water does not freeze overnight.
- **Near a river or lake,** place latrines well downstream, and never discharge washing water directly into the watercourse.

## Waste

- Collect rubbish in covered bins, away from living areas.
- Dispose of rubbish regularly, by burying or collecting it.
- Keep food waste sealed, since it attracts flies and rodents.

## Cleaning

- Clean shared surfaces daily with a bleach solution.
- Clean toilets and wash stations at least once a day.
- Wear gloves when cleaning, and wash hands afterwards.

## Practice before you need it

Mark out the latrine and wash station positions in the planning stage, and dig a trial trench in a spare area if you have time. Check the layout against the slope of the ground, the water source and the prevailing wind. Store the soil, ash, spade, soap and cleaning supplies in one labelled box, so the routine starts on day one.

## Water supply

Keep drinking water separate from washing water, and label each container clearly. Store water in clean covered containers, and never let the lid or tap touch the ground. If the source is unclear, boil water before drinking or treat it with a proper purifier, and tell the shelter manager about the source so it can be checked.

## Common mistakes

- Placing a latrine uphill of the water source, where rain carries waste down.
- Leaving the latrine open, which lets flies spread germs to food.
- Letting the wash station run dry, so people stop washing.

## Checklist

- Toilets are at least 30 metres from water and sleeping areas.
- Waste is covered after each use.
- Wash stations have water, soap and a towel.
- Rubbish is sealed and collected regularly.
- Surfaces and toilets are cleaned daily.

## When it fails

If toilets begin to smell or overflow, close them, dig a new latrine in a fresh site, and cover the old one with soil. If a wash station runs out, refill it at once and tell the shelter manager so the supply can be replaced.

**Warning:** report any sign of diarrhoea or vomiting in the centre at once, so the cause can be found quickly.
