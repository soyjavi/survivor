# Living in an Evacuation Centre

An evacuation centre brings many families together in one space. Good routines protect health, privacy and peace. Most problems in a crowded shelter come from small habits repeated by many people, so each household's choices affect everyone else.

## Why it matters

A crowded hall lets illness travel quickly, and noise and uncertainty wear down people who are already under stress. Families who arrive with a clear plan settle faster, lose less sleep and are better placed to help others. The centre is run by staff with limited time, so a household that organises itself frees those staff to deal with those who cannot manage alone.

## Register and inform

- Register when you arrive, so the family can be found.
- Ask where the water, toilets, food and medical help are.
- Learn the centre's rules and its emergency exits.
- Note the name of the staff member you can ask for help.

## Keep your space

- Mark your area with a blanket or sheet, to keep a sense of privacy.
- Keep your belongings together and labelled, so nothing is mixed up.
- Keep valuables with you, and keep important documents in a waterproof bag.
- Keep walkways clear so people can move and leave quickly.

## Health

- Wash hands often, especially after the toilet and before food.
- Keep children's hands clean, and teach them to wash for long enough.
- Report symptoms of illness early, such as vomiting or diarrhoea, so the cause can be found.
- Keep the area around your space clean.

## Community

- Offer help where you can, such as carrying water or looking after children.
- Share information in a calm, factual way, and check rumours with staff before repeating them.
- Respect others' need for sleep and quiet, especially after dark.

## Daily routine

A simple routine keeps the family steady. Wake, wash and eat at roughly the same times each day. Plan one short walk or stretch in the morning and one period of quiet reading or rest in the afternoon. Set a time each evening to check the day's information with the rest of the household, so everyone knows the plan for tomorrow.

## Children and older people

Keep children within sight, and agree a meeting point in case they become separated. Older people and those with medical needs should be near the toilets and the staff desk, not in the busiest corridor. Keep their medicines with them and labelled, and remind them to drink and eat.

## Plan to leave

Keep your go bag packed, and know when the centre is expected to close. Ask staff for help finding housing, repairs or support for your family.

## Sharing supplies

Share food and water fairly, and agree who collects the household's portion so the queue moves quickly. Keep a shared list of what each household has, such as blankets, batteries and medicine, so people who need items can be matched with those who have spares. Give surplus to the staff rather than keeping it, since it can then reach families who arrived with nothing.

## Keeping the peace

Disputes are common when people are tired and crowded. Speak to the other person calmly, and if that does not work, ask a staff member to help. Avoid taking sides in arguments you did not see, and keep children away from conflicts between adults.

## Noise and light

Night noise is one of the most common sources of conflict in crowded shelters. Ask staff whether hall lights can be dimmed after a set hour, and use a sleep mask or earplugs if they help. Keep phones on silent, and point torches at the floor so others are not dazzled.

## Common mistakes

- Leaving a child unattended in a busy hall.
- Storing food near sleeping areas, which attracts pests.
- Waiting until symptoms are severe before reporting them.

## Checklist

- Registration is complete for everyone in the family.
- Personal area is marked and kept tidy.
- Hands are washed before meals and after toilets.
- Important documents are in a waterproof bag.
- The go bag is packed and kept near the family.

## When it fails

If the centre becomes crowded or noisy, ask staff about a quieter area, and use earplugs or a shared quiet hour. If someone becomes unwell, tell staff at once and keep them apart from others until help arrives.

**Warning:** do not leave children unattended in a shared shelter.
