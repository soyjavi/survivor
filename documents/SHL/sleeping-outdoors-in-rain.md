# Sleeping Outdoors in Rain

Rain is more dangerous than cold for an unprepared sleeper. Wet clothing and wet bedding pull heat from the body far faster than dry air does, and a night of damp can bring on hypothermia even when the temperature is not very low. Staying dry through a rainy night depends on three things: where you lie, what you sleep on and under, and how you manage the clothes you wear.

## Choosing Where to Sleep

The site matters more than any single piece of gear.

1. Look for a slightly raised area with a gentle slope so that water runs away from you rather than under you.
2. Avoid low spots, dry creek beds, and hollows where water collects after rain.
3. Stay clear of trees with heavy canopies, because they hold water and drip for hours after the rain stops.
4. Check overhead for loose branches that rain can loosen during the night.

## Managing Drainage

Water on the ground is the first threat, and you can divert it with simple work before you settle down.

- Dig a shallow channel, about ten centimetres deep, around the upslope side of your sleeping area so water runs past rather than through it.
- Keep the channel running downhill and away from your camp.
- Clear leaves, sticks and mud from the area where you will lie.
- Build the sleeping surface slightly raised with a bed of dry branches or a layer of stones beneath your ground sheet.

## Keeping Sleeping Gear Dry

Dry bedding is the difference between a rough night and a dangerous one.

- Place a waterproof ground sheet or tarp beneath your sleeping mat, with the edges tucked slightly under the bed.
- Use a sleeping mat or a thick layer of insulation between you and the ground, because wet soil conducts heat away fast.
- Keep your sleeping bag inside a waterproof bag or stuffed into a dry sack until you are ready to get in.
- Shake out and inspect the bag before you enter it, and check for any damp patches.
- If the bag becomes damp, turn it inside out during daylight and let it dry in sunlight or near a safe heat source.

## Avoiding Wet Clothing

Wet clothing does more harm than people expect, because it loses its insulating value and keeps taking heat from the skin.

1. Change out of wet clothes before getting into your sleeping bag.
2. Wear a dry set of thermal clothing that you have kept in a sealed bag.
3. Avoid cotton, which holds water and stays cold. Wool and synthetic fabrics insulate better when damp.
4. Keep a spare pair of socks and a hat, and wear them to bed.
5. Do not sleep in clothes you sweated heavily in during the day, because they will be damp by morning.

## Overhead Cover

A tarp or a shelter above you keeps rain from reaching the bedding.

- Pitch the cover high enough that you can sit up without touching it, so rain running down the sides does not drip onto you.
- Angle the tarp so the rain runs off the lowest edge, away from your sleeping area.
- Tie guy lines to strong anchors and check them during the night, because wet ropes stretch and loosen.
- Keep the entrance on the side away from the wind, and close it when you sleep.

## Morning Routine

Plan for drying as well as sleeping. Shake out the bedding, dry what you can in the sun, and repack damp items separately so they do not wet everything else. Check that your skin and fingers are not white or numb, and warm up slowly if they are.
