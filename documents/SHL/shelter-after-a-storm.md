# Shelter After a Storm

Storms bring falling trees, broken windows, torn roofs and loose debris. Make the building safe before you rely on it. The first hours after a storm are often the most dangerous, because damage is hidden and aftershocks of wind can bring down what was left standing.

![Side view: tarp-a-frame over a damaged roof, line-solid battens across, rock weights at each corner, arrow-straight wind lifting an unweighted edge; labels: battens, weights, tarp; battens and weights, not nails, hold the cover. NEEDS: roof, batten](assets/shelter-after-a-storm-1.svg)

## Immediate checks

1. Look for downed power lines. Keep away and treat them as live, even if they seem dead.
2. Check for gas leaks and smell, and leave if you find one.
3. Look for structural damage: a sagging roof, cracked walls, a leaning chimney.
4. Watch for trees or branches that could fall further.

> @image Storm-damaged house with a torn roof, scattered tiles and a fallen tree branch on the lawn (documentary photograph)

## Cover the damage

- Cover broken windows with plywood, plastic sheeting or thick cloth, taped or nailed.
- Tarp a damaged roof, with a few layers of tarp secured under battens or weights.
- Board up doors that no longer close properly.

## Clear and stay safe

- Wear gloves and sturdy boots when clearing glass and debris.
- Move heavy items with a partner, bending the knees and keeping the back straight.
- Take photos of damage before cleaning, for any insurance claim.
- Rest often, since fatigue leads to falls and cuts.

![Side view: person-carrying with bent knees and straight back, load close to the body, arrow-straight down over the feet; labels: bent knees, straight back, load close; holding close is safer than reaching.](assets/shelter-after-a-storm-2.svg)

## When to leave

Leave if the building shows any sign of collapse, or if it is flooded or on fire. Go to a designated shelter, and tell someone where you are going.

## Why it matters

After a storm, the danger is not only the wind that has passed. Damaged structures can fail under their own weight, and rain that enters through an open roof soaks the rooms below. A few hours of careful work now can keep a home usable for the days or weeks needed to arrange proper repairs.

## Temporary repairs

1. Measure the hole from the outside, and cut a tarp about 1 metre larger on each side.
2. Lay the tarp over the damage, and fix the top edge first, so water runs over rather than under it.
3. Nail or screw battens along the edges, through the tarp into the timber, to clamp it firmly.
4. Add weights such as sandbags or tyres at the bottom edge, so wind cannot lift it.

## Protect the household

- Keep the fridge and freezer closed, since food keeps longer when they are not opened.
- Store clean water in covered containers before the supply is checked.
- Secure pets in a room away from broken glass.
- Keep children away from windows and loose debris while adults work.

## Get help

Contact your insurer and the local authority once the house is safe. Keep receipts for any temporary repairs and for food or accommodation, since these may be claimable. Ask neighbours which tradespeople they trust, and agree a schedule for checking on older residents who cannot climb or lift.

## Shelter in the meantime

If the roof is badly damaged, move the household into the part of the house that is still dry and structurally sound, usually a room beneath a solid section of roof. Keep bedding off the floor, and set buckets under any drip, emptying them regularly so the floor does not become slippery.

Check the roof space from below each evening for new drips, since water that enters during the night often stains the ceiling first.

## Managing stress

Repairs and clean-up are exhausting. Share the tasks, take breaks, and keep to a simple daily routine. Talking with neighbours about what they saw is a common way to recover, and it helps children understand that the damage can be repaired.

## Common mistakes

- Walking under a damaged roof or a leaning wall.
- Moving heavy debris alone, which causes back injuries.
- Throwing away damaged items before photographing them.

## Checklist

- Power lines are avoided and reported.
- Gas has been checked, with no smell or hiss.
- Structure has been inspected from outside first.
- Broken windows and doors are covered.
- Photos of the damage are taken before clearing.

## When it fails

If the tarp blows loose, re-secure it at once, or move the family to a sheltered room until the wind drops. If the structure shifts or new cracks appear while you are inside, leave immediately and call for help from outside.

**Warning:** do not climb onto a damaged roof. Use a ladder placed on firm ground, and have someone hold it.
