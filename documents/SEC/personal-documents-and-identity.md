# Personal Documents and Identity in a Crisis

Identity documents open doors in a crisis. They prove who you are for access to shelter, aid, medical care, school, banking, and travel. Losing them can leave a person unable to receive help or prove family ties. Protecting them takes a little work before an emergency and clear steps after one.

## What to copy

Make copies of the documents that establish identity, family relationships, and rights. Keep the list short enough to manage, but complete enough to rebuild your records.

1. Identity cards, national ID, or passports for every adult and child.
2. Birth certificates, and marriage or divorce certificates where relevant.
3. Property deeds, rental agreements, or proof of address.
4. School records, professional licences, and certificates of qualification.
5. Bank account details, insurance policies, and pension or benefit records.
6. Medical records, vaccination cards, and prescriptions for regular medicines.
7. Driving licences and vehicle registration papers.
8. A list of important phone numbers and reference numbers for each document.

Copy each document in colour where possible, and write the date of copying on the back. Make at least two copies, and store one with a trusted person outside the area.

## Keeping originals safe

Originals should be kept with you where it is safe, or in a secure place you can reach quickly. Carrying everything at once is risky, but leaving everything behind is also risky if you are forced to move.

1. Place originals in a waterproof, sealed bag or document wallet.
2. Keep the bag on your body, inside clothing or in a secure inner pocket, rather than in a visible bag.
3. Store extra originals in a dry, hidden container in the home, not in an obvious place.
4. Keep the bag with you when you sleep, and check it regularly.
5. Never hand over originals to people you do not know well. Show them, or provide copies if the request is legitimate.

## Digital copies

A phone photograph can preserve a document, but only if it is clear and stored safely. Digital copies should be backed up in more than one place, and protected with a strong passcode.

- Photograph each page in good light, with the edges visible.
- Save the photographs in an encrypted folder or secure app, and back them up to a second device or drive.
- Keep the phone charged and carry a small power source if possible.
- Avoid sharing images openly, because they contain personal details that can be misused.

## Lost or destroyed documents

When documents are lost, start with the authority that issued them. Replacements are usually possible, but may take weeks or months, so begin as early as you can.

1. List every document that is missing, with the approximate date of issue and the issuing office.
2. Gather what remains, such as copies, photographs, letters from employers, or witness statements from community leaders.
3. Visit the issuing office in person or contact it through the official channel, and ask what evidence it will accept.
4. Ask for a temporary or emergency certificate if the process is slow.
5. Keep a record of each application, including the reference number, date, and name of the officer you spoke to.
6. Follow up regularly, and keep copies of all correspondence.

Some agencies offer registration or documentation services in camps and shelters. Ask the camp manager or a humanitarian organisation where these are available and whether they can help with applications.

## Proving identity without papers

Where documents are missing, identity can still be established through a combination of evidence. Gather anything that links you to your name, family, and place of residence.

- Letters or certificates from schools, employers, or religious and community leaders.
- Witness statements from people who know you well, signed and dated.
- Photographs showing you with family, at home, or at work.
- Records of medical treatment, vaccinations, or aid received under your name.

## Protecting identity from misuse

Crises are a period of heightened identity theft and fraud. Be cautious with anyone who asks for personal details, especially by phone or message, and verify their identity through the official source before providing information.

1. Do not give your full identity number, password, or bank details to anyone who contacts you unexpectedly.
2. Confirm any request for payment or help by calling the organisation through a number you already trust.
3. Report stolen or lost documents to the police and the issuing office as soon as possible.
4. Ask the issuing office to flag a lost document where that option exists.

## Children's documents

Children's identity papers are especially important after separation. Keep a copy of each child's birth certificate and a recent photograph in the same secure bag. Record the names and contact details of guardians, so that a child can be identified and returned quickly.

Review all documents every few months. Update contact details, replace damaged copies, and refresh photographs as children grow.

**Warning:** Never leave original identity documents unattended in a shelter, vehicle, or queue. Theft of papers is a common route to identity fraud and to lost access to aid.
