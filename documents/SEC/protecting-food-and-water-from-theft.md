# Protecting Food and Water from Theft

In a crisis, food and water attract attention quickly. Theft is often opportunistic, carried out by people who did not plan to steal but saw an unguarded store. Good protection rests on three habits: store carefully, speak discreetly, and share deliberately. None of these guarantees safety, but together they make a household far less tempting.

## Store supplies carefully

1. Keep the main stock in one secure place, ideally a room or container that can be locked or latched from inside.
2. Use containers with lids that seal well, and label them neutrally, avoiding words like food or water.
3. Keep a small day supply separate and easily accessible, so the main store is not opened repeatedly.
4. Store heavy items low down and near the back of the space, where they are hard to remove quickly.
5. Check the store once a day, noting what is used and what remains, and keep the count on paper, not where others can read it.

Keep the store cool and dry. Damp or heat spoils food and can make it unsafe, which is itself a loss worth preventing.

## Keep discretion

What others know about your supplies is often the greatest risk. Talking about stock, even innocently, can invite visits from people you do not trust.

- Do not discuss how much food or water you have, even with people who seem kind.
- Avoid carrying visible bags of food or large containers of water through crowded areas.
- Do not advertise your stock with comments such as "we have plenty".
- Keep cooking quiet and rarely in view of the street or a busy path. Smells and smoke attract attention.
- Move water and food in small, regular amounts instead of large, obvious loads.

## Share with trusted people

Sharing can build support and reduce the chance that neighbours turn against you, but sharing without thought can drain stock and expose the household. Decide in advance who will receive help and how much.

1. Identify a small group of people you trust, such as family, close friends, or neighbours you have known for a long time.
2. Agree on a clear rule, for example a fixed daily share for certain people, and keep to it.
3. Give help openly for specific needs, such as children or the sick, rather than handing out goods in front of a crowd.
4. Keep a reserve for your own household, so you do not become dependent on others.
5. Ask people to keep your arrangement private, and explain why.

Organised aid distributions, when they exist, are often safer than private sharing. Ask community leaders or aid staff how distributions work, and whether they can provide help without publicity.

## Avoid conflict

Conflict over food and water usually grows from anger, fear, and a feeling of unfairness. Calm, honest communication often prevents violence.

1. Speak calmly and keep a clear distance from anyone who is shouting or agitated.
2. Explain your limits honestly without giving details of what you hold. Say something like "we cannot help with more right now".
3. Never bargain under threat. Doing so only encourages repeated demands.
4. Ask a trusted neighbour, community leader, or camp manager to mediate when tensions rise.
5. If someone tries to take supplies by force, do not resist physically. Protect people first, then report the incident.

## Protecting water sources

Water is harder to hide than food and is often collected from shared points. Protect your container first, then your access.

- Carry water in a container with a secure cap, and keep it with you rather than leaving it unattended.
- Fill containers at quieter times of day, avoiding crowds at the main point.
- Mark containers with your household name or a number if it helps you recognise them.
- Avoid leaving water near doors or windows where it can be taken easily.

## If stores are stolen

Report theft to the camp manager, community leader, or authority. Keep a record of what was taken, when, and any witnesses. Do not confront suspected thieves alone, and avoid accusing people without evidence, because false accusations can create new conflict.

Review your storage afterwards and adjust. Lock doors, change routines, and tighten discretion. Share the lessons with trusted people so that the whole group is more secure.

## Plan ahead

Build your stock gradually, and plan for at least two weeks of food and water. Keep track of what you need, and rotate stock so the oldest items are used first. A well-stocked household that does not announce its situation is far less likely to be targeted.

**Warning:** Never display, sell, or openly trade your entire supply. Showing a large store is one of the fastest ways to make a household a target.
