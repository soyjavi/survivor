# Securing the Home

In a crisis, a home that looks empty or easy to enter is at higher risk. Most intrusions are opportunistic, which means the intruder chooses the easiest target. Small, inexpensive changes make a large difference, and they work best when you make them before you need them.

## Why it matters

Intruders rarely want a fight. They look for the quickest way in, and they move on when the effort looks high. A door with a bar, a window that locks and a light on at the entrance each add time and attention to the intruder's task, and that is often enough to make them choose another house. Securing the home also protects the people inside, because it gives them time to react.

## Doors and windows

- Lock doors and windows at night, and whenever you leave.
- Fit a strong bolt or bar on each external door, and reinforce the frame.
- Keep spare keys in the same place, known to everyone, and never hidden outside.
- Replace broken glass or board it up quickly.

Check the hinges and the strike plate, because a strong lock fitted into a weak frame can be forced with little effort. Screws should be long enough to reach the framing behind the wall.

## Lighting

- Keep a light on at night in the entrance and in rooms facing the street.
- Use motion lights, or keep a torch by the door.
- Trim vegetation so that doors and windows are visible from the street.

Motion lights are useful when the power is on, but they fail in a power cut. Keep a battery torch in the same place as the house keys, so that it is always to hand.

## Keep the house looking lived in

- Do not post your location or absences publicly.
- Ask a neighbour to collect post and check the house if you leave.

A house with a full mailbox, closed curtains for days and no movement is an obvious signal. Ask a trusted neighbour to open the curtains and move the bin at intervals.

## Keep valuables away from view

- Store documents, cash and medicines out of sight, in a sealed bag.
- Keep ladders, tools and fuel locked away.

## Safe room

Choose a room with a strong door and a lock, close to the phone or radio, and keep it stocked. A safe room gives the household a secure place to wait while help is called.

## Practice before you need it

1. Walk around the outside of the house and list each door, window and gate.
2. Check that every lock and bolt works from the inside, and that the household can open them quickly.
3. Test each motion light and torch, and replace any batteries that are weak.
4. Repeat the walk each season, especially after the garden or the trees change.

## Adjusting for conditions

If you rent, ask the landlord before fitting any bolt, because some changes need permission. In a flat, secure the shared entrance first, and agree with neighbours who holds a key for the building. In winter, check that window frames are sealed and that the front light still works, because short days mean the house is dark for longer.

## Working through the house

Start at the front door and move clockwise, checking each opening in turn. Test whether a door can be lifted off its hinges, because hinge pins that are easy to reach offer an easy route. Look for gaps beside locks, loose letterboxes and glass panels near the handle, which can be broken to reach a lock from outside. Make sure the garage or shed door has its own lock, and that any gate onto the back garden fastens from the inside. Work methodically rather than rushing, and write each finding down so that the fixes can be prioritised.

## Common mistakes

- Leaving a spare key under a mat or in a flower pot.
- Relying on a single lock on a back door.
- Keeping valuables in the bedroom where they are easy to find.
- Forgetting the windows on upper floors.

## Checklist

- Every external door has a bolt or bar.
- Windows lock from the inside.
- Lights are on at the entrance and facing the street.
- Keys and valuables are stored where only the household knows.

## When it fails

If a lock fails, fit a temporary brace or move heavy furniture across the door. If a window is broken, cover it with board or thick cloth, and move the household away from the gap until it is secured.

**Warning:** do not confront an intruder. Get family into the safe room and call for help.
