# Staying Safe in Crowds

Crowds can turn dangerous quickly, through pressure, panic and crushing. Avoid them where you can, and know how to leave if you are caught in one. Most crowd injuries come from being squeezed, not from violence, so the aim is to keep space around you and keep moving with the flow.

![Crowd flow plan: line-solid edge lane, arrow-straight diagonals toward start-or-end exits, dense central block with caution; labels: edge lane, exit, dense centre; move along the edge and diagonally, never against the flow. NEEDS: crowd](assets/staying-safe-in-crowds-1.svg)

## Why it matters

A crowd is a system that can change direction without warning. Once people are packed shoulder to shoulder, they cannot control their own movement, and the pressure from behind can lift feet off the ground. Because the people around you are also trapped, the crowd can be dangerous even when no one intends harm. Recognising the early signs and leaving before the crowd tightens is the single most useful skill.

## Avoid

- Do not go to the centre of a large gathering.
- Avoid queues that block roads or are unsupervised.
- Check local information before travelling, and avoid areas with reported unrest.

A queue with no marshal, no barrier and no clear order is a warning sign. Ask a staff member or an official what is happening, and be ready to leave if the answer is vague.

## Move with care

1. Keep to the edge of the crowd, where it is less dense.
2. Keep your feet under you, and keep your arms in front of your chest to create space.
3. Move diagonally, not against the flow.
4. Keep children close, holding a hand or with a clear meeting point.

## Crowd crush

Crushing happens when people are packed too tightly to move, and it can happen within minutes.

- If you feel the crowd pressing, move sideways to an exit.
- If you fall, curl up with your arms over your head and protect your neck.
- Do not push against the crowd, because pushing makes the pressure worse for everyone.

## Getting out

Identify two exits when you arrive. Note where the nearest open space is, and keep your route in mind as you move.

Count the seconds it would take to reach each exit, and remember which way the exit opens. Many exit gates open only one way, and they can jam under pressure.

## Practice before you need it

1. At any gathering, spend the first minute finding the exits and the open space.
2. Stand near the edge, and move along it to the nearest exit to get a feel for the route.
3. With family members, agree a meeting point outside the venue before you go in.
4. Practise keeping arms in front of the chest while walking slowly through a quiet corridor.

## Adjusting for conditions

In heat or rain, crowds can become tense sooner, so leave earlier. At night, lights may be poor, so keep to the route you noticed on arrival. If you use a wheelchair or a walking aid, choose the edge with clear space and ask for a steward to guide you to the exit before the crowd becomes dense.

## Reading the crowd

A crowd that is moving calmly rarely needs intervention. A crowd that is tightening shows clear signs: people begin to sway, the front stops moving while the back keeps pushing, shouts change to cries, and gaps between people disappear. Listen for sudden silence, which can mean that people nearby have stopped breathing comfortably. Look for raised hands, which often signal that someone is trying to get out. Once you see these signs, move toward the nearest edge or exit at once, because the window for leaving safely closes quickly.

## Common mistakes

- Standing in the middle of a crowd to see the event.
- Holding a phone up in front of the face, which removes the arms from the chest.
- Staying in a crowd when it is clearly tightening.
- Losing track of children in the first minutes.

## Checklist

- Two exits are identified on arrival.
- Children are held or have a meeting point.
- Arms are kept in front of the chest.
- The edge route is known.

## When it fails

If you are separated, move to the nearest open space or the agreed meeting point, and call for help. If you are carried by the crowd, keep your feet, move diagonally and aim for a wall or a railing where you can hold on.

**Warning:** a crowd crush can kill without anyone meaning harm. Leave early when the crowd becomes tight.
