# Personal Safety at Night

Night is when people are most at risk: visibility is low, hazards are hidden and help is slower to arrive. Plan for it during the day, when you can think clearly, so that night-time decisions are simple and quick.

![Night checklist: step-1, step-2, step-3, step-box in order with arrow-straight, flashlight, whistle; labels: lock doors and windows, torch by bed, shoes and coat in reach, household signal; the order of the routine before bed. NEEDS: door, window, shoes](assets/personal-safety-at-night-1.svg)

## Why it matters

Darkness hides steps, kerbs, broken glass and people. It also changes how the body responds: fatigue rises, judgement slows and fear grows out of proportion to what is actually happening. A household that has decided in advance what to do at night avoids improvising while tired and frightened, which is when most mistakes happen.

## At home

- Lock doors and windows before dark, and check them once more before bed.
- Keep a torch by the bed and another by the door.
- Keep shoes and a coat within reach, so you can leave quickly if needed.
- Agree a sound or signal the household uses to call for help, and practise it.

Keep a phone charged and within reach of the bed, and set its brightness low so that the screen does not blind you in the dark.

## Moving at night

- Move only when necessary, and with someone else.
- Stay on known paths, and use light sparingly to see the way.
- Keep to well-lit or populated areas where possible.
- Tell someone where you are going and when you expect to arrive.

![Night path: person-standing, flashlight with beam low, line-solid lit stretches, line-dotted dark stretches, footprints; labels: lit, dark, next step; light is used sparingly, only to see the next step.](assets/personal-safety-at-night-3.svg)

## Sleeping

- Sleep where the household can hear each other.
- Keep a watch on rotation if the group is large, with one adult awake at a time.
- Keep valuables and documents close, but out of sight.

A rota can be simple: two hours each, with the watch-keeper waking the next person on time. Make sure the watch-keeper has a torch, a charged phone and a quiet place to sit near the door.

## Practice before you need it

1. Turn off the lights in the home and walk the route from bed to the door and to the meeting point.
2. Practise the household signal, and check that everyone wakes and responds within seconds.
3. Test each torch and replace any batteries that are weak.
4. Repeat the drill with children, using a game to keep it calm.

## Adjusting for conditions

In a power cut, the streetlights may also be out, so use a torch only when you must and cover its lens with a cloth to reduce the glow. In cold weather, keep the sleeping area warm with blankets rather than a flame. If a neighbour has not been seen for some time, check on them with a companion rather than alone. Keep a whistle beside the bed, since its sound carries further than a voice and does not require anyone to be visible. Keep the torch on a hook or cord where it can be grabbed in the dark, and in a house with stairs, sleep on the floor that has the quickest route out.

## Keeping watch without a light

Eyes take several minutes to adjust to darkness, and a single bright torch undoes that adaptation for everyone nearby. Turn lights off in the room before you look out of a window, and wait a minute for your eyes to settle. Watch the street from behind a curtain rather than from an open door, and listen as much as you look. Sounds carry well at night: footsteps, voices, the snap of a branch or a car door can tell you more than a glimpse. Keep a notebook by the window to record times and descriptions, because a memory written in the dark is easy to confuse with a memory written at dawn.

## Common mistakes

- Leaving a door unlocked because the night seems quiet.
- Using a bright light that announces your position.
- Walking alone through unlit streets to check on something.
- Sleeping in a room with no second way out.

## Checklist

- Doors and windows are locked and checked.
- A torch is by the bed and by the door.
- Shoes and a coat are within reach.
- The household signal is known to everyone.

## When it fails

If you hear someone enter the home, stay out of sight with the household, lock the door of the safe room if you have one, and call for help. If you lose your torch, move slowly along a wall, keep one hand on it, and wait for daylight rather than improvising.

**Warning:** a light can draw attention. Use it only when needed, and point it down, not at people.
