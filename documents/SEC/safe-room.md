# Safe Room

A safe room is a room in the home that can be secured and where the household can wait for help. It is useful against intruders, storms and some hazards, but it works only if it is chosen and stocked before it is needed.

## Why it matters

When a threat arrives, there is rarely time to gather people, find supplies and decide where to go. A prepared room gives the household one clear destination, a locked door between it and the threat, and enough supplies to wait for help without going out. It also reduces the number of decisions people must make under stress, which is when mistakes are most likely.

## Choose the room

1. Pick an interior room, away from outside walls and windows.
2. Choose a room with a solid door that can be locked from inside.
3. Keep it close to a phone or radio, and to the water supply if possible.
4. Check that everyone in the household can reach it quickly, including children and people with limited mobility.

A bathroom or a small bedroom with a solid door often works well, because it is near water and has few windows. Avoid rooms that rely on a single narrow exit or a door that opens outward into a corridor.

## Stock it

Keep the supplies in labelled containers, and check them every six months.

1. Water, for at least three days for everyone in the room.
2. Food that needs no cooking, and a manual tin opener.
3. A torch, a battery radio and spare batteries.
4. A first aid kit and a supply of any regular medicines.
5. A way to signal for help, such as a whistle or a bright cloth.
6. A toilet bucket with a lid, and wipes.

Add a list of phone numbers written on paper, a copy of key documents and a small amount of cash. Phones may fail, and paper keeps working.

## Make it safe

- Keep the door locked, and do not open it unless you know who is there.
- Keep a clear path from the room to the door and to the way out.
- Do not block any ventilation, and do not use fuel or fire inside.

## Agree the rules

Everyone knows where the safe room is, how to get there, and how to stay quiet inside. Agree who opens the door, who keeps watch and what signal ends the lockdown.

## Practice before you need it

1. Walk from every bedroom to the safe room with a timer, in daylight and in the dark.
2. Practise locking and unlocking the door with everyone present, and check that the key or bolt works.
3. Sit in the room together for ten minutes, and notice what is missing, such as a light or a seat.
4. Rotate the stock every six months, and note the date on each container.

## Adjusting for conditions

In a storm or flood, check that the safe room is above the likely water level and is not directly under a loose roof. In a violent event, keep the lights off and stay away from the door. In hot weather, the room may heat up quickly, so keep water nearby and make sure there is airflow without opening the door.

## Signs the room needs attention

Check the room every few months for damp, peeling paint, loose hinges and a door that no longer closes fully. A door that rubs on its frame may not lock reliably. Test the lock from both sides, and confirm that the bolt or key reaches the strike plate. Look at the walls for hairline cracks and at the ceiling for stains that suggest a leak. If any of these appear, repair them before relying on the room, because a weakened door or a damp ceiling can fail at the moment it is needed most.

## Common mistakes

- Choosing a room with large windows because it is bright.
- Storing supplies in a different room from the one you will use.
- Forgetting the toilet, which becomes essential after a few hours.
- Keeping a phone with no charge.

## Checklist

- The room has a solid, lockable door.
- Water, food, light, radio and first aid are inside.
- The household knows the route and the rules.
- A signal for help is ready.

## When it fails

If the room is not safe, for example because of smoke or flooding, leave it using the planned route and go to the meeting point. If the door cannot be locked, block it with heavy furniture and move everyone to the far side.

**Warning:** a safe room is for waiting, not for fighting. Stay inside, stay quiet and call for help.
