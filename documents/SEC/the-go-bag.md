# The Go Bag

A go bag is packed in advance and kept by the door. When you must leave, you take it and go, without searching for essentials in a hurry. Each adult should be able to carry their own bag, so pack for the weakest person in the household and for the longest walk you might face.

## Why it matters

Evacuations often happen with little notice, at night or during bad weather, when shops are closed and power is off. Without a bag, people forget medicines, documents and water, and they spend precious minutes gathering what they need. A packed bag turns a chaotic departure into a short walk to the door, and it keeps the most important items with the household wherever it goes.

## Contents for each person

- Water: at least 2 litres, or a water bottle and a filter.
- Food: high-energy food that needs no cooking, for at least three days.
- Clothing: a change of clothes, warm layers, rain gear and sturdy shoes.
- Medicines: a few days' supply, and a copy of the prescriptions.
- First aid: a small kit with plasters, bandages and any personal items.
- Documents: copies of ID, insurance and the contact list, in a waterproof bag.
- Light: a torch with spare batteries, and a head torch.
- Tools: a multi-tool or knife, a whistle, and a lighter or matches in a waterproof tin.
- Money: cash in small notes, since card systems may fail.

Pack a small phone power bank and its cable, and keep a paper copy of the meeting point and the contact numbers inside the bag.

## Family items

- Comfort items for children, and a change of clothes for each child.
- Care items for babies or elderly relatives, such as nappies or spare medicines.
- Pet food, a lead and identification for animals.

## Keep it ready

1. Pack the bag and keep it by the door, or in the car if you have one.
2. Check the contents every six months, and rotate food and water.
3. Keep a list inside the bag, so you can check what is missing at a glance.
4. Update the bag when a child grows, or when a medicine changes.

## Practice before you need it

1. Pick up each bag and walk to the door and then to the meeting point, timing the walk.
2. Ask each person to name three items in their own bag without looking.
3. Weigh each bag, and remove anything that makes it too heavy to carry comfortably.
4. Repeat the drill every year, and compare the time with the last one.

## Adjusting for conditions

In cold weather, add a spare pair of socks, gloves and a hat, and store a foil blanket in the top of the bag for quick access. In hot weather, carry extra water and a light hat, and avoid storing food in direct sun, where it can spoil. If you have a car, keep a second, lighter bag there in case the first is left behind.

## Choosing what to carry

Start with the items that cannot be replaced, such as medicines, identity documents and a small amount of cash. Add water and food for the expected trip, then light, warmth and first aid. Personal items such as glasses, a spare charger and a toothbrush come next, and they matter more than they seem. Leave out anything bulky that you could replace later, such as large photo albums or spare clothing beyond one change. A useful test is to imagine walking with the bag for an hour, then remove one item for each kilogram that feels unnecessary.

## Common mistakes

- Packing a bag so heavy that it cannot be carried for an hour.
- Storing cash, medicines or documents in a bag that is not waterproof.
- Forgetting to check expiry dates on food and batteries.
- Leaving the bag in a different room from the one you use to leave.

## Checklist

- Each person has a bag that they can carry.
- Water, food, light, first aid and documents are inside.
- Medicines and copies of prescriptions are included.
- The bag is checked every six months.

## When it fails

If a bag is lost or damaged, use the list to replace the missing items as soon as possible. If you must leave without it, take water, medicines, documents and a torch first, and leave the rest.

**Warning:** do not pack anything you would not be able to carry for several kilometres.
