# Keeping a Low Profile

Attention is a risk in a crisis. Visible supplies, predictable movements, and obvious valuables tell others where to look and when a household is vulnerable. A low profile does not mean hiding from neighbours or refusing help. It means making your situation hard to read from the outside, so that people who do not need to know do not know.

## Why visibility matters

Most people who take from others are not planning a major raid. They notice what is easy to see, then return when the chance seems good. Each visible sign adds to that picture: a full water container at the door, a lamp lit late, a bag carried every day, or a pattern of visits.

1. Think about what an outsider can see from the street, the path, and the nearest shelter entrance.
2. Notice what can be heard, such as cooking sounds, a generator, or loud arguments.
3. Consider what people learn from your routines, such as when you leave, when you return, and when you collect water.
4. Make a short list of the three most obvious signs of your household's supplies or belongings.

## Reduce visible supplies

Supplies that cannot be seen are much harder to target. Work through the places where goods are most obvious.

1. Move water containers and food out of doorways, windows, and open courtyards.
2. Store extra items in a room or container that is not visible from outside.
3. Avoid stacking supplies high, where their size and shape show from a distance.
4. Empty and fold packaging, and dispose of it away from the home so that stock cannot be estimated from the rubbish.
5. Carry only what you need for each trip. Do not bring large bags or loads through busy areas.

## Vary routines

Routines make a household predictable. Predictable households are easier to watch and easier to rob. You do not need to be erratic, but a little variation in timing and route makes patterns harder to read.

- Collect water at different times of day, and on different days where you can.
- Use more than one route to the water point, the market, or the aid distribution.
- Vary when you cook, and keep meals simple so smoke and smells do not last long.
- Avoid announcing departures or arrivals, such as calling out to family as you leave.
- Keep regular daily contact with trusted neighbours, so that someone notices if something is wrong.

## Control noise and light

Noise and light carry information. A lamp in a window at night, or a generator running every evening, tells people that your home is occupied and has resources.

1. Use a small light, pointed downward, and cover windows where possible.
2. Keep voices low, especially at night and when discussing supplies or money.
3. Avoid playing music or radios loudly enough to be heard from outside.
4. If you use a generator, place it away from the living area and keep it quiet where you can.

## Keep valuables quiet

Visible valuables such as jewellery, phones, cash, or expensive clothing attract attention quickly. Keep them out of sight, and do not show them to people you do not know well.

- Wear only what is necessary, and keep the rest in a hidden container.
- Do not count or display cash in public.
- Avoid mentioning your savings, goods, or assets in conversation.
- Store valuable papers as described in the guide on documents and identity.

## Stay connected without being exposed

A low profile should not isolate you. Trusted networks are one of the strongest protections in a crisis, because neighbours and families notice trouble and help when needed.

1. Choose two or three people you trust, and agree on simple check-ins each day.
2. Share your general needs, such as needing help with children, without revealing the details of your stores.
3. Watch for people who ask many questions about your supplies or routines, and be polite but brief.
4. Learn the local leaders and aid staff who can help if you face pressure or threats.

## Recognise when the profile slips

Sometimes the signs of a household's situation become known despite careful effort. Respond calmly and early rather than waiting for trouble to build.

1. Notice when someone seems to know more than they should, or when visitors arrive with specific questions.
2. Move supplies to a more secure place and review your routines.
3. Tell a trusted neighbour or community leader what you have noticed, so that others can watch the area.
4. If threats or force appear, prioritise safety over property, and report the incident to the authority responsible for the area.

## Why it matters

A low profile reduces the number of people who want what you have, and makes the people who do want it less likely to act. It gives your household time to plan, to rest, and to recover. It also protects others, because a household that does not attract attention is less likely to draw conflict into a shared space.

Review your profile every week. Ask what has changed, what has become visible, and what could be simplified. Small adjustments made regularly are more effective than dramatic changes made in a panic.

**Warning:** Never store supplies in a place that is easy to reach from the street or a public path without a lock or a trusted person nearby. Visible and unguarded stock is the most common target.
