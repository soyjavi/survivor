# Child Safety in a Crisis

Children are more at risk in a crisis: they move fast, they are curious, and they can be separated. Plan around them from the start, before anything happens, and make sure every adult in the household knows the plan.

![Parts diagram of an ID card: table-header and table-grid rows with leader lines; labels: child's name, home address, contact number; sealed card sits on the child's body, not in a bag. NEEDS: id-card, person-standing-child](assets/child-safety-in-a-crisis-1.svg)

## Why it matters

Children do not yet have the experience to judge danger, and most cannot tell where they are or how to reach help. In a crowded or smoky place, a child who is out of sight for a minute may not be found for much longer. Adults often assume a child is fine because they are close, yet a crisis shrinks the margin for error quickly.
## Hazards at home

Children reach things adults forget about. Walk through the home at a child's height and look for hazards before a crisis begins.

- Keep matches, lighters, knives, medicines and cleaning products locked away.
- Keep fuel, generators and candles out of reach, and store them outside living areas where possible.
- Keep pets and young children apart when food is cooking or hot liquids are being moved.

Repeat the walk-through every season, because furniture moves, new objects arrive and children grow taller and more capable.

## Supervision

Most injuries in emergencies happen in the minutes when attention drifts. Supervision is a rota, not a hope.

- Keep young children within sight, always. A quick glance is not enough.
- When the household moves or sleeps in a shared space, name one adult responsible for each child.

The level of supervision should change with age, as shown below.

| Age group | Supervision level | Main risk |
|---|---|---|
| Under 5 | Within arm's length at all times | Wandering, hazards within reach |
| 5 to 11 | Within sight, with a named adult | Separation in crowds |
| 12 and over | Agreed check-in times | Going alone to look for help |

## Separation

Separation is the most serious risk. Prepare each child so that they can act sensibly for a short time without an adult.

1. Give each child a card with their name, address and a contact number, sealed in a plastic pocket inside their clothing.
2. Teach them their family name, their parents' names and the meeting point.
3. Practise the meeting point with them until they can describe the route without help.
4. If separated, teach them to stay where they are, find an adult in uniform or a police officer, and ask that person for help.

A card kept in a bag is useless if the bag is lost, so the card should travel on the child's body. Re-check the cards each month.

## Strangers

Teach simple rules: do not go anywhere with someone they do not know, and always tell a trusted adult where they are going. Explain that a stranger can look kind and still be unsafe, and that a trusted adult will never ask a child to keep a secret from their parents.

Agree a family password that only the parents and the child know. An adult who collects a child should give the password first, and a child should never go with anyone who cannot provide it.

## Talk about it

Explain what is happening in simple, honest words. Children cope better when they know what to expect, and they will ask questions. Answer them calmly, and repeat the plan often, using games or drawings so it stays in memory.

## Practice before you need it

1. Choose a quiet afternoon and walk the route to the meeting point together.
2. Ask the child to lead the way while you stay one step behind.
3. Time the walk, then repeat it with a pretend interruption, such as a locked gate.
4. Ask the child to say the family name, the address and the password out loud.
5. Praise every correct answer.

## Adjusting for conditions

At night or in smoke, children cannot see far and may be too frightened to speak. Keep a torch with each child, hold hands in dark corridors, and agree a short whistle or call that a child can make from a distance.

## Common mistakes

- Relying on one adult to watch several children at once.
- Storing the identification card in a bag the child may not carry.
- Assuming an older child will remember the address under stress.
- Forgetting that a frightened child may hide rather than call out.

## Checklist

- Each child carries an identification card.
- Each child knows the family name and the meeting point.
- A named adult is responsible for each child in each shared space.
- Dangerous items are locked away.

## When it fails

If a child is missing, search the area they know best first, then the meeting point. Ask neighbours and people in uniform for help, and report the child to the authorities at once. Do not leave the search area unless the group agrees a plan.

**Warning:** never leave children alone in a building with a fire, a generator or a body of water.
