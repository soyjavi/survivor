# Evacuating a Building Quickly

When you must leave a building, speed matters, but a calm and orderly exit is faster than a panicked one. Plan the exit before you need it, because the first minute of an emergency is the worst time to work out a route.

![Floor plan: start-or-end exit twice, line-solid primary route, line-dashed second route, arrow-straight along each; labels: exit 1, exit 2, primary, second; every room has two ways out, never one. NEEDS: room, door](assets/evacuating-a-building-quickly-1.svg)

## Why it matters

Fire and smoke spread faster than most people expect, and smoke kills more often than flame. Crowds at stairwells slow everyone down, and people who hesitate lose the clear path. A household that has rehearsed its exit leaves in a fraction of the time, with less risk of being trapped on the wrong floor.

## Before

- Know two exits from every room.
- Practise the route with the household, including at night.
- Keep shoes and a torch by the bed, so you can leave in the dark.

Check that exit doors open easily, that stairwells are clear and that no bolt or chain needs a key. Keep the route free of storage, bicycles and bins.

![Stairwell cross-section: ground-line, line-solid route, do-not on a blocking bicycle, tip on clear landing; labels: exit door, handrail, clear landing, bicycle; clear obstacles before a drill. NEEDS: stairs, door, bicycle, handrail](assets/evacuating-a-building-quickly-2.svg)

## During

1. Leave as soon as the alarm sounds or you are told to leave.
2. Take only essentials. Do not stop for valuables, and do not search for phones or keys.
3. Use the stairs, not the lift.
4. Feel doors before opening them. A hot door means fire behind it: use another exit.
5. If there is smoke, crouch low and move along the wall, where the air is clearer.
6. Close doors behind you to slow the spread of smoke and fire.

![Numbered sequence: step-1, step-2, step-3, step-box with person-kneeling low, line-solid wall, arrow-straight; labels: crouch low, hand on the wall, feel the door, close it behind you; air is cleaner near the floor, so stay low. NEEDS: door, wall](assets/evacuating-a-building-quickly-3.svg)

## Help others

- Take children by the hand, and carry the youngest.
- Help people who need help to walk. Tell them the route before you move, and offer an arm rather than pulling.
- Check on neighbours who may not have heard the alarm, particularly if they are deaf, asleep or unwell.

Agree beforehand who will help which neighbour. A named helper for each person who needs assistance means nobody is forgotten in the rush.

## Gather

Once outside, move well away from the building, to the meeting point, and count the household. Tell the person in charge if someone is missing, but do not return to look for them.

## Practice before you need it

1. Walk the route from every bedroom, timing how long it takes.
2. Practise closing doors behind you and feeling a door before opening it.
3. Practise a head count at the meeting point, with each person answering aloud.
4. Repeat the drill at night at least once a year, with the lights off.

![Outdoor meeting point: waypoint-pin, person-standing in a line, person-standing holding table-grid checklist, tip callout; labels: meeting point, checklist, answer aloud; the head count comes before anyone goes back.](assets/evacuating-a-building-quickly-5.svg)

## Adjusting for conditions

In a high-rise, do not use the stairs if the building has a designated refuge floor or staff have instructed otherwise. In a dark corridor, keep one hand on the wall, and if the power has failed, use a torch rather than a phone screen that drains quickly. For wheelchair users, plan with the building manager ahead of time, because the stairs may not be a safe option.

## Signs that the exit is closing

Smoke is the first warning in most fires, and it is more dangerous than the flames. Thick, dark smoke at head height means the air near the floor may still be breathable, but the margin is small. Heat rising through a door crack, a smell of burning plastic and a sudden drop in visibility all mean the route is shrinking. Move earlier rather than later. A decision made at the first sign of smoke is easier than one made when the corridor is already dark.

## Common mistakes

- Waiting to see whether the alarm is real.
- Taking time to collect belongings.
- Opening a hot door without checking it first.
- Using a lift, which can fail or trap people.
- Re-entering the building once outside.

## Checklist

- Two exits are known from each room.
- The route has been walked with everyone in the household.
- Shoes and a torch are within reach of the bed.
- The meeting point is outside and well clear of the building.

## When it fails

If the stairs are blocked by smoke, go back to a room with a window, close the door, seal gaps with cloth, and signal from the window. If you are trapped, stay low, keep the door closed and wait for rescue. Do not jump from height.

**Warning:** do not use a lift, and do not go back inside for anything.
