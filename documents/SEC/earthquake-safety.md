# Earthquake Safety

Earthquakes give little warning. The safest actions are decided in advance: where to shelter, how to protect the head, and how to check for damage afterwards. Practise the steps with the household so they become automatic.

> @image Drop, cover and hold on positions indoors (government safety leaflet line drawings, if licensed or public domain)

## Why it matters

Shaking lasts seconds to a minute, yet most injuries happen in that window or in the minutes after. People are hurt by falling objects, by running into the wrong place, and by stepping on glass. A calm, rehearsed response keeps the body protected while the ground moves, and it lets you think clearly once the shaking stops.

## During the shaking

1. **Drop** to the hands and knees, before the shaking throws you down.
2. **Cover** the head and neck with your arms, and crawl to a sturdy table if one is close.
3. **Hold on** to the table or shelter until the shaking stops.

Stay where you are if you are indoors. **Do not run outside**, because falling debris is the biggest danger near buildings. Keep away from windows, mirrors, heavy shelves and anything that can fall.

## If outdoors

Move to an open space, away from buildings, trees, power lines and walls. Drop to the ground and cover your head until the shaking stops.

## If in a vehicle

Stop in an open area, away from buildings, bridges and overpasses. Stay inside until the shaking stops, and wait for it to pass before driving on carefully.

## If in bed

Stay in bed, turn toward the wall, and cover your head and neck with a pillow. Most injuries in bed come from people trying to stand up and reaching for furniture, so wait until the shaking has stopped before moving.

## After the shaking

1. Check for injuries, and give first aid where you can.
2. Check for gas leaks by smell. If you smell gas, open a window, leave and call for help.
3. Check for structural damage. Do not re-enter a damaged building.
4. Expect aftershocks, and drop, cover and hold on again if they occur.

Put on shoes before walking across the house, because broken glass and debris are common. Use a torch rather than a candle, and keep a radio on for updates.

## Prepare the home

Most injuries come from falling objects, not from the building itself.

- Fix heavy furniture, cabinets and shelves to walls with brackets.
- Keep heavy items on lower shelves, and store breakable items in closed cupboards.
- Keep a torch and sturdy shoes by the bed, because broken glass is common.

## Practice before you need it

1. Walk through the house with the household and identify the safest spot in each room.
2. Practise drop, cover and hold on until each person can do it without thinking.
3. Show every adult where the gas and water shut-off valves are, and how to turn them.
4. Repeat the drill twice a year, using a timer to keep the practice realistic.

## Aftershock planning

Aftershocks can be strong enough to cause new damage, and they often arrive within hours or days of the main event. Treat every aftershock as a fresh drop, cover and hold on moment. Keep shoes near the bed, and do not return to a damaged room to fetch belongings. If the house has been cracked or shifted, sleep outside or in the most solid room you can reach, and keep the torch and radio nearby. Check the radio for updated guidance before deciding to return indoors, because conditions can change within a single day.

## Common mistakes

- Running to the stairwell or the doorway during shaking.
- Using a lift after the event, when power may be unstable.
- Striking a match or switching on electrical equipment near a suspected gas leak.
- Assuming the building is safe because the shaking has stopped.

## Checklist

- Drop, cover and hold on has been practised.
- Heavy furniture is fixed to walls.
- A torch and sturdy shoes are kept by the bed.
- The gas supply shut-off is known to everyone.

## When it fails

If someone is trapped, do not move them unless they are in immediate danger. Call for help, tap on a pipe or wall so rescuers can find you, and keep the voice and the noise going. If the building is damaged, leave it and wait outside.

**Warning:** do not use lifts, and do not light a match after an earthquake until you are sure there is no gas leak.
