# Heat Illness and Hypothermia

Both extremes of temperature can kill. The signs are easy to miss at first, so check people often. Heat illness and hypothermia are preventable with simple habits: water, shade, dry layers and shelter.

## Why it matters

The body is designed to hold a narrow temperature range, and both heat and cold push it past what it can correct on its own. Confusion is often the first sign of trouble, and the person experiencing it may not realise they are unwell. Early action is what separates a headache and a cool-down from an emergency. Because the two conditions develop in opposite directions, the same person can be at risk of either depending on the weather, activity and clothing.

## Heat exhaustion

- Heavy sweating, pale skin, headache, nausea, cramps.
- Treatment: move to shade, rest, sip water, loosen clothing, cool the skin with damp cloths.

## Heatstroke

- Confusion, hot dry or damp skin, very high temperature, collapse or seizure.
- Treatment: cool the person quickly with water and fanning, and call for help. This is an emergency.

## Hypothermia

- Shivering that stops, slurred speech, clumsiness, drowsiness, confusion.
- Treatment: move to shelter, remove wet clothing, wrap in dry layers and blankets, warm the centre of the body first. Give warm sweet drinks if the person is awake.

![Warming: step-1, step-2, step-3, step-box with arrow-straight, cup, person-sitting; labels: dry layers, blanket, warm drink, heat to chest and trunk; the body centre warms before the limbs. NEEDS: blanket](assets/heat-illness-and-hypothermia-3.svg)

## Step-by-step in detail

For heat illness, move the person to the coolest available space and remove outer layers. Apply cool, wet cloths to the neck, armpits and groin, where large blood vessels lie close to the skin, and keep them wet by re-wetting as they warm. Fan the body while the cloths are in place. For hypothermia, handle the person gently, move them out of wind and wet, and place dry layers over the chest and trunk first. Keep the head covered and avoid rapid heat on the skin, which can cause harm.

## Adjusting for conditions

- **Children and older adults:** they regulate temperature less efficiently, so act at the first sign of change.
- **People with heart or kidney conditions:** heat can strain them quickly, so seek care earlier.
- **People taking diuretics or other regular medicines:** heat and fluid balance can shift, so mention these to responders.
- **Wet or windy weather:** wet clothing can cause hypothermia even when the air feels mild.

## Materials

- Water and a clean cup.
- Shade, a tarpaulin or a cool room.
- Dry blankets, spare clothes and a waterproof layer.
- A thermometer if one is available, and a watch for timing.

## Common mistakes

- Waiting until the person is confused before acting.
- Cooling heatstroke too slowly, or stopping too early.
- Letting a cold person sit in wet clothes in the wind.
- Assuming shivering means the person is fine.
- Giving large volumes of water quickly to someone who is confused.

## Troubleshooting

If the person does not improve after moving to shade and sipping water, treat it as heatstroke until proven otherwise. If a cold person stops shivering but is still conscious, handle them very gently and seek care, because shivering stopping can be a warning sign. If the cooling cloths dry out quickly, use fresh ones and keep the person in a draught-free space.

## Checklist

- Shade or shelter found.
- Water given in small sips.
- Wet clothes replaced with dry ones.
- Centre of the body warmed first.
- Person watched for changes in alertness.

## Practice before you need it

Plan the shelter and water supply before a hot day or a cold storm. Keep a set of dry layers in a bag by the door during winter, and agree on a cool room in the house for heatwaves. Teach household members the early signs, so they can speak up for themselves.

## When it fails

If a person with heatstroke does not cool or becomes unconscious, call for emergency help and keep cooling. If a person with hypothermia stops shivering, becomes very drowsy or loses consciousness, handle them gently and seek urgent medical care.

## Prevent

- Drink regularly in heat, and wear light, loose clothing.
- In cold, wear layers, keep the head covered, and keep dry.
- Watch children and older people closely.

**Warning:** a person with hypothermia who seems dead may still be alive. Keep warming and checking breathing for longer than you think.
