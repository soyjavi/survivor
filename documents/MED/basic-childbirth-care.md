# Basic Childbirth Care

Most births progress without problems, but a birth outside medical care can go wrong quickly. Get help if you can, and know the signs of trouble before labour starts. Your role is to support the mother, keep the space clean and recognise danger early.

## Why it matters

Birth complications rarely announce themselves with a single clear sign. A labour that stops progressing, heavy bleeding or a baby who does not start breathing can each change the plan within minutes. Helpers who have thought through the steps in advance make fewer rushed decisions. The aim is not to perform a complicated procedure, but to keep the mother and baby stable until trained care arrives.

## Before the birth

- Know the expected date and the signs of labour: regular contractions, a release of fluid and bloody show.
- Keep clean cloths, towels, a clean blanket, scissors and clean string for the cord.
- Clean the room, wash your hands thoroughly and keep the area free of animals and visitors.
- Agree in advance on who will call for help, and how.

## During the birth

1. Support the mother in a position that is comfortable for her, often upright or kneeling.
2. When the head appears, support it gently and do not pull.
3. Check whether the cord is around the baby's neck, and if so, gently ease it over the head.
4. Support the baby as the shoulders and body emerge, guiding the body downward first and then upward.
5. Dry the baby and keep it warm, skin to skin with the mother.

## Step-by-step in detail

Once the baby is out, lay it on the mother's abdomen or chest and dry it firmly with a clean towel. Remove the wet cloth and wrap the baby in a dry one. Watch the chest for regular movement and the colour of the lips and skin. A baby that is pink and crying, or making regular breathing sounds, is doing well. Keep track of the time of birth, because responders will ask.

## Cord and afterbirth

- Tie the cord in two places with clean string, then cut between them with clean scissors.
- Do not pull on the cord. Keep the placenta, and watch for bleeding.
- Put the baby to the breast soon after birth, if the baby is breathing well.

## Adjusting for conditions

- **Breech birth (feet or buttocks first):** this is not a situation to manage alone. Support the body, do not pull, and seek trained help as early as possible.
- **Early or small babies:** keep the baby extra warm, and avoid long periods of cold exposure.
- **Multiple births:** expect a second baby and be ready to repeat the same steps.
- **Mothers with existing illness:** heart, blood pressure or diabetes problems raise the risk, so flag these to responders.

## Materials

- Clean cloths and towels, plus a dry blanket for the baby.
- Clean string or thread, and scissors or a blade that has been boiled or wiped with clean alcohol.
- Gloves if available, a bucket of clean water and soap.
- Sanitary pads for the mother and a clean bag for used materials.

## Common mistakes

- Pulling the baby or the cord to speed up the birth.
- Cutting the cord too early before the baby is breathing well.
- Letting the mother lie flat for hours, which makes bleeding harder to see.
- Forgetting to keep the baby warm after the first minutes.
- Leaving the placenta unrecorded, so responders cannot check whether all of it has come out.

## Troubleshooting

If the placenta has not come out after a long wait, or the mother is bleeding and feels faint, lay her down and seek urgent care. If the baby looks blue but is breathing and crying, keep them warm and watch closely. If the colour does not improve or breathing becomes irregular, check breathing again immediately.

## Checklist

- Clean hands, clean surface and clean tools.
- Baby dried, warm and breathing.
- Cord tied and cut.
- Placenta kept, bleeding watched.
- Mother resting and drinking.
- Time of birth recorded.

## Practice before you need it

Rehearse with a doll or a folded towel: practise tying string, supporting a head and drying a small body. Agree with your helper on the order of tasks and who will call for help, so that nobody is searching for supplies during the birth.

## When it fails

If the baby does not start breathing within about a minute, call for help and rub the back gently. If you have training, begin basic newborn resuscitation. If bleeding does not slow, lay the mother flat with the legs raised and massage the lower belly firmly to help the womb contract.

## Warning signs

Seek urgent help for:

- Heavy bleeding that does not slow.
- No breathing or a blue baby after the first minute.
- Fever, severe headache or seizure in the mother.
- A baby that cannot feed or is not passing urine.

**Warning:** after birth, the mother must rest and drink clean water. Watch for bleeding in the first hours.
