# Skin Infections and Boils

Skin is the body's first barrier, and when bacteria get past it, they can form a painful lump, a spreading area of redness, or a pocket of pus. Most small skin infections stay local and respond to simple care. A boil is a deep infection of a hair follicle, and it can grow large. Your task is to keep the area clean, encourage the body to drain the infection on its own, and recognise the signs that the infection is moving into deeper tissue or the bloodstream.

## Recognising a boil or skin infection

A boil typically begins as a tender, red bump that grows over a few days. It becomes firm and swollen, then softens at the top as a yellow or white head forms. A carbuncle is a cluster of connected boils that is deeper and more serious.

Other common skin infections include:

- Infected cuts or scrapes, with spreading redness and warmth.
- Impetigo, which forms honey-coloured crusts, most often in children.
- Cellulitis, a spreading area of red, hot, swollen skin that feels tender.
- Infected insect bites, which become enlarged and painful.

## Warm compresses

Warm compresses help draw the infection toward the surface and encourage a boil to come to a head and drain naturally.

1. Wash your hands thoroughly before touching the area.
2. Soak a clean cloth in warm water that is comfortable to the back of your hand.
3. Wring the cloth out and hold it against the boil for about ten to fifteen minutes.
4. Repeat several times a day, using a fresh cloth each time.
5. Wash the cloth after use in hot water, and do not share it with anyone else.
6. Dry the area gently and cover it with a clean dressing if it is likely to drain.

**Warning:** Use water that is warm, not hot. Burning the skin will delay healing and add a second injury.

## Do not squeeze or cut

It is tempting to squeeze a boil, but doing so pushes infected material deeper into the skin and into surrounding tissue. It can also spread bacteria to nearby hair follicles and, in rare cases, into the bloodstream.

1. Leave the boil alone unless it has already drained naturally.
2. Do not attempt to lance a boil with a blade or needle unless you are trained to do so.
3. If the boil opens on its own, clean the area gently with clean water and cover it.
4. Replace the dressing whenever it becomes soaked, and wash hands after each change.

**Warning:** Squeezing a boil on the face, especially near the nose, mouth, or eyes, is particularly dangerous because of the veins that drain to the brain. Do not squeeze these areas under any circumstances.

## Keeping the area clean

- Wash the surrounding skin daily with soap and water, rinsing well.
- Keep the area covered with a clean, dry dressing when it drains.
- Do not share towels, razors, or clothing that touched the boil.
- Wash clothing and bedding that has been in contact with pus in hot water.
- Keep the skin dry, because moisture encourages bacteria to multiply.

## Signs that the infection is spreading

Watch for changes over the following days. Spreading infection is the reason professional assessment matters.

- Redness that extends beyond the original area, or red streaks running away from the boil.
- Warmth, swelling, and tenderness that increase instead of settling.
- Fever, chills, or feeling generally unwell.
- Pus that increases, changes colour, or smells bad.
- A boil on the face, in the armpit, groin, or along the spine.
- Multiple boils appearing at the same time, or new boils forming around the first.
- Lymph nodes near the infection becoming swollen and painful.

**Warning:** Red streaks, fever, or a boil that becomes hard and very painful may indicate that the infection is spreading. Seek professional care without delay, because these signs can progress quickly.

## Recognising a severe skin infection

Some infections need urgent assessment even without these signs:

1. A large area of skin that is rapidly becoming red, hot, and swollen.
2. Skin that turns dusky, purple, or dark, or develops blisters.
3. Severe pain out of proportion to the visible appearance.
4. A person with diabetes, a weakened immune system, or poor circulation who develops a new skin infection.
5. A wound near the eye or spreading over a joint.

## Preventing boils and skin infections

- Keep cuts and scratches clean, and cover them until they heal.
- Wash hands before and after treating any skin problem.
- Avoid squeezing pimples and spots, which can lead to boils.
- Keep clothing and bedding clean, particularly in hot or humid conditions.
- Address sweating and friction in skin folds, which can encourage infection.
