# Temporary Dental Care When No Dentist Is Near

Dental problems rarely kill, but they hurt badly, stop people eating, and can spread infection into the face, jaw, or bloodstream. Without a dentist, the goal is comfort, protection of the damaged tooth, and early recognition of a problem that has become dangerous. A tooth can be managed for a while in the field. It cannot be fixed there.

## Pain control without harming the mouth

1. Start with an approved over-the-counter pain reliever, following the label for the person's age and body weight.
2. Apply a cold pack to the outside of the cheek for short periods, with a cloth between the pack and the skin.
3. Rinse the mouth gently with warm water to clear food and debris from around the tooth.
4. Keep the head slightly raised when lying down, which can ease throbbing pain.
5. Avoid very hot, very cold, very sweet, or very acidic food and drink while the tooth is sensitive.

**Warning:** Never place aspirin or any other tablet directly against the gum or tooth. Doing so causes a painful chemical burn of the soft tissue and does not relieve the pain any better than swallowing it.

## Covering a sharp or broken tooth

A tooth edge that cuts the tongue or cheek can be covered with dental wax, which is sold in dental kits and is a soft, inert material that sticks to a dry tooth.

1. Dry the area with a clean cloth or gauze.
2. Pinch a small piece of dental wax and roll it between your fingers until soft.
3. Press it over the sharp edge and hold it briefly until it stays in place.
4. Check that it does not cover the bite surface in a way that causes the jaw to lock or the bite to shift.
5. Replace the wax after eating or drinking, since it will wash off.

## Lost fillings and crowns

If a filling or crown has come out, the tooth may be sensitive and may trap food. A commercial temporary dental filling material from a first-aid or dental kit can be pressed into the cavity as a short-term barrier.

1. Clean the cavity gently with warm water and remove any loose debris.
2. Dry the tooth as much as possible.
3. Press the temporary material into the cavity and smooth it so it does not catch the tongue.
4. Avoid chewing on that side.
5. If the crown has come off whole, keep it in a clean container and bring it to the dentist. Do not glue it back with household adhesives.

## Recognising a dental emergency

These signs mean the problem is more than a broken tooth and needs professional care as soon as it can be reached:

- Swelling of the face, jaw, or neck, especially if it is growing.
- Fever together with tooth pain.
- A bad taste or pus that drains from the gum or near the tooth.
- Pain that does not ease with appropriate medication.
- Difficulty swallowing, opening the mouth, or breathing.
- A tooth knocked out or displaced in a crash or fall.

**Warning:** Swelling that makes swallowing or breathing hard, or that spreads toward the eye or under the jaw, is a serious infection. Seek professional care immediately and do not wait for the pain to settle.

## A knocked-out tooth

1. Pick up the tooth by the crown, not the root.
2. Rinse it gently with clean water if it is dirty, without scrubbing.
3. If possible, place it back in the socket and hold it with a clean cloth while the person bites down gently.
4. If it cannot be replaced, keep it moist in milk or in the person's own saliva.
5. Get to a dentist as quickly as possible, because the time window is short.

## Preventing problems

- Brush with a soft brush and small amount of fluoride toothpaste if available.
- Clean between teeth with floss or a small brush when food becomes trapped.
- Reduce sugary food and drink, which feed the bacteria that cause decay.
- Keep a small dental repair kit with dental wax, temporary filling material, and a mirror.
