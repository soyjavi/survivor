# Field Dressings and Pressure Bandages

A dressing does three jobs: it absorbs blood and fluid, it keeps dirt and bacteria out, and in some cases it holds pressure on a bleeding vessel. Choosing the right type matters as much as applying it well. A dressing that is too small, too loose, or too tight can fail at the moment it is needed most.

## Types of dressing

1. **Absorbent pads or gauze squares** cover the wound and soak up fluid. They are the basic layer for most injuries.
2. **Roller gauze** holds pads in place and can add firm pressure when wound around a limb.
3. **Elastic or crepe bandages** provide a stretchy wrap that can be tightened and re-applied.
4. **Hemostatic gauze**, which is impregnated with a clotting agent, is used for deep bleeding where direct pressure alone is not controlling blood loss.
5. **Non-stick dressings** lie flat against the wound and do not stick when changed, which protects healing tissue.
6. **Occlusive dressings** are airtight and used for certain chest wounds. They must be vented or removed at the right time, and this requires training.
7. **Adhesive strips and tape** keep dressings in place and close small edges.

Keep at least one large pressure dressing and several smaller pads in any kit. A pressure dressing is simply a larger pad with a bandage wrapped over it.

## Applying firm pressure

1. Put on gloves if available. Expose the wound enough to see where the bleeding is coming from.
2. Place the thickest pad you have directly on the wound and press with your hand or fist.
3. Keep pressing for several minutes without lifting to check. Lifting breaks the clot that is forming.
4. Wrap roller gauze firmly over the pad, starting below the wound and moving upward on a limb.
5. Keep the wrap snug enough that it resists pressure, but not so tight that it cuts off circulation.
6. Tie or fasten the bandage so it does not unravel.
7. Check the fingers or toes beyond the bandage. They should be warm, pink or normal in colour, and able to move or feel touch.

**Warning:** A bandage that makes fingers or toes pale, blue, cold, numb, or tingling is too tight. Loosen it at once and re-wrap, or the limb can be damaged.

## Adding layers instead of removing them

When blood soaks through a dressing, do not peel it off. Removing it tears the clot that is forming underneath.

1. Place a new pad on top of the soaked one.
2. Add more roller gauze over the top and maintain pressure.
3. Keep the original pad in place as the base layer.
4. If bleeding continues through several layers, tighten the pressure at the wound or apply pressure at a point above it as directed by your training.

## Changing a dressing

Dressings should be changed when they are soaked, dirty, loose, or when the wound needs inspection. For most wounds, changing once a day is a reasonable default if the dressing stays clean and dry.

1. Gather clean gloves, a clean pad, and tape or bandage.
2. Look at the dressing from the outside first. If it is dry and not soiled, you may leave it.
3. If you must remove it, soak it loose with clean water if it is stuck. Do not tear it off.
4. Look at the wound. Note the colour, smell, and any discharge.
5. Clean around the wound before cleaning the wound itself, moving from the cleanest part outward.
6. Apply a fresh non-stick pad and secure it without pressing too hard on healing tissue.

## What to note at every change

- Whether the bleeding has stopped, slowed, or continued.
- Whether the wound is larger, redder, hotter, or more painful than before.
- The colour, amount, and smell of any discharge.
- Whether the person has developed fever, chills, or feels worse overall.

Record the time of each change. This helps whoever takes over care, and it gives you a baseline if the wound begins to change.

## Materials to avoid

- Cotton wool placed directly on an open wound, because fibres stick to the surface.
- Adhesive plasters too large to remove without damage to new skin.
- Dressings that have been used on another person, even when they look clean.
- Any dressing you cannot confirm is sterile or clean when you need it to be.
