# Eye Injuries

The eye is delicate and easily damaged. Most injuries need careful cleaning and protection, and some need urgent care. The first rule is always the same: do not rub, and do not press.

## Why it matters

The surface of the eye, the cornea, scratches easily and heals slowly, and the inside of the eye can be damaged by a blow even when the outside looks normal. Rubbing drives grit deeper and can turn a small scratch into a painful wound. Good early care protects vision, and it also reduces the chance of infection. Many eye injuries look minor, which is why any change in vision deserves a professional check.

## Foreign body (dust, grit)

1. Do not rub the eye.
2. Rinse with clean water or saline, pouring gently from the inner corner outward.
3. If the object is visible on the eyelid, lift the eyelid and remove it with a clean, damp cloth.
4. If it is stuck in the eye itself, cover the eye with a clean pad and seek help.

![Eyelid: eye outline, leader to debris, arrow-curved upper lid turn, do-not on rubbing; labels: lid lifted, debris; search with the lid lifted, not by rubbing. NEEDS: eye](assets/eye-injuries-2.svg)

## Step-by-step in detail

Sit the person facing a light source, with their head tilted slightly back and to the side of the affected eye. Ask them to look up and down slowly while you check. If the grit is floating, a gentle pour of water may carry it out. If the grit lies under the upper lid, ask them to look down, then gently draw the lid over the lower lashes so the particle can be seen and lifted away from the surface. Stop if the person cannot keep their eye still or the object is embedded.

## Chemical splash

- Rinse immediately and continuously with clean water for at least 15 to 20 minutes.
- Hold the eye open and rinse from the inner corner outward.
- Seek urgent medical care after rinsing.

## Blow to the eye

- Apply a cold pack wrapped in cloth for short periods.
- Do not press on the eye.
- Seek care if there is pain, blurred vision, bleeding or a change in the shape of the eye.

## Impaled or sharp object

Do not remove the object. Cover the eye and the object with a shield or cup, and seek urgent help.

## Adjusting for conditions

- **Children:** they may panic and rub, so hold the head steady and keep the explanation simple.
- **Contact lens wearers:** remove lenses only if it is easy and safe to do so, and never reinsert a lens after a chemical splash.
- **People with existing eye disease:** they may have less tolerance for pressure or light, so seek care sooner.

## Materials

- Clean water or sterile saline, and a clean bottle or cup to pour.
- Clean cloths or non-fluffy pads.
- A rigid cup or shield for protecting an impaled object.
- Gloves to protect both helper and patient.

## Common mistakes

- Rubbing the eye and scratching the surface.
- Using tap water that may be contaminated when clean water is available.
- Removing an object that is stuck in the eye.
- Covering the eye with a tight bandage that presses on the globe.
- Judging the injury as minor because the person can still see.

## Troubleshooting

If the eye keeps watering after the grit is removed, rinse gently once more and check the eyelid again. If light hurts badly, cover the eye with a loose pad and keep the person in a dim room. If the pupil looks unusual or the person sees flashes or shadows, seek urgent care.

## Checklist

- Hands washed before touching the face.
- Eye rinsed or covered as needed.
- No pressure applied to the eye.
- Vision changes noted.
- Medical review arranged if pain or vision loss continues.

## Practice before you need it

Keep a small clean eyewash bottle in the kit and check that it is sealed. Practise holding a lid open gently on a family member's arm, so that the technique is familiar before an injury happens.

## When it fails

If the eye stays painful, blurred, light-sensitive or bleeding after rinsing, seek care without delay. If the object cannot be removed easily, leave it in place and cover the eye. If the person loses vision, treat it as an emergency.

**Warning:** any eye injury with loss of vision, severe pain, or a chemical splash needs professional care as soon as possible.
