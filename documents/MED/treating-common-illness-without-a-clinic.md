# Treating Common Illness Without a Clinic

Most common infections, including the viruses that cause colds, flu-like illness, and many sore throats, run their course in about a week without treatment. Your role is to keep the person comfortable, keep them drinking, and watch carefully for the few signs that mean the illness has become something else. Most of the work is attention, not medicine, and the carer's steady observation is the main treatment on offer.

## Flu-like illness

Flu-like illness usually comes on quickly, with fever, body aches, headache, tiredness, and a cough. The person may feel very unwell for a few days.

1. Keep the person in bed or resting, and reduce demands on their time.
2. Offer fluids regularly in small amounts, even if they are not hungry.
3. Use an approved fever and pain reliever following the label, to reduce discomfort rather than to cure the illness.
4. Keep the room cool and ventilated, and use light bedding so the person can adjust to changing temperature.
5. Check temperature at least twice a day and write down the readings.
6. Offer simple, soft food when the person is ready to eat.

**Warning:** Do not give aspirin to children or teenagers with a viral illness, because it is linked with a serious condition affecting the liver and brain. Use a different approved reliever for them.

## Sore throat

A sore throat often accompanies a cold or flu. Most are viral and improve without antibiotics.

1. Drink warm fluids such as broth, herbal drinks, or plain water in sips.
2. Use a warm salt-water gargle, mixing salt into warm water and spitting it out afterward.
3. Suck on hard sweets or lozenges if the person is old enough to do so safely.
4. Keep the air humid if possible, by standing in a steamy room or near a pot of hot water.
5. Use approved pain relief as needed for comfort.

**Warning:** Do not give lozenges or hard sweets to very young children, because they can choke on them.

## When a sore throat needs assessment

Seek professional care if the sore throat comes with any of these:

- Difficulty swallowing saliva, drooling, or a muffled voice.
- A swelling on one side of the throat or neck that keeps growing.
- A rash, or a fever that stays high for more than a couple of days.
- Ear pain, a stiff neck, or pain when opening the mouth.
- A person who cannot keep fluids down.

## General home care for a sick person

1. Give the person a separate cup and utensils and wash them carefully.
2. Keep a notebook with temperature, fluid intake, urine output, and symptoms.
3. Encourage rest, but get the person up to walk around a little each day to prevent weakness.
4. Keep the sickroom clean, with frequent handwashing for anyone who enters.
5. Change bedding and clothing when they become soaked with sweat.

## Red flags that require urgent help

These signs override any plan to manage the illness at home:

- Difficulty breathing, rapid breathing, or the person cannot finish a sentence without stopping.
- Chest pain, or blue or grey lips or face.
- Confusion, unusual drowsiness, or difficulty waking the person.
- A stiff neck together with a headache or a sensitivity to light.
- A rash that does not fade when pressed with a glass.
- Signs of severe dehydration, such as very little urine for many hours, a dry mouth, or sunken eyes.
- Fever that is very high, or that returns after a period of improvement.
- Symptoms that continue to worsen after several days rather than improving.

**Warning:** A person who is getting worse, even slowly, should be assessed. Do not wait for a fixed number of days when a red flag appears.

## Knowing when home care is working

Home care is working when the person is drinking, passing urine regularly, able to sleep, and gradually feeling more like themselves over several days. Fever should ease and energy should slowly return. If the person is sicker on the fifth day than on the second, that is a sign to seek help, regardless of how the illness began.
