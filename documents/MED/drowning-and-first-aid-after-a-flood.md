# Drowning and First Aid After a Flood

Floods cause drowning, cuts, hypothermia and infections. A drowned person may need breathing support before anything else. Rescue is also dangerous, so the first decision is always about the rescuer's safety.

## Why it matters

Floodwater hides debris, holes and currents, and it moves faster than most people expect. Many people are hurt while trying to help someone else, so a safe rescue is the first form of first aid. Once a person is out of the water, the most urgent problems are breathing, warmth and bleeding. Injuries from floods often appear hours or days later as infection, so follow-up matters as much as the first response.

## Rescue

- Reach, throw or extend an object before you enter the water.
- Do not enter fast or deep water yourself. Most rescuers are injured trying to save someone.
- Once out, keep the person lying on their side if they are breathing.

## Step-by-step in detail

Stand on firm, high ground with a stable footing and a clear view of the person. Call out to them so they know help is present. Extend a pole, branch or rope so they can grip it, and pull them toward the bank slowly while keeping your own weight back. If the person is unconscious when they reach the bank, lift them out only if this can be done without risk to you, and move the group away from the water before starting the breathing checks.

## Check breathing

1. Check if the person is breathing normally.
2. If not breathing normally, start CPR. See Basic CPR.
3. Keep them warm, because cold water lowers body temperature quickly.

![Recovery: recovery-position figure; labels: on side, head tilted back, top leg bent; keeps the airway clear.](assets/drowning-and-first-aid-after-a-flood-3.svg)

## Adjusting for conditions

- **Children:** they can drown in shallow water and can deteriorate quickly, so keep close watch for hours after the incident.
- **Cold water:** cold water can cause a gasp reflex and rapid heart changes, so keep the person still and warm.
- **Contaminated water:** wash any cuts with clean water as soon as possible, and keep wounds covered.

## Injuries to expect

- Cuts and puncture wounds from debris.
- Hypothermia from cold water.
- Infections from contaminated water.
- Crush injuries from fallen objects or vehicles.

Treat each one as described in the other first aid guides, and watch for infection afterwards.

## Materials

- A throw bag, rope or pole for reaching the person.
- A flotation device if one is available, which should be used by the rescuer, not handed on.
- Dry blankets and clothing once the person is out.

## Common mistakes

- Entering the water without a flotation aid.
- Trying to carry a heavy person across a flooded road.
- Putting a drowned person on their back in a way that blocks the airway.
- Assuming a person who seems fine does not need checking.
- Delaying a medical check because the person was pulled out quickly.

## Troubleshooting

If the person coughs up water and breathes normally, keep them on their side, warm and watched. If they vomit, turn them to the side to clear the airway. If they become drowsy or their breathing changes later, seek urgent care even if they seemed fine at first.

## Checklist

- Rescue from a safe position.
- Breathing checked once the person is out.
- CPR started if breathing is not normal.
- Warm dry clothing applied.
- Cuts and infections watched over the following days.

## Practice before you need it

Know which throw bag or pole is nearest to your home and check that it is in working order. Discuss with your household that nobody goes into floodwater to help, and rehearse calling for emergency help and shouting instructions to a rescuer on the bank.

## When it fails

If you cannot reach the person safely, call the emergency services and do not enter the water. If the person is not breathing and you cannot start CPR, keep the scene calm and wait for trained help. Do not try to drain water from the lungs by pressing on the stomach.

**Warning:** a person who was in floodwater must be seen by medical care if they swallowed water and have trouble breathing, even if they seem fine.
