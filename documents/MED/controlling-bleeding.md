# Controlling Bleeding

Severe bleeding can kill within minutes. Stop it first, then deal with everything else. Direct pressure is the first and most effective tool, and most bleeding stops with it if it is applied firmly and kept in place.

## Why it matters

A person can lose a dangerous amount of blood before the wound looks alarming, especially from a limb or a scalp. Blood loss also reduces the blood supply to the brain and organs, which leads to shock. Stopping bleeding early keeps the person conscious, warm and able to cooperate with the rest of the care they need. Most people who bleed to death from a wound would have survived with pressure applied in the first few minutes.

## Steps

1. **Protect yourself.** Wear gloves if you have them.
2. **Apply direct pressure.** Press a clean pad or cloth firmly on the wound with both hands. Keep pressing.
3. **Add more layers** on top of a soaked pad. Do not remove the first one, because that can dislodge a clot.
4. **Raise the limb** above the heart if it is safe to do so.
5. **Bandage firmly** over the pad to hold it in place.

## Step-by-step in detail

![Cross-section: bleeding icon deep cut, bandage-roll gauze layers, leader to fingertip; labels: gauze layers, fingertip; packing fills the full depth before the outer pad. NEEDS: gauze-pad](assets/controlling-bleeding-2.svg)

Kneel beside the injured person so that your arms are straight and your body weight presses down through your hands. Press into the wound rather than just resting on it. If the bleeding slows, keep the pressure on for several minutes before checking, because a clot needs time to form. When you add layers, bind them with a bandage that is firm enough to hold but does not cut off the blood supply past the wound. Check the fingers or toes beyond the bandage, and loosen it if they turn pale or cold.

## When direct pressure fails

- Pack the wound with gauze if it is deep, pressing firmly into it.
- Apply a tourniquet on a limb with severe bleeding that does not stop. Place it two to three inches above the wound, not on a joint, and tighten it until the bleeding stops.
- Write the time on the tourniquet, and do not loosen it.

## Shock

Bleeding can cause shock. Lay the person down, keep them warm, and raise the legs if there is no injury to the legs or hips. Do not give food or drink.

## Adjusting for conditions

- **Scalp and face wounds:** these bleed heavily but rarely need a tourniquet. Use firm pressure and a pad, and watch for signs of head injury.
- **Embedded objects:** do not remove an object from a wound. Pad around it and press on either side, and seek urgent care.
- **Children:** they lose blood faster relative to their size, so act earlier and call for help sooner.
- **People on blood thinners:** bleeding can be harder to stop, so expect to maintain pressure for longer.

## Materials

- Clean pads, cloths or sanitary towels.
- Gauze and roller bandages.
- A commercial tourniquet if you have one, or a wide strip of cloth with a stick to twist it.
- Gloves and a plastic bag for used materials.

## Common mistakes

- Lifting the pad every few seconds to check the wound.
- Placing a tourniquet on a joint or too low on the limb.
- Using a thin strap that cuts the skin without stopping blood flow.
- Forgetting to keep the person warm while bleeding.
- Stopping pressure as soon as the bleeding looks slower, before a clot has formed.

## Checklist

- Gloves on if possible.
- Pressure applied with both hands.
- Layers added, first pad kept.
- Limb raised and bandage firm.
- Tourniquet time written if used.

## Practice before you need it

Practise packing gauze and binding a pad on a cushion so that the motion is familiar. Know where the commercial tourniquet is kept and check that it is complete and in its packaging. Agree on who will call for help before an emergency happens.

## When it fails

If blood soaks through several layers, keep adding pads and maintain pressure. If bleeding continues from a limb after a tourniquet has been placed, tighten it further and check the placement. If the person becomes pale, cold or confused, lay them flat and call for help.

**Warning:** a tourniquet is for limbs only. Leaving it on too long can cost the limb. Once applied, do not remove it until medical care is available.
