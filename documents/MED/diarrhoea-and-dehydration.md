# Diarrhoea and Dehydration

Diarrhoea and vomiting drain water and salts from the body. In a crisis they are one of the biggest threats, especially for children and older people. Replacing fluid early, in small amounts, prevents most serious cases.

## Why it matters

The body loses far more fluid through diarrhoea and vomiting than most people expect, and the loss continues while the illness lasts. Children and older adults have less reserve, so they become dehydrated faster. Dehydration weakens the heart and kidneys, and it can cause confusion. Most people recover from diarrhoea at home when fluids keep pace with losses, which is why steady replacement matters more than any single drink.

## Signs of dehydration

- Thirst, dry mouth and dark, scarce urine.
- Dizziness when standing.
- Sunken eyes, dry skin that stays up when pinched.
- In children: no tears when crying, sleepiness, or a sunken soft spot on the head.

## Oral rehydration

1. Use oral rehydration salts if you have them, mixed as the packet says.
2. Without salts, mix one litre of clean, boiled water with six level teaspoons of sugar and half a teaspoon of salt.
3. Give small, frequent sips, about a cup after each loose stool or vomit.
4. Keep feeding, even if the person eats only a little. Plain rice and soup are good.

![Timeline: timeline-axis with small cup markers every few minutes, larger cup marker after loose stool; labels: small cup, larger cup after each loss; fluid is paced to losses, not drunk at once.](assets/diarrhoea-and-dehydration-3.svg)

## Step-by-step in detail

Mix the solution in a clean container and label it with the time it was made. Offer it with a spoon for infants and small children, and let them rest between sips. If the person vomits, wait a few minutes and start again with an even smaller amount. Keep a rough tally of fluid in and out, using the cups or spoons you use for giving. Mixed solution does not keep indefinitely, so make a fresh batch each day and discard leftovers.

## Adjusting for conditions

- **Infants:** give small amounts often, and use only the measured recipe without adjusting salt or sugar. Seek care early.
- **Older adults:** they may not feel thirsty, so offer drinks on a schedule rather than waiting for them to ask.
- **Pregnancy:** dehydration can affect both mother and baby, so seek care sooner than you would otherwise.
- **Long-term kidney or heart conditions:** fluid targets may differ, so check with a clinician before increasing intake.

## Materials

- Oral rehydration salts, or clean sugar and salt.
- Clean, boiled and cooled water.
- A clean cup or spoon for giving small amounts.
- A notebook to record how much is drunk and how often the person passes stool or vomits.

## Common mistakes

- Giving large amounts at once, which can trigger more vomiting.
- Mixing too much salt, which can be dangerous for infants.
- Using undiluted fruit juice or sugary drinks as the main fluid.
- Stopping food completely for days.
- Waiting until the person is thirsty before offering a drink.

## Troubleshooting

If the person vomits every sip, slow down, wait ten minutes and offer a smaller amount. If the stools become frequent and watery despite fluid, keep going and check the warning signs. If the mixture tastes too salty or too sweet, make a fresh batch, because an unbalanced solution can make things worse.

## Checklist

- Oral rehydration ready and mixed correctly.
- Small sips offered regularly.
- Urine output checked every few hours.
- Food kept simple and gentle.
- Hands washed after every toilet visit.

## Practice before you need it

Mix a batch of the homemade solution once so that you know the measures and the taste, and keep the ingredients together with the first aid kit. Check that the salts in the kit are in date, and note who in the household has the most trouble keeping fluids down.

## When to seek help

- Blood in the stool, or continuous diarrhoea for more than two days.
- Unable to keep fluid down for several hours.
- Signs of severe dehydration, or confusion.
- A baby or very young child with any of these signs.

## When it fails

If the person cannot keep any fluid down, or the signs of dehydration get worse, seek help. Do not wait until the person is confused. A child who has stopped passing urine for many hours needs care urgently.

## Prevent spread

Wash hands with soap and clean water after the toilet and before food. Clean toilets and surfaces with bleach solution.

**Warning:** do not give anti-diarrhoea medicine to children, and avoid it when there is blood in the stool.
