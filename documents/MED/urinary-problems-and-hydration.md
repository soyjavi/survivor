# Urinary Problems and Hydration

The urinary system filters waste from the blood and keeps the body's fluid balance steady. When it is inflamed or infected, the pain can be intense and the person may become dehydrated quickly, especially with fever or vomiting. Your first task is to keep fluids flowing and ease discomfort. Your second is to recognise when an infection has moved beyond the bladder and into the kidneys, which is a serious condition.

## Common urinary problems

- Burning or stinging when passing urine, often with a frequent urge to go.
- Lower abdominal discomfort or pressure over the bladder.
- Urine that looks cloudy, dark, or has a strong smell.
- Blood in the urine, which can appear pink, red, or brown.
- A feeling that the bladder has not emptied completely.

Problems like these are most often caused by bacteria entering the urinary tract. They are more common in women, but men, older people, and anyone with a catheter or an enlarged prostate may also be affected.

## Hydration first

Fluids are the single most useful measure you can provide. Drinking more water helps flush the urinary tract and prevents dehydration.

1. Offer a glass of water every hour or so while the person is awake, in small sips if drinking is uncomfortable.
2. Accept a wide range of fluids, including water, weak tea, broth, and oral rehydration solution.
3. Avoid strong coffee, alcohol, and very sugary drinks, which can irritate the bladder or worsen dehydration.
4. Keep a simple chart of how often the person passes urine and roughly how much.
5. Aim for urine that is pale yellow. Dark urine suggests more fluid is needed.

## Easing discomfort

1. Use an approved pain reliever following the label, adjusted for the person's age and body weight.
2. Apply a warm cloth or a warm, not hot, pack to the lower abdomen for short periods.
3. Encourage the person to pass urine when the urge arises rather than holding it.
4. Keep the genital area clean and dry, wiping from front to back.
5. Wear loose cotton underwear and change clothing that becomes damp.

**Warning:** Do not use home remedies that claim to dissolve infections or cure the urinary tract. Symptoms may ease temporarily while the infection continues to spread.

## Signs of a kidney infection

An infection that reaches the kidneys is a medical emergency. Look for these signs together or separately:

- Fever, shivering, or sweating, especially with a sense of feeling very unwell.
- Pain in the side or back, below the ribs, often on one side.
- Nausea or vomiting that prevents fluids from being kept down.
- Confusion, unusual drowsiness, or a sense that the person is not themselves.
- Very little urine passed over many hours.

**Warning:** A person with fever, back pain, and vomiting who cannot keep fluids down needs urgent professional care. Kidney infections can become life-threatening within a short time.

## When to seek professional care

Seek assessment promptly if any of these occur:

- Symptoms that have not improved after a day or two of fluids and comfort measures.
- Blood in the urine that does not clear.
- Pain or symptoms in a pregnant person, since urinary infections require prompt treatment during pregnancy.
- A man with urinary symptoms, particularly if there is pain in the groin or testicles.
- A child with urinary symptoms, or with fever and no other clear cause.
- An older person with confusion, a fall, or a sudden change in behaviour.
- Inability to pass any urine for many hours.

## Preventing problems

- Drink enough fluid through the day so urine stays pale.
- Pass urine when the urge comes, rather than holding it for long periods.
- Empty the bladder after sexual activity.
- Wipe from front to back and keep the genital area clean.
- Avoid prolonged dehydration during heat, illness, or long journeys.

## Keeping records

Note the start of symptoms, the amount and colour of urine, any fever readings, and whether the person can keep fluids down. This information is useful to whoever assesses the person, and it helps you judge whether the situation is improving or worsening.
