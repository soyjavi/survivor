# Basic First Aid Kit

A first aid kit only helps if it is complete, clean and easy to find. Check it every six months, and make sure every adult in the household knows where it is and what is inside.

## Why it matters

Most first aid is delayed by searching, not by lack of skill. In a power cut, a flood or a late-night injury, the people who need the kit are often stressed and in the dark. A kit that is packed deliberately, stored in one place and checked on a schedule removes guesswork. It also gives every household member the same starting point, so a helper who is not familiar with the house can still find what they need.

## Contents

- Adhesive dressings in several sizes.
- Sterile gauze pads and roller bandages.
- Triangular bandage for slings.
- Adhesive tape and safety pins.
- Disposable gloves.
- Antiseptic wipes or a bottle of antiseptic solution.
- Scissors and tweezers.
- Thermometer.
- Instant cold packs.
- Oral rehydration salts.
- Any prescription medicines you take, with a few days of spare doses where possible.
- Basic pain relief and anti-diarrhoea tablets, used as the label says.
- A note with the medical history and allergies of each person.

## Why each item matters

Dressings and gauze control bleeding and protect wounds from infection. Triangular bandages hold splints and slings. Gloves protect both the helper and the injured person. Oral rehydration salts prevent dehydration, which is one of the most common threats after a disaster. The written note saves time when a person cannot speak for themselves.

## Step-by-step in detail

1. Choose a container that closes firmly and can be carried in one hand.
2. Pack the bulkiest items first, then the small pieces in labelled pouches.
3. Write the expiry date on the lid or on a card visible from the outside.
4. Place the note with the medical details on top, where it is seen first.
5. Store a copy of the list in a phone photo or a waterproof wallet.

## Adjusting for conditions

- **Children:** add paediatric dressings, a child-sized thermometer and medicines dosed for children, kept clearly separate from adult supplies.
- **Hot or humid climates:** keep the kit in a cool, shaded spot, and check adhesive and medicine packaging more often.
- **Cold climates:** store the kit where it will not freeze, because some liquids and gels lose their effectiveness.
- **Long-term conditions:** add the specific supplies that a household member depends on, such as spare glucose or inhaler spacers.

## Keep it ready

1. Store the kit in one place, dry and out of reach of children.
2. Check expiry dates every six months and replace items that are used or past their date.
3. Keep a second small kit in the bag you take when you evacuate.
4. Replace the kit after any use, even if only one item was taken.

## Know the contents

Practise using the kit. Open the bandages, wrap a sprain and clean a cut before you need them. Knowing the kit in advance means you do not waste time reading packets during an emergency.

## Common mistakes

- Storing the kit in a damp bathroom where packaging degrades.
- Keeping expired medicines because they seem to still work.
- Using the kit for everyday needs and never replacing it.
- Leaving out a copy of the medical notes.
- Keeping the kit in several scattered places, so nobody knows which one is complete.

## Troubleshooting

If the adhesive dressings no longer stick, the packaging may have been exposed to heat or damp, so replace the whole box. If the thermometer reading seems wrong, compare it with a second thermometer and replace it if the readings do not match. If a pouch is torn or stained, empty it and check every item inside.

## Checklist

- Kit found and accessible in under a minute.
- Every item in date and undamaged.
- Prescription medicines listed and refilled.
- Second kit packed for evacuation.
- Last check recorded.

## Practice before you need it

Once a year, time yourself opening the kit and locating the three most common items: dressings, gloves and oral rehydration salts. Repeat the exercise with a family member who did not pack the kit, because they will be the one searching under pressure.

## When it fails

If an item is missing or damaged, replace it the same day. If the kit is wet, dry or replace the contents and check each packet. If a medicine has changed colour or is cloudy, discard it and use the supply you have set aside elsewhere.

**Warning:** keep all medicines in the kit labelled, and never give a child medicine meant for an adult.
