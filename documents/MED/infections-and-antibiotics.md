# Infections and Antibiotics

Infections are a leading cause of death after a disaster, especially from wounds and contaminated water. Antibiotics are powerful, but only when used properly. The best antibiotic is a clean wound and clean water; medicines come second.

## Why it matters

Most infections that become dangerous do so slowly, over days, and the early stages look like ordinary illness. Bacteria that spread into the blood can trigger a body-wide reaction called sepsis, which can cause collapse within hours. Recognising the pattern early matters more than any single treatment choice. Antibiotics also work only against bacteria, not viruses, and using them without a clear reason is a major cause of resistance that makes later infections harder to treat.

## Signs of a serious infection

- Fever with chills and fast breathing.
- Confusion or extreme drowsiness.
- A wound that is spreading redness, swelling and pus.
- Red streaks from a wound.
- Cold, pale or mottled skin.

These signs can mean sepsis, which is life-threatening. Seek help as soon as possible.

## Antibiotics

- Use only antibiotics prescribed for the person, for the condition they have.
- Complete the full course if you have started it, unless a clinician tells you otherwise.
- Do not save antibiotics for later use. Stopping early can leave bacteria that resist treatment.

Never give antibiotics to someone with a known allergy to them. Watch for rash, swelling or breathing trouble after any dose.

## Step-by-step in detail

Before starting any antibiotic, write down the drug name, the dose as printed on the label, the number of days, and the time of the first dose. Give each dose at the same times each day, using a timer or a phone reminder. Keep the packet with the instructions. If a dose is missed, follow the leaflet or ask a pharmacist or clinician rather than doubling up. Note any reaction in the same notebook, including the time it started.

## Keep the wound clean

Clean wounds reduce the need for antibiotics. See Cleaning and Dressing Wounds.

## Adjusting for conditions

- **Children:** doses are often based on weight, so keep the latest weight recorded with the prescription.
- **Older adults and people with kidney or liver disease:** they may clear medicines more slowly, so check dosing with a clinician.
- **Pregnancy:** some antibiotics are not suitable, so seek a clinician's advice before starting any treatment.
- **People with previous reactions:** record every reaction, even mild ones, so future clinicians can plan safely.

## Materials

- The prescription, with the drug name, dose and number of days.
- A clean dressing kit and clean water.
- A thermometer to track fever.
- A notebook to record each dose and any reaction.

## Common mistakes

- Taking half a course because the person feels better.
- Sharing antibiotics between people with different illnesses.
- Using leftover tablets from an earlier illness.
- Ignoring a rash that appears after the first dose.
- Treating a viral illness such as a common cold with antibiotics.

## Troubleshooting

If the person is still getting worse after two or three days of treatment, do not simply increase the dose. Seek a clinician's review. If the tablets are damaged, discoloured or past their date, do not use them. If the person vomits soon after a dose, tell a clinician, because the dose may not have been absorbed.

## Checklist

- Diagnosis or clinical advice obtained where possible.
- Dose and duration written down.
- Allergy history checked.
- Wound cleaned and covered.
- Warning signs watched each day.

## Practice before you need it

Keep a simple card in the first aid kit listing each household member's allergies and any antibiotics they have tolerated. Review the card when someone travels or changes doctor, so that the information stays current.

## When it fails

If the person does not improve after two or three days, or gets worse, seek care. If a reaction such as swelling, wheezing or a widespread rash appears, stop the medicine and treat it as an allergic reaction. If the antibiotic is not available, keep the wound clean and seek a clinic.

## Do not

- Do not use antibiotics meant for animals.
- Do not use leftover antibiotics from a previous illness.
- Do not stop treatment when the person feels better, unless the prescription is finished.

**Warning:** never give antibiotics to someone with a known allergy to them. Watch for rash, swelling or breathing trouble.
