# Foot Care and Blisters on Long Walks

On a long walk, the feet take the full weight of the body for hours each day. Friction, moisture, and pressure combine to form blisters, and a blister that is ignored can become a serious infection that stops a journey. Good foot care is mostly prevention: the right socks, the right footwear, and the habit of stopping to check before a small problem grows.

## Preventing blisters

1. Choose boots or shoes that fit well, with room for the toes to move and no pressure points.
2. Break in new footwear gradually before a long trip, using short walks first.
3. Wear socks made of wool or a synthetic blend that wicks moisture away from the skin.
4. Carry a spare pair of dry socks and change them when the first pair becomes damp.
5. Keep toenails trimmed short and straight to avoid pressure on the toes.
6. Apply a protective tape or friction-reducing product to known hot spots before walking.
7. Stop at the first sign of a hot spot, remove the boot, and address it immediately.

**Warning:** A hot spot that you walk through becomes a blister within minutes. Stop, check, and cover the area as soon as you feel it.

## Treating a blister

A blister is a fluid-filled pocket that forms when the skin separates under friction. Its roof protects the healing skin underneath, so the roof should stay in place when possible.

1. Wash your hands, then clean the blister and the surrounding skin with clean water.
2. If the blister is small and the roof is intact, cover it with a blister dressing or a padded adhesive that cushions it.
3. Leave the roof on. It is a natural barrier against bacteria.
4. If the blister is large, tense, or likely to burst under pressure, consider a sterile approach only if you are trained to do so.
5. Change the dressing daily, or sooner if it becomes wet or dirty.
6. Keep the area dry and let the boots air out at every rest stop.

**Warning:** Do not pop a blister with a non-sterile needle or pin. Opening the skin invites infection, and a deflated blister can be painful for days.

## Caring for a burst blister

If a blister has burst on its own, the roof may be torn and the skin beneath is raw.

1. Clean the area gently with clean water.
2. Trim away any loose flaps of skin only if they are dirty or ragged, using clean scissors.
3. Cover the area with a non-stick dressing and secure it with tape or padding.
4. Change the dressing daily and watch for signs of infection.

## Recognising an infected foot

These signs suggest that an infection has taken hold and needs professional care:

- Redness that spreads beyond the blister, or red streaks running up the foot or leg.
- Increasing swelling, heat, and tenderness around the area.
- Pus that is yellow, green, or thick, or a bad smell.
- Fever, chills, or feeling generally unwell.
- Pain that increases rather than easing over a day or two.
- Numbness or a change in colour of the toes, especially pale, blue, or black skin.

**Warning:** A foot that turns dark, cold, numb, or extremely painful may have lost its blood supply. This is an emergency, and professional care must be sought without delay.

## Foot care during a long walk

1. Stop every one to two hours to rest and inspect the feet.
2. Remove the boots and socks, and let the feet air out for a short time.
3. Check for red spots, hot spots, and any blister forming between the toes or on the heels.
4. Dry the feet thoroughly, including between the toes.
5. Put on dry socks before continuing.
6. Massage the feet gently to improve circulation, avoiding any broken skin.

## Long-term care

- Wash feet daily and dry them fully, paying attention to the skin between the toes.
- Keep an inventory of dressings and tape in the pack, and replace items that are used.
- Keep a foot problem list in your notebook, including the date, location, and treatment given.
- Replace worn boots before they break down, because worn footwear is a frequent cause of injuries.
