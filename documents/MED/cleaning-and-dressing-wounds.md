# Cleaning and Dressing Wounds

Most wounds heal well if they are cleaned properly and kept covered. Infection is the main risk, and it is far easier to prevent than to treat. A clean wound, a dry dressing and a watchful eye solve most problems.

## Why it matters

Bacteria and grit trapped in a wound are the main reasons ordinary cuts become infected. Once bacteria multiply in damp tissue, the body must work much harder to heal, and treatment becomes more involved. A few minutes of careful cleaning at the start prevents most of this, and it costs almost nothing. The steps below apply to shallow, clean-edged wounds; deeper injuries are covered in a later section.

## Clean the wound

1. Wash your hands, and wear gloves if available.
2. Stop the bleeding first. See Controlling Bleeding.
3. Rinse the wound with clean water. Boiled, cooled water is best.
4. Remove dirt and debris with clean tweezers or a clean cloth. Scrub gently around the edge.
5. Do not use strong antiseptics deep inside the wound, because they damage healing tissue.

![Irrigation: bleeding icon as cut, water-drop and arrow-straight flowing outward, bowl as catch dish; labels: clean water, catch dish; the flow carries dirt away rather than deeper.](assets/cleaning-and-dressing-wounds-2.svg)

## Step-by-step in detail

Hold the wound under a steady flow of clean water or pour water over it from a clean container, so that loose dirt washes away. Clean the surrounding skin from the wound outward, using a fresh cloth stroke each time, so you do not drag dirt back into the cut. Pat the area dry with a clean cloth, avoiding the open surface. Then apply a thin layer of antiseptic only to the skin around the wound, unless the label directs otherwise.

## Dress the wound

- Cover it with a sterile pad and secure it with a bandage.
- Change the dressing every day, or sooner if it gets wet or dirty.
- Keep the wound clean and dry between changes.

![Stack: four step-box layers with bracket; labels: non-stick pad, gauze, rolled bandage, tape; the pad touches the wound and the outer layers only hold it. NEEDS: gauze-pad](assets/cleaning-and-dressing-wounds-4.svg)

## Adjusting for conditions

- **Children:** they often resist cleaning, so explain each step and let them hold the cloth.
- **Older adults and people with diabetes:** wounds heal more slowly, so check them more often and seek care sooner.
- **Hot and humid settings:** change dressings more often, because damp covers encourage bacteria.
- **Animal bites:** these carry a higher infection risk, so cleaning is only the first step and medical review is needed.

## Watch for infection

Infection usually appears within one to three days. Look for:

- Increasing redness, swelling or pain.
- Pus or cloudy discharge.
- Red streaks spreading from the wound.
- Fever or feeling unwell.

## Materials

- Clean water, ideally boiled and cooled.
- Sterile or clean gauze pads and a roller bandage.
- Tweezers, cleaned with boiling water or alcohol.
- Gloves and a clean bowl for used dressings.

## Deep or dirty wounds

- A wound that is deep, gaping, from an animal bite, or contaminated with soil needs medical care.
- Puncture wounds, especially from rusty or dirty objects, carry a high risk of infection.
- Wounds on the face, hands or over a joint often need professional closure.

## Common mistakes

- Scrubbing so hard that the wound bleeds again.
- Using cotton wool that leaves fibres behind.
- Leaving a wet dressing in place for days.
- Putting cream on a wound that is still bleeding.
- Checking the wound every few minutes, which disturbs the healing surface.

## Troubleshooting

If the dressing sticks, soak it with clean water before lifting it so the wound is not torn. If the wound oozes clear fluid for a short time, that can be normal, but thick or coloured discharge is not. If the edges will not stay together, do not force them, and seek care.

## Checklist

- Hands washed and gloves on.
- Bleeding under control.
- Wound rinsed and debris removed.
- Dressing clean, dry and secure.
- Infection signs checked each day.

## Practice before you need it

Keep a small clean basin, gauze and a roller bandage together in one place, and practise cleaning a scrape on a family member's forearm. This makes the process routine, which matters when a cut happens during a stressful moment.

## When it fails

If the wound reopens, keeps bleeding through the dressing or develops pus and red streaks, seek care. If clean water is not available, use the cleanest water you have and cover the wound as soon as possible. A wound that cannot be cleaned properly should be left covered rather than rinsed with dirty water.

**Warning:** do not close a dirty wound with tape or glue. It traps bacteria inside.
