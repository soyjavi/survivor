# Fever

A fever is the body's response to infection. It is usually not dangerous on its own, but it needs watching. The aim of treatment is comfort and hydration, not necessarily bringing the temperature all the way down.

## Why it matters

Fever itself is a sign that the immune system is working, and high readings alone do not show how sick a person is. What matters more is how the person behaves: whether they drink, wake easily, pass urine and respond to you. Families who track the reading and the behaviour together make better decisions about when to act. Overreacting to a number can lead to unnecessary medicine, while ignoring a child who is listless can delay needed care.

## Measure it

- A fever is a temperature of about 38 °C or above.
- Measure under the arm or in the mouth, and wait a few minutes between readings.
- Record the time and the reading.

![Underarm: thermometer, person-sitting, arrow-straight to armpit, leader; labels: under arm, arm against chest; the arm stays closed so the reading holds steady. NEEDS: arm-clamp](assets/fever-2.svg)

## Reduce it

- Rest, and drink plenty of clean water.
- Keep the person lightly dressed. Do not wrap them up in blankets.
- Use a cool, damp cloth on the forehead if they are uncomfortable.
- Paracetamol or ibuprofen can help, following the dose on the label for the person's age and weight.

## Step-by-step in detail

Before giving any medicine, write down the time, the reading and the name of the product. Check the label for the dose by age or weight, and confirm that the same active ingredient is not already in another product. Wait for the interval stated on the label before giving another dose.

![Chart: table-header, table-grid rows, pills icon, cup; labels: time, reading, medicine given, fluids taken; each entry is written before the next dose.](assets/fever-3.svg)

Offer water between doses, in small sips if the person is reluctant. Keep the room comfortable, not hot, and let the person set their own covering.

## Adjusting for conditions

- **Babies under three months:** any fever is a reason to seek medical care, so do not wait for other symptoms.
- **Young children:** they can become drowsy with fever, so check that they wake and interact with you.
- **Older adults:** fever may be lower than expected or absent, so watch for confusion and reduced appetite.
- **Pregnancy:** a fever should be reported to a clinician promptly.

## When to act

Seek medical help for:

- A baby under three months with any fever.
- A fever above about 40 °C that does not fall.
- A fever with a stiff neck, severe headache, confusion or a rash that does not fade when pressed.
- Fever for more than three days.
- Difficulty breathing or chest pain.

## Materials

- A working thermometer, and a note of the last reading.
- Clean water and a clean cup.
- Paracetamol or ibuprofen suitable for the person's age, with the dose written on a card.
- A clean cloth and a bowl of cool water.

## Common mistakes

- Using the wrong dose for the person's weight or age.
- Giving two medicines with the same active ingredient by accident.
- Wrapping the person up to sweat out the fever.
- Waiting several days before measuring again.
- Treating the number alone and ignoring how the person is behaving.

## Troubleshooting

If the reading is unexpectedly high, shake the thermometer, wait, and measure again in the same place. If the person is shivering, the temperature may still be rising, so offer a light covering and recheck soon. If the fever keeps returning after treatment, record the times and seek a clinician's advice.

## Checklist

- Temperature measured and recorded.
- Water intake tracked.
- Dose correct for age and weight.
- Warning signs checked each few hours.
- Medical help arranged if any red flag appears.

## Practice before you need it

Test the thermometer on a healthy family member so you know what normal looks like for each person. Write the dosing chart for each child on a card kept with the medicine, so nobody has to calculate under pressure.

## When it fails

If the fever does not come down after the medicine, or the person becomes drowsy, stiff, confused or rash-covered, seek care. If the person is too sick to drink, arrange help the same day.

## Keep watch

Check the temperature every few hours, and keep drinking water going. Note any new symptom.

**Warning:** do not give aspirin to children. Do not give ibuprofen to someone who is dehydrated or has a stomach bleed.
