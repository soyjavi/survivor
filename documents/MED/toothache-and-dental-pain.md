# Toothache and Dental Pain

Tooth pain is very distressing and can become serious if an abscess forms. Keep the mouth clean and relieve the pain. Most toothache comes from decay or an infected nerve, and only a dentist can fix the cause, so the aim at home is comfort and control of infection.

## Why it matters

A tooth infection can spread from the root into the bone and surrounding tissues, and in rare cases it spreads into the neck or bloodstream. Pain often signals that the infection has reached the nerve, and the pain may ease without the infection going away. Recognising the difference between comfort measures and the need for professional treatment is the most important decision. Home care buys time, but it does not replace a dentist.

## Relieve pain

- Rinse the mouth with warm salt water, about half a teaspoon of salt in a glass of clean water.
- Take pain relief as the label says for adults or children.
- Apply a cold pack to the outside of the cheek for short periods.
- Avoid very hot, very cold or sugary food near the tooth.

## Step-by-step in detail

Mix the salt water fresh each time, using clean water. Take a small sip, hold it gently around the painful tooth, and spit it out without swallowing. Repeat several times, then rinse once with plain clean water. Apply the cold pack to the outside of the cheek in short bursts, never directly on the skin. If the person wants to lie down, keep the head raised on a pillow to reduce throbbing.

## Signs of an abscess

- Swelling of the face or gum.
- A bad taste or pus.
- Fever, or a swollen neck or jaw.

An abscess needs professional care, because it can spread into the face or blood. See When to Seek Professional Care.

## Adjusting for conditions

- **Children:** adult doses are not suitable for children, so use the dose chart for their weight.
- **Pregnancy:** some painkillers are not suitable, so check with a clinician or pharmacist before taking anything.
- **Older adults and people with diabetes:** infections can progress more quickly, so seek care sooner.
- **Pain while swallowing or breathing:** this is a reason to seek urgent care rather than home treatment.

## Prevent problems

- Brush with a small amount of fluoride toothpaste, or with a little salt and water if none is available.
- Clean between teeth with thread or a thin cord.
- Avoid sugar and sticky food.

## Materials

- Salt and clean water for rinsing.
- Fluoride toothpaste, a soft brush or a cloth, and dental floss or thread.
- Pain relief suitable for the person's age and weight.
- A clean cloth and a cold pack.

## Common mistakes

- Placing an aspirin tablet against the gum.
- Chewing on the painful side and making it worse.
- Ignoring swelling in the face, which can become serious within a day.
- Using sharp objects to clean between teeth.
- Taking painkillers without checking whether the same ingredient is already being taken.

## Troubleshooting

If the pain comes and goes with cold drinks, the nerve may still be healthy, but a dentist should still see the tooth. If the pain throbs when lying down, raise the head and try again. If a piece of the tooth has broken off and is sharp, cover it with dental wax if you have it, and keep it away from the tongue.

## Checklist

- Mouth rinsed with warm salt water.
- Pain relief given at the correct dose.
- Cold pack used in short bursts.
- Swelling watched each day.
- Dental care arranged as soon as possible.

## Practice before you need it

Keep the salt, the cold pack and the pain relief label together in the kit. Write down the name of the nearest dental clinic, and check whether it is open during emergencies.

## When it fails

If the pain does not ease, or the face or neck begins to swell, seek dental or medical care quickly. A fever with facial swelling needs urgent care. If the swelling makes it hard to swallow or breathe, call for emergency help.

## If a tooth is knocked out

Hold it by the crown, not the root. Rinse gently with clean water if it is dirty, and put it back in the socket if you can. Otherwise keep it in milk or in the person's mouth, and seek dental care quickly.

**Warning:** do not put aspirin or any painkiller directly on the gum. It can burn the tissue.
