# Caring for Babies Without Supplies

A baby without a cot, clean clothes, or nappies still needs three things every day: warmth, cleanliness, and a safe place to sleep. Most of the risk comes from getting these basics wrong, not from missing equipment. Plan around the three needs first, then improvise only where it is safe.

## Keeping the baby warm

Babies lose heat quickly through their heads and bodies, and they cannot shiver effectively. Cold can be as dangerous as hunger.

1. Dress the baby in layers, with one more layer than an adult would wear in the same place.
2. Cover the head with a soft hat or cloth, but never let it fall over the face.
3. Keep the baby skin to skin with a caregiver when there is no other heat source. This is one of the most effective ways to warm a newborn.
4. Check the body, not the hands and feet alone. The chest and back should feel warm, not cold or clammy.

- Dry any wet clothing or cloth immediately.
- Keep the baby away from draughts and bare floors.
- Do not place the baby directly beside an open fire, stove, or heater.

**Warning:** Never leave a baby near open flames or hot stones wrapped in cloth. Burns and smoke injuries happen quickly and are hard to treat in a shelter.

## Washing and hygiene

Clean hands and clean skin prevent most infections in young babies. Water is precious, so use it carefully.

1. Wash your own hands with soap, or with ash or sand and water, before touching the baby and after every nappy change.
2. Wipe the baby with a damp, clean cloth, working from front to back. Use a separate corner of the cloth for each wipe.
3. Clean the umbilical stump with a dry cloth and keep it dry. Do not put anything on it.
4. Bathe the baby only when it is warm enough and water is available. A quick wipe is enough on most days.

## Nappies and changing

Nappies can be replaced with clean cloth, folded and fastened safely, or with absorbent material placed inside clothing. Change them often, because wet skin breaks down quickly.

1. Keep a bundle of clean cloths ready, one for each change.
2. Change the baby at least every few hours and after every wet or dirty episode.
3. Dispose of soiled material away from food and water, and wash reusable cloths in hot water when you can.
4. Let the skin air dry briefly before fastening a new cloth.

Watch for nappy rash, which appears as red, raw skin in the folds and around the bottom. Keep the area clean and dry, and expose it to air when possible.

## Sleep safety

A safe sleep space matters more than any object. Many infant deaths in unsafe conditions come from suffocation, overheating, or falling.

1. Lay the baby on its back on a firm, flat surface.
2. Keep the sleeping surface clear of loose blankets, pillows, and soft toys.
3. Share a room with the baby, but do not share a bed or sofa with a baby who is very young or unwell.
4. Keep the baby's face uncovered, with feet at the end of the sleeping space.
5. Never sleep with the baby if you have taken alcohol, sedating medicine, or are extremely tired.

## Improvised items to avoid

Some common substitutes do more harm than good. Avoid these even when they seem convenient:

- Plastic bags, which can suffocate a baby.
- Pillows, thick duvets, and loose adult blankets in the cot or sleeping space.
- Bottles or dummies made from anything other than clean, approved materials.
- Heated stones, bricks, or metal objects placed against the body.
- Cotton wool, string, or small parts that a baby could pull free and swallow.
- Adult medicines, herbs, oils, or alcohol applied to the skin or given by mouth.

## When to seek help

Get care quickly if the baby is feeding poorly, has a fever, is unusually sleepy, breathes fast, has a rash that does not fade when pressed, or has an umbilical area that is red, swollen, or leaking. Caregivers should also seek help if the baby seems cold to the touch despite layers and skin contact. Bring a note of what the baby eats, how often it passes urine, and when the problem started.

**Warning:** Never use a plastic bag, sheet of plastic, or any material that could cover the face as bedding or a wrap. Babies can suffocate within minutes.
