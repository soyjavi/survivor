# Children's Medicine Doses and Safety

Children are not small adults. Their bodies process medicines differently, and the correct amount depends far more on weight and age than on appearance. Most medicine errors in children come from guessing, from confusing strengths, or from doses given by a sibling or neighbour. Careful, slow, and written checks prevent most harm.

## How doses are set

Doses for children are normally calculated from body weight, and sometimes from age or body surface area. The packet or the health worker's guidance should give a dose per kilogram, a range, or a fixed amount for a specific weight band.

1. Find out the child's current weight, not a guess or a remembered figure. Use a scale if one is available.
2. Read the dose written for that weight, and check the units carefully, for example milligrams or millilitres.
3. Confirm the concentration of the medicine, because the same dose can be a very different volume depending on the strength.
4. Use the measuring device supplied with the medicine, and only that device.
5. Write down the time, the dose, and the person who gave it.

Medicines given by volume should use a marked syringe or dropper, not a kitchen spoon. Household spoons vary widely in size and can deliver a large error with every dose.

## Never guess

A dose that is too small may do nothing. A dose that is too large can damage the liver, kidneys, heart, or brain, and children can become seriously ill from amounts that seem trivial.

- Do not give an adult dose cut in half or quartered unless the instructions say so.
- Do not give a second dose early because the first one did not seem to work.
- Do not combine medicines without advice, because many contain the same active ingredient.
- Do not use medicine prescribed for one child or one illness for another child.

**Warning:** Never guess a dose from a child's age, height, or appearance. Ask a trained health worker, pharmacist, or the instructions on the packet, and wait for an answer if one is not available.

## Reading labels

Labels can be faded, damaged, or in a language you cannot read. Treat any unclear label as unsafe until it is checked.

1. Read the name, strength, and expiry date before opening the container.
2. Look for the dose per weight or age, the frequency, and the maximum number of doses in one day.
3. Check whether the medicine is for internal use, external use, or injection.
4. Reject medicine with a broken seal, cloudy liquid, changed colour, or a date that has passed.
5. If the label is unreadable, do not use the medicine. Take it to a health worker.

Keep original packaging and the leaflet when possible. If medicine is repackaged or divided, label each container with the name, strength, dose, and date.

## Keeping medicines safe

Children are drawn to bright colours and sweet smells, and they can swallow a tablet or sip syrup in seconds. Medicines are one of the most common causes of accidental poisoning in young children.

- Store all medicines in a locked box, high cupboard, or sealed container that a child cannot open or reach.
- Keep medicines in their original containers, away from food, cooking areas, and water supplies.
- Store medicines away from heat, damp, and direct sunlight.
- Keep a list of medicines in the household, with dates given and remaining quantities.
- Remind all caregivers, including older children and visitors, where the medicines are kept.

## Poisoning and overdose

If you think a child has taken too much medicine, or has swallowed something unknown, act quickly. Do not wait for symptoms to appear, because some medicines cause harm before a child looks unwell.

1. Remove any remaining medicine from the child's reach.
2. Keep the container, or a photograph of it, so the health worker can identify the substance.
3. Get help immediately, and note the time of the event and the estimated amount taken.
4. Do not make the child vomit unless a health worker tells you to, because this can cause more harm.
5. Keep the child awake and watch breathing, colour, and alertness while waiting for care.

## Simple checks before every dose

1. Confirm the child's name and the medicine name.
2. Confirm the dose against the weight and the label.
3. Confirm the time since the last dose.
4. Confirm the measuring tool is clean, dry, and the right one.
5. Give the dose, record it, and store the medicine again immediately.

These checks take less than a minute and prevent most errors. When in doubt, pause and ask.

**Warning:** Call for urgent help right away if a child is drowsy, breathing slowly or with difficulty, has fits, or has unusual colouring of the lips or skin. Do not wait to see whether symptoms develop.
