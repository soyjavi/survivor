# Sleep and Stress

Stress and poor sleep feed each other. Lack of rest makes people irritable, careless and slower to think, which increases risk for everyone. A tired person may misjudge a fire, a step or a conversation. Protecting sleep is therefore a safety task, not just a comfort.

![Pair: table-grid habits list beside table-grid watch rota with table-header; labels Fixed bedtime, Night watch; fixed bedtimes and shared watch are the two levers.](assets/sleep-and-stress-1.svg)

## Why it matters

The body repairs itself during sleep, and the mind sorts the day's events. When sleep is cut short, stress hormones stay high, hunger signals become louder and the ability to control emotions weakens. In a crisis, these effects appear quickly. A household that sleeps badly for three nights will often argue more, make more errors and recover more slowly from illness. Good sleep is one of the cheapest and most effective protections the group has.

## Why sleep suffers

![Hub: bed at centre with five arrow-straight pointing in; labels Noise, Cold, Light, Hunger, Worry; hunger and worry disrupt sleep as much as the environment. NEEDS: bed](assets/sleep-and-stress-2.svg)

- Noise, cold, light and worry keep the body alert.
- Hunger and thirst disturb rest, and a full bladder wakes people often.
- Watch duties and night noises break sleep into pieces.

## Habits that help

Build these habits gradually, and keep the ones that work:

1. Keep a regular bedtime and wake time, even without clocks. Use the light and the call to prayer, the radio or the group bell.
2. Keep the sleeping area dark, quiet and as warm as possible.
3. Avoid caffeine, alcohol and heavy meals late in the day. A small snack is better than an empty stomach.
4. Take a short walk or stretch before bed, to release tension in the body.
5. Write worries down before sleep, and leave them on the page. Put the paper away until morning.

## Step-by-step in detail

1. **Set the time.** Choose a bedtime and a wake time that match the light, and keep them for a week.
2. **Prepare the space.** Clear the sleeping area of rubbish, cover the windows and place the bedding away from draughts.
3. **Eat and drink early.** Finish the main meal at least two hours before bed, and drink water in the evening rather than just before sleep.
4. **Wind down.** Spend ten minutes on quiet activity, such as a story or slow breathing, with no tasks.
5. **Write and close.** Record the worries, then put the notebook away until the morning meeting.

## Sharing watch

![Rota: table-header with night hours, table-grid with person-standing pairs in two-to-three hour blocks followed by sleep blocks; labels Pair A, Pair B, Sleep; no one stands watch more than one block in a row.](assets/sleep-and-stress-3.svg)

If you must keep watch at night, share the duty in blocks of two or three hours, and let each person sleep fully between shifts. Two people on watch together can keep each other awake and safe, and a written rota stops anyone from being forgotten.

## Recognise overload

> @image Photograph of an exhausted adult resting their head on their arms at a table, documentary style, black and white

Signs include constant irritability, poor memory, sudden tears or anger, and mistakes with simple tasks. Rest, tell someone, and reduce tasks where you can. Sleep loss that lasts more than a few nights needs attention, not willpower.

![Sleep chart: timeline-axis from bedtime to morning, waves alternating light and deep with deeper waves early; labels Bedtime, Light, Deep, Morning; deep sleep is early, so early rest matters most.](assets/sleep-and-stress-4.svg)

## Naps

![Day clock: timeline-axis for daylight with a short step-box nap in the afternoon, threshold marking the last hours; labels Nap 20 minutes, Last daylight; a late nap harms night sleep.](assets/sleep-and-stress-5.svg)

A short nap of 20 minutes in the afternoon can restore alertness, if sleep at night is poor. Longer naps can make night sleep harder, so keep them short, and avoid them in the last hours of daylight.

## Adjusting for conditions

In hot weather, sleep in the shade or in the cooler part of the building, and rest through the hottest hours if there is no shade. In cold weather, sleep close together under shared blankets, and keep a second layer by the bed. When there is a loud night noise, such as a generator or a crowd, use a steady sound such as a fan or a quiet voice to mask it. When a child cannot sleep, keep the routine and the bedtime story, and sit with them until their breathing slows.

## Common mistakes

- Sleeping in a noisy, bright or cold space because it is the only place available.
- Using tea, coffee or alcohol to push through fatigue, which worsens the next night.
- Keeping watch alone for many nights in a row.
- Checking the time or the news repeatedly during the night.

## Troubleshooting

If sleep does not come after thirty minutes, get up, sit quietly in dim light, and return to bed only when drowsy. If the mind races with worries, write one line on the paper and move on. If night watch causes exhaustion, change the rota so that each person has at least one full night off.

## Practice before you need it

Test the sleep routine for a week on an ordinary schedule, so that the habits are familiar. Set up the sleeping space and the night-watch rota before you need them. Practise the wind-down steps with children and adults, so that the group can settle together.
