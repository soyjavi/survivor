# Reuniting Separated Children

Children can become separated from their families in crowds, evacuations, or sudden moves. Most reunifications succeed when the family acted early: prepared a plan, kept good records, and reported the separation quickly. Preparation before any emergency is worth more than any search that follows.

## Prepare before separation happens

1. Agree on a meeting place near home and a second place further away, and make sure every child knows both.
2. Teach each child their full name, age, home area, and the names of parents or guardians, in a form they can repeat.
3. Give younger children a card in a pocket or on a cord, with the same information written clearly.
4. Choose one trusted adult outside the area who can receive messages if local phones fail.
5. Practise the plan at home so children know what to do and who to approach.

Teach children to recognise uniformed staff and to ask for help from a woman with children when they need it. Tell them clearly never to leave with a stranger, even one who seems kind, unless a known adult has told them to.

## What to do if a child goes missing

Act immediately. The first hour matters most, because children are often found close by.

1. Stop, and ask the people nearby whether they have seen the child.
2. Search the immediate area in a rough circle, calling the child's name and checking shelters, toilets, water points, and the route you came by.
3. Ask one trusted adult to stay in the original place, in case the child returns there.
4. Report the child to the nearest camp, shelter manager, or authority within the hour.
5. Give a clear description: name, age, height, clothing, distinctive marks, and when and where the child was last seen.
6. Keep searching and reporting until you are told otherwise. Do not assume the child is being cared for by someone else.

## Reporting to authorities

Report to whichever formal body is responsible in the place you are staying. This may be a camp manager, police, a humanitarian agency, or a registration desk. Ask for the name and contact of the person who takes the report and write it down.

- Give the report in person when possible, and bring a photograph if one exists.
- Ask whether the child is being recorded in a tracing system, and how to check its status.
- Report the child's disappearance even if you have heard that the child is safe, because official confirmation matters.
- Report any child found alone, without identification, to the same office, and never take that child into your own care without official approval.

## Keeping records

Records are the single most useful tool in tracing a child. Write them down as early as possible, and keep copies in more than one place.

1. For each child, record full name, date of birth or estimated age, sex, home address, and language spoken.
2. Note the names and contact details of parents, guardians, and close relatives, and where they were last known to be.
3. Keep recent photographs, taken from the front and both sides if possible, and dated.
4. Write down identifying features such as scars, birthmarks, or medical conditions, and any medicines required.
5. Record the time, place, and circumstances of any separation, and any names of people who were present.
6. Store copies in a waterproof bag, and give a second copy to a trusted person outside the area.

Update the records each time something changes, such as a move to a new shelter or a new contact number. Old information can slow a reunion.

## Caring for a child who is found

Children who have been separated are often frightened, confused, or grieving. Reassure them with a calm voice, and let them tell their story at their own pace. Do not ask leading questions or push for detail.

1. Give the child water, food, and a safe place to sit near familiar faces.
2. Keep the child with one or two consistent adults until the reunion is arranged.
3. Avoid making promises about when family will arrive, because delays are common.
4. Let the child keep familiar objects, and encourage gentle play where possible.

## Checking identity before reunion

Confirm the identity of any adult claiming a child before handing the child over. Ask for documents where available, ask specific questions about the child's details, and check the answers against the written records. Hand over the child only through the official process, and keep a record of the handover.

Some people try to take children they are not entitled to. Trust your concerns, and report any doubts to the responsible office at once.

**Warning:** Never leave the area where a child was last seen unless someone else is there to keep watch. Unsupervised searching can create a second gap in the search.
