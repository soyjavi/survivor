# Recovering After a Disaster

Recovery takes months or years. It moves through stages: immediate needs, returning to daily life, and rebuilding. Each stage needs different actions, and people often try to rebuild before the earlier stage is complete. Working through the stages in order protects health and money, and it keeps people from exhausting themselves.

![Timeline: timeline-axis with three step-box, the last longest; labels Immediate needs, Daily life, Rebuilding; rebuilding starts only once the first two are reliable.](assets/recovering-after-a-disaster-1.svg)

## Why it matters

Recovery is often harder than the disaster itself, because the emergency ends and the attention of outsiders moves on. Households are left with damaged homes, debts, grief and long lists of repairs. Without a clear order of work, people spend scarce money on cosmetic fixes while the roof still leaks. A staged approach gives each week a purpose and makes it easier to see progress, which keeps morale and cooperation alive for the long haul.

## Stage one: immediate needs

Water, food, shelter, medical care and safety. Focus on these first, and do not move on until they are reliable. The other guides in this library cover each of them in detail.

## Stage two: daily life

![Ladder: ladder with step-box rungs from bottom; labels Roof, Doors, Electricity, Drainage; repairs follow safety, so the roof comes before cosmetic work.](assets/recovering-after-a-disaster-2.svg)

Once the basics are secure, restore a normal rhythm.

- Restore routines: meals, sleep, school or work where possible. Routine helps children and adults feel that the world is returning.
- Repair the home in order of priority, starting with what keeps people safe: roof, doors, electricity and drainage.
- Re-establish contact with family and neighbours. Check who has moved, who is missing and who is ill.

## Stage three: rebuilding

> @image Photograph of a damaged house before repair with a dated photo record, documentary style, black and white

This stage is slow, and it often costs the most.

- Plan longer-term repairs and insurance claims. Take photos before any repair.
- Build back stronger: better drainage, better shelter, better supplies. Learn from what failed.
- Review what worked and what did not, with the group, and write it down.

## Equipment and materials

Recovery needs a few practical items: a notebook or the crisis log, a camera or phone for photographs, a tape measure, a box for receipts and small parts, and basic hand tools such as a hammer, a saw and a spade. Label the box with the date and the name of the household, so that papers do not get mixed up.

## Step-by-step in detail

1. **Assess the damage** room by room, and write down what is broken, unsafe or missing.
2. **Photograph everything** before moving or repairing anything, with the date visible.
3. **Rank the repairs** in order of safety, then cost, then convenience.
4. **Check the structure** with a trained person before any heavy work begins.
5. **Assign each repair** to a named person and a deadline on the rota.
6. **Record the cost** and keep every receipt in the labelled box.

## Community

![Rota: table-grid with step-box task cards, each with person-standing name; labels Task, Neighbour; every repair has a named neighbour so work stays visibly shared.](assets/recovering-after-a-disaster-3.svg)

Share tools, labour and information. Neighbours who work together recover faster, and they are less likely to fall into isolation. A rota for repairs, with names on it, keeps the work fair.

## Keep a record

![Archive: table-grid as folder with timeline-marker date tabs and dated sheets; labels Photos, Receipts, Date; evidence is indexed by date so claims can be traced back. NEEDS: folder, photo](assets/recovering-after-a-disaster-4.svg)

Keep the crisis log, photos of damage and receipts. They help with claims, grants and planning, and they give you a clear view of progress.

## Adjusting for conditions

When the rainy season is near, put drainage and roof repairs at the top of the list, even before cosmetic work. When money is short, prioritise the repairs that stop further damage, such as covering an open roof with a tarpaulin. When many households need the same tool, build a shared schedule and keep a log of who borrowed what. When the building is partly standing, keep children and older people away from it until a professional has checked it.

## Common mistakes

- Rebuilding a damaged structure before it has been checked.
- Throwing away receipts and records too early.
- Expecting the household to return to normal within weeks.
- Repairing the visible damage while the drainage is still blocked.

## Troubleshooting

If the repair rota falls behind, check whether one household is carrying the work alone, and rebalance the tasks. If claims are rejected because of missing evidence, look at the photographs and receipts together, and rebuild the chain of proof. If a repair fails within days, return to the stage it belonged to and check the work before moving on.

## Expect setbacks

Recovery is not a straight line. Expect bad days, and plan for them. A heavy rain, a delayed payment or a family argument can reopen old fear. Name the setback, rest, and return to the next small task.

![Trend: slope as rising line-solid, arrow-curved dips marked with warning; labels Progress, Setback; dips are expected and still sit on the line of progress.](assets/recovering-after-a-disaster-5.svg)

## Practice before you need it

Walk the neighbourhood with a partner and photograph a few houses as a practice, so that the photo record becomes a habit. Agree in advance who keeps the receipts box and who manages the rota. A calm first run makes the real process far less overwhelming.

## Checklist

![Sequence: four step-box in a row joined by arrow-straight, each with do tick in order; no box can be ticked before the one before it.](assets/recovering-after-a-disaster-6.svg)

- [ ] Immediate needs are reliably met.
- [ ] Repairs are ordered by safety priority.
- [ ] Damage has been photographed, and receipts are filed.
- [ ] Neighbours have agreed who helps with which task.

**Warning:** do not start major rebuilding until the site is safe. Check for structural and electrical hazards first, and do not re-enter a building that a professional has marked as unsafe.
