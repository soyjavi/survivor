# Starting a Fire with Matches and Tinder

A fire fails most often because the fuel is added too early or is too large. Build it in stages, and let each stage catch before adding the next. Patience at the start saves a great deal of wood later.

![side view: tinder-pile under a kindling teepee, gap on the windward side, arrow-straight wind, flame at the gap; labels Tinder, Kindling, Wind, Light here; the first flame goes on the side facing the wind.](assets/starting-a-fire-with-matches-and-tinder-1.svg)

## Why it matters

Fire is a chain. Each size of fuel has to heat the next size up to ignition. If the chain breaks, the fire goes out, and it usually breaks at the step where too much fuel was added too soon. Most of the skill lies in judging when each stage is ready, not in striking the match. Once you understand the chain, the method works in almost any weather.

![stack: three step-box bottom to top, tinder-pile, kindling, log-stack, arrow-straight upward between them; labels Tinder nest, Kindling, Fuel logs, Catch first; the fire is built stage by stage.](assets/starting-a-fire-with-matches-and-tinder-2.svg)

## Three stages

1. **Tinder:** dry, fine material that catches from a small flame. Dry grass, shredded bark, birch bark, dry leaves or cotton wool smeared with petroleum jelly.
2. **Kindling:** small dry twigs, from pencil thickness up to a finger.
3. **Fuel:** larger dry wood, added once the kindling is burning steadily.

## Equipment and materials

- Matches in a waterproof case, or a refillable lighter
- Tinder gathered in a bundle, with a pile of fine shavings or cotton wool
- Kindling sorted by thickness, from pencil to finger
- Fuel logs split to a size the fire can take, and a metal bucket for ash
- A flat, clear patch of ground, with a stone ring if the area is open

| Stage | Size | Test before moving on |
|---|---|---|
| Tinder | Fine, hair-thin to pencil | Catches from one small flame |
| Kindling | Pencil to finger | Crackles and stays lit without help |
| Fuel | Wrist to forearm | Burns steadily when the kindling has built a bed of coals |

## Build the fire

1. Clear a flat, safe area and gather everything before you light anything.
2. Place a small pile of tinder in the centre.
3. Arrange the kindling in a tepee or lean-to shape over the tinder, leaving a gap to reach the flame.
4. Light the tinder in several places, on the windward side.
5. Blow gently at the base once the flame takes hold.
6. Add kindling a little at a time, then fuel.

## Matches and lighters

- Keep matches dry, in a waterproof container.
- Strike them away from your body and shelter the flame from the wind.
- Light the tinder before the match burns down to your fingers.

![close-up: match and cupped-hand, flame, tinder-pile just below, arrow-straight down; labels Match, Sheltered flame, Tinder; the match must light the tinder before it burns down. NEEDS: match, cupped-hand.](assets/starting-a-fire-with-matches-and-tinder-4.svg)

## Adjusting for conditions

- **In wind,** build the tepee against a log or a stone wall, and keep the gap pointing into the shelter.
- **In damp weather,** use finer tinder and more of it, and keep the kindling off the ground on a flat base.
- **In cold weather,** the tinder takes longer to catch. Warm the kindling near the fire first, and add fuel more slowly.
- **In snow,** clear to bare ground, then build on a platform of stones or split logs.

## Common mistakes

- Adding the big logs before the kindling has become a steady bed of flame.
- Packing the tepee so tightly that air cannot reach the tinder.
- Using a coarse or damp tinder that smokes but will not light.
- Lighting from the leeward side, so the flame is blown away before it can spread.
- Leaving the match or lighter to burn down in the hand, which causes burns and dropped matches.

## Practice before you need it

Build a fire at least once a month in good weather, using only the tinder you carry. Time how long it takes from the first match to a fire that can take fuel. Try the method with one match, then with two, and note how the fire behaves. A fire you have built many times will light the first time you need it. Keep a note of the weather and the tinder you used, so you can repeat what worked.

## Troubleshooting

If the tinder goes out before the kindling catches, the tinder was too damp or too coarse. Replace it with finer, drier material, and build the tepee more openly so air can reach the flame. If the kindling smokes but does not flame, the wood is too wet, so split a piece to reach the dry inner wood, or move to smaller sticks.

## When it fails

If the tinder goes out before the kindling catches, the tinder was too damp or too coarse. Replace it with finer, drier material, and build the tepee more openly so air can reach the flame.

**Warning:** never use petrol, paraffin or alcohol to light a fire. The flash can burn you badly.
