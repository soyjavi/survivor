# Chimney Fires

A chimney fire happens when soot and creosote built up inside a flue ignite. The fire can burn at extreme temperatures, crack the flue liner, and spread into roof timbers or walls. It can start with little warning and grow quickly, so recognising the signs early gives you the best chance to act.

## Signs of a Chimney Fire

Watch for these signals, especially during or just after a hot fire:

- A loud roaring or cracking sound from the stove or flue, sometimes like a train or a jet.
- Flames or sparks visible at the top of the chimney or from the joints of the pipe.
- Dense, dark smoke pouring from the chimney top, or sudden changes in the stove's draw.
- The flue pipe or wall around it becoming unusually hot to touch.
- Thick black creosote dripping from joints or the cap.
- A smell of burning tar or a strong, sharp smoke smell throughout the building.

## What to Do Immediately

Speed matters, but so does a calm sequence. Follow these steps in order.

1. Get everyone out of the building and keep them together outside, away from the chimney.
2. Close the stove or fireplace doors and the air vents to starve the fire of oxygen.
3. Do not open windows or doors to ventilate, because that feeds the fire.
4. Move any combustible items away from the stove and the wall the flue passes through, if you can do it safely.
5. Call the emergency number for your area and tell them you have a chimney fire.
6. Stay outside and wait for the fire service, even if the fire seems to have died down.

## Why Water Is the Wrong Response

Many people reach for a bucket, and that is the mistake to avoid.

- Water poured into a stove or fireplace can crack hot cast iron or a ceramic liner, which damages the appliance.
- Steam generated inside the flue can push smoke and fire back into the room.
- Water can spread burning creosote and hot debris to other parts of the building.
- Water does not reach the burning deposits high in the flue, so it does little to stop the fire.

If the stove itself is burning, keep the doors closed and leave the response to professionals.

## After the Fire

A chimney fire, even one that seems to have stopped, can leave the flue weakened and the structure damaged.

- Do not light the stove again until a qualified person has inspected the entire flue and the surrounding framing.
- Expect the chimney to need cleaning, relining or replacement after a severe fire.
- Keep a record of the date and the fuel burned, because repeated fires usually point to a fuel or air supply problem.

## Preventing the Next One

- Burn only dry, seasoned wood, because wet wood produces heavy creosote deposits.
- Keep the stove drafting well and avoid smouldering fires overnight with the air fully closed.
- Have the flue swept at least once each heating season, and more often if you burn a lot.
- Install a spark arrester cap on the chimney top to reduce the chance of embers escaping onto the roof.
