# Putting Out Fires

A small fire caught early can be stopped with the right material. Once a fire spreads, leave and call for help. The first minute usually decides the outcome, so keep the means to fight a small fire within reach at all times.

![four panels around flame: bucket with water-drop on the base, bag of sand, fire-blanket over pan, pan with sliding lid; labels Water, Sand, Blanket, Lid; the material must match the fuel. NEEDS: fire-blanket.](assets/putting-out-fires-1.svg)

## Why it matters

Most fires that grow out of control began as something small: a pan of oil, a rag near a heater, a campfire that was left with a glowing centre. Acting in the first minute is what keeps a small fire small. Hesitating, hunting for the right tool or trying to save belongings turns a manageable flame into a room that cannot be entered. Knowing which material to use before the alarm sounds is the difference between a quick fix and a loss.

## Choose the method

- **Water:** for wood, paper, cloth and most solid materials. Pour it on the base of the fire, not the flames.
- **Sand or soil:** for campfires and small spills. Smother the fire fully.
- **Fire blanket:** for cooking fires and clothing. Cover the fire to cut off air, and leave it in place until cool.
- **Lid or cover:** for pans. Slide it over the pan and leave it on until cool.

## Equipment and materials

- A bucket of water kept within reach of the fire area
- A bag of dry sand or a shovel of soil
- A fire blanket in a wall-mounted case, not in a drawer
- A metal lid large enough for the largest pan you use

## Do not use water on

- **Cooking oil or grease:** it spreads the fire and can cause a burst of flame.
- **Electrical fires:** switch off the power first if it is safe to do so, then smother the fire.
- **Petrol or chemical fires:** use sand or a fire blanket, never water.

## Campfire

1. Pour water over the fire until the hissing stops.
2. Stir the embers and pour again.
3. Feel the ash with the back of your hand. Repeat until cold.

## Step-by-step for a pan fire

1. Turn off the heat if you can reach the controls without leaning over the flame.
2. Slide the lid over the pan from the side nearest the handle, so your hand stays behind the edge.
3. Leave the lid on until the pan is fully cool. Lifting it early lets air back in.
4. If the flame spreads to the surrounding cloth or cabinet, use the fire blanket or extinguisher, then leave.

## Clothing on fire

If clothing catches fire, stop where you stand, drop to the floor, cover your face with your hands and roll slowly. Running feeds the flames, and rolling smothers them. Cool any burn with cool running water for several minutes, and seek medical help for anything more than a small blister.

## When to leave

- The fire is spreading faster than you can control it.
- Smoke is filling the room.
- You are not sure what is burning.

Leave, close the door, and call for help.

## Adjusting for conditions

- **In wind,** fires spread quickly and sparks carry over firebreaks. Keep the water supply on the windward side and be ready to leave sooner.
- **On dry grass or leaves,** the fire can jump a gap of several metres. Wet the ground around the fire before you approach it.
- **Indoors,** smoke is the main threat. Keep low, close doors behind you and do not stay to fight a fire that is beyond the first flame.
## Common mistakes

- Throwing water on a grease fire, which spreads burning oil across the kitchen.
- Fighting the fire with the exit behind you, so that the escape route is cut off.
## Practice before you need it

Once a year, practise with a test fire on bare ground outdoors, using sand, water and a fire blanket in turn. Time how quickly you can gather each tool. Check that the extinguisher pressure is in the green zone and that the blanket case opens easily.
## Troubleshooting

If the fire does not go out with the first method, do not keep fighting it past the point of safety. Switch to a second method only if it is safe, and otherwise leave. If the fire flares again after you think it is out, cover it or soak it once more, and keep watch until it is completely cold.
