# Wildfire Home Defence

Most homes are lost to embers carried by the wind, not to the flames themselves. Reduce what embers can catch on, and create a buffer around the house. Small changes made before the season matter more than any last-minute effort.

![top-down: hut centre with three concentric line-dashed rings, shrub and tree thinned in ring two, short grass beyond, flame outside, arrow-straight for embers landing; labels Zone 1 clear and watered, Zone 2 thinned, Beyond short grass; the closest zone matters most because embers land there first.](assets/wildfire-home-defence-1.svg)

## Why it matters

Embers can travel well ahead of a fire front and settle in gutters, under eaves, in vents and in piles of dry debris. Once a small spot fire starts against a wall, it can enter the house before anyone notices. A cleared zone close to the building removes the fuel that embers need, while mesh and tight seals stop them getting inside. Both steps work together, and both are cheap compared with rebuilding a home.

## Defensible space

- **Zone one, within about 5 metres of the house:** remove dry leaves, dead plants and flammable mulch. Keep lawns watered and short. Do not store wood piles against walls.
- **Zone two, about 5 to 30 metres:** thin shrubs and trees, keep branches at least 3 metres apart, and remove low branches.
- **Beyond 30 metres:** keep grass short and remove dead material.

Work outwards from the house, starting with zone one, because that is where embers do the most damage. Vegetation grows back quickly, so repeat the clearing each season rather than treating it as a one-off job.

## Protect the house

1. Clean gutters, the roof and under-eave spaces of leaves and needles.
2. Cover vents with fine metal mesh to keep embers out.
3. Close windows and doors, especially on the side the wind comes from.
4. Store fuel and gas cylinders away from the house.

## Before the season

- Keep a garden hose connected and a bucket of water ready.
- Keep a ladder and a fire extinguisher in an easy place.
- Plan your evacuation route and a meeting point.
- Keep a list of which neighbours have ladders, tools or pumps, and agree how you will help each other.
- Store a spare pair of gloves and goggles near the hose for quick use.
- Make sure the house number is visible from the road for responders.

## Adjusting for conditions

- **On a slope,** fire moves uphill much faster. Extend zone one and zone two further on the uphill side, and keep the slope clear of dry grass.
- **In strong, dry winds,** the ember risk grows. Check the vents and gutters more often, and keep the hose ready before the wind arrives.
- **In the driest months,** vegetation dries out fast. Clear again later in the season, not only before it begins.
- **Near woodland,** widen the buffer and remove dead trees that could fall or carry fire toward the house.

## Common mistakes

- Clearing zone two thoroughly but leaving leaves in the gutters, where embers collect.
- Planting shrubs with resinous or oily foliage right against the walls.
- Storing firewood, sheds or stacked garden furniture against the house.
- Leaving vents uncovered because the mesh looks unsightly.
- Relying on the hose alone, without a working water source or enough length to reach the roof.

## Practice before you need it

Once a season, walk the property as if an ember had just landed on the roof. Check where a small fire could start, and whether the hose reaches each corner. Time how long it takes to fill the buckets and to bring the ladder out. Note any new debris from storms and clear it that week.

## Troubleshooting

If embers reach the house and small spot fires start, put them out with water or sand and check the roof and under-eaves. Look in gutters, behind downpipes and inside vents for smouldering material. If the fire is beyond control, leave. Do not stay to defend the house once smoke and heat make it unsafe.
