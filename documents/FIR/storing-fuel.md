# Storing Fuel

Fuel stored badly is wasted, and some fuels are dangerous to keep near the house. Plan the amount and the place before you need them. Store each type in its own location, away from living spaces, ignition sources and children.

![yard plan: log-stack, gas-cylinder upright, jerrycan cabinet, hut, arrow-both-ways distance from each to the house; labels Wood, Gas, Liquid fuel, House; each fuel gets its own spot, separated from living space. NEEDS: gas-cylinder.](assets/storing-fuel-1.svg)

## Why it matters

Each fuel fails in its own way. Wood rots and grows mould, charcoal soaks up moisture until it will not light, gas can leak invisibly, and petrol loses its quality over months and gives off vapour that travels along the ground. Storing the fuels together, or near the house, multiplies every one of those risks. Separate, labelled and ventilated storage keeps each hazard contained.

## Planning the store

| Fuel | Where to keep it | Container | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wood | Covered stack, open sides, away from the house | Stack on pallets | Rot, insects and damp |
| Charcoal | Ventilated shed, off the floor | Sealed bags or tins | Moisture |
| Gas cylinders | Outdoors, upright, shaded | Approved cylinder | Leaks into low spaces |
| Liquid fuels | Cool, ventilated outbuilding | Approved, labelled cans | Vapour and spills |

## Wood

- Stack it off the ground on pallets or bricks, with the top covered and the sides open to air.
- Keep stacks at least several metres from the house.
- Seasoned wood, dried for six months or more, burns cleaner and hotter.
- Check stacks for insects, mould and rot, and remove affected pieces.

## Charcoal

- Keep it dry in sealed bags or containers.
- Store it in a well-ventilated shed, never in a closed room.
- Keep it off the floor. Damp charcoal is hard to light and smokes.

## Gas cylinders

- Store upright, outdoors, in a shaded place away from heat and ignition sources.
- Keep them away from drains, basements and low spots where gas could collect.
- Check valves and hoses for cracks each time you use them.

## Liquid fuels

- Keep petrol, paraffin and kerosene in approved containers, labelled.
- Store them in a cool, ventilated outbuilding, away from the house and from children.
- Keep only what you need, and rotate the stock.

## Adjusting for conditions

- **In humid climates,** charcoal and wood take on moisture quickly. Use desiccant packs in sealed containers and check the seals monthly.
- **In flood-prone areas,** raise every store above the highest expected water line. Move liquid fuels to an upper shelf or a higher location before rain.
- **In hot weather,** keep gas cylinders out of direct sun, and keep liquid fuels in shade. Heat increases pressure and vapour.
- **In cold weather,** keep containers from freezing if they hold water-based products, and check that valves do not stick.

## Common mistakes

- Storing wood directly against the house wall, where moisture and insects collect.
- Keeping gas cylinders in a shed with no ventilation, so any leak has nowhere to go.
- Decanting petrol into unlabelled bottles that look like drinks.
- Storing charcoal in a damp corner because the shed door is usually left open.
- Forgetting how old the liquid fuel is, and using it without checking its condition.

## Practice before you need it

Once a month, walk the storage area and look at each fuel. Check wood for rot, charcoal bags for holes, cylinder valves for corrosion and the fuel cans for leaks or missing labels. Write the check on a list pinned inside the shed. Label each store with its contents and the date of the last check, so the monthly inspection is quick. Keep a fire extinguisher near the fuel store, but outside it. Practise moving one cylinder or can to a safe outdoor spot, so the route is clear if a leak ever happens.

## Troubleshooting

If you smell fuel near a store, move any container that is leaking outdoors, away from ignition sources, if you can do so safely. Ventilate the area and keep people and pets clear. If charcoal smokes or will not light, it has taken on moisture, so dry it in a warm, ventilated place or set it aside for sparing use. Do not burn mouldy wood. Remove it from the stack and dispose of it safely.

## Checklist

- [ ] Each fuel type in its own place
- [ ] Wood stacked off the ground and covered
- [ ] Gas cylinders upright and outdoors
- [ ] Containers labelled and sealed

## When it fails

If you smell fuel near a store, or a container is leaking, move it outdoors, away from ignition sources, if you can do so safely, and ventilate the area. Do not use the fuel until the leak is fixed or the fuel has been disposed of safely.

**Warning:** never store fuel in drinking water containers, or near an open flame. Fuel vapour can travel and ignite.
