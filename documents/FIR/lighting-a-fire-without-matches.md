# Lighting a Fire Without Matches

Matches and lighters run out, get wet or get lost. Learn at least one method that does not depend on them, and practise it in dry conditions long before you need it. A method you have never tried is not a plan.

![three panels side by side: ferro-rod with sparks, magnifying-lens with arrow-straight sunlight, battery with steel wool; tinder-pile under each; labels Ferro rod, Lens, Steel wool; each method needs a different condition, such as sun or dryness. NEEDS: ferro-rod, magnifying-lens, battery.](assets/lighting-a-fire-without-matches-1.svg)

## Why it matters

Ignition is the first point where a fire can fail, and it is the point most people never rehearse. Lighters work until they are empty, and matches are ruined by a single soaking. A second method that you have practised gives you a real backup, while a third method you have only read about is little more than a rumour. The goal is not to be clever with sparks. It is to be able to make a flame when the usual tools are gone.

## Equipment and materials

- A ferro rod with a striker, or a knife with a steel back
- A small magnifying lens, clear and free of scratches
- Fine steel wool and a small battery, such as a AA or 9V
- A bow drill set with a fireboard, spindle and bearing block, if you have trained with one
- Dry tinder stored in a sealed tin, ready for any method

| Method | Speed | Skill needed | Works when wet |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ferro rod | Fast | Low | Yes |
| Magnifying lens | Slow | Medium | No, needs sun |
| Battery and steel wool | Fast | Low | Yes, if the wool stays dry |
| Friction | Very slow | High | Hard |

## Ferro rod

A ferro rod throws sparks when struck with a steel blade or the back of a knife. Hold the rod close to the tinder and scrape firmly downward, aiming the sparks into it. It works even when wet, and one rod can last for thousands of strikes.

## Magnifying lens

Focus sunlight onto dry, fine tinder until it smoulders. Use a clear, undamaged lens and hold it steady until a bright spot forms. Keep the tinder dry, and blow gently once it glows.

## Battery and steel wool

Touch fine steel wool across the two ends of a battery. The wire glows and can light fine tinder. Use a small battery and keep the wool dry. Do not leave the battery shorted for long, because it gets hot.

## Friction

A hand drill or bow drill creates heat by friction. It takes skill and practice, and it is very tiring. Learn it when you have time, not in an emergency.

![side view: bow-drill with spindle in a fireboard notch, bow above, coals forming in a dust pile, arrow-both-ways along the bow; labels Spindle, Bow, Fireboard, Ember; the ember forms slowly, so keep the dust collecting. NEEDS: bow-drill.](assets/lighting-a-fire-without-matches-5.svg)

## Keep a backup

- Store matches in a waterproof container.
- Carry a ferro rod and a lighter.
- Keep a magnifying lens in your kit, and check its surface for cracks.

## Adjusting for conditions

- **In wind,** shelter the tinder with your body or a cupped hand, and make the spark or the glowing wool contact the tinder directly.
- **In wet weather,** a ferro rod is the most reliable choice, because the sparks do not depend on dryness. Keep the tinder under cover.
- **In low sun or at dusk,** the lens will fail. Switch to the ferro rod or battery method early, instead of waiting for light to improve.

## Common mistakes

- Trying a method for the first time when the fire is urgent and the light is failing.
- Striking the ferro rod with a blade that is too thin, which blunts the spark.
- Using damp or coarse tinder, which will never catch regardless of the spark.
- Leaving a battery shorted with steel wool attached, which heats quickly and can burn.
## Practice before you need it

Once each season, set aside an hour to practise every method you carry. Start with the ferro rod on a dry tinder nest, then try the lens on a sunny day, and finally the battery and wool under supervision. Record which method lit the tinder first and how many attempts it took. Keep only the methods that work in your own hands.

## Troubleshooting

If sparks do not catch, check that the tinder is dry and very fine, then bring the rod closer. If the lens does not smoulder, the sun may be too weak, so try again in brighter light and hold it still. If the steel wool burns out before lighting the tinder, use a finer wool or a thicker tinder bundle that can take the heat.
