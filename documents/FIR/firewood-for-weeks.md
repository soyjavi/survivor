# Firewood for Weeks

Firewood is the heating and cooking fuel that most homes can still use when gas and power fail. Plan the amount for the whole cold period, and build the stock up before it is needed. Running short in the middle of winter is the most common problem.

## Why it matters

A cold spell rarely arrives on schedule. Deliveries stop during storms, roads flood, and wood that was cheap in autumn becomes scarce in January. A household that runs out is forced to burn wet or unseasoned wood, or to remove doors and furniture, which is both dangerous and wasteful. Counting the stock early turns an emergency into an ordinary routine.

## Estimate the amount

- A small stove heating one room uses about 1 to 2 cubic metres of seasoned wood per month in a cold season.
- Cooking adds more, especially if you cook every day on the same stove.
- Add a margin of at least 30 per cent for bad weather and wet fuel.
- Write the calculation down, so the stock can be checked against it each month.

## Equipment and materials

- A splitting maul or axe, and a chopping block at a comfortable height
- Heavy gloves, safety glasses and sturdy boots
- Pallets or bricks to lift the stack off the ground
- A tarpaulin or roofing sheet for the top of the stack
- A stack marker, such as a row of stakes, to show the months of drying

## Gather and season

1. Collect dead, dry wood from the ground and from standing dead trees.
2. Split the wood, because split wood dries much faster.
3. Stack it off the ground, with the top covered and the sides open to air.
4. Leave it for at least six months, or longer for thick logs.

## Hardwood and softwood

| Type | Burn behaviour | Drying time | Best use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hardwood, such as oak or ash | Dense, long-lasting heat | Longer, often a year for thick logs | Steady warmth and overnight fires |
| Softwood, such as pine or spruce | Quick, bright flame | Shorter, but sap and resin build-up | Kindling and fast starts |

Mix the two with care. Softwood cooks quickly and leaves more creosote in a flue, while hardwood gives a steady bed of coals.

## Test dryness

Knock two pieces together. Seasoned wood sounds hollow and light. Wood that feels heavy or sounds dull is still wet. Check the end grain as well, where cracks and a greyer colour often show that the wood has dried.

## Keep a supply

- Store the wood under cover, away from the house.
- Rotate the stack, using the oldest wood first.
- Keep a dry supply inside the house for a few days.

## Adjusting for conditions

- **In a wet climate,** store wood under a roof on all sides, not only on top, and raise the stack higher so the ground stays dry.
- **In a dry climate,** check for insects and rot less often, but protect the stack from wind-blown embers and dust.
- **For a small household,** keep a rolling stock of about one month ahead, and top up each month.
- **For a large household,** split and stack the wood in batches, so the wood that will be burned next winter is already seasoning.

## Common mistakes

- Waiting until the first cold week to order wood, when prices and availability are at their worst.
- Stacking wood directly on wet soil, which lets moisture wick into the bottom layer.
- Covering the whole stack with plastic, which traps moisture and encourages mould.
- Burning the freshest logs first and leaving the seasoned wood for later.
- Estimating from memory rather than the written calculation.

## Practice before you need it

Run the stove for a full week using only the stock you plan to keep. Note how many days the wood lasts, how much goes into cooking and how the burn rate changes in the coldest nights. Use that trial to correct the estimate before the real emergency arrives.

## Troubleshooting

If the wood smokes, hisses or will not catch, it is not seasoned. Mix it with dry wood in small amounts, or set it aside for later and use it only in an emergency. If the stack has turned black and soft, the bottom layer is rotting. Lift it onto new supports and remove the damaged pieces before they spread decay to the rest.

## Checklist

- [ ] Amount estimated with a margin

## When it fails

If the wood smokes, hisses or will not catch, it is not seasoned. Mix it with dry wood in small amounts, or set it aside for later and use it only in an emergency.

**Warning:** do not burn treated timber, painted wood or plastics. They release toxic fumes.
