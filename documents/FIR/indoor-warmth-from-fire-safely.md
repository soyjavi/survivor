# Indoor Warmth from Fire Safely

A fire in a home is only safe in a stove or fireplace designed for it, with a working flue. Anything else is a serious risk, because smoke and carbon monoxide need a clear route out of the building. Warmth always comes second to the flue.

## Why it matters

A stove heats a room well only when its smoke leaves the house quickly and its air supply is controlled. When either part fails, the room fills with fumes while the fire keeps burning. Many people who are harmed by indoor fires were not careless with the fire itself. They simply did not notice the flue was not drawing, or they assumed a closed door was enough protection.

## Use only a proper stove

- A stove or fireplace with a flue or chimney that carries smoke outside.
- A flue that is clean, undamaged and built for the appliance. See Chimney and Flue Safety.
- Enough fresh air for the fire to burn fully, without a draught strong enough to pull smoke into the room.

## Never use indoors

- Open fires on a floor or in a room with no flue.
- Barbecues, camping stoves or braziers.
- Generators or gas heaters not made for indoor use. See Carbon Monoxide: Never Burn Indoors.

## Before you light

1. Check that the flue is clear from the base and that the cap is in place.
2. Make sure the room has a window or vent that can be opened, and that an extractor fan is not running.
3. Lay out the wood, kindling and a bucket of water or sand before you light anything.
4. Test the draw by holding a lit paper or match near the open stove door. Smoke should be pulled into the stove.

## Safe operation

1. Burn only dry, seasoned wood.
2. Keep flammable items at least one metre from the stove.
3. Keep a metal guard around the stove if there are children.
4. Let ash cool completely before removing it, and put it in a metal bin outside.

## Clearances at a glance

| Item | Minimum distance from the stove | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Furniture and curtains | One metre | Move them before lighting |
| Bedding and clothing | One metre | Never dry clothes on or above the stove |
| Children and pets | Behind a guard | Guard must be fixed firmly |
| Wall behind the stove | As the maker specifies | Check the installation label |

## Check for smoke

Smoke entering the room, a strong smell of smoke, or soot on the walls means the flue is blocked or leaking. Put out the fire and fix the problem before using it again.

## Adjusting for conditions

- **On cold nights,** the stove is tempted to run hot. Load it moderately and keep the air control in its middle range.
- **In damp or windy weather,** the flue may draw poorly or push smoke back. Check the draw more often, and open a window slightly if the room is airtight.
- **With several rooms,** heat only the room you occupy. A closed door keeps smoke and warmth in the stove room.
- **With humidity,** burning wet wood makes more smoke and deposits more creosote. Use seasoned wood only.

## Common mistakes

- Leaving the stove running overnight unless the appliance is specifically designed for it.
- Overloading the firebox, which makes the stove glow and damage the flue.
- Drying clothes or towels on the stove rail.
- Placing a rug, basket or log pile within a metre of the stove.
- Opening the stove door while the fire is burning hard, which can throw sparks and smoke into the room.

## Practice before you need it

Before the cold season, practise lighting and loading the stove in daylight, with the windows open and a bucket beside it. Learn where the air control sits, how far it turns for a hot fire and how to close the door safely. Note how long the room takes to warm and how quickly the stove cools when shut down.

## Troubleshooting

If the room fills with smoke when you open the stove door, close it at once and check the flue draw. Smoke coming back into the room usually means a cold flue, a blocked cap or a strong extractor fan elsewhere in the house. If the stove glows red or the flue roars, reduce the air supply, let the fire settle and stop loading it until the stove cools.

## Checklist

- [ ] Stove approved for indoor use
- [ ] Flue clear and sealed
- [ ] Clearance kept from furniture and bedding
- [ ] Carbon monoxide alarm working

## When it fails

If the room fills with smoke, open the door, leave and close it behind you. Put the fire out only if it is safe to do so, then call for help.

**Warning:** never sleep in a room with a fire that is not designed for overnight use.
