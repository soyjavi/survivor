# Campfire Cooking Safety

Cooking over an open fire is one of the most common ways people get burned in camp. Pots tip, sleeves catch, and embers jump onto dry ground. Most of these accidents are predictable and can be prevented with a few habits set up before the first flame is lit.

## Setting the Fire Up

Build the cooking fire in a spot you have already cleared and checked.

1. Choose a flat area with no overhanging branches, dry grass or roots that could smoulder underground.
2. Clear a circle at least three metres wide down to bare soil or stone.
3. Build a ring of rocks around the fire to contain it and keep embers from rolling out.
4. Keep a bucket of water, a shovel and a fire blanket within arm's reach before you light anything.

## Keeping People at a Safe Distance

Heat and flying sparks reach further than most people expect.

- Set a boundary line at least two metres from the fire, and ask everyone to stay behind it unless they are actively cooking.
- Place seating so that no one has to walk past the fire to get to the water or the shelter.
- Keep the cooking area away from tents, fuel containers and gas canisters.
- Never leave a fire unattended while it is hot enough to cook on.

## Clothing and Hands

Loose clothing and long sleeves are the usual cause of injuries at the fire.

- Roll up or fasten long sleeves before reaching over flames or coals.
- Avoid nylon, polyester and other synthetic fabrics near heat, because they melt onto skin.
- Use proper gloves or a folded towel to handle hot pans, and never grab a pot handle that has been sitting over the fire.
- Keep hair tied back and remove dangling scarves or cords.

## Children and Animals

Children move quickly, and their curiosity puts them close to heat.

- Give children a specific job away from the fire, such as gathering water or sorting wood.
- Teach them the rule of stopping, dropping and rolling if their clothing catches.
- Keep pets tethered well away from the fire ring and cooking area.

## Water Nearby

Water is your first response to a spreading fire, and it is also needed for cooking and cleaning.

- Keep at least two full containers within reach, not stored across the camp.
- Pour water on the base of the fire rather than the flames to cool the embers.
- Do not let a pot of water boil dry over the fire, because the metal can warp and the contents can spill.

## Extinguishing the Fire Safely

When cooking is finished, the fire is not finished until the embers are cold to the touch.

1. Let the fire burn down and spread the remaining embers into a thin layer.
2. Pour water slowly over the whole area, watching for steam and sizzling.
3. Stir the ashes with a stick or shovel and add more water until nothing hisses.
4. Press the back of your hand close to the ash and hold it there. If you feel heat, add more water.
5. Only leave once the area is cool and wet to the touch.

Sand and soil can help smother embers, but they do not cool them as well as water.
