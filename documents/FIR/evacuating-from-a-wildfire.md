# Evacuating from a Wildfire

Leave early. Waiting to see the fire is the most common mistake, and it leaves little time to get out safely. Smoke, falling embers and blocked roads can arrive faster than most people expect, so a warning is the signal to go, not the moment to start preparing.

## Why it matters

Wildfire moves with the wind and can cover a long distance in minutes. The danger is not only the flames. Dense smoke cuts visibility on the road, embers can start spot fires ahead of the front, and power and water supplies often fail. Every minute you spend deciding is a minute taken from your margin. A plan made in daylight, with the family in the room, is far safer than one improvised at night with smoke in the air.

![map: map with two arrow-straight routes leaving a fire zone in different directions, flame at the zone, waypoint-pin meeting point opposite the fire, compass-rose; labels Primary route, Alternate route, Meeting point; plan two routes and a destination off the fire's path.](assets/evacuating-from-a-wildfire-2.svg)

## Before the fire comes

1. Keep the car fuelled and the go bag packed in a known place.
2. Know two ways out of the area, and a destination that is not in the same direction as the fire.
3. Write down the family's contact list and a meeting point outside the area.
4. Keep documents and medicines together so they can be grabbed in one movement.

## Packing the go bag

- Water, at least a few litres per person, and easy snacks that need no cooking.
- A battery radio with spare batteries, and a phone charger that works in the car.
- Medicines, glasses, hearing aids and copies of prescriptions.
- Important documents in a waterproof bag, including identity papers and insurance details.
- A torch, a first aid kit, a mask or cloth for smoke, and a change of clothes.

## When a warning comes

1. Leave immediately when told to. Do not wait for the flames to appear.
2. Take the go bag, medicines, documents, pets and phone charger.
3. Close windows and doors, but leave them unlocked so responders can enter.
4. Turn off gas and propane at the source, if there is time.
5. Wear long sleeves, long trousers, sturdy boots and a mask or cloth over the nose and mouth.

## On the road

- Drive with headlights on, slowly, and follow official routes only.
- Keep windows closed and the air vent set to recirculate, to limit smoke.
- Do not stop to take photos, and do not drive through smoke you cannot see through.

## If you cannot leave

- Stay inside a building that is solid, with doors and windows closed.
- Fill the bath with water and keep wet towels for the doors and vents.
- Stay away from outside walls and windows, and lie low on the floor if smoke enters.
- Call for help and tell them exactly where you are.

## Adjusting for conditions

- **At night,** smoke and embers are harder to see. Start earlier and use a lit torch for signalling.
- **With children,** agree who carries which item and keep them within sight at every stop.
- **With mobility limits,** tell the emergency services before a warning, so help can be arranged.
- **With pets and livestock,** move animals early in the day, and never leave them tied in a paddock.
- **Without a car,** agree in advance on a bus route, a lift or a neighbour who will drive.

## Common mistakes

- Waiting for official confirmation before packing anything at all.
- Driving toward the fire to see what is happening, or to collect belongings.
- Leaving the go bag in a cupboard that is hard to reach.
- Choosing a meeting point that lies in the same direction as the fire.
- Forgetting phone chargers, which leaves the family unable to get news.

## Practice before you need it

Once a year, run the plan as a drill. Time how long it takes to load the car and reach the first junction, using the route you chose. Check that everyone knows the meeting point and the contact list by heart. Note where the plan failed, and fix it that week, while the routine is fresh.

## Troubleshooting

If smoke or fire blocks the road, do not try to push through it. Turn back to the nearest solid building and close it up, then keep a battery radio on for official updates. If you are stuck in a queue, switch off the engine when it is safe, keep the windows shut and wait for instructions rather than driving into unknown smoke.

## When it fails

If smoke or fire blocks the road, turn back to the nearest solid building rather than driving into the fire. Keep a battery radio on, and wait for official instructions.

**Warning:** sheltering in place is a last resort. Only do it when leaving is no longer possible.
