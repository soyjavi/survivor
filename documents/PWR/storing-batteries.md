# Storing Batteries

Batteries lose charge over time, and some types leak or ignite when damaged or stored badly. Store them correctly and check them regularly, because a battery that fails in a cut is a battery that was never checked.

## Why it matters

A battery store is only as good as its weakest cell. A torch that works on the first night may be flat by the third, a leaking alkaline cell can ruin the device it sits in, and a damaged lithium cell can start a fire in a cupboard. Storage practice costs little and protects the rest of the emergency kit, so treat batteries as stock that needs rotation, not as objects that can be forgotten.

## Equipment and materials

- A digital multimeter or a battery tester, for checking voltage.
- A plastic storage box with a lid, dry and fire-resistant if possible.
- Electrical tape or caps, to cover terminals.
- Insulated gloves and a dry cloth, for handling leaking cells.
- Labels and a marker, to record the purchase date and the type.

## Types

Each chemistry behaves differently in storage, so group them by type rather than by device.

- **Alkaline:** long shelf life, low risk. Good for torches and radios.
- **Lithium primary:** very long shelf life, high energy. Use for critical devices.
- **Rechargeable NiMH:** loses charge over months. Recharge before use.
- **Lithium-ion:** used in phones and power banks. Store partly charged, around half, in a cool place.

## Storage rules

Good storage slows the loss of charge and prevents short circuits.

1. Keep batteries cool and dry, away from heat and direct sun.
2. Store them in their packaging, or in a plastic box with the terminals covered.
3. Keep different types apart, and never mix old and new cells in one device.
4. Label each box with the date of purchase.

## Step-by-step inventory

Once a season, go through the stock in a fixed order, so nothing is skipped.

1. Take every battery out of its device and sort it by type.
2. Check each cell with the meter, and note the reading on the label.
3. Set aside any cell that reads low, leaks or is swollen.
4. Recharge the rechargeable cells that are in the store, then return them to their boxes.
5. Put the oldest stock at the front of each box.

## Check regularly

A stored battery can look fine and still be flat or leaking.

- Test batteries every few months with a meter or a device.
- Replace leaking, swollen or corroded batteries at once.
- Recharge rechargeable stock at least every few months.

## Adjusting for temperature

Heat is the enemy of most batteries, because it speeds the loss of charge and can cause swelling. Cold reduces the energy a battery can deliver, which matters for torches used outdoors. A dry room between about 10 and 20 °C is a good store. Do not keep batteries in a loft or in a shed that is exposed to direct sun, and do not expect a cold battery to deliver its rated power until it has warmed up.

## Disposal

Dispose of used batteries as your local rules say. Many areas have collection points for batteries, and shops often accept them.

Never burn them, and never put them in a fire. Heat can make a cell vent or burst.

## Common mistakes

- Storing batteries loose in a drawer, where metal objects can touch the terminals.
- Keeping batteries in a hot car or near a heater.
- Assuming a rechargeable cell is always full.
- Leaving batteries inside a torch for months, where a slow leak can damage the contacts.

## Troubleshooting

- **A device works with one battery and not two:** the cells are mismatched. Remove them, test each one, and use only cells of the same age and type.
- **A battery is warm after a short use:** remove it, place it on a non-flammable surface, and test it only once it has cooled.

## Checklist

1. Each type stored separately.
2. Terminals covered.
3. Dates written on each box.
4. Storage cool and dry.
5. Stock tested every few months.

## When a battery fails

If a battery leaks or swells, remove it with gloves, wipe the area with a dry cloth, and place it in a sealed container. If a lithium battery is hot or smoking, move it outside onto a non-flammable surface and keep away from it until it cools.

**Warning:** damaged lithium batteries can catch fire. Keep them away from flammable material, and place them outdoors in a metal container if they are damaged.
