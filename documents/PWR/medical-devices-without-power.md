# Medical Devices Without Power

People who rely on electrical medical equipment face the greatest risk in a power cut. A device that stops can become an emergency within hours. Plan for it before the cut happens, and agree who will act if it does.

![Sorted list: table-header row (device, power source, hours left) over table-grid rows ordered by shortest backup first, arrow-straight pointing at the top row; labels device, power source, hours left; the shortest-backup device sits at the top, so it is checked first.](assets/medical-devices-without-power-1.svg)

## Why it matters

Many medical devices are built for a mains supply and have only a short battery reserve, often a few hours. A clinic or hospital can restore power or move a patient, but a home cannot. The plan must therefore be made in advance, with the timings written down, so that a person who is unwell can still act without having to work out the sums under stress.

## Equipment and materials

- A written device list, with power ratings and backup times.
- Spare batteries, charged, and a cable for each device.
- A cool box with ice packs, for medicines that must stay cold.
- A car charger or solar charger, if the device allows it.
- A contact list for the supplier, the prescriber and the emergency services.

## Identify the devices

List every device that needs power, and note how long each one can run without the mains. Include devices used only some of the time.

Common examples are oxygen concentrators, CPAP machines, nebulisers, dialysis, insulin pumps, glucose meters, hearing aids, and any battery-dependent aids.

Write the list on paper and keep a copy in the emergency bag, so that helpers can read it if the household cannot.

## Plan the power

Each device needs a plan for its own power source.

1. Ask the equipment supplier about battery backups and how long they last.
2. Keep spare batteries, charged, stored in a cool dry place.
3. Use a solar or car charger for longer outages, if the device allows it.
4. For oxygen, keep backup cylinders and know how long they last at the prescribed flow.

## Step-by-step during a cut

1. Check the device's own indicator and the time left on its battery as soon as the power fails.
2. Switch the device to its backup source, if it has one, and confirm it is running.
3. Write down the time, the battery reading and the cylinder gauge reading.
4. Recheck every hour, and move to the next source before the reserve runs out.
5. Call the supplier or the emergency number if the device cannot be kept running.

## Keep medicines cold

Insulin and some other medicines must stay cool.

Keep them in a cool box with ice packs, and keep a small thermometer inside so the temperature can be checked. Do not put them in direct contact with ice, because it can freeze them.

## Adjusting for conditions

Heat and cold both shorten battery life, so keep spare batteries in a room that stays close to normal temperature. In hot weather, device fans and dehumidifiers add load, so check whether the device can run in a cooler room. In cold weather, batteries drain faster and the equipment may need protection from frost. Speak to the prescriber before changing any dose or timing, because a medicine that is too warm or too cold may need a different plan.

## Tell the authorities

Register the household with the local emergency services or utility if the rules allow it. They can prioritise power for people who depend on equipment.

## Common mistakes

- Assuming the supplier's battery lasts as long as the box claims, when it is worn or cold.
- Storing spare batteries uncharged, so they are useless when needed.
- Forgetting that a charger in the car needs the engine running, which must be done outdoors.
- Keeping the device list on a phone that will be flat when it is needed.

## Troubleshooting

- **The device alarms but still runs:** check the cable, the power source and the connection first, then the battery reading.
- **The battery charges but drains in minutes:** replace the battery, because it has lost its capacity.

## Checklist

- Device list written and kept in the emergency bag.
- Battery and cylinder times known for each device.
- Spare batteries charged and dated.
- Medicines stored cool.
- Emergency services or utility informed.

## When power cannot be kept

- Seek medical care or a safe place with power.
- Do not run an oxygen concentrator from an unsafe source.
- Keep the person warm, calm and close to help.

**Warning:** never run oxygen near a flame or a fire. It makes fire burn much faster.
