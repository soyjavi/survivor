# Electrical Hazards After a Disaster

Electricity remains dangerous after a disaster, even when the power is off. Wet floors, damaged wiring and fallen lines can kill, and the danger often lasts for days after the storm, flood or earthquake has passed. Many people are hurt not by the event itself but by the clean-up that follows.

## Why it matters

After a disaster, damage to the grid is often invisible from the street, and power can return without warning when a utility restores a line elsewhere. A circuit that was dead an hour ago may be live now. Treat the whole property as possibly live until a qualified person has said otherwise.

## Fallen lines

A line that is down may still carry current, and it can energise the ground, fences, puddles and metal objects around it. Stay back and make others stay back too.

- Treat every power line as live, even if it looks dead.
- Stay at least 10 metres away, and keep children, pets and onlookers away too.
- Call the utility or emergency services. Do not try to move the line or anything touching it.
- If a vehicle touches a line, stay inside unless you must leave because of fire. Jump clear, with feet together, and shuffle away with small steps so that both feet never leave the ground at the same time.

## Flooded buildings

Water conducts electricity. Cables that run under floors or up walls can stay live even when the rooms look empty, so the supply must be checked before anyone goes inside.

- Do not enter a flooded building until the power has been switched off at the main supply by a qualified person.
- Do not touch switches, sockets or appliances in wet areas.
- Do not stand in water when operating anything electrical.

## Equipment and materials

Keep a small kit ready for inspection work, and use it only from a dry surface.

- A torch with fresh batteries, and rubber-soled boots.
- A non-contact voltage tester. A silent tester does not prove that a circuit is dead.

## Damaged wiring

Damage can show up as scorch marks on sockets, a crackling sound, or a plug that feels hot. Act on these signs at once.

- Look for scorch marks, sparks, a burning smell or a warm socket.
- Turn off the power at the main supply if it is safe to do so.
- Do not use appliances that were wet until an electrician has checked them.

## Step-by-step in detail

Work through the steps in order, and do not skip ahead because the room looks dry.

1. Stay outside the building until you have checked the outside for fallen lines and standing water.
2. Find the meter box and the main switch from a dry spot. Do not touch either while standing in water.
3. If you can reach the main switch safely and dry, switch the supply off. If you cannot, call the utility and wait for it.
4. Walk the route into the building on dry ground only.
5. Check each socket for scorch marks before using it, and do not plug anything into a damaged socket.
6. Once the supply is confirmed off, leave switches alone until a qualified electrician has inspected the wiring.

## Adjusting for conditions

Each kind of disaster brings its own main risk.

| Event | Main hazard | First action |
|---|---|---|
| Flood | Live cables under standing water | Stay out until the supply is off |
| Earthquake | Exposed wiring in cracked walls | Keep clear of damaged walls and ceilings |
| Storm | Fallen lines and debris on the ground | Keep 10 metres from every line |

## Common mistakes

- Assuming a socket is dead because the lamp next to it is off.
- Wiping water from a socket with a cloth while the supply is still on.

## Safe practices

- Use only dry hands and dry tools near electrical equipment.
- Keep extension cables off wet ground, and never run them under rugs or doors.
- Unplug appliances that were in water, and leave them unplugged until they are inspected.

## Checklist

1. Assume every line and wire is live.
2. Check that the path is dry before you step onto it.
3. Confirm the main supply is off before you touch any wiring.
4. Call a qualified electrician before reconnecting anything that was wet.

## When something goes wrong

- If a person is in contact with a live source, do not touch them with bare hands. Switch off the supply if you can reach it safely, or move the source away with a dry wooden or plastic object, then call emergency services.
- If you smell burning or see smoke from a socket, leave the area and call the fire service from outside.
- If a person has been shocked, call for help at once. Start first aid only once the danger has been removed.
