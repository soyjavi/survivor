# Oil Lamps and Candles Safely

Candles and oil lamps are cheap and effective, but they cause many home fires during power cuts. The flame is small, yet it can ignite curtains, bedding and paper within minutes. Use them with care, and treat every flame as a hazard that needs watching.

## Why it matters

Power cuts raise the number of open flames in a home, and they often happen at night, when people are tired and rooms are dark. A candle that would be harmless in daylight becomes dangerous when it is moved to a bedside table and forgotten. Most fires start with a flame that was left alone for a few minutes, so the habits matter as much as the equipment.

## Equipment and materials

- Heat-resistant candle holders with a wide, flat base.
- A lamp designed for the fuel it burns, with an intact glass chimney.
- Matches or a lighter, kept in a metal or sealed container.
- A fire blanket or a bucket of water, placed close to the lighting area.
- Sealed, labelled containers for lamp oil.

## Candles

A candle is safe only when it is stable, clear of anything flammable, and watched.

- Place them on a stable, heat-resistant holder, on a flat surface away from curtains and paper.
- Keep them at least 30 cm from anything that can catch fire.
- Never leave a candle burning in an empty room or while you sleep.
- Put them out by snuffing, not by blowing near a child, because blowing can spray hot wax.

## Oil lamps

Oil lamps give better light than candles for reading, but they hold fuel and must be handled as carefully as a fire.

- Fill the lamp outdoors and away from any flame.
- Use the fuel the lamp is made for. Do not use petrol or solvents, which vaporise easily and can explode.
- Trim the wick evenly, and keep the glass clean so the flame burns steadily.
- Place the lamp on a firm base, away from walls and curtains.
- Turn it down or off before you leave the room.

## Step-by-step lighting

1. Check the holder is stable and the surface is flat and dry.
2. Clear the area within 30 cm of every flame.
3. Light the wick or candle with a long match or lighter, keeping your hand below the flame.
4. Adjust the wick for a steady, blue-based flame, and replace the chimney.
5. Note the time, and plan to put the flame out before sleep or before leaving the room.

## Air quality

Candles and lamps produce soot and smoke. In a closed room, these can irritate the lungs and make the air unpleasant over hours. Keep a window slightly open and do not use them in a sealed room for long periods.

## Adjusting for draughts and pets

Draughts make flames flicker, lean and sometimes go out, and a leaning flame can touch a curtain. Place lights out of the path of doors and fans. Pets and small children move unpredictably, so keep the lit area behind a barrier or on a high shelf that cannot be reached from a bed or chair.

## Storage

Fuel and wax stored badly add risk before any lamp is lit.

- Store candles in a dry, cool place away from heat.
- Keep oil in sealed containers, labelled, away from the lamp area.

## Common mistakes

- Placing a candle on a shelf above a bed, where rising heat can ignite the bedding.
- Filling a hot lamp, which can spill fuel onto the burner.
- Using a lamp with a cracked glass chimney.
- Leaving a candle to burn down in a jar, which can crack the glass and spread heat.

## Troubleshooting

- **Smoke or soot from the lamp:** the wick is too high or uneven. Lower it, trim it and clean the chimney.
- **The flame keeps going out:** the wick may be dirty or the air is moving too much. Move the lamp to a sheltered spot.

## Checklist

1. Holder stable and on a flat, heat-resistant surface.
2. Nothing flammable within 30 cm.
3. Room ventilated.
4. Lamp filled outdoors, with the correct fuel.
5. Flames out before sleeping or leaving the room.

## When a flame gets out of hand

If the fabric or paper near a flame catches, use a damp cloth or a fire blanket to smother it, or move a bucket of water close by. Do not use water on an oil fire. Leave the room if the fire spreads, and call the fire service.

**Warning:** a child or pet can knock over a lamp or candle. Keep them out of reach, and keep the area clear of fabric.
