# Power Cut Checklist

A power cut can last hours or weeks. These steps keep the household safe from the start, and they set the pace for the days that follow. Print this list and keep it with the emergency supplies, because a phone may be dead when it is most needed.

![Horizontal timeline: timeline-axis with three timeline-marker items, a step-box of tasks under each; labels first hour, first day, first week; early tasks such as medical needs come before comfort tasks such as the budget.](assets/power-cut-checklist-1.svg)

## Why it matters

Most of the harm in a cut comes from reactions made in the dark: a generator started indoors, a candle left near a curtain, a fridge opened ten times in an hour. A printed sequence removes the need to think clearly while tired and frightened. It also makes sure that the important early tasks, such as checking on people who depend on equipment, happen before the comfortable ones.

## Equipment and materials

- A printed copy of this checklist, kept in the emergency bag.
- A battery or hand-crank radio, with spare batteries.
- Torches and lanterns, with spare batteries stored separately.
- A notebook and pen for the cut log.
- A list of contacts: utility, neighbours, the doctor and anyone who needs care.

## First hour

The first hour decides how well the rest of the cut goes. Work through these steps in order.

1. Check whether neighbours are affected, and whether the cut is local or wider. A wider cut often means a longer wait.
2. Turn off appliances you were using, to avoid surges when the power returns.
3. Keep the fridge and freezer closed. See Fridge and Freezer During a Cut.
4. Find torches and light sources, and check the water is stored.
5. Check on people who depend on medical equipment.

## First day

By the first day, the household should be running on a plan rather than on reactions.

1. Listen to the radio for official information.
2. Start the energy budget. See Energy Budgeting.
3. Use the most perishable food first.
4. Set up the heating room and close off the others.

## First week

A week into a cut, the question is how long the supplies will last.

1. Review the stock of water, food, fuel and medicines.
2. Keep a written log of supplies and events.
3. Check the power supply plan, and adjust the budget.
4. Check neighbours who may be struggling.

## Adjusting for the household

Every household needs a different version of this list. Households with infants need extra formula, nappies and a way to warm bottles safely. Older people may need help with stairs in the dark. Households with pets must plan for water and for cages or doors that rely on power. Add these items to the printed copy, and tick them off during the first hour, not later.

## Do not

These are the most common causes of injury and death during a cut.

- Do not use generators, fires or cookers indoors.
- Do not plug a generator into the house wiring.
- Do not touch fallen power lines.

## Common mistakes

- Opening the fridge repeatedly to check the food.
- Running several appliances at once when power returns.
- Waiting until the water runs out before checking the stock.
- Relying on a single phone for news, torch and contacts.

## Troubleshooting

- **The phone battery is almost flat:** switch it to its lowest power mode, close apps, and use the radio for news. Save the phone for calls.
- **Nothing in the house works after the power returns:** check the main switch and the breaker before calling the utility.

## Practice before you need it

Run a short drill once a year, at night, with the main supply switched off for a few minutes if it is safe to do so. Time how long it takes to find the torches, the radio and the medicine list. Check that each adult knows where the main switch and the water stopcock are. Note every gap in the printed copy and fix it before the next drill.

## Checklist for quick reference

- Neighbours checked.
- Appliances off.
- Lights ready.
- Medical needs covered.
- Log started.

## When the cut goes on

If the cut passes the first week, move to a longer routine. Check supplies every two days, rotate fuel, and keep one day in reserve for emergencies. Ask the utility or local authority for updates at fixed times.

**Warning:** when power returns, switch on appliances one at a time, so the surge does not damage them.
