# Fridge and Freezer During a Cut

A fridge and freezer are the biggest food-saving appliances in a home, and the biggest energy users when they run on a generator or battery. Manage them with care, because a little planning can keep food safe for days and save fuel at the same time.

## Why it matters

Food loss is the first cost of a long cut, and it is the one most families underestimate. A freezer full of meat can be worth a week of shopping. Keeping the appliances closed and cold also protects the fuel you need for lighting, charging and medical equipment, so the fridge is part of the power plan as well as the food plan.

## Equipment and materials

- A fridge thermometer and, if possible, a second one for the freezer.
- Bottles of water, frozen where possible, to fill empty space.
- A cool box with lid, for food taken out during the cut.
- A marker and paper, to note the time the power failed and each temperature check.

## Keep them closed

Every time the door opens, cold air escapes and warm air comes in. Closed appliances hold their temperature far longer than open ones.

- A closed freezer keeps food frozen for about a day and a half if it is full, and about a day if it is half full. A full freezer holds its cold better because the frozen food itself acts as a store of cold.
- A closed fridge keeps food cold for about four hours.
- Open the doors only when you need something, and decide what you want before you open them.

## Run them on backup power

If the cut lasts longer than the stored cold, the appliances must run on backup power. Choose the source carefully and check the temperature as you go.

1. Check the fuel or charge available before starting.
2. If you have a generator or battery, run the freezer first, then the fridge.
3. Run them in short cycles to save fuel, if the appliance allows it.
4. Check temperature with a thermometer. The fridge should stay below about 5 °C and the freezer frozen.

## Use the cold well

Ice and cold water hold temperature far better than air, so use them to fill the appliance.

- Fill empty space with bottles of water, which hold cold for longer.
- Put frozen bottles in the fridge to cool it, and replace them when they melt.
- Store the food that spoils fastest near the bottom, where it is coldest.

## Adjusting for conditions

Hot weather shortens every figure in this guide. In a warm room, the fridge warms faster and the freezer loses cold sooner, so check the temperature more often and move food to a cool box early. In cold weather, food can stay safe outdoors in a sealed container, but it must not freeze and thaw repeatedly, and it must be kept away from animals.

## When to empty

Food safety depends on temperature and time, not on how the food looks or smells. Check the thermometer before deciding.

If the temperature goes above 5 °C for more than two hours, throw away food that is perishable: meat, fish, dairy, cooked food, and any food that has thawed and refrozen.

## Common mistakes

- Opening the freezer to check on food, which lets the cold escape.
- Refreezing food that has thawed completely, which is unsafe.
- Running a generator in a closed space to keep food cold, which can kill.
- Leaving the thermometer out of the appliance, so the reading shows the room rather than the food.

## Troubleshooting

- **The fridge will not cool on backup power:** the generator or battery may be too small. Switch the freezer on first, and run the fridge only when the freezer is stable.
- **Water in the freezer tray:** the seal may be damaged or the door was left open. Wipe it dry, and check the seal before the next cut.

## Checklist

1. Keep both appliances closed.
2. Fill empty space with water bottles or ice.
3. Check the temperature twice a day.
4. Plan backup power so the freezer runs first.
5. Note which food must be eaten first.

## When it fails

If the power does not return and the temperature rises past the safe limit, eat or cook the food that is still safe, and discard the rest. Do not taste food to test it.

**Warning:** never run a generator indoors or in a garage to power a fridge. See Generator Safety.
