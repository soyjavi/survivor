# Planning Power Autonomy

Autonomy means being able to meet your power needs without the grid for a set time. Choose a target, then build towards it in stages. Most households overspend on a large system before the basics are covered, so the order matters as much as the size.

## Why it matters

A plan turns a vague wish for resilience into a list of purchases and tasks. Without one, households buy whatever is on offer, then discover that the torches have no batteries, the generator has no fuel and the solar panel cannot charge the phone at night. Writing the target down also shows what it will cost in fuel, space and money before anything is bought.

## Equipment and materials

- A notebook or spreadsheet for the target, the essential list and the stage checklist.
- The energy budget for the household, from the guide Energy Budgeting.
- Storage space that is dry, cool and away from fuel.
- A test log, for dates and results of each check.

## Choose a target

A practical target for most households is three to seven days for essentials: light, communication, medical devices and small appliances. Longer targets need more equipment and fuel, and fuel supply is often the first thing to run short in a wide outage.

Write the target down, together with the list of essentials it must cover. Without that list, the target has no meaning.

## Build in stages

Each stage adds a layer of independence, and each one works on its own.

1. **Stage one:** torches, lanterns, a radio with spare batteries, power banks.
2. **Stage two:** a small solar panel with a charge controller, and a storage battery.
3. **Stage three:** a generator for essential loads, with fuel stored safely.

## Work through an example

Take a household with two adults, one child and a fridge. The target is four days for essentials. The energy budget shows about 1,500 watt-hours per day for lights, charging, the fridge and a radio. Four days therefore need about 6,000 watt-hours of usable storage, or about 7,500 watt-hours of nominal capacity once losses are included. Stage one covers the lights and radio, stage two supplies the fridge and charging, and stage three is only needed if the cut is expected to pass the four-day mark.

## Keep it balanced

A plan works when its parts match. A large battery with no charger is wasted, and a generator with no fuel is only a heavy object.

- Do not buy one large system before the basics are covered.
- Keep a list of what each stage powers, and test it.
- Review the target once a year, because household needs change.

## Adjusting for climate

A cold climate needs more fuel and heating, and less solar output in winter. A hot climate needs more cooling and battery protection from heat. A region with frequent storms should favour portable, moveable parts over fixed ones. Set the target for the worst season, not the average one, and write the seasonal exceptions next to the target.

## Maintain

Equipment that is never used will fail when it is needed. Build maintenance into the calendar.

- Service the generator and test the battery each season.
- Clean solar panels and check the wiring.
- Rotate fuel and batteries.

## Plan the use

Work out which devices run on which source, so the most important loads are protected. See Energy Budgeting.

![Calendar strip: timeline-axis across a year, timeline-marker for each month, a larger timeline-marker at each season change, a distinct timeline-marker for the annual full test; labels months, seasons, annual test; checks cluster where weather and fuel needs change.](assets/planning-power-autonomy-4.svg)

## Common mistakes

- Buying a generator without storing enough fuel to run it for the whole target period.
- Testing the system only once, at installation.
- Assuming that the stage one kit covers medical needs, which usually require more.
- Counting nominal battery capacity as usable energy.

## Troubleshooting

- **The system falls short of the target in a test:** log the day, the load and the weather, then compare with the budget.
- **A stage works in summer but not winter:** the target was set for the wrong season. Revise it and add capacity at the weak stage.

## Checklist

- Target chosen and written down.
- Essential loads listed and ranked.
- Stage one complete before stage two begins.
- Annual review date set.
- Maintenance tasks scheduled.

## When the plan falls short

If the system cannot meet the target, first reduce the load, then add capacity at the stage where the gap is. Do not add a new stage until the one below it works reliably.

**Warning:** autonomy is only real if it is tested. Run the system for a whole day before you rely on it.
