# Generator Safety

A generator gives useful power, but it kills people every year through carbon monoxide poisoning. The gas it produces is colourless and odourless, and it builds up quickly in buildings, garages and sheds. Run it only outside and well away from the house.

## Why it matters

Most generator deaths happen to people who ran the machine for warmth or light in a place they thought was safe, such as a garage with the door open or a porch under a window. Exhaust does not stay where it is released. It moves with the wind and pours through gaps, and a person who falls asleep nearby may never wake. Good generator practice is about distance, air flow and monitoring, not about the machine itself.

## Equipment and materials

- A portable generator rated above the total wattage you plan to run.
- A heavy-duty outdoor extension cable of the correct gauge, with no damage to its sheath.
- Approved fuel containers, labelled, and kept outside the living area.
- A working carbon monoxide alarm, with fresh batteries.
- A small ground mat or pallet, to keep the generator dry and level.

## Placement

Where the generator sits matters as much as how it runs. Exhaust must be carried away from every opening.

- At least 6 metres from doors, windows and vents, and downwind of them.
- On a dry, level surface, outdoors and never in a garage, shed or basement.
- Protected from rain by a cover that does not enclose the sides, because an enclosed generator fills with exhaust.

## Fuel

Fuel is both a fire hazard and a supply that must last.

- Use only the fuel the manufacturer specifies.
- Store fuel in approved containers, away from the generator and the house.
- Let the generator cool before refuelling, and never refuel while it is running.

## Connections

Connecting a generator wrongly can kill people far from the house. Power sent back into the grid can reach line workers who believe the lines are dead.

- Plug devices into the generator directly, or use a heavy-duty extension cable rated for outdoor use.
- Never connect a generator to the home's wiring without a qualified electrician fitting a transfer switch. Backfeeding can kill line workers.
- Do not overload it. Add up the wattage of the devices first, and leave a margin below the rated output.

## Step-by-step start-up

1. Confirm the generator is outdoors, on a level surface, six metres from every opening.
2. Check the oil and fuel level before starting, with the engine cold.
3. Switch on the carbon monoxide alarm, and check that it shows no fault.
4. Start the generator, let it settle, then connect devices one at a time.
5. Recheck the total wattage after each new device is added.

## Adjusting for weather

Rain, snow and wind change the risk. In wet weather, keep cables off puddles and use covers that leave the sides open. In strong wind, exhaust can blow back toward the house, so move the generator to a different side of the building. In cold weather, the temptation to bring a running generator into a porch is strongest, and that is exactly the place it must not go.

## Carbon monoxide

Install a carbon monoxide alarm nearby, and check it. Alarms need working batteries and must be replaced when they reach the end of their life.

## Shut down

Turn off the devices, let the generator run without load for a short time if the manual advises, then switch it off.

## Common mistakes

- Running the generator in a shed with the doors open, which still lets exhaust collect.
- Leaving a cable plugged in while the generator is refuelled.
- Trusting a carbon monoxide alarm with a flat battery.

## Troubleshooting

- **The generator stops under load:** it is overloaded. Remove the least important device and restart.
- **The output voltage fluctuates:** the engine is struggling, so reduce the load and check the fuel filter.

## Checklist

1. Generator outdoors, six metres from openings, downwind.
2. Fuel stored outside, in approved containers.
3. Wattage of devices checked against the rating.
4. Carbon monoxide alarm working.
5. Generator switched off and cooled before refuelling.

## When it fails

If the generator stops, do not restart it indoors to check. Move it outside, let it cool, and inspect the fuel and air filter before trying again. If the alarm sounds, leave the building and call for help.

**Warning:** if a person feels dizzy or ill near a generator, move everyone into fresh air at once and call for help.
