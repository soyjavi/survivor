# Lighting Without Electricity

Light helps with cooking, reading, first aid and moving safely at night. A dark house is also a hazard in itself, because falls and cuts are common when people cannot see. Choose sources that are safe to use indoors, and keep a good supply of each.

## Why it matters

Light changes how the whole household copes. People who can see can cook, read the radio, check medicines and find the toilet without a fall. Without a plan, a cut quickly turns into a series of small accidents, and most of them happen in the first night. A few well-placed, charged lights prevent most of them.

## Equipment and materials

- At least one LED torch per adult, and one smaller torch for each child.
- A head torch for cooking and repair work.
- A rechargeable lantern for shared rooms.
- Spare batteries of the correct size, stored separately from the devices.
- A small box of matches or a lighter, kept dry and out of children's reach.

## Choose the right light

Different tasks need different lights. Pick the source for the job, not one light for everything.

- **LED torch:** the best for moving around. Lasts many hours on small batteries.
- **Head torch:** keeps both hands free for work and cooking.
- **Battery or rechargeable lantern:** good for a whole room.
- **Solar lantern:** useful for daytime charging and evening light.
- **Oil lamp or candle:** see Oil Lamps and Candles Safely.

## Plan the supply

A light is only useful if it works when you reach for it. Plan the supply the way you would plan food.

1. Count the hours of light your household needs each day.
2. Keep spare batteries for each torch, stored separately from the devices.
3. Test each light every month, and replace any that flicker or dim quickly.

## Room by room

Set up lighting before the cut, so the first night needs no searching.

1. **Bedroom:** a torch within reach of the bed, switched off and placed the same way every night.
2. **Hallway and stairs:** a lantern or a torch at the top and bottom of the stairs.
3. **Kitchen:** a lantern for the work surface, and a head torch for the sink.
4. **Living room:** a lantern on a stable table, placed away from curtains and furniture edges.

## Move safely

Most injuries in a power cut happen on the stairs and in the dark between rooms.

- Keep a torch by each bed and by the main door.
- Keep paths clear of objects, and mark the steps with light-coloured tape.
- Use a low, indirect light for children at night, so they can see without being blinded.

## Adjusting for people and conditions

Children and older people need more light, and a light that is too bright can dazzle them. Use a low lamp in a corridor for them, and a brighter torch only for tasks. In winter, days are short, so the light plan must cover longer nights. In a shared shelter, light the route to the toilet first, because that is where most night trips happen.

## Save battery

Light is one of the easier loads to control, so small habits add many hours.

- Use the lowest brightness that works.
- Switch lights off when you leave a room.
- Use a lantern for a room, and a torch for movement.

## Common mistakes

- Relying on one torch with no spare batteries.
- Storing batteries in the torch, where they can drain or leak.
- Using a bright lamp for a long task in a room with no ventilation.
- Leaving a torch on a table where a child can switch it on and drain it.

## Troubleshooting

- **A torch dims quickly:** the battery is old or the bulb is weak. Replace the batteries and test again.
- **A lantern does not light:** check the charge, then the switch and the seal. Do not open it near a flame.

## Checklist

- At least one working torch for each adult, and one for each child.
- Spare batteries stored separately.
- Lantern for shared rooms.
- Torch by each bed and by the door.
- Lights tested every month.

## When it fails

If a torch dies, switch to a lantern or a candle placed safely on a stable holder. If all lights fail, stay in the room you know best and move slowly with a hand on the wall.

**Warning:** do not use a phone screen as the main light for a task. It drains the battery quickly and gives poor light.
