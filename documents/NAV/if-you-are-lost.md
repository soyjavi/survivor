# If You Are Lost

Most people who get lost are found when they stay in one place and signal. Moving in panic makes the search harder, because searchers then have to guess where you have gone and cover a much larger area.

![Decision flow: start-or-end lost, step-box stop, step-box check time and water, decision stay or move, step-box ground-signal-x or signal-fires-3, arrow-straight between; labels stop, check, signal, stay put; signalling comes before walking.](assets/if-you-are-lost-1.svg)

## Why it matters

Fear pushes people to walk fast in a random direction. Each step away from your last known point makes the search area bigger and your energy smaller. A calm first hour gives you the best chance of being found quickly.

The first hour also decides how the rest of the day goes. Shelter, water and signals are easy to arrange while you still have light and warmth, and very hard to arrange after dark, when you are cold and tired.

## Materials

- A whistle, which carries much further than the voice.
- A torch with a spare battery, and a small bright cloth.
- Water, food and a warm layer.

## Stop

1. Stop walking. Sit down and take a breath.
2. Note the time, and check how much water, food and daylight you have.
3. Look at the map and your last known point.

## Your first hour

| Minutes | Action |
|---|---|
| 0 to 5 | Stop, sit, breathe, and look at the time |
| 5 to 15 | Check water, food, daylight and the map |
| 15 to 30 | Start a shelter or find a sheltered spot |
| 30 to 60 | Set up the signal, and make the bright marker visible |

## Stay put

- If you have not been reported missing, do not go far. Move only if you cannot stay safely where you are.
- Make a shelter and stay warm and dry, before the temperature drops.
- Keep your energy up with small, regular sips of water and food.

## Signal

Use three signals, such as three whistle blasts or three fires. Three of anything is a sign that a person, not an animal or the wind, made it.

## Adjusting for conditions

- **Cold:** insulate yourself from the ground with branches or a pack, and put on every layer before you start to shiver.
- **Heat:** find shade, rest through the hottest hours, and drink small amounts often.
- **Rain:** build the shelter first, and keep the signal materials dry in a bag.
- **Wind:** place the shelter behind a bank or a row of trees, away from the open side.

## Before you go

Most lost walkers had a plan they did not share. Before any walk, tell someone where you are going, which route you will take, and when you expect to return. If you change the route, send a short message. This information lets searchers start from the right place.

## If you must move

- Follow a stream downhill, or a fence or track, which lead to people.
- Mark your route as you go. See Marking a Route.
- Do not walk in the dark. Wait for daylight.

## Troubleshooting

- **You hear voices but cannot see anyone:** signal with three whistle blasts and wait. Do not walk toward the sound until you know where it comes from.
- **Your torch dies:** keep the spare battery for the night, and switch to the whistle and the bright cloth.
- **The phone shows one bar but no call connects:** send a short text with your location, and turn the phone off to save battery.

## Common mistakes

- Walking on to find a view, which pulls you further from the search area.
- Leaving a note or marker that is too small to see from the air.
- Assuming a phone will work, and spending the battery on the wrong calls.

## Checklist

- [ ] Stopped and calmed down.
- [ ] Time, water, food and daylight checked.
- [ ] Shelter started while there is still light.
- [ ] Three-signal pattern ready.

## When it fails

If you are not found by the first night, keep the shelter, keep the signals going, and ration water carefully. Do not start a long walk on a cold, tired body. Staying put with a plan is still the safer choice.

**Warning:** do not try to cross a river or climb a steep slope while lost. The most common fatal mistakes happen then.
