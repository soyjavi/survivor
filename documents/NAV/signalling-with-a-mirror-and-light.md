# Signalling With a Mirror and Light

A signal mirror can send a bright flash across many kilometres on a clear day, far beyond what a shout or a flag can reach. Light signals at night work similarly, using torches, fires and simple codes. Both depend on aiming accurately and on a receiver who is watching. The skill is less about the equipment than about practice, patience and a plan for what each signal means.

## Aiming the Mirror

Hold the mirror near your face, with one eye looking through the sight hole or at your own reflection. Reflect sunlight onto your hand, then bring the mirror up until the spot of light falls on the target. Keep your eye on the target, not on the mirror, and move the mirror slowly toward it. The bright spot should land on the target and remain there while you move.

1. Stand facing the sun or with it at your side, never looking directly at it.
2. Raise the mirror to eye level with the sight hole toward the target.
3. Adjust until the reflected spot is visible on your palm.
4. Move the mirror so that the spot passes over the target, then hold it still.

Practise on a nearby object such as a fence post before attempting a long-range signal.

## Reflection Basics

A flat mirror reflects light at the same angle it arrives, so the beam leaves at an angle equal to the angle of incidence. This means that as the sun moves, your aim must change. Keep the sun behind or to the side of you when possible. Clean the mirror surface of dust and fingerprints, since these scatter the beam and reduce its strength.

- Use a mirror with a sight hole or a reflective surface that can be aimed precisely.
- On a hazy day, the signal may be dimmer, so work closer to the receiver.
- Water, snow and glass can help reflect light in unusual ways, but test them first.

## Signals by Light

At night, a torch or lantern can send coded flashes. Flash long and short bursts to represent a message, using a shared code such as Morse-style timing. Cover the light with a hand or cloth to shorten each flash. Keep the light steady when aimed at the receiver, and use a standard pattern repeated several times so the receiver knows it is a signal.

1. Agree a pattern beforehand, with clear start and end markers.
2. Send each signal three times before pausing.
3. Wait for a response before sending a new message.
4. Stop signalling when the receiver acknowledges, to save battery and avoid confusion.

## Fire Signals

A fire can be seen at great distance at night, and by day when smoke is produced. Smoke signals on a clear day should be white and thick, made by adding green leaves or damp material. Keep the fire controlled and never leave it unattended. Build it in a safe, cleared area away from dry grass, trees and shelters.

## Choosing a Signalling Position

Stand on a high, open point with a clear view of the area where the receiver is likely to be. Avoid signalling from inside a valley or among trees, since the light will be blocked. If you can, face the receiver's direction so that the sun is behind you and the target is in shade, which makes your signal stand out more clearly.

Once positioned, take a moment to scan the horizon for any sign of a response, such as a reflection, smoke or movement. Keep your signalling steady and repeat it at intervals rather than constantly, so that a reply is not missed.

## Practising With a Partner

Practice works best with a partner who can confirm the signal from a known distance. Agree on a set of simple signals, such as three flashes for yes and two for no, and rehearse them until both people respond without hesitation. Note the distance at which the signal is reliably seen, since this helps you plan future signals.

**Warning:** Never shine a mirror or torch into the eyes of people, animals or aircraft pilots, since bright light can cause temporary blindness. Keep fires under control and never light them near dry vegetation. Do not use signals in an emergency unless you are sure someone can see them, and carry extra batteries and fuel so that your signals do not fail when they are most needed.
