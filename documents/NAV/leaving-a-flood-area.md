# Leaving a Flood Area

Floods change routes, cut roads and hide dangers under the water. Leave early, using the highest ground and the official routes. Water that looks shallow can hide open drains, fallen debris and missing road surface.

![Depth scale: bracket scale beside car on waves, threshold lines at six inches, twelve inches, two feet, car floating at top; labels 6 in, 12 in, 2 ft; shows depths where footing is lost and a car is carried off. NEEDS: car.](assets/leaving-a-flood-area-1.svg)

## Why it matters

Most flood deaths happen to people who tried to drive or walk through water they could not judge. The moving water is far stronger than it looks, and it can carry a person or a car away quickly.

Timing matters as much as route. A road that is passable at the start of a warning can be under water an hour later, so the decision to leave should be made while the water is still low and the roads are still open.

## Materials

- A paper map of the area, with the high ground and official routes marked.
- A torch, a whistle and a charged phone, kept in a waterproof bag.
- A stick or pole, to test the ground ahead.

## Plan the route

- Use the highest roads and the known evacuation routes, which officials usually mark.
- Avoid low underpasses, bridges and roads near rivers.
- Choose a destination on higher ground, outside the flood zone.

![Cross-section: ground-line with slope low side, person-standing on raised edge, waves shallow water, shadow-stick probing stick ahead, do-not at channel; labels high edge, shallow, moving channel; stay on the higher edge.](assets/leaving-a-flood-area-3.svg)

## Before the water rises

Prepare while you still have time. Move documents, medicines and valuables to a high shelf or an upper floor. Charge the phone and the torch, and fill a bag with water, food and a warm layer. If you have been told to switch off electricity or gas at the main, do so only if it is safe to reach the switch without wading.

## On foot

1. Walk on the high side of the road, away from the water.
2. Test the ground with a stick before stepping on it.
3. Do not walk through water deeper than your knees, or flowing faster than walking speed.

## By vehicle

- Do not drive through moving water. Six inches can knock you off your feet, and about two feet can float a car.
- Never drive into water you cannot see the bottom of.
- If the vehicle stalls in water, leave it and move to higher ground.

## Adjusting for conditions

- **Darkness:** do not travel after dark unless you have no alternative. Hidden drains and debris are impossible to judge at night.
- **Fast water:** if the water is moving across the road, treat it as too deep, regardless of its visible depth.
- **Debris:** floating branches and rubbish can strike you or snag a vehicle, so keep well clear of them.
- **Cold water:** even shallow flood water can be cold enough to drain strength quickly, so keep the warm layer dry.

## Practice before you need it

Learn the highest roads in your area on a dry day. Find two routes out of each place you spend time, and time each one. Mark the high ground on your map, and discuss with the family where you will meet if the phone network fails.

## Common mistakes

- Following other vehicles through water, which may be deeper than it looks.
- Waiting too long, so the only route left is through the water.
- Going back for belongings once the water has started to rise.

## Troubleshooting

- **The road ahead is under water with no sign:** stop well before the water, turn around safely, and take the second route.
- **The car has stalled in water:** get out through the window or door, move uphill, and signal for help.
- **A neighbour needs help:** raise the alarm with the emergency number, and do not enter the water yourself.

## Checklist

- [ ] Route chosen before you leave.
- [ ] Destination on high ground.
- [ ] Vehicle left only if water is moving or unknown.
- [ ] Family told the route and the time you left.

## If you are cut off

Move to the highest safe point in the building or on the land. Signal for help. Do not wait for the water to reach the danger level before you act.

**Warning:** do not enter flood water to rescue a vehicle or belongings. Call for help instead.
