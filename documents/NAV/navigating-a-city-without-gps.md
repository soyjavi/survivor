# Navigating a City Without GPS

Cities are full of signs, landmarks and grid layouts that help you find your way. A paper map and attention to detail work better than a phone with no signal, especially in dense districts where tall buildings block the sky and confuse the compass.

![Map: map with grid streets, line-solid route, waypoint-pin landmarks (NEEDS: tower, station), river, tree park, bracket counting blocks; labels tower, station, river, park, blocks; landmarks anchor the count where the grid breaks down.](assets/navigating-a-city-without-gps-1.svg)

## Why it matters

Phone navigation fails when batteries die, networks are overloaded or signal drops in tunnels and between tall buildings. A city you cannot navigate on foot leaves you dependent on strangers, and a paper plan keeps you in control.

Cities also reward a little preparation. A map with the route marked, a few street names written down and a rough sense of where the river lies turn a confusing place into a set of familiar reference points.

## Materials

- A printed map of the area, with the route and key points marked.
- A notebook with the names of streets and landmarks.
- A battery pack for the phone, used only for emergencies.

## Before you go

- Print or copy a map of the area, with the route and key points marked.
- Note the names of major streets, bridges and landmarks along the route.
- Keep a note of the way back.

## Use the city

1. Read street signs at every junction, and check them against the map.
2. Use large landmarks: towers, stations, rivers, parks and public buildings.
3. Follow the main roads, which are easier to find and often lead to help.
4. In a grid city, count the blocks to estimate distance.

## Reading a city map

- Most city maps place north at the top, but check the compass rose on the map before you rely on it.
- Note one-way streets, pedestrian zones and closed bridges, which can force a detour.
- Use the scale bar to judge distances. A ten-minute walk is usually well under a kilometre on flat streets.
- Mark your position with a small cross, and update it at every landmark you pass.

## Find direction

- The sun works in cities too, if you can see it. See Finding North Without a Compass.
- Rivers and coastlines give a clear direction.
- Ask people for directions. Most will help if asked politely.

## Using public transport as a fallback

When walking is no longer safe or practical, a bus or a tram can take you to a known stop. Note the line number, the direction of travel and the stop name before you leave. Stations usually display a map of the local network, and staff can help with tickets. Keep a few coins or a card with a small balance, because mobile payment may fail with the phone.

## Adjusting for conditions

- **Night:** stay on well-lit main streets, and use landmarks with lights, such as stations and shops that are open.
- **Rain:** protect the map in a clear sleeve, and keep the phone dry in a bag.
- **Crowds:** move to the edge of the pavement and step out of the flow before you check the map.
- **Language barrier:** carry a card with the address written in the local language, and show it to the person you ask.

## Practice before you need it

Walk a short route through your own city during a quiet hour, using only the printed map. Note the junctions where you hesitated, and the landmarks that helped most. Repeat the route with the phone switched off, and time yourself so that you know how long a walk in the city takes.

## Common mistakes

- Counting blocks in an irregular district, where the grid breaks down.
- Walking away from the river without checking which bank you are on.
- Showing a phone openly in a crowd, which invites theft.
- Trusting a street name without checking the junction, because some streets share a name across districts.

## Troubleshooting

- **The street does not match the map:** check the junction you are standing at, then look for the nearest landmark to confirm your position.
- **The phone battery is low:** switch to the printed map, and keep the phone off until you need a call.
- **Everything looks the same:** walk to the nearest main road, which is usually easier to read than a side street.

## When lost

Stop, take a breath, and check the map. Go to the last point you were sure of. If you are still lost, find a public building or a police or emergency post, and ask for help.

**Warning:** in crowds and unrest, avoid streets with barricades or large groups. Take a longer route.
