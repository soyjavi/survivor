# Map and Compass Basics

A map and compass tell you where you are and which way to go, without any electronics. Learn them in daylight before you need them, because a bearing that is easy to set on a table is harder to hold in wind and rain.

![Sequence on a map: step-1 compass orient, step-2 line from person-standing to waypoint-pin destination, step-3 turn bezel to grid lines, read index; arrow-straight between; labels orient, align, turn, read; the bearing is set on the map before the body turns.](assets/map-and-compass-basics-1.svg)

## Why it matters

Phones fail when batteries drain or signal is lost. A map and compass do not, and they let you check the direction a guide or a friend gives you. Used together, they turn a vague plan into a line you can follow.

The pair also protects you from small errors. A bearing that is off by a few degrees becomes a large error over a long walk, so a practised navigator checks the bearing against landmarks as the walk goes on.

## Materials

- A waterproof map of the right scale, in a clear case.
- A baseplate compass with a rotating bezel and a direction arrow.
- A pencil, for marking your position.

![Parts diagram: compass with leaders to rotating bezel, direction-of-travel, north-arrow orienting arrow with needle inside, index line, graduated edge; labels as listed; the needle must sit inside the orienting arrow before reading a bearing.](assets/map-and-compass-basics-2.svg)

## Read the map

- Check the scale: the distance on the map that equals a real distance on the ground.
- Find the legend, which explains the symbols for roads, water and high ground.
- Contour lines show the shape of the land. Close lines mean steep ground.

## Orient the map

1. Place the compass on the map, with its direction arrow along a north-south line on the map.
2. Turn the map and the compass together until the compass needle lies inside its orienting arrow.
3. The map is now oriented. The top of the map points north.

## Set a bearing

1. Locate your position and your destination on the map.
2. Lay the compass edge between them, with the direction arrow pointing toward the destination.
3. Turn the compass bezel until the lines match the map's north-south lines.
4. Read the bearing at the index line.
5. Hold the compass level, turn your body until the needle is in the arrow, and walk on that bearing.

![Map with three waypoint-pin landmarks, line-solid bearing lines drawn back, meeting at survivor-mark; labels bearing 1, bearing 2, bearing 3, you are here; two bearings give a rough fix and a third confirms it.](assets/map-and-compass-basics-4.svg)

## Magnetic declination

The compass needle points to magnetic north, which does not match true north everywhere. The gap between the two is called declination, and it changes with location. Many maps print the local value on the margin. If the margin shows a value, set it on the compass before you take a bearing, so that the bearing matches the map. Ignoring declination puts you off line by a steady amount that grows with distance.

## Find your position

Take bearings to two or three landmarks you can see on the map, and draw the lines back from them. Where they cross is roughly where you are.

## Practice before you need it

Sit at a table with a map of a familiar area. Orient it, set a bearing to a church or a hill, then walk the bearing in a park or on a quiet field. Repeat the exercise until you can set a bearing in under a minute with the compass held level. Practise in light rain as well, because the bearing becomes harder to read when your hands are wet.

## Adjusting for conditions

- **Wind:** the map flaps, so hold it with both hands and keep one edge against your forearm.
- **Fog:** the landmarks disappear, so rely on a short leg with a known bearing and count your paces.
- **Low light:** use a torch with a red setting to read the map and the needle, and avoid bright white light that blinds you.

## Common mistakes

- Reading the bearing with the arrow pointing away from the destination.
- Holding the compass tilted, which swings the needle.
- Forgetting that the bezel must line up with the map's north-south lines, not the compass's own edge.
- Forgetting to correct for declination when the map shows it.

## Troubleshooting

- **The needle keeps swinging:** move away from metal, and check that the compass is level.
- **The bearing does not match the landmark:** check that the map is oriented, then take a new bearing from a second landmark.
- **The map and the ground disagree:** stop, look for a second feature, and check the map scale before you go on.

## Checklist

- [ ] Map oriented before you take a bearing.
- [ ] Bearing read and written down.
- [ ] Compass held level, away from metal.

## When it fails

If the needle will not settle, move away from metal objects, such as a car, a fence or a belt buckle, and try again. If your bearing disagrees with a landmark you can see, trust the landmark and check the map again.

**Warning:** keep magnets, metal knives and phones away from the compass, as they can throw off the needle.
