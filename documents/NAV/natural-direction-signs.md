# Natural Direction Signs

Nature gives clues to direction, but each one is a guide, not a rule. Use them with the sun, the stars and a map. A single sign can be wrong in shade, on a slope or near buildings, so always look for a second one that agrees.

![Sky view: arrow-curved sun arc east to west above shadow-stick on ground-line, line-solid shadow marked east-west; labels east, west, shadow line; the sun path is the primary check, the shadow line confirms it.](assets/natural-direction-signs-1.svg)

## Why it matters

When a compass is lost and the sky is covered, the landscape still offers hints. Knowing which signs are reliable, and which are not, keeps you from following a misleading clue into more trouble.

Most natural signs describe the past or the prevailing conditions, not the present direction. A tree that leans shows where the wind usually blows, not where you are now. Treat every sign as evidence to weigh, not an answer to accept.

## Sun and shadows

- The sun rises roughly in the east and sets roughly in the west.
- In the northern hemisphere, the sun is due south at midday. In the southern hemisphere, it is due north.
- Shadows are longest in the early morning and late afternoon, and shortest near midday.

## Trees

- In the northern hemisphere, moss and lichen often grow more on the north side of trees. This is not reliable on every tree.
- Trees and bushes often lean away from the prevailing wind.

## Snow and wind

- Snowdrifts often form on the lee side of a ridge or a tree.
- Wind shapes trees and dunes, leaning them away from the prevailing wind.

## Water and slope

- Streams flow downhill, and usually toward a larger river or lake.
- Following a stream downhill often leads to people, roads or villages.

## Rating the signs

Not every sign deserves the same trust. The table below gives a rough guide to how each one should be used.

| Sign | Strength | Main limit |
|---|---|---|
| Sun position | High when visible | Hidden by cloud |
| Star position | High after dark | Hidden by cloud or city light |
| Streams downhill | Medium | Can lead into a gorge or bog |
| Snowdrifts | Low to medium | Depend on recent wind |
| Moss and lichen | Low | Grow in damp shade on any side |
| Tree lean | Low to medium | Shaped by walls, slopes and past storms |

## Adjusting for conditions

- **Near buildings:** walls, fences and sheds shelter trees and shade the ground, so signs near them are unreliable.
- **On a slope:** the sun reaches one side of a slope earlier, which changes moss, frost and snow patterns.
- **Near the coast:** prevailing winds can be strong and steady, so tree lean is more useful there than inland.
- **Cloud:** with the sun hidden, rely on the stream test and the wind, and hold off on any long route until the sky clears.

## Practice before you need it

Spend an afternoon in a local park or wood. Find the sun's position at midday, then look at moss, lichen and tree lean, and write down what each sign suggests. Return the next day, and check whether the signs agree with the sun. Over time you learn which signs fit your area and which ones mislead you.

## Common mistakes

- Trusting moss alone, which grows in damp shade on any side of a trunk.
- Reading wind lean from a single tree that has grown next to a wall.
- Following a stream that disappears into a bog or a gorge.
- Assuming the sun is due south at all times, when it moves across the sky through the day.

## Troubleshooting

- **The signs point in different directions:** stop, and find a second source such as the sun or a bearing from a map.
- **A stream has become a deep gorge:** do not follow it into the gorge. Climb to the ridge and look for a path.
- **The sun is behind thick cloud:** wait, and use the time to check the map and mark your last known point.

## Checklist

- [ ] Two signs compared before you trust one.
- [ ] Sun or stars checked when the sky allows.

## When the signs disagree

If moss points one way and the sun points another, trust the sun or stars, and wait for a clearer view before you commit to a route. Stop and reassess instead of choosing the sign you like best.

**Warning:** these signs are approximate. Always confirm direction with the sun, the stars or a compass before you commit to a route.
