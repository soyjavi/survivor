# Marking a Route

A marked route helps the group, rescuers and people coming behind you. Marks must be clear, consistent and removable. A trail that makes sense to you but not to the next walker is of little use.

![Row of four: trail-marker cloth strip at eye level on tree, cairn, arrow-straight turn mark, do-not dead-end cross; labels strip, cairn, turn, dead end; one symbol per meaning, kept constant along the route.](assets/marking-a-route-1.svg)

## Why it matters

Marks reduce the chance of the group splitting up, and they give a searcher a clear trail to follow. They also let you retrace your steps when the weather closes in and the view disappears.

A marked route is also a record. Read back over the marks at the end of the day and you can see where the group hesitated, where a turn was missed and where a shortcut was taken. That record improves the next plan.

## Materials

- Bright cloth strips, in a colour that stands out against the ground.
- A pencil and a small notebook, for dates and destinations.
- Chalk or paint, only where it is permitted and safe.

## Choose marks

- Use bright cloth strips tied to branches at eye level.
- Use stones piled in a cairn, at visible points on open ground.
- Use chalk or paint on rock or walls, if permitted and safe.

## Mark consistently

1. Place a mark at every turn, every junction and every point of doubt.
2. Place a second mark a short distance on, so the next one is always in sight.
3. Mark a route back as you go, so you can return.

## Plan the marks before you walk

Mark the route on the map before the walk begins. Put a small dot at each junction, each water crossing and each point where a turn is needed. Then decide what mark you will use at each dot, and write it in the notebook. A plan like this means the marks are placed with purpose, not in the moment when the group is tired and the light is fading.

## Spacing and visibility

| Terrain | Mark spacing | Best mark type |
|---|---|---|
| Open moor or grass | Within sight of the last mark | Cairn or cloth strip |
| Woodland | Every tree junction | Cloth strip at eye level |
| Rock or scree | Each change of direction | Chalk where permitted, or a cairn |
| Snow | Every pole or fixed point | Bright flag on a pole |

![Path: line-dotted faint path through fog with trail-marker marks, bracket between neighbours; labels fog spacing, clear spacing; spacing follows visibility, so marks sit closer in fog.](assets/marking-a-route-3.svg)

## Leave clear messages

- Use an arrow to show direction, and a cross to show a dead end.
- Write the date and the destination on a note, if the route will be used by others.
- Keep the same symbol for the same meaning on the whole route.

![Three stages left to right with arrow-straight: cairn stacked, cairn lifted down, stones laid flat on ground-line; labels stack, dismantle, flat; removal takes the whole cairn so no false trail remains.](assets/marking-a-route-4.svg)

## Adjusting for conditions

- **Snow:** marks sink or blow away, so use tall poles with bright flags, and check them as you pass.
- **Fog:** place marks closer together, and walk slowly so that you do not overshoot the next one.
- **Forest:** cloth strips can blend with leaves, so choose a colour that stands out at every season.
- **Night:** reflective tape is easier to see than paint, if you carry it and it is permitted.

## Practice before you need it

On a short walk near home, place marks at every turn, then walk back and follow them without your map. Ask a friend to walk the route with you using only the marks. Any place where you hesitated is a mark that needs to be moved or repeated.

## Troubleshooting

- **A cloth strip has blown away:** stop at the last mark you can see, and place a new strip on the same branch line.
- **A cairn has been knocked over:** rebuild it on the same spot, using the stones around it, so the next walker sees the same signal.
- **Two routes use the same marks:** add a distinct symbol for your route, and note the difference in the notebook.

## Remove marks

Take down marks when the route is no longer needed, so they do not confuse others or harm the land.

## Common mistakes

- Placing marks too far apart, so the next one is lost in the terrain.
- Using natural-looking piles of stones that others may not recognise as marks.
- Leaving marks behind after the route has been used.

## Checklist

- [ ] Mark at every turn and junction.
- [ ] Next mark always in sight.
- [ ] Return route marked too.
- [ ] Marks removed when the trip ends.

## When a mark is lost

Stop, and look back to the last mark you were sure of. Search in a small circle around the spot, then return to it and try again. Do not guess a new direction and walk on.

**Warning:** never mark a route that crosses private land or sensitive areas without permission.
