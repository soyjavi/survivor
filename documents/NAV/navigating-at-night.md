# Navigating at Night

Night movement is slower and more dangerous than daylight travel. Move only when you must, and plan a safe place to stop. Terrain that is easy in the day can hide holes, rocks and drops that you only discover with a foot.

![Loop: person-standing, flashlight distant light ahead, footprints pace count, compass bearing check, waypoint-pin stop, arrow-loop around; labels light, count, bearing, stop; three checks repeat every 50 metres, not only at start.](assets/navigating-at-night-1.svg)

## Why it matters

In the dark, you lose the view of the land and much of your sense of distance. Small errors in direction add up quickly, and a missed step can cause a serious injury far from help.

The dark also changes how you think. Tiredness and cold make small problems feel large, and the urge to keep moving can overrule good judgement. A plan made before dark, with a clear stop point, protects you from that pressure.

## Materials

- A head torch with a red setting, and a spare battery.
- A compass with a luminous or lit dial.
- A map in a clear sleeve, with bearings and distances written on it.

## Before dark

- Choose a route you have seen in daylight.
- Mark key points on the map with a bearing and a distance.
- Check that your torch works, with a spare battery.

![Close-up: flashlight angled low over map with red light, compass beside, note margin with bearings; labels torch, map, compass, margin; keeping light close to the map protects night vision. NEEDS: red-light.](assets/navigating-at-night-2.svg)

## Keep direction

1. Use a compass with a luminous or lit dial, or a head torch to read it.
2. Pick a distant landmark, such as a star or a light, and walk toward it, checking your bearing often.
3. Use the North Star for direction in the northern hemisphere. See Finding North Without a Compass.

## Move carefully

- Walk slowly, using your feet to feel for obstacles.
- Count your paces to estimate distance. A common rough measure is about 60 paces per 50 metres on flat ground.
- Stop often to check direction and listen.

## Step-by-step in detail

Pace counting needs a steady rhythm. Practise it on a flat road in the evening, and write down your count for 50 metres. Then, on the route, count each pace, and make a mark on the notebook every 50 metres. Reset the count at every landmark you pass, so that a missed number does not carry forward for kilometres.

## Use light well

- Use the lowest brightness that lets you see the path.
- Turn the torch off for a few seconds to let your eyes adjust to the dark.
- A red light keeps night vision better than white.

## Adjusting for conditions

- **Clear moonlight:** the path is easier to read, but shadows can hide holes, so still slow down.
- **Overcast nights:** there are no stars for direction, so rely on the compass and a landmark on the horizon.
- **Fog:** stop if you cannot see the next marker. Fog turns the simplest path into a loop.
- **Cold and wind:** the cold saps concentration. Keep your hands warm, and stop to rest before the shiver starts.

## Practice before you need it

Walk a short route at dusk, and keep walking until it is fully dark. Check your bearing with the torch and the compass, then switch the torch off for thirty seconds and try to hold the same bearing by sight. Practise stopping, counting and finding your shelter spot, so the routine is familiar when it matters.

## Troubleshooting

- **The torch dims suddenly:** change to the spare battery at once, and keep the old one for later.
- **You cannot find the landmark:** stop, check the map, and take a new bearing from a second landmark.
- **Your night vision has gone:** use the red light for reading and wait a few minutes before you move.

## Common mistakes

- Using a bright white torch that blinds you for several minutes afterwards.
- Trusting a distant light that turns out to be a vehicle on a road you have not reached.
- Continuing after the route has become unclear.
- Starting a night route later than planned, so the walk ends in full darkness.

## Checklist

- [ ] Route walked in daylight, or clearly marked.
- [ ] Spare battery in the pack.
- [ ] Bearings and distances written down.
- [ ] Stop point chosen before dark.

## When the path is unclear

Stop, and make a shelter before you are cold. Wait for light, and check the map by torch only when you have to. Moving on unknown ground in the dark causes many injuries.

**Warning:** never cross water, a cliff edge or an unknown slope in the dark.
