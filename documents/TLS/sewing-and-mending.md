# Sewing and Mending

Clothing and gear wear out quickly in a crisis. Basic hand sewing keeps them in use, and a small kit is light to carry. A few stitches in time stop a small tear from becoming a lost jacket, so mend as soon as you notice damage.

![Strip: table-grid of three rows, labels running, backstitch, whipstitch; arrow-straight needle entry and exit on each; each stitch path is matched to its use on a seam or an edge; NEEDS: needle, thread, stitch-line.](assets/sewing-and-mending-1.svg)

## Why it matters

Clothing is protection. A torn jacket lets in wind and rain, a split glove lets in cold, and a ripped strap can drop a load on the way to safety. Replacements may not be available, and buying new items can drain money and time that are needed elsewhere. Hand mending also builds confidence: each repair shows that a worn item can be brought back into use, which matters for morale as well as warmth.

## Sewing kit

- Needles in several sizes, and a thimble.
- Strong thread: polyester or heavy cotton.
- Small scissors.
- Safety pins and a few buttons.
- Patches of cloth and a little repair tape.

## Basic stitches

- **Running stitch:** in and out in a straight line. Good for seams and hems.
- **Backstitch:** stronger than running stitch. Use it for seams under load.
- **Whipstitch:** over an edge to stop fraying and to join two edges.

![Numbered threading: step-1 thread cut, step-2 overhand-knot at the end, step-3 needle with leader labels eye and tail; the knot sits at the end of the double thread, not near the eye; NEEDS: needle, thread.](assets/sewing-and-mending-2.svg)

## Mend a tear

1. Clean the area and lay the edges flat.
2. Place a patch behind the tear, larger than the hole.
3. Stitch around the edges of the patch, then across the tear.

> @image Backstitched patch sewn over a torn sleeve, close-up photo

## Step-by-step in detail

Sewing a backstitched seam:

1. Start with a double thread, knotted at the end, and take the first stitch from the back of the fabric.
2. Bring the needle up one stitch length ahead of the first stitch.
3. Go back down into the end of the previous stitch, then come up one stitch length ahead again.
4. Repeat along the seam, keeping each stitch the same length.
5. Finish with two or three small stitches on top of each other, and cut the thread close to the fabric.

## Mend a bag or gear

Use waxed thread or strong cord, and sew through both layers with a backstitch. Reinforce stress points with an extra layer. Stitch around the edge of the patch rather than across the middle, so the load spreads.

## Keep it tidy

Store the kit in a small waterproof case, with a note of what you have.

## Adjusting for conditions

- **Cold hands** cannot feel the needle, so warm them first, and use a thimble or a cloth to protect the fingertips.
- **Wet fabric** slips under the needle and puckers. Dry the cloth before sewing, or lay a stiff backing under it.
- **Stiff leather or canvas** needs a stronger needle and a slower pace. Pre-punch holes with an awl to ease the way.
- **Low light** makes small stitches uneven. Work near a steady light, and pause often to check the line.

## Common mistakes

- Using thread that is too thin for the load, which snaps.
- Pulling stitches too tight, which puckers the cloth.
- Sewing a patch with no backing, so it tears away from the fabric.

## Troubleshooting

- **Thread keeps breaking:** the thread is too thin, or the needle eye is rough. Change to a heavier thread and a smoother needle.
- **Patch puckers:** the stitches were too tight or too far apart. Unpick a few stitches, and resew with even tension.
- **Seam opens after washing:** the stitches were too short or the seam allowance was too narrow. Restitch with a backstitch and a larger allowance.
- **Needle bends:** the fabric is too thick for the needle size. Switch to a thicker needle, or pre-punch the holes.

## Checklist

- [ ] Right needle and thread for the fabric
- [ ] Edges laid flat before stitching
- [ ] Patch larger than the hole
- [ ] Stress points reinforced

## Practice before you need it

Sew a practice sampler from scraps of cloth: a row of each basic stitch, a tear patch and a seam. Repeat it until your stitches are even and your knots hold. Keep the sampler with the kit, so you can check your technique when a repair looks harder than expected.

## When it fails

If a seam keeps opening, restitch it with a backstitch and a second row beside the first. If a patch lifts, remove it and try a larger patch on the reverse side. A repair that keeps tearing needs a stronger material, not more thread.

**Warning:** never sew near a fire or with the sewing kit next to a flame. Keep sharp points away from children.
