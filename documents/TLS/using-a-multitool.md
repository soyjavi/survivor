# Using a Multi-Tool

A multi-tool combines pliers, blades, a screwdriver and other tools in one piece. It is one of the most useful items you can carry, but it is a compromise, so use it for light tasks. Its strength is that it is always with you, which means it is often the tool that solves the problem first.

![Multitool opened: leader labels blade, pliers, screwdriver; knife and handle outline; arrow-straight to the lock; all tools share one handle, so one lock check covers each one; NEEDS: multitool, pliers, screwdriver.](assets/using-a-multitool-1.svg)

## Why it matters

A full toolbox stays at home, but a multi-tool goes wherever you go. It handles the small, unexpected jobs that come up every day: a loose bag buckle, a cut strap, a stuck lid, a splinter that needs grip. Those small jobs often decide whether a day runs smoothly. Knowing the tool's strengths and limits means you reach for it at the right moments and put a bigger tool to work when the job needs more force.

## Choose one

- Look for solid, locking pliers and a blade that opens and locks.
- Check that the tools fold and unfold smoothly, and that the locks engage.
- Carry it in a sheath on your belt, where you can reach it.

## Common uses

- Pliers for gripping wire, nuts and small parts.
- Wire cutters for fence wire and electrical wire without power.
- Screwdrivers for small fasteners.
- Blades for cutting cord, opening packets and light carving.

![Wire cutter jaws closing on fence wire: arrow-both-ways closing direction, leader labels cutting edge, jaw tip, hinge; the cut is made at the jaw tip, not the hinge, so cut close to the tip; NEEDS: wire-cutter, fence-wire.](assets/using-a-multitool-3.svg)

## Step-by-step in detail

Cutting a cable tie or strap with the wire cutters:

1. Open the pliers fully and check that the cutting jaws are clean and free of grit.
2. Slide the strap or tie between the cutting edges, as close to the fixing as you can reach.
3. Close the handles slowly and firmly, so the cut is clean rather than crushed.
4. Keep your fingers away from the cut end, which can spring loose and fly.
5. Close the cutters, then wipe any debris from the jaws before you put the tool away.

## Care

1. Rinse off dirt and salt after use.
2. Dry it completely to prevent rust.
3. Oil the hinges and locks every few months.
4. Check that the locks hold before each use.

## Limits

A multi-tool cannot replace a full set of tools. Do not use it for heavy prying, chopping or tasks that need force. Small screwdriver tips wear quickly, so match the tip to the screw head.

## Adjusting for conditions

- **Cold weather** makes metal stiff and locks harder to release. Keep the tool in a pocket close to the body until you need it, and use gloves with grip.
- **Wet or muddy work** lets grit into the hinges. Clean the pivot after use, and rinse out any sand before folding the tool.
- **Dusty sites** clog the blade locks. Wipe the lock mechanism each evening and oil it lightly.
- **Salty air** pits blades and hinges. Dry the tool thoroughly, and oil it more often than you would inland.

## Common mistakes

- Using the pliers as a hammer, which bends the jaws.
- Leaving the tool wet in a sheath, where rust forms.
- Locking a blade that is not fully open, which can close on the fingers.

## Troubleshooting

- **Blade closes unexpectedly:** the lock is worn or dirty. Clean it, check that the lock engages fully, and stop using that blade if it still slips.
- **Pliers feel loose:** the pivot is worn or the screw has backed off. Tighten it gently, then test the grip on a known piece of wire.
- **Screwdriver slips out of the head:** the tip is worn or the wrong size. Change the bit or tip, and press firmly straight into the screw.
- **Tool sticks when folded:** grit or dried residue is in the hinge. Rinse, dry and oil the pivot, then work the tool open and closed several times.

## Checklist

- [ ] Locks engage before use
- [ ] Tool clean and dry
- [ ] Hinges oiled
- [ ] Matching tip for each screw

## Practice before you need it

Use the multi-tool for a simple task every week: tighten a screw, cut a piece of cord, open a sealed packet. Practise opening and closing each blade with one hand, since that may be the only hand free in a real situation. Carry a spare small screwdriver tip if your model accepts one, and check the locks before each trip.

## When it fails

If a lock slips, stop using that blade and clean the lock. If a pair of pliers loosens, tighten the pivot screw gently and check it again. A tool that will not close firmly should be repaired or replaced before you rely on it.

**Warning:** keep fingers clear of the pliers jaws and the blade edge when closing and opening the tool.
