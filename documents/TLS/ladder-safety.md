# Ladder Safety

Falls from ladders cause serious injury every year. Set the ladder up correctly, and never work alone at height if you can avoid it. Most falls happen during setup or when the climber reaches too far, not from a ladder that breaks.

![Side view: ladder against a wall, ground-line, bracket for base distance labelled one quarter of height, three leader contact points; arrow-straight marks reach; the base distance sets the angle, not reach; NEEDS: ladder-leaning, wall.](assets/ladder-safety-1.svg)

## Why it matters

Height changes the cost of a mistake. A fall of two metres can break a wrist or a hip, and a fall from a roof can be fatal. In a crisis, the people who climb are often the ones who are tired, hurried and carrying tools, which is exactly when balance and judgement slip. A few minutes spent on setup protects the climber, the helper and anyone standing below the work.

## Choose the ladder

- Use a ladder long enough to reach the work without standing on the top rungs.
- Check for cracks, bent rungs and loose fittings.

## Set it up

1. Place the feet on firm, level ground. Use a board under the feet on soft soil.
2. Set the angle so the ladder's base is about one quarter of its height away from the wall.
3. Secure the top to the wall or gutter, or have someone hold the base.
4. Keep the ladder clear of power lines.

> @image Ladder foot set on firm ground beside a wall, with a helper steadying the base, outdoor photo

## Step-by-step in detail

Before you climb, check the ladder as a whole:

1. Look at the stiles, the rungs and the joints for cracks, bends and loose rivets or bolts.
2. Make sure locking braces on a stepladder are fully open and latched.
3. Clean the rungs and your shoes of mud, oil and water.
4. Walk the ladder into place, rather than lifting it over your head, so you do not swing it into wires or branches.
5. Set the feet, then bring the ladder up to the wall and check the angle with your arm or a stick.
6. Tie or hold the top, and test the base by pushing it gently before you climb.

## Climb

- Face the ladder, and keep three points of contact: two hands and a foot, or two feet and a hand.
- Keep your belt buckle between the stiles.
- Do not overreach. Move the ladder instead.

## Roofs

Working on a roof is a high-risk task. Avoid it when wet or in high wind. Use a stabiliser, and wear soft-soled shoes with grip. A ladder that rests against a gutter can bend the gutter, so set the top on a firm point instead.

## Adjusting for conditions

- **Wind** pushes a ladder sideways and can topple it. Do not climb in gusts, and tie the top to a fixed point when it is windy.
- **Frost and dew** make rungs and shoes slippery. Wait for the surface to dry, or add grip to the rungs with tape.
- **Uneven ground** can tilt a ladder in use. Use shims or a levelling board, never a loose stone or a stack of bricks.
- **Low light** hides cracks and slippery surfaces. Work only with a clear, steady light on the top of the ladder.

## Common mistakes

- Setting the ladder too steeply, so it tips backward.
- Standing on a paint tray or a box to gain height.
- Climbing with tools in hand, which unbalances you.

## Troubleshooting

- **Ladder slides at the base:** the feet are on a slope or a smooth surface. Reset it on firmer ground, with the feet fixed or padded.
- **Top slips from the wall:** the contact point is not solid. Move the top to a fixed post or a secure stud, and check it again.
- **Rung moves underfoot:** the rung is loose or cracked. Take the ladder out of use and replace it.
- **Stepladder wobbles:** the spreader bars are not locked. Lock them open before climbing, and check the feet are level.

## Checklist

- [ ] Ladder long enough for the job
- [ ] Feet on firm, level ground
- [ ] Angle about one quarter of height
- [ ] Top secured, or base held

## Practice before you need it

Set up a ladder at home on a flat, dry patch, and check the angle with a stick before you climb. Practise three points of contact on the lowest rungs until it feels natural. Keep a second person trained in holding the base, so the setup does not depend on one tired helper.

## When it fails

If the ladder shifts while you are on it, stay calm, keep three points of contact, and climb down slowly. Do not jump. If the ladder slides, reset it on firmer ground before you climb again.

**Warning:** do not stand on the top two rungs of a ladder, and never lean out to the side.
