# Essential Knots

Learn a few knots well rather than many badly. These cover almost every task in a crisis. A knot you can tie in the dark, with cold hands, is worth more than a dozen you only half remember.

> @image Two-loop bowline, front and side view

![Two-loop bowline, front and side view](assets/essential-knots.png)

## Why it matters

A knot is the only part of a rope system you control at the moment of use. A shelter line that slips in wind, a load that shifts on a vehicle or a casualty line that releases under weight can all come down to one poor tie. Good knots are quick to make, easy to check and simple to untie after strain. Most failures come from choosing the wrong knot for the load, not from the rope itself.

## Bowline

Makes a fixed loop that does not slip, even under load, and is easy to untie after loading.

![Numbered bowline: step-1 rope-coil with small loop, step-2 working end up through, step-3 around and back down, arrow-curved; labels: loop, working end, standing part; pull the standing part to tighten; the loop holds and unties after load; NEEDS: bowline-knot.](assets/essential-knots-1.svg)

1. Make a small loop in the rope, with the working end coming up through it.
2. Pass the working end up through the loop, around the standing part, and back down the loop.
3. Tighten by holding the loop and pulling the standing part.

Use it for: tying a rope around a post, a rescue loop, a secure line.

## Clove hitch

Attaches a rope to a pole or post quickly. Easy to adjust, but it can slip if the load goes in one direction.

1. Wrap the rope around the pole.
2. Cross the rope over itself.
3. Wrap again and tuck the end under the last turn.

Use it for: starting a lashing, tying a line to a tree.

![Clove hitch: line-solid pole, rope-coil with two wraps and end tucked, arrow-both-ways load direction; labels: wraps, tucked end, two half hitches; it slips one way, so add half hitches; NEEDS: clove-hitch, half-hitch.](assets/essential-knots-2.svg)

## Reef knot

Joins two ends of the same rope. Not for loads that might come apart.

Use it for: tying bandages, joining two ropes of the same thickness, tying a parcel.

## Taut-line hitch

An adjustable knot for guy lines. It grips when pulled and slides when loosened.

Use it for: tightening tarps and shelter lines.

> @image Taut-line hitch tightening a tarp guy line, field photo

## Figure of eight

A stopper knot that stops a rope running through a hole or block.

Use it for: preventing a rope from pulling through.

![Figure of eight: rope-coil end, overhand-knot style bight and loop, leader labels bight, loop, tail; arrow-curved tail back through; the bulky knot stops the rope pulling through a hole; NEEDS: figure-eight-knot.](assets/essential-knots-3.svg)

## Trucker's hitch

Gives a mechanical advantage to tighten a load.

Use it for: securing loads on a vehicle or a roof.

## Choosing a knot

| Task | Knot | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed loop on a rope end | Bowline | Holds and unties easily |
| Start a lashing on a pole | Clove hitch | Quick to make |
| Tighten a shelter line | Taut-line hitch | Adjusts while holding |
| Stop a line running through | Figure of eight | Bulky and secure |
| Tighten a load | Trucker's hitch | Mechanical advantage |

## Adjusting for conditions

- **Wet rope** swells and shrinks, so knots jam. Tie knots while the rope is dry if you can, and loosen them soon after loading.
- **Cold hands** cannot feel small movements. Tie the bowline with gloves off in short bursts, or use a bigger loop that is easier to shape.
- **Stiff or new rope** holds its shape and may need extra tightening. Tie a test knot and load it before relying on it.

## Common mistakes

- Tying a reef knot under load, where it can capsize and come undone.
- Leaving too short a tail, which lets the knot work loose.
- Forgetting to tighten a bowline before loading it.

## Troubleshooting

- **Bowline loosens or capsizes:** the tail is too short or the loop was not closed firmly. Retie it with a clear loop and a tail of at least a hand's width.
- **Taut-line hitch slips:** it has too few turns around the standing part. Add one more turn before pulling.
- **Clove hitch loosens on a pole:** the load pulls in one direction only. Add two half hitches on the working end.
- **Knot jams and will not untie:** pull on the working end, not the standing part, and work a thin tool into the knot gently.

## Checklist

- [ ] Knot matches the task
- [ ] Tails at least a hand's width long
- [ ] Knot dressed neatly, with no crossed strands
- [ ] Load tested gently before use

## Practice before you need it

Tie each knot ten times a week for a month, with a rope you can carry in a pocket. Practise with your eyes closed and with gloves on, because those are the conditions you will meet in a real emergency. Tie a knot once in the dark, to check that your hands remember the sequence.

## When it fails

If a knot slips or jams, untie it and tie it again from the start. Do not force a jammed knot with a blade, as that cuts the rope. Check the rope for damage before trusting it.

**Warning:** practise every knot until you can tie it with your eyes closed. Test a knot before trusting it with weight.
