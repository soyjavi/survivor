# Improvised Tools

When tools are lost, a few common materials can do most of the job. Use improvised tools carefully, and replace them with proper ones when you can. Think about the task first, then the material. A digging stick suits soft soil, while a hard task needs a stronger tool.

![Four-panel table-grid: digging stick, stone, rope-coil as wire binding, bottle scoop; each panel with leader and use label; tools carry labels dig, cut, bind, scoop; each tool matches one task; NEEDS: digging-stick, sharpened-stone, bottle-scoop.](assets/improvised-tools-1.svg)

## Why it matters

Most emergencies do not leave you without materials, only without the right tool. A stone, a length of wire and a plastic bottle can each stand in for a missing item, and knowing what they can do saves time and stress. The risk lies in using them past their limit. A tool that fails in the middle of a task can injure you, waste the effort already spent, and leave the job half finished.

## Digging

- A flat stone or a piece of wood can dig soft soil.
- A large tin can, with the edge flattened, can scoop.

## Cutting

- A broken glass edge can cut, but is fragile and can shatter. Use only in an emergency.
- A sharpened stone can shape wood.

## Lashing and binding

- Strong cord, wire or strips of cloth bind poles and joints.
- Wire from a fence or old wire can be used for repairs.

## Container and carrying

- A plastic bottle can hold water, and a cut-down bottle can scoop.
- A tarp, blanket or sheet can be used as a sling or a stretcher. See Fractures and Splinting.

## Light

- A tin with a wick and oil makes a simple lamp. See Oil Lamps and Candles Safely. Fill it outdoors, away from any flame, and place it on a flat, stable surface where it cannot be knocked over.

## Making a fire-hardened digging stick

1. Choose a green hardwood stick about as thick as your thumb, and cut it to the length of your forearm.
2. Shape one end to a flattened, chisel-like point by scraping with a stone.
3. Warm the pointed end slowly over embers, turning it so the heat is even. Stop before the wood blackens or cracks.
4. Let it cool, then scrape off the charred surface so the hardened layer remains.

## Adjusting for conditions

- **Wet conditions** weaken cord and wood glue, and rust weakens metal. Dry the material before use, and check bindings after each hour of work.
- **Cold conditions** make plastic brittle. A bottle that is flexible in summer may crack when knocked in winter, so keep it indoors until you need it.
- **Hard ground or stone** needs a stronger tool than soil does. Switch to a metal blade or a stout digging bar, and keep the stick for softer work.

## Common mistakes

- Choosing a material that is too brittle for the load, such as glass for prying.
- Using a sharp improvised edge without a grip or handle, which cuts the hand.
- Leaving the lamp unattended, where it can tip over.

## Troubleshooting

- **Handle slips in the hand:** wrap cloth or cord around the grip, and tie it off so it does not unwind.
- **Lashing loosens after use:** the binding was not pulled tight or the material stretched. Retie it with a tighter wrap and an extra turn.
- **Edge chips quickly:** the stone is too soft or the strike is too hard. Use a harder stone and shape the edge with short, light blows.
- **Container leaks:** the seam or cut edge is weak. Seal the gap with tape, or switch to a sturdier container.

## Checklist

- [ ] Material suits the task
- [ ] Edges smoothed or covered
- [ ] Handle or grip added where needed
- [ ] Tool tested on a small job first

## Practice before you need it

Make one digging stick and one cut-down bottle scoop at home, and use them in a garden or on a campsite. Note how long each takes, how well it works and where it fails. Keep a small roll of cord and a roll of tape in the kit, since most improvised tools depend on them.

## When it fails

If an improvised tool bends, cracks or slips, stop using it at once. Look for a sturdier material, or set the task aside until you have a proper tool. Never keep forcing a failing tool.

**Warning:** do not use glass or metal with sharp edges near children, and do not use improvised tools for tasks that need precision.
