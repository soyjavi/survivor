# Repairing Footwear

Good boots are a survival asset. A sole that comes loose or a torn upper can stop you walking. Most repairs need only tape, cord and glue. Repair early, while the damage is small, because a small tear is far easier to close than a large one.

![Numbered sole repair: step-1 sole with adhesive on both faces, step-2 tacky wait with timeline-marker, step-3 rope-coil cord clamp; arrow-straight presses together; the clamp holds the sole while the glue cures; NEEDS: boot, sole, adhesive.](assets/repairing-footwear-1.svg)

## Why it matters

Walking is the main way most people move when vehicles and fuel run short. A boot that lets in water causes blisters, and blisters turn into infections that keep a person off their feet for days. Feet also cool quickly when they are wet, which raises the risk of cold injury. Looking after footwear is therefore a health task as much as a repair task, and a pair of boots kept sound is worth more than a spare pair kept unused.

## Keep a repair kit

- Strong cord or thin wire, for stitching.
- Waterproof adhesive designed for footwear.
- A small awl or needle.
- Spare laces.
- Duct tape, wrapped around a card.

## Sole repair

1. Clean the sole and the edge of the upper, and dry them.
2. Apply adhesive to both surfaces, and let it go tacky.
3. Press firmly together and clamp or bind with cord until dry.
4. Reinforce the edge with stitching or tape.

![Boot sole with rope-coil cord binding over the flex zone, bracket marking the tightest turns, leader labels flex zone, cord; the flex zone is where a sole lifts first, so it gets the tightest turns; NEEDS: boot, sole.](assets/repairing-footwear-2.svg)

## Step-by-step in detail

Stitching a split upper:

1. Clean the split and press the edges together so they sit flat, with no gap between them.
2. Punch holes with the awl about a finger's width apart, roughly 3 mm from the edge, on both sides of the split.
3. Thread the cord through a needle, or fix a blunt end to it.
4. Stitch in and out through the holes, pulling each stitch firmly but not so tight that the leather puckers.
5. Tie off on the inside, and seal the knot with a little adhesive.

## Upper repair

- Stitch a tear with strong cord, pulling each stitch firmly.
- Patch a hole from the inside with a piece of cloth or leather, glued and stitched.

![Cutaway: torn upper, line-solid tear, line-dashed running stitches across it, glued patch inside, leader labels stitches, patch; the patch takes the stitch load, not the torn edge; NEEDS: boot, patch.](assets/repairing-footwear-4.svg)

## Laces

Keep a spare pair in the kit. A broken lace can be replaced with a length of strong cord, tied off neatly so it does not catch on brush or rock.

## Care

- Dry boots slowly, away from direct heat.
- Clean mud and salt after use, and treat leather with wax or oil.

## Adjusting for conditions

- **Wet ground** softens glue and loosens stitches. Repair in a dry place, and allow a longer cure time before walking.
- **Cold weather** stiffens leather and makes glue set slowly. Warm the boots gently, then repair.
- **Rocky terrain** tears uppers at the toe and sole edge. Reinforce these areas first, with tape or an extra row of stitches.
- **Sandy or salty conditions** wear stitching and corrode metal eyelets. Rinse the boots with clean water and dry them thoroughly.

## Common mistakes

- Using ordinary glue that softens in wet conditions.
- Pressing the sole before the adhesive is tacky, so it never bonds.
- Stitching with thin thread that snaps under flex.

## Troubleshooting

- **Sole lifts at the toe:** the flexing zone was not reinforced. Glue again, then bind the toe with tight cord and extra stitches.
- **Stitches pull through the leather:** the holes are too close to the edge or the cord is too thick. Re-punch further from the edge.
- **Patch peels away:** the surface was oily or dusty. Clean it with a dry cloth, roughen it lightly, and glue again.
- **Laces keep breaking at the eyelet:** the cord is rubbing a sharp edge. Round the end of the lace and wrap it with tape.

## Checklist

- [ ] Sole and upper clean and dry
- [ ] Adhesive tacky before pressing
- [ ] Clamp or binding in place until cured
- [ ] Stitches pulled firm

## Practice before you need it

Practise one sole repair on an old shoe at home, so you know how long the glue needs to become tacky and how long the binding should stay on. Practise a running stitch on a spare piece of leather or heavy canvas. Keep the repair kit with the boots, not in a separate bag, so the two are never apart.

## When it fails

If a sole comes loose again, clean and re-glue it with a wider contact area, and bind it harder. If the upper tears along the same line, replace the boot if you can. A repair that keeps failing wastes time you need elsewhere.

**Warning:** never dry leather boots close to a fire. They can crack and shrink.
