# Basic Sewing Repairs for Clothing

Clothing is expensive to replace when resources are limited, and a small repair done early prevents a tear from becoming a lost garment. A few simple hand-sewing skills cover most needs: running stitch, backstitch, whipstitch and a secure knot. Patience and tidy stitches matter more than speed or fancy technique.

## Tools and Materials

Keep a small kit: several needles of different sizes, strong polyester or cotton thread in neutral colours, sharp scissors, a thimble if you use one, pins, a tape measure, and scraps of fabric for patches. Sort buttons into a labelled tin. Reuse fabric from garments too worn to wear, as long as it is clean.

1. Match thread weight to the fabric, using heavier thread for denim and canvas.
2. Choose a needle with an eye that the thread passes through easily but that leaves small holes.
3. Thread the needle, tie a knot at the end, and keep working strands no longer than an arm's length.

## Essential Stitches

The **running stitch** is the simplest. Pass the needle in and out of the fabric at even intervals. The **backstitch** is stronger: bring the needle up one length ahead, then go back down into the last stitch, and repeat. The **whipstitch** joins two edges over the top and works well for hems and edges. Use backstitch for seams that take strain.

## Patches

Patch a tear or hole with a scrap larger than the damage by about a centimetre on each side. Turn the raw edges under and pin the patch in place. Sew around the edge with small whipstitches, keeping the stitches close. For thin fabric, place a backing patch underneath and sew through both layers.

1. Trim frayed threads around the hole and tuck in loose edges.
2. Pin the patch right side up, centred over the hole.
3. Sew in a tight, flat circle around the edge.
4. Fasten the thread securely on the inside with two small back stitches.

## Hems and Seams

A hem is a fold at the bottom edge of a garment. Measure the required length, fold the edge twice to hide the raw edge, and pin it. Sew with small slip stitches along the inside, catching only a few threads of the outer fabric so that stitches do not show.

Seams split under strain, often under the arms or at the crotch. Re-sew the split with backstitch, extending a few centimetres beyond the damage on each end. Reinforce with a second row of stitching inside if the garment will see heavy use.

## Buttons

Replace a lost button with one matched in size and colour. Sew it on with a shank or with thread wrapped around the stitches to form a stalk, which gives the button room to lie flat. Stitch through the holes several times, then wind thread tightly beneath the button and fasten off.

- Use a thread of the same weight as the original.
- Check the other buttons for weak threads and resew them now.

## Reinforcing Stress Points

Some areas of a garment take far more strain than others. Knee patches on trousers, elbows of jackets, the crotch seam and the corners of pockets all split first. Reinforce these before damage appears by sewing a second row of stitches inside the seam or by adding a small patch on the inside. Small reinforcement work now prevents a larger repair later.

Work on a flat, clean surface and turn the garment inside out when possible, so that the stitching is hidden. Keep the stitches small and evenly spaced. Large, loose stitches pull and tear fabric, while very tight ones pucker the cloth.

## Keeping Repairs Neat

Trim loose threads close to the fabric with sharp scissors, taking care not to cut the garment itself. Press repairs with a warm iron if one is available, or smooth them flat under a heavy book overnight. Neat repairs last longer and are more comfortable to wear.

**Warning:** Never sew while tired or in poor light, since needle punctures and cuts are common and can become infected. Keep needles counted and stored in a case, and never leave them in fabric where a child could find them. Wash hands before handling wounds or open cuts, and disinfect any puncture promptly.
