# Sharpening an Axe or Hatchet

A dull axe is more dangerous than a sharp one, because it requires more force and slips more easily. Sharpening is a matter of restoring the edge to a consistent angle, removing damage, and polishing the bevel. Most edges can be restored with a flat file and a stone, and the work rewards patience and steady, even strokes.

> @image Hatchet clamped in a vise on a wooden bench, with a flat mill file and a fine whetstone resting beside it.

## Understanding the Edge

An axe edge is made of two bevels that meet at a line. The angle between them determines sharpness and durability. Thin angles cut easily but chip quickly, while thick angles last longer but cut less cleanly. For a general-purpose axe or hatchet used for splitting and felling, an included angle of roughly 25 to 30 degrees suits most work. Check the existing bevel before you begin and match it.

1. Examine the edge in good light for nicks, rolls or chips.
2. Note whether the bevels are even on both faces.
3. Decide whether the damage needs filing to remove, or only honing to refine.

## Securing the Tool

Clamp the head in a vise with the blade facing away from you and the edge protected by a block or cloth. Hold the handle steady and keep your hands well clear of the cutting path. If no vise is available, rest the head on a sturdy bench with a wooden block against the edge, and brace the handle with your body away from the blade.

- Never sharpen a tool held only in your hand.
- Keep the work area free of clutter and other people.

## Filing the Edge

Use a flat mill file of medium cut. Place the file against the bevel at the chosen angle and push it along the edge in one direction, away from you. Use long, even strokes and apply pressure only on the forward motion. Work both sides equally so the edge stays centred.

1. Start near the heel of the edge and move steadily toward the tip.
2. Check progress by looking for shiny metal along the edge, which shows where you have filed.
3. Continue until nicks are gone and the edge is a continuous line.
4. Clean the file regularly with a wire brush to keep it cutting.

## Honing

Once the edge is even, refine it with a fine whetstone. Wet the stone with water or oil, depending on its type, and hold the bevel at the same angle. Sweep the blade across the stone in smooth arcs, alternating sides. A burr, a thin raised curl of metal, will form on the opposite face. When it forms along the entire edge, remove it by light strokes on the stone, moving away from the edge.

Test sharpness on a piece of paper or by gently drawing the edge across a fingernail. A sharp edge catches. Never test with your fingertip.

## Maintenance

Store the axe with a sheath or cover over the edge. Oil the blade lightly to prevent rust. Check the edge after every few uses and touch it up before it becomes dull. Regular light honing is quicker and safer than rare heavy filing.

## Checking the Head and Handle

Before sharpening, check that the head is firmly fixed to the handle. Grip the head and push it against a solid surface to feel for movement. A loose head must be wedged or replaced before the tool is used. Look along the handle for cracks, especially near the eye, where the stress is greatest.

Handles should be smooth and free of splinters. Sand any rough patches lightly and apply linseed oil to the grain if the wood looks dry. Keep the grip free of oil on the striking end, since a slippery handle reduces control.

## Choosing the Right Stone

A medium stone shapes the edge quickly, while a fine stone refines it. Coarse stones remove material fast but leave deep scratches, so follow them with finer stones. Keep the stone flat by rubbing it on a flat block, since a dished stone cannot produce an even bevel.

**Warning:** Always keep fingers and hands away from the path of the file and stone, and never sharpen with the blade pointing toward your body or legs. Cover the edge when carrying the tool. Wear eye protection, since metal particles can fly. Never use a damaged handle, and replace a cracked or loose head before using it, because it can separate in use and cause serious injury.
