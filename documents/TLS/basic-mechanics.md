# Basic Mechanics

Machines break in the moments you need them most. Learn to check the basics, carry the spares that matter, and do simple repairs. Most failures give warning signs first: a new noise, a drip, a loss of power or a loose part. Catching them early turns a breakdown into a short delay.

![Checklist: table-grid with four rows, step-box and do icons left; labels: fuel level, tyre, battery terminals, fastener; timeline-marker shows before every start; the check repeats at every start, not once a season; NEEDS: tyre, battery.](assets/basic-mechanics-1.svg)

## Why it matters

Pumps move water, generators keep fridges and radios alive, and engines drive saws and vehicles. When a machine stops, the job it supports stops too, and no workshop may be within reach. A check that takes two minutes before each use prevents most of the failures that happen far from help. Mechanical problems also spread: a loose bolt can shake off a guard, and a worn belt can damage a pulley that is expensive to replace.

## Check before use

- Fuel and oil levels, and look for leaks under the machine.
- Tyres or belts, for wear and correct tension.
- Battery terminals, for white or green corrosion.
- Fasteners, which can loosen with vibration.

## Carry spares

- Spark plug, filter and fuel line for small engines.
- Fuses and a jump lead for vehicles.
- Inner tube and patch kit for bicycles.

## Simple repairs

1. Clean the part before you work on it. Dirt hides cracks and worn surfaces.
2. Note the order of parts before you remove them. A photo or a row of labelled bags helps.
3. Replace worn parts rather than forcing them. Force bends and breaks what it was meant to protect.
4. Test the machine in a safe place before relying on it.

## Step-by-step in detail

Checking the oil in a small engine:

1. Stop the engine and let it cool for several minutes. Hot oil can burn, and it drains faster.
2. Stand the machine on level ground, because a tilted engine gives a false reading.
3. Remove the dipstick or filler cap, wipe it clean, and reinsert it without screwing it in. Read the level.
4. Top up slowly to the upper mark, using the grade the manufacturer specifies.
5. Replace the cap and wipe any spill before starting the engine.

## Fuel and oil

- Use the fuel and oil the manufacturer specifies.
- Store them in sealed containers, away from heat. See Storing Fuel.

> @image Fuel being poured from a labelled sealed can into a funnel on a workbench, outdoor photo

## Adjusting for conditions

- **Cold starts** are harder with old fuel and thick oil. Use fresh fuel, and let the engine warm up before loading it.
- **Dusty sites** clog air filters quickly. Check the filter more often than the manual suggests.
- **Wet weather** can make electrical connections fail. Dry terminals and plugs before testing, and keep the machine away from splashing water.
- **High altitude** thins the air, so an engine produces less power and may run rich. Expect slower performance, and do not adjust carburettor settings unless you understand the effect.

## Keep a log

Note the hours run, the service dates and the repairs done. The log shows patterns, such as a part that wears faster than expected.

## Common mistakes

- Overtightening bolts, which strips threads or cracks housings.
- Mixing fuel types or using old fuel, which clogs small engines.
- Skipping the test run and trusting a repair that has not been checked.

## Troubleshooting

- **Engine will not start:** check fuel, then the spark and the air filter, in that order.
- **Power drops during use:** a clogged filter or a fuel line with a kink is the usual cause.
- **Engine overheats:** check the oil level first, then clear dust from cooling fins or vents.
- **Pump runs but delivers little water:** look for air in the suction line and a clogged strainer.

## Checklist

- [ ] Fuel and oil checked for level and leaks
- [ ] Fasteners snug, not overtight
- [ ] Spares packed and labelled
- [ ] Repair tested in a safe place

## Practice before you need it

Run each machine in a safe, open area once a month, and let it run for a few minutes. Practise one routine job, such as changing a spark plug, while nothing depends on the result. Keep a printed copy of the manual with the machine, because a phone battery may be dead when you need it.

## When it fails

Stop the machine as soon as you hear a new knock, smell burning or see smoke. Do not keep running it to finish a task. Note the symptom and the last change you made, then check the basics again.

**Warning:** never run an engine in an enclosed space. Exhaust gases are deadly.
