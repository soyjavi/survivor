# Bicycle Repair

A bicycle moves people and loads far from fuel and roads. Keep it simple: check the tyres, brakes and chain, and carry a basic repair kit. Most roadside problems are punctures, loose wheels or brakes that rub, and all three are fixable with a few tools.

## Why it matters

When fuel is scarce or roads are blocked, a bicycle can cover ground that a car cannot. It needs little fuel, and most of its parts can be repaired by hand. A bicycle that is not checked, however, becomes a heavy object at the worst moment. A tyre at the wrong pressure wears out faster and fails more often, and a brake that is slightly out of adjustment can stop you in a crowd, on a hill or in the dark.

## Repair kit

- Tyre levers, a patch kit and a spare inner tube.
- A small pump.
- Allen keys and a spanner.
- Chain lubricant.

> @image Tyre levers, patch kit and spare tube laid out on a cloth, product photo

## Fix a puncture

1. Remove the wheel and take off the tyre with levers. Work slowly, and pry only a short section at a time.
2. Find the puncture by inflating the tube and listening, or by feeling in water. Bubbles show the hole.
3. Clean the area, glue the patch, and let it dry. Press the patch firmly for a few seconds.
4. Refit the tube and tyre, and inflate to the pressure printed on the sidewall.

## Step-by-step in detail

Adjusting a rim brake:

1. Loosen the cable clamp bolt so the cable can move freely.
2. Squeeze the brake lever gently, and hold the pads against the rim by hand.
3. Adjust the pads so they meet the rim evenly across their full face, with the front edge slightly toward the front of the wheel.
4. Tighten the cable clamp while holding the cable taut, and release the lever.
5. Spin the wheel. It should run free, with no rubbing, and stop firmly when the lever is squeezed.

## Brakes

- Check the pads for wear and the cables for fraying.
- Adjust the brake until the pads touch the rim evenly and stop the wheel.

## Chain

- Clean the chain of grit, and lubricate the links lightly. Wipe off any extra oil.
- Replace a chain that stretches or skips.

## Adjusting for conditions

- **Wet roads** reduce braking grip and hide grit. Ride more slowly, and clean the brake pads and rims after each wet ride.
- **Sandy or dusty routes** wear chains and bearings quickly. Lubricate less often but clean more often, because oil collects dust into a paste.
- **Cold weather** makes rubber stiffer and more prone to cracking. Check tyre pressure more often, since it drops as the air cools.
- **Heavy loads** raise the risk of pinch flats. Increase tyre pressure within the sidewall limit, and check the rack bolts before each trip.

## Common mistakes

- Pinching the new tube with the levers, which causes a second flat.
- Overinflating the tyre, which makes it hard to grip and more likely to burst.
- Lubricating a dirty chain, which turns grit into a grinding paste.

## Troubleshooting

- **Tyre goes flat again within a day:** the tube may be pinched or the patch has lifted. Check the tyre inside for a sharp object before refitting.
- **Brake squeals:** the pads are dirty, worn or set at the wrong angle. Clean the rim with a dry cloth, then readjust.
- **Chain skips under load:** the chain is stretched or the gears are out of line. Measure the chain and replace it if it has stretched.
- **Wheel wobbles:** a spoke may be loose. Tighten the spokes gently, and stop before the wheel is ridden further.

## Checklist

- [ ] Tyres inflated to the sidewall pressure
- [ ] Brake pads even on the rim
- [ ] Chain clean and lightly oiled
- [ ] Quick release or axle nuts tight

## Practice before you need it

Fix one puncture at home, in daylight and without time pressure, before you ever need to do it by the roadside. Time yourself, and aim to finish in under half an hour. Practise a brake adjustment on the same bicycle so you know what a correct setting feels like.

## When it fails

If a patch does not hold, try a new patch on a clean, dry area, or replace the tube. If a brake will not stop the wheel, do not ride. Fix the cause or walk the bicycle to safety.

**Warning:** a loose wheel or brake can cause serious falls. Check the quick release and nuts before every ride.
