# Knife Safety

A sharp knife is safer than a blunt one, but any blade can cause serious injury. Use good technique and keep the blade covered when not in use. Most knife injuries happen when the hand slips into the path of the edge, so most of the safety rules aim to keep your hands out of that path.

![Three panels in table-grid: knife in a sheath, person-standing holding the knife with arm clear, arrow-straight cutting away from the body; labels: sheath, grip, away; the blade points away from the body and other hand; NEEDS: sheath.](assets/knife-safety-1.svg)

## Why it matters

In a crisis, knives prepare food, cut cord, open packaging and shape wood, often for hours at a stretch. Fatigue, cold hands and poor light all raise the chance of a slip, and a slip with a knife is fast. Wounds from blades also tend to be deep and dirty, and in a place without a clinic they can become infected. Good habits cost nothing, and they protect the people who depend on you as well as yourself.

## Carry

- Keep the blade closed or in a sheath.
- Carry a knife on the belt or in a pocket, with the edge away from your body.
- Do not carry a knife in a bag without a cover.

## Cut

1. Cut away from your body and from your other hand.
2. Keep your fingers behind the blade, and your other hand out of the path.
3. Cut on a stable surface, with a board or a cutting mat under the work.
4. Keep the blade sharp, because a blunt blade slips more.

![Close-up: person-kneeling free, curled fingers behind the blade, knife with arrow-straight away from the fingers, leader labels knuckles, edge; hand position, not speed, prevents cuts; NEEDS: hand-curled-fingers, cutting-board.](assets/knife-safety-2.svg)

## Step-by-step in detail

Preparing a rope or cord with a knife:

1. Place the cord on a stable board, and hold it down with the hand that is not holding the knife.
2. Hold the knife with the blade angled away from the hand that holds the cord.
3. Make a shallow cut first, to set the line. Then cut through the cord with a slow, controlled stroke.
4. Keep your holding hand flat and well back from the cut line, so the blade passes beside your fingers, not onto them.
5. Put the knife down closed, or sheathed, before you pick up the cut piece.

## Pass a knife

Hand it handle first, with the blade pointing away from the other person.

## Cuts from a blade

- Clean the wound and stop the bleeding. See Controlling Bleeding. See Cleaning and Dressing Wounds.
- Deep or gaping cuts need medical care.

## Adjusting for conditions

- **Cold weather** numbs fingers and makes them clumsy. Cut with gloves only if the grip stays secure, and take breaks to warm your hands.
- **Wet or greasy hands** cause slips. Dry your hands and the handle before cutting, and wipe the blade when it becomes slick.
- **Low light** hides the edge and the line you are cutting. Work in daylight when you can, and use a steady light that falls on the work, not on your eyes.

## Common mistakes

- Cutting toward the hand that holds the work.
- Leaving the blade open on a table where someone can reach it.
- Using a knife as a screwdriver or pry bar, which snaps the blade or the tip.

## Troubleshooting

- **Blade keeps slipping on a cut:** the edge is dull or the material is too slippery. Sharpen the blade, or secure the material with a clamp or a second person.
- **Handle feels loose:** the tang or pins may be failing. Stop using the knife for heavy work until it is repaired or replaced.
- **Sheath falls off the belt:** the retention loop is worn. Replace the loop before carrying the knife again.
- **Hand tingles after cutting:** a tendon or nerve may be affected. Keep the wound clean, cover it, and seek medical care promptly.

## Checklist

- [ ] Blade closed or sheathed when not in use
- [ ] Cutting surface stable
- [ ] Fingers out of the path of the edge
- [ ] First aid kit within reach

## Practice before you need it

Practise carrying and drawing the knife from its sheath a few times at home, so you do not fumble it in a hurry. Practise a simple cut on a stable board with a dummy or a piece of cord, until your holding hand stays clear without thinking. Keep a first aid kit with dressings and a roll of tape near your cutting area, and check it every few months.

## When it fails

If the blade slips, let go of the work and check your hand before anything else. If the cut is deep, keep pressure on it and seek medical care. Do not carry on with the task until the wound is covered.

**Warning:** never try to catch a falling knife. Step away and let it fall.
