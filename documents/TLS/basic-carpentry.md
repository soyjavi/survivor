# Basic Carpentry

Basic carpentry lets you build and repair without special training. Measure twice, cut once, and keep the work square and secure. Good woodwork is mostly patience. A joint cut accurately needs less force, fewer fixings and far less time to fix later.

![Numbered flow: step-1 line-solid measure and pencil line, step-2 triangle of line-solid labelled 30, 40, 50 cm, step-3 joints; arrow-straight links steps; the corner check comes before the first cut; NEEDS: tape-measure, pencil, butt-joint, half-lap-joint.](assets/basic-carpentry-1.svg)

## Why it matters

In a crisis, wood is often the only material you can shape on site. A shelter frame, a repaired door or a shelf for stored food all depend on pieces that meet cleanly. A joint that is slightly out of square pushes the load onto a single fixing, and that fixing tears out first. Careful marking costs minutes, while a weak joint can cost a day or a collapsed corner.

## Materials

- Tape measure, pencil and a carpenter's square.
- A saw, plane or chisel, and a drill with bits.
- Nails, screws, wood glue and a few clamps.
- Sandpaper and a wood filler for small gaps.

## Set up a work area

- Work on a flat, dry surface at waist height.
- Keep the cutting line clear of offcuts.

## Measure and mark

1. Measure with a tape and mark with a pencil. Draw a line rather than a dot, so the saw has a clear guide.
2. Check corners with a square, or with the 3-4-5 rule: a triangle with sides 30, 40 and 50 cm has a right angle.
3. Cut a little long, then trim to fit. A board can always lose material, but it cannot gain it back.

> @image Carpenter measuring a board with a square and pencil, workshop photo

## Step-by-step in detail

Cutting a board to length:

1. Mark the cut line all the way round the board, using the square against an edge. Mark the top face and both sides.
2. Clamp the board to the bench with the waste side outside the support, so the cut does not drag.
3. Start the saw with a few short backward strokes to form a shallow groove. Hold the blade at a low angle, then bring it level.
4. Cut on the waste side of the line with long, even strokes that use the full length of the blade.
5. Let the saw's weight do the work, without pushing harder.
6. Ease off as you reach the finish, so the offcut does not tear the grain.
7. Dry-fit the piece before gluing or fixing anything.

## Joints

- **Butt joint with nails or screws:** the simplest joint. Pre-drill near the end of the board to stop splitting.
- **Half lap:** remove half of each board where they overlap, for a flush joint.
- **Lashing:** join poles with cord, for shelters and frames. See Essential Knots.

![Numbered steps: step-1 board one with upper half removed, step-2 board two with lower half removed, step-3 both overlapping flush; labels: half thickness, mirrored cuts; the two cuts must match exactly; NEEDS: board, half-lap-joint.](assets/basic-carpentry-3.svg)

| Joint | Strength | Best use |
|---|---|---|
| Butt with screws | Low to medium | Light shelves and boxes |
| Half lap | Medium to high | Frames and flush ends |
| Lashed pole joint | Medium, flexes under load | Shelter frames |

## Repairs

- Replace a broken board rather than patching it weakly.
- Use a wood filler or glue for small gaps, then clamp until dry. Apply steady pressure. Clamping so hard that the glue squeezes out leaves a starved joint.

![Parts diagram: two line-solid boards stacked, line-dashed glue line between, arrow-straight pressure arrows evenly spaced above and below; labels: boards, glue line, clamp jaws; pressure spreads evenly along the joint; NEEDS: clamp, board.](assets/basic-carpentry-4.svg)

## Hand tools

- Use a saw with the teeth facing away from you on the cutting stroke.
- Hold the work firmly, with clamps or a vice.
- Keep the blades sharp. A dull saw forces you to push harder, and it slips more easily.

## Adjusting for conditions

- **Wet or freshly cut timber** shrinks as it dries. Leave joints slightly loose, and check them again after a week.
- **Cold weather** slows glue setting. Keep the workpiece above freezing and allow longer clamp time.
- **Humid air** makes wood swell. Leave a small gap at the edges of panels so they can move.

![Cross-section: line-solid board with arrow-both-ways across the width long and short arrow along the length; labels: across grain, along grain, edge gap; wood moves most across its width, so panels need edge gaps; NEEDS: board, grain-lines.](assets/basic-carpentry-5.svg)

## Common mistakes

- Cutting before checking the square, so every later part comes out wrong.
- Driving nails into the end grain of thin boards, which splits them.
- Skipping the clamp, so the joint moves while the glue sets.

## Troubleshooting

- **Gap in a joint:** the cut was off square, or the pieces moved during setting. Re-cut the piece rather than filling a large gap.
- **Frame wobbles:** a corner is out of square. Measure both diagonals; when they differ, push the frame until they match, then brace it.
- **Nail heads bend:** the nail is too thin or the wood too hard. Pre-drill and use a longer screw.
- **Board splits when fixed:** pre-drill, and move the fixing further from the end.

## Checklist

- [ ] Measurements checked twice
- [ ] Corners square
- [ ] Holes pre-drilled near board ends
- [ ] Glue clamped until dry

## Practice before you need it

Cut a few offcuts into butt and half lap joints before you need them for a repair. Practise the 3-4-5 check on scrap plywood until it takes under a minute.

## When it fails

If a joint splits, stop, remove the fixings and start again with a new piece. A joint forced together will work loose under load, and it can fail when you need it most.

**Warning:** wear eye protection when cutting or splitting. Keep fingers well clear of the saw path.
