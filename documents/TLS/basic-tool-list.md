# Basic Tool List

A small set of good tools covers most repairs. Buy quality over quantity, and store them dry. Each tool should have a clear job. If you cannot name the task a tool solves, it probably does not belong in the kit.

![Grouped layout: five bracket groups, labels cutting, fastening, measuring, light, cooking; knife, saw, hammer under cutting and fastening, flashlight under light, pot-with-lid under cooking; groups follow the job, so a missing job shows a missing tool; NEEDS: tape-measure, spanner.](assets/basic-tool-list-1.svg)

## Why it matters

Without tools, a broken hinge, a leaking pipe or a torn tent becomes a problem that cannot be solved. A written list also protects you from two common errors: buying a hundred small gadgets that duplicate each other, and missing the one item you need. Build the kit from the jobs you expect, then choose the tools that do those jobs well. Most households already own half of the list, so start by finding and checking what you have.

## Cutting and shaping

- Sturdy knife with a fixed blade, plus a folding knife.
- Hand saw, folding or with a sheath.
- Axe or hatchet, for splitting and cutting wood.
- Sharpening stone and a file.

## Fastening and repair

- Hammer and a set of nails and screws.
- Adjustable spanner and a set of wrenches.
- Pliers, pipe wrench and wire cutters.
- Screwdrivers, flat and Phillips.
- Duct tape and strong cord.

## Measuring

- Tape measure and a spirit level.
- Ruler, and a compass for navigation. See Map and Compass Basics.

## Light and power

- Torch and head torch. See Lighting Without Electricity.
- Multi-tool, with pliers, blades and a screwdriver.

## Cooking and water

- Pot with a lid, and a cup and spoon for each person.
- Strainer and a funnel for water.

## Build the kit in order

1. Start with the cutting tools, because they are needed most often and are hardest to replace.
2. Add the fasteners and a basic set of wrenches that match the hardware in your home.
3. Add measuring tools and a spirit level, so later repairs are straight and level.
4. Add light, with a spare set of batteries for each torch.
5. Add the cooking and water items, and test each one at home before you need it.
6. Write the list on the lid of the box, and tick items off each time you check the kit.

## Store them

Keep tools clean, dry and oiled. Store them in one container, labelled. See Tool Storage. Keep the kit in the same place at home, so anyone in the household can find it quickly during an emergency.

## Adjusting for conditions

- **Cold climates:** oil can thicken, and metal handles become painful to grip. Keep gloves with the kit, and check that hinged tools still move freely.
- **Wet or coastal areas:** salt speeds rust, so rinse and dry tools after use and check blades more often.
- **Vehicle kits:** heat and vibration loosen fasteners and soften plastic. Keep the kit in a closed case, and check it after long journeys.

## Common mistakes

- Buying many cheap tools that break on first use.
- Mixing tools with different jobs in one drawer, so the right one is hard to find.
- Forgetting spare batteries and cord, which fail quietly in the kit.

## Troubleshooting

- **A wrench slips off the nut:** the size is wrong or the nut is rounded. Use a correctly sized tool, and do not hammer a wrench onto a nut.
- **A screwdriver tip cams out:** the tip does not match the screw head. Change to the matching size before turning.
- **A torch dims quickly:** the batteries are old or the contacts are dirty. Replace the batteries and clean the contacts.
- **A saw binds in the cut:** the blade is dull or the wood is not supported. Sharpen or replace the blade, and support both sides of the cut.

## Checklist

- [ ] Cutting tools sharp and sheathed
- [ ] Fasteners and wrenches in matching sizes
- [ ] Measuring tools present and readable
- [ ] Light sources charged and tested

## Practice before you need it

Use each tool on a small real job once a season: hang a shelf, cut a length of rope, adjust a bicycle seat. A tool you have used is easier to grab in a hurry, and you will discover gaps in the kit while the shop is still open. Make a note of anything you struggled to use, and replace it with a better tool when you can.

## When it fails

If a tool breaks, set it aside and replace it before the next job. Do not repair a cracked handle with tape and keep using it. Note which tool failed, and buy a better one when you can.

**Warning:** do not use damaged tools. A broken handle or loose head can cause serious injury.
